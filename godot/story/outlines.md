# Voidsworn story outlines: six faction arcs (for Ryan's approval)

Status: DRAFT, waiting for Ryan's approval (plan B5 exit gate, R37). Nothing below is built into the game until he says yes.

## How an arc works

- One arc per faction, three beats, one per sector. Each beat is a short text card (100 to 150 words) with a choice.
- The rival is always the run's **Nemesis** (R7): the one named enemy commander stream B rolls at the start of every run. His name and his faction change from run to run, so the text says `{rival}` where his name goes. In the examples below a sample name is shown in brackets.
- The rival can come from any of the other five factions, so each faction's beat 3 carries one line spoken by him in **his** faction's voice (the table at the end).
- Every choice only uses things the campaign already does: credits, repairs, experience, relics, a ship joining, the Nemesis clock and intel, an ambush. Each choice also writes a flag (the event system already does this), so beat 2 and beat 3 can remember beat 1, and a blue option can appear when you made a certain earlier choice.
- Beat 3 is the run's last Nemesis fight. Beating him there ends him (stream B's rule). The arc then closes with a short win or loss line and lands in a Chronicle on the Career screen. No rules reward for finishing (keeps the battle and campaign rules unchanged); a hull variant payout can be added later if Ryan wants one.
- Text only, no voice. Crest symbols, no portraits.
- The dead star at the end of every arc is the same place, the **Hollow Sun**, so the six stories share one map and one finale backdrop (the dead-star board, R2).

## 1. Voidsworn Crusade: "The Oath at the Hollow Sun"

Crest: a cracked halo over a sword. Voice: litany, oaths, saints, confession.

### Beat 1 (sector I, after the sector intro): The Saint's Hand

The fleet holds station above the shrine-moon of Saint Vesper while the litanists read the crusade's charge aloud on every deck. Three sectors of heresy lie between you and the Hollow Sun, the dead star where the first oath-fleet burned. Your charge is to relight its beacon or join them. The confessors bring a second matter. A heretic commander, {rival} [Warlord Grim Rattigan], has stripped the moon's reliquary and left the keepers nailed to their own doors. He did not take everything. The saint's iron hand was left on the altar, untouched, with a message cut into the stone beneath it: I will return for this, and for you. The keepers beg you to carry the hand into battle. The quartermasters want it sold before it draws him down on the fleet.

- **Carry the hand into battle.** Gain a relic. He knows you have it: the Nemesis clock moves on one. (flag `vesper_hand`)
- **Sell it to the pilgrims.** +60 credits. (flag `vesper_sold`)

### Beat 2 (sector II, entering the sector): The Burning Choir

The hymn-ship Ashen Vigil drifts out of the dark with her bridge gutted and her choir still singing over open channels, a recording looped by a dead hand. {rival} hung her in your path as bait, and his fleet waits in the debris beyond. Your captains want blood. Your confessor wants the choir's last verse recovered first, because it names the jump routes to the Hollow Sun. Every hour spent salvaging the recording is an hour he spends preparing. Every hour spent hunting him is an hour the choir sings to no one. The oath allows no answer that is both swift and holy. [If you carry the hand: the saint's hand has grown warm in its casket, and the crew call it a sign.] Choose which sin you will confess.

- **Recover the verse.** A Nemesis weakness is revealed, but he is ready for you: the clock moves on one.
- **Strike now.** The crews fight in fury: every captain gains 15 experience.
- Blue, needs the hand: **Raise the hand before the crews.** A weakness revealed and 15 experience for every captain.

### Beat 3 (sector III, entering the sector): Relight the Hollow Sun

The Hollow Sun fills the viewports, a black coal ringed in its own slow fire. The wrecks of the first oath-fleet still orbit it, cathedrals of iron bleached by a thousand years of cold. {rival} has anchored his flagship among them, using the dead as his armour. He hails you on an open channel. [rival line] Your litanists are already singing the Rite of Last Light. Every captain has written their name on the flagship's hull in chalk and ash, as the first crusaders did before they burned. If he falls here, the beacon is yours to relight and the crusade has a road home. If you fall, no one will be left to read your names. The oath does not ask you to survive. It asks you to finish.

- **Sing the Rite of Last Light.** Every ship repairs 20% of its hull.
- **Answer him with the litany of names.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- Win: *His flagship breaks against the old cathedrals and drifts into the star. The beacon catches on the third attempt, and for the first time in a thousand years the Hollow Sun throws a light. The litanists add a verse with your name in it.*
- Loss: *The chalk names burn with the hull. Somewhere a confessor writes the crusade's ending in a book no one will open.*

## 2. Hegemony Navy: "Pacification Order 41"

Crest: a wedge inside a ring of five stars. Voice: orders, ledgers, cold duty, the Admiralty.

### Beat 1 (sector I): The Listening Post

The Admiralty's orders arrive sealed, numbered and without courtesy. Pacification Order 41: secure the three sectors between the frontier and the Hollow Sun, a dead star the Navy wants for a fleet anchorage. Losses are to be held within acceptable limits. The definition of acceptable is attached as an appendix of nine pages. A second packet carries a single image: a Navy listening post, its crew lined up on the hull in vacuum, arranged to spell a name. {rival} [Dynast Setekh the Patient]. Intelligence has a file on him and nothing in it that helps. The Admiralty requests that you not pursue him without authorisation. Your intelligence officer quietly asks whether you would like the post's black box recovered anyway, on your own authority and off the record.

- **Recover the black box, off the record.** A Nemesis weakness is revealed; the salvage crew is seen: the clock moves on one. (flag `hg_offbook`)
- **Follow orders to the letter.** Admiralty commendation: +50 credits. (flag `hg_orders`)

### Beat 2 (sector II): Acceptable Limits

A Navy supply column, eleven haulers and a single escort, has been driven against the gravity shoals of sector II. {rival} holds the only exit and has not fired a shot. He is waiting for you. The Admiralty signal is precise: the column is within acceptable limits, proceed to the objective. Your flag captain reads it twice, then hands you a second message, from the column's escort commander, a lieutenant of nineteen: we will hold as long as we can. [If you went off the record: the black box gave you his patrol pattern, and you know where he is weakest.] To break through to the column is to disobey. To leave it is to follow orders and watch eleven crews die on schedule.

- **Disobey and break them out.** The grateful haulers patch your fleet: every ship repairs 25% of its hull. The noise carries: the clock moves on one.
- **Proceed to the objective.** Admiralty bonus: +80 credits.
- Blue, needs the black box: **Strike where he is weak.** The column lives, a weakness is revealed, and every ship repairs 15%.

### Beat 3 (sector III): The Anchorage

The Hollow Sun is exactly as cold as the survey said. The Navy wants it, and so does {rival}, whose fleet has dug in among the shattered survey buoys at the anchorage mouth. Authorisation has finally arrived, eleven days late and stamped in triplicate. [rival line] Your flag captain asks if there are orders beyond the obvious. There is one tradition left in the Navy that no appendix governs. Before a decisive engagement, a commander speaks to the whole line on an open channel, unrecorded. Every ship hears it. What you say will not be in any report, and the crews will remember it anyway. Then the guns synchronise, the column forms on your flagship, and the Hegemony does what it was built to do.

- **Speak of duty.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- **Read the names from the listening post.** Every ship repairs 20% of its hull.
- Win: *The anchorage is secured at 0412 ship time. Your report runs to a single line. The Admiralty files it, and then, without precedent, frames it.*
- Loss: *Order 41 is marked incomplete. Within a month a new commander receives it, sealed, numbered and without courtesy.*

## 3. Ashfall Corsairs: "Saints' Gold"

Crest: a skull with a gear for a jaw. Voice: shares, debts, grudges, gallows jokes.

### Beat 1 (sector I): The Gift Box

Word in the Ashfall dens is that a treasure fleet went down at the Hollow Sun, a dead star three sectors out where nobody sane goes. Old Mott swears his grandmother saw the manifests: crowns, reactor cores, a whole hold of saints' gold. Nobody believes him and everybody is going. You are going first, which makes you either clever or dead. Before you clear the docks, a runner brings a gift box. Inside is the tattooed hand of your last fence, and a chit with a name on it: {rival} [Seraph Caelis Nightbloom]. He wants the treasure, and he wants your crews to know he takes what he wants. The quartermaster suggests sending the box back with a bomb in it. The bosun suggests a party. Both have a point.

- **Send it back, rigged.** The dens bet on you: +40 credits. He takes it personally: the clock moves on one. (flag `cs_rigged`)
- **Throw the party.** The crews swear to the last scrap: every captain gains 15 experience. (flag `cs_party`)

### Beat 2 (sector II): The Rattlehold Auction

The treasure fleet's black box is up for sale at Rattlehold, a rock full of thieves in sector II. Every crew with a ship and a grudge has come to bid. So has {rival}. He has bought the auctioneer, the guards and most of the docking clamps, and he sits in the front row smiling at you. The bidding opens high and climbs. Your quartermaster counts what you have and goes quiet. The old knife crews say there is another way: steal the box, start a riot, and run for it under cover of the burning docks. The bosun says the honest way is to pay what it costs and owe nobody. Nobody has ever called the bosun honest before, and he looks uneasy about it.

- **Steal it and burn the docks.** A Nemesis weakness is revealed, but your next fight starts as an ambush.
- **Outbid him (80 credits).** The box is yours and he loses face: a weakness revealed, and the clock goes back one.
- Blue, needs the rigged box: **Remind him what was in the last box.** He flinches, and you take the box for 30 credits: a weakness revealed.

### Beat 3 (sector III): Saints' Gold

The treasure fleet is real. It hangs in the Hollow Sun's cold glow, a dozen haulers locked together by ice and old harpoon lines, their holds still sealed. Old Mott is weeping openly. {rival} got here first and has chained his fleet to the wrecks, daring you to shoot through his own prize to reach him. [rival line] The crews are counting shares out loud already, which is bad luck. The quartermaster reminds you of the Ashfall rule: the captain who wins sets the split, and the captain who loses gets a shove out of the airlock and a toast. Some of your crews would rather die rich than live poor. Some would rather live. Tonight you find out which crews are which.

- **Promise double shares.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- **Promise nothing, and mean it.** +60 credits held back for repairs.
- Win: *The holds open on the fourth cutting torch. Mott's grandmother was right about the crowns and wrong about the saints. Nobody minds. The split takes three days and two fistfights.*
- Loss: *Your hull joins the treasure fleet, locked in the ice. Some later crew will hear the legend and come looking.*

## 4. The Chorus: "Eat the Sun"

Crest: a ring of nine open mouths. Voice: hunger, many-as-one, song, short lines.

### Beat 1 (sector I): The Silent Node

The Chorus stirs in the dark between stars, and every throat aches with the same wanting. Far ahead, three sectors deep, something old is singing at the Hollow Sun: a dead star with a living heart, a seed of the first song. The Chorus must eat it or forget how to be many. The hunt begins. On the first night the swarm finds a thing it does not understand. A brood-node has been cut open and left to rot, its young untouched, its song carved out. Whatever did this, {rival} [Rear admiral Petra Lindqvist], wanted the Chorus to find it. It is a message. The swarm can eat the dead node and grow stronger, or carry its silence onward like a wound, so the hunger remembers whom to hate.

- **Eat the dead.** Every ship regrows 30% of its hull. (flag `ch_ate`)
- **Carry the silence.** The swarm learns his scent: a Nemesis weakness is revealed. (flag `ch_silence`)

### Beat 2 (sector II): The Drowned Song

In sector II the swarm finds a world that sang once. Its oceans are full of drifting spawn, still warm, still listening for a voice that will not come back. {rival} has seeded the shallows with mines and waits in orbit for the Chorus to feed. The oldest throats argue in a sound like grinding teeth. One part of the swarm wants to feed regardless, take the losses, and come out heavier. Another wants to teach the spawn the song, so they rise and fight beside the swarm, though the teaching will take time the hunt does not have. [If the swarm carries the silence: the silence has a shape now, and the swarm knows exactly where he waits.] The hunger has never had to choose before.

- **Feed through the mines.** Every ship regrows 30% of its hull. He hears the feeding: the clock moves on one.
- **Teach the spawn the song.** A Chorus drone joins the swarm.
- Blue, needs the silence: **Hunt him where he waits.** A weakness revealed, and the clock goes back one.

### Beat 3 (sector III): Eat the Sun

The Hollow Sun is singing. The Chorus hears it now with every throat, a slow, vast note under the static, old as the first hunger. {rival} stands between the swarm and the song with every gun he has left. [rival line] The swarm does not answer in words. It has never needed them. The throats are open, the broods are full, and every ship pulses in time with the dead star's heart. When this is over, the Chorus will be larger than it has ever been, or it will be a silence in the dark that some later hunger finds and does not understand. There is only forward. There has only ever been forward.

- **Open every throat.** Every ship regrows 20% of its hull.
- **Sing back to the sun.** The song shows his weak places: a weakness is revealed.
- Win: *The swarm closes over the dead star like a hand. For a long moment there is no sound at all. Then the Chorus sings with one more voice than it had before.*
- Loss: *The hunger goes quiet. In the cold, the Hollow Sun keeps singing, waiting for the next mouth.*

## 5. The Ithar: "The Grave of Houses"

Crest: a single sail over a falling star. Voice: grief, memory, long time, light.

### Beat 1 (sector I): The Lamp-Buoy

The Ithar remember when the Hollow Sun was bright. It was the beacon of their first empire, and the songships that died there were the last of the great houses. Now it is dark, and the voyage your house begins tonight is a pilgrimage to a grave. The elders ask one thing: bring home the light-archive sleeping in its core, before the younger races break it open for salvage. On the first night out, a lamp-buoy older than any living Ithar wakes as you pass and sings a warning in a dead dialect. A predator has passed this way, {rival} [Warlord Bones Sharkey], and fed on the buoys' memories. The buoy offers you what is left of its song. Taking it would end it.

- **Take its song.** You learn his routes: a Nemesis weakness is revealed. (flag `it_song`)
- **Let it sleep.** Your captains carry its warning instead: every captain gains 15 experience. (flag `it_mercy`)

### Beat 2 (sector II): The Glass Choir

In sector II the voyage passes through the Glass Choir, a field of crystal wrecks that were once songships, still ringing faintly when starlight touches them. {rival} has turned the field into a trap: every wreck he breaks screams, and the screams blind your sensors. He breaks them slowly, one by one, so you can hear it. Your captains are young, as the Ithar count it, barely four centuries, and some are weeping at their stations. The oldest of them, a seraph who sang in this very choir, says the wrecks can be quieted with their lament. It would cost time, and he would see you coming. [If you took the buoy's song: it rises unbidden in your throat, because it knows this place.]

- **Sing the lament.** Your captains steady: every captain gains 20 experience. He sees you coming: the clock moves on one.
- **Go dark through the screaming.** Every ship takes 5% hull damage, but you catch him unready: a weakness is revealed.
- Blue, needs the buoy's song: **Sing the buoy's song.** The wrecks fall silent: a weakness revealed, no damage.

### Beat 3 (sector III): The Grave of Houses

The Hollow Sun is a black pearl wrapped in a halo of dead light. At its heart the archive waits, the memory of every house that ever sang. {rival} has reached it before you and is cutting into it with cold, careful lances, as if peeling fruit. [rival line] Your sails catch the last of the dead star's glow. The voyage has cost more than any of your captains expected to give. When the Ithar die now, nothing is born to replace them. Every ship in this fleet is a line of a song that ends with it. And yet you are here. The elders sang that the Ithar would fade. They never sang that the Ithar would fade quietly. Raise the sails.

- **Raise the sails.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- **Sing to the archive.** It answers with his weak places: a weakness is revealed.
- Win: *The archive wakes in your hands like a held breath. On the voyage home your youngest captain learns a song nobody has sung in nine thousand years, and sings it badly, and the elders weep.*
- Loss: *The last sail folds. The archive keeps its silence, and the Hollow Sun keeps the archive.*

## 6. The Deathless Dynasty: "The Court Restored"

Crest: a crowned eye over a closed tomb door. Voice: patience, law, census, contempt for the living.

### Beat 1 (sector I): The Opened Tomb

The Dynasty wakes in orderly ranks, tomb by tomb, as it has always woken. The census engines report what was lost in the long sleep. Three sectors of the old crownworlds are held by squatters, and at their heart turns the Hollow Sun, the dead star where the Dynasty's first court was entombed. The court must be restored. The squatters must be corrected. One anomaly troubles the census. A tomb at the edge of the first sector was opened before your arrival, its warden dismantled with care and his crown taken. The thief, {rival} [Starwarden Ilyra Glasswing], left the warden's head facing the door, watching for you. The overseers propose a vengeance protocol. The archivists propose patience. The Dynasty has always been very good at patience.

- **Run the vengeance protocol.** The legions are honed for it: every captain gains 20 experience. He sees you hunting: the clock moves on one. (flag `dd_vengeance`)
- **Be patient.** The archivists salvage the tomb's tithe: +50 credits, and they keep studying the crown. (flag `dd_patient`)

### Beat 2 (sector II): A Crown on the Wrong Head

In sector II the stolen crown surfaces, worn openly by {rival} in a broadcast to every squatter world in range. He offers to return it if the Dynasty kneels. The overseers have calculated the insult to eleven decimal places. Below your fleet lies a second tomb-world, its legions sleeping, a reserve the old law says may only be woken to defend the court. Waking it now would bring more hulls to the field. It would also break a law older than most stars. [If you were patient: the archivists have finished their study of the crown, and they know where its maker hid a flaw.] The Dynasty does not forget a broken law. Neither does it forget a crown on the wrong head.

- **Wake the reserve.** A Deathless jackal joins the fleet. The law is broken, and he hears the tomb open: the clock moves on one.
- **Keep the law.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- Blue, needs patience: **Trigger the crown's flaw.** He is shamed before his own fleet: a weakness revealed, and the clock goes back one.

### Beat 3 (sector III): The Court Restored

The Hollow Sun turns overhead, black and enormous, its corona long since gone to ash. Beneath it the first court waits in its tomb, and {rival} has drawn up his fleet across the processional route as if he has any right to stand there. [rival line] Your fleet aligns in the old formation without being ordered, as it did when the Dynasty was young and the stars were brighter. The census engines finish their count: every warden, every crown, every insult, recorded and weighed. The law of reclamation is very old and very simple. What was ours is ours. What stands in the way is corrected. You have waited ten thousand years. You can wait a few more minutes for the guns to charge.

- **Recite the census.** Every captain gains 20 experience.
- **Wake the court's honour guard.** Every ship rebuilds 20% of its hull.
- Win: *The processional route is cleared. The court rises from its tomb and takes its seats, one by one, in perfect silence. The census records the correction, and moves on.*
- Loss: *Your fleet returns to the dust it came from. The Dynasty notes the setback. The Dynasty has had setbacks before.*

Used by whichever faction he comes from. Example: a Corsair player whose Nemesis is Deathless hears the Deathless lines.
