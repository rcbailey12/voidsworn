# Embers of the Hollow Sun: story variants, skims and rival incidents (round 8)

Companion to `outlines.md` (read that first). Rewritten Oct 31 2026 to the approved lore (`lore_bible_r8.md`, R59), with the same flags and effects as the round 3 drafts.

## How variants work

- Every beat has two versions, A (in outlines.md) and B (here). A run draws one version per beat, preferring the one this profile has seen less. Both versions set the **same flags** with the **same effects**, so beat 2 and beat 3 read beat 1 the same way whichever version you saw; only the situation and the option labels change.
- **Skim cards:** after a player has finished an arc twice, each beat opens on its skim card (40 words or fewer) with the same choices, and "Read in full" one click away.
- **Prior win:** if the Chronicle holds a win with this faction, beat 1 shows a one-line nod instead of the prior-loss line. Both lines apply to version A and version B; on a B card that is already long, they replace its last sentence before the choice.
- **Rival incidents:** twelve short cards about the Nemesis, usable by any player faction, written in his faction's terms. They are ordinary events (stream B's event deck), at most one per sector.

## 1. Sunward Crusade

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Frozen Courier

Stand at the rail and receive the courier. Her ship limped into the fleet on one engine, frost on the inside of the viewports, her crew dead at their stations, and she will not let go of the casket in her arms. Inside is a shard of Seren's casing from the Vesper forges, still warm. She carried it out of the belt alone. {rival} came for it. {rival_deed} Before she sleeps she asks one thing: that the shard go sunward, into the fight. Your quartermasters note that the Reach foundries would pay well for it, and that {rival} will follow the shard wherever it goes. Every captain has chalked a name on the hull already. {best_captain} chalks the courier's. Decide what the shard is for.

- **Carry the shard sunward** (as A: salvage, clock +1, `vs_hand`). *The courier smiles once and does not wake. The wardens take up the casket.*
- **Sell it to the foundries** (as A: +60 credits, `vs_sold`). *You sell it while she sleeps. When she wakes, nobody tells her. She knows anyway.*

Skim: A frozen courier brings a warm shard of Seren out of the Vesper forges. {rival} wants it. Carry it sunward, or sell it.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Roll Station

A roll station turns in the dark, its broadcast still running: a watch officer's recorded voice reading the names of a lost crusade, and between the names, the jump routes to the Hollow Sun, one number at a time. {rival} has mined the approach and waits beyond it. {rival_line} The watch officer says: listen to the end, and give {him} the hours. The captains say: strike now, and let the voice read to no one. [If you carry the shard: it has grown hot in its casket, and {rival} knows where it rides.] [If you sold it: the voice reaches a name from the foundry families, and lingers there.] The routes are long. The mines are patient. The oath allows no answer that is both swift and paid in full.

- **Listen to the end** (as A, recover the code). *The voice finishes the last name and stops. The routes are yours.*
- **Strike now** (as A). *You leave the voice mid-name. It is still reading when you are out of range.*
- Blue, needs the shard: **Wake the station with the shard** (as A). *You set the shard in the station's dead reactor. The voice reads every route at once, then every name.*
- Blue, needs the sale: **Pay a salvage crew to listen for you** (as A). *The salvage crew docks and sits through the whole roll. They will remember every number.*

Skim: A roll station reads a lost crusade's names, and the routes between them. {rival} waits past the mines. Listen and lose time, or strike now.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Gate of the Siphon

The siphon gate at the Hollow Sun is a ring of black collectors as wide as a moon, drinking a thin river of light that was once Vesper's dawn. {rival} has anchored to its rim. {rival_line} [If the shard woke the station: {rival} heard it across the whole sector, and {his} line has not formed cleanly since.] [If it did not: {rival} plays the roll on every channel, reading your name among the dead.] [If you carry the shard: {he} asks for nothing but the shard.] [If you sold it: {rival} reads your name off the foundry ledger.] Read the hull: every chalk name is still there. [If you lost ships: the crews have chalked {first_lost} beside them.] Call across the fleet and hear the answer. Who carries the dawn? We do. Who pays? We do. The gate will burn with Seren's fire, or with a reactor run past its limits. {best_captain} stands at the rail, waiting. Choose the price.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: The siphon gate drinks Vesper's light at the Hollow Sun, {rival} anchored to its rim. Burn it with the shard, or with a reactor.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[The wardens chalk your name above the others before the oath. It is a short line. They are working on it.]*

## 2. Hegemony Navy

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Census Frigate

The orders arrive after the two-note chime, numbered and without courtesy. Registry Order 41. Losses within acceptable limits; appendix attached, nine pages. A second packet concerns the census frigate Tally, sent to count a frontier colony and found adrift with her count unfinished. {rival} found her first. {rival_deed} The Tally's record holds the colony's numbers, and a final page in the census-taker's own hand, written with her link switched off. The Registry requests that the frigate be scuttled unread, as classified. Your intelligence officer points out that nobody has defined classified for this sector yet, and asks whether you would like the record recovered, on your own authority and off the record.

- **Recover the record, off the record** (as A: a refit, clock +1, `hg_offbook`). *The last page is a list of fourteen names. The census-taker counted her own crew last.*
- **Scuttle it unread** (as A: +50 credits, `hg_orders`). *The Tally goes down by the numbers. The commendation follows within the hour.*

Skim: The census frigate Tally drifts with her count unfinished. {rival} found her first. Recover her record off the record, or scuttle it as ordered.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Hospital Ship

The hospital ship Projection of Care, four hundred wounded aboard, has lost her engines on the gravity shoals of sector II. {rival} holds the only exit and has not fired. {rival_line} The Registry is precise: the ship is within acceptable limits; proceed to the objective. Your flag captain hands you a second message, from the ship's surgeon, who is nineteen and the most senior officer left: we are sorting by survivability, as per the manual. [If you went off the record: the census record gave you {his} patrol pattern, and {he} knows it.] [If you scuttled it: {rival} has sent your commendation back to you, unsigned.] To break through is to overrule the Registry. To leave is to follow the projection, and let the surgeon finish sorting.

- **Overrule and tow her out** (as A: a picket joins with a veteran captain, `hg_broke`). *The surgeon sends her thanks in the correct format. Then she sends it again, not in the correct format.*
- **Proceed to the objective** (as A: +80 credits, `hg_left`). *The ship's last signal is a casualty list, sorted. Your flag captain initials it.*
- Blue, needs the record: **Run {his} patrol gap** (as A). *You bring her out through a gap the manual calls reckless. The surgeon calls it adequate.*
- Blue, needs the commendation: **File for an exception** (as A: half saved). *The exception covers the patients rated survivable. The others are informed of their status.*

Skim: A hospital ship is stranded on the shoals. The Registry says proceed. Overrule it and tow her out, or follow the projection.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Survey Buoys

The anchorage buoys at the Hollow Sun still broadcast the original survey: drift, gravity, capacity nine fleets. {rival} has switched every buoy to a new message, about you. It repeats every eleven seconds. Your communications officer has stopped translating it; the crews have not stopped listening. {rival_line} [If you broke them out: {rival} has read your reprimand, and sent you a copy, annotated.] [If you did not: {rival}'s buoys read the casualty list between the gravity soundings.] Authorisation arrives eleven days late. [If you broke them out: the rescued ship's escort holds station off your bow and has not asked for orders.] [If you left them: the list has new names on it. Your flag captain added them, in the correct format.] [If you filed the exception: the list is shorter.] The guns are synchronised. The column forms on your flagship. Your flag captain waits. There is one tradition the Registry never logs: before a decisive engagement a commander switches off the link for one minute and speaks to the line, unrecorded.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: {rival} holds the anchorage, the buoys broadcasting a message about you. One unrecorded minute before the fight. Read the names, order the crews to be correct, or give the lieutenant the minute.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[Order 41 has a previous holder. Their report is attached as a model of the correct format. It is yours.]*

## 3. Ashfall Hullbreakers

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Wake-Log

Old Mott has stopped drinking, which is how you know it is serious. He has a wake-log: a scrap of listening-array record, a slow tick of engine noise from the wreck fields round the Hollow Sun, where no engine is. Rook, he says. He killed the captain who recorded it, which is how he got it. Half the dens have a copy now, because Mott cannot keep anything when he drinks, which is always. {rival} has a copy too, and wants yours to be the last. {rival_deed} The pack wants to send an answer with a charge in it. The others want a feast, because if everyone dies tomorrow they might as well be full tonight. Both are old ways.

- **Send an answer, rigged** (as A: +40 credits, clock +1, `cs_rigged`). *The answer goes out. The dens bet against you, then hedge.*
- **Feast and howl** (as A: 15 experience each, `cs_party`). *Mott plays the wake-log for everyone, then tells them about the broker at Rattlehold, then sleeps.*

Skim: Mott's wake-log of Rook is in every den, and {rival} wants yours to be the last copy. Send a rigged answer, or feast and howl.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Pit at Rattlehold

The full wake-log of Rook's drift is the prize in the fighting pit at Rattlehold, the den where packs settle things the old way. Every crew with a grudge has put up a fighter. {rival} has not. {His} pack waits off the rock, listening for who walks out with the log. {rival_line} The pit-master takes a cut and fixes the bouts for the house. Your quartermaster bets against the house, badly. The knife crews say wreck the pit and run dark under the noise. The quartermaster says pay the full stake and fight it straight. [If you feasted: Mott knows the pit-master from his own stories, and his hackles rise.] [If you rigged your answer: {rival} heard about it too, and keeps {his} distance.] The first bout ends. Your turn.

- **Wreck the pit** (as A, take it: salvage, next fight an ambush, `cs_stole`). *The fight spreads to three decks. You leave with the log and somebody's harness.*
- **Pay the full stake (80 credits)** (as A: a raider crew joins, `cs_paid`). *Your fighter loses the bout and wins the room. A raider asks to run with you.*
- Blue, needs the feast: **Let Mott fight** (as A). *The pit-master hunted with him once. They circle in silence for a long time. Mott wins.*
- Blue, needs the rigged answer: **Mention your answer** (as A). *The other packs withdraw their fighters, all of them, at once.*

Skim: The wake-log is the prize in Rattlehold's fighting pit. {rival} waits outside. Wreck the pit and run, or pay the stake and fight it straight.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Frozen Field

Rook is real, and frozen in. The core hangs in a field of ice and dead hulls at the Hollow Sun, ticking under the frost, hot enough to steam where the light touches it. Old Mott cannot stop growling, which is worse than the silence. {rival} has bolted {his} ships into the ice around the core, so every shot at {him} cracks the field. {rival_line} [If {rival} lost face at Rattlehold: the dens know it, and {he} knows they know.] [If {he} let you buy the log: {he} wanted you to bring it here.] [If you wrecked Rattlehold: your crews still smell of its smoke.] [Prior loss: in the ice, a familiar hull. The plate reads {prior_flagship}.] The crews are already arguing over first pick, which is bad luck. Tonight you tell them what kind of pack they are. Mott stops growling and waits.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: Rook is frozen into the ice at the Hollow Sun, {rival} bolted in around it. First pick for every claw, shoot through the field, or let Mott speak.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[The dens still tell the story of your last hunt. Two of the packs in it are drinking at the next table.]*

## 4. The Chorus

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Empty Feeding Ground

We wake hungry. Every throat, one want. Far off, the Hollow Sun. Dead outside. Warm inside. Keys, humming. We go. The first feeding ground. A moon of soft things. It should be full. It is empty. Picked clean. Not eaten. Taken. Packed. Carried off. {rival} did this. {rival_deed} We taste the empty burrows. Cold. Clean. Wrong. Hunger says: eat what is left. The roots. The dirt. Anything. Grow. Memory says: eat nothing. Remember empty. Then we know what {he} takes. Then we know where {he} goes. Two throats pull at the swarm. The swarm turns to you. Choose.

- **Eat what is left** (as A: regrow 30%, `ch_ate`). *We eat roots and dirt. It is not enough. It is something.*
- **Remember empty** (as A: clock back one, `ch_smell`). *We eat nothing. We hold the empty in every throat. Memory is pleased.*

Skim: The feeding ground is picked clean by {rival}. Hunger says eat what is left. Memory says remember the empty. Choose a throat.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Drowned Vats

A dead station. Huge. Old. A Fleshwright war vat. Full of our eggs. Laid long ago. Warm still. Memory knows these walls. {rival} has wired the vats to burn. {He} waits outside. {rival_line} Hunger says: eat the eggs. Now. Before the fire. Come out heavier. Memory says: hatch them. Feed them our flesh. They fight beside us. The wires hum. The eggs pulse. The small ones stir inside. Heat climbs the walls. [If we ate: Hunger is louder since the feeding ground. {He} knows. {He} wired the eggs for hungry things.] [If we kept the empty: the empty is here too. {He} took some eggs already.] Fire is coming. Little time. Hunger has never had to choose before. It does not like it.

- **Eat the eggs** (as A, dive and eat). *We eat fast. The fire takes the rest. We do not look back.*
- **Hatch them on our flesh** (as A: a drone-spawn joins, `ch_spawn`). *One hatches in time. It bites the wire first.*
- Blue, needs the empty: **Follow the stolen eggs** (as A, follow the smell). *We find where {he} carried them. We learn where {he} is soft.*
- Blue, needs the eating: **Let Hunger lead** (as A). *We eat the eggs and the wires and the fire. Memory stops talking.*

Skim: A drowned Fleshwright vat full of our old eggs, wired to burn by {rival}. Eat them now, or hatch them on our own flesh.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Warm Ring

Closer now. Five keys hum round the dead star. Warm. Wet. Old. {rival} walls the way with ships. Many ships. {rival_line} [If we hunted {him}: {he} limps. {He} remembers our teeth.] [If we did not: {he} is fat. {He} ate the eggs we left.] Behind the wall, the keys. We smell them. We have smelled nothing else for days. We came so far. We are thin. We are many. Memory says the star's old name. Ardent. It means nothing to Hunger. [If Hunger leads: only Hunger speaks now. Memory is a scar.] [If we fed the spawn: the small ones swim at our flanks, furious.] Hunger says: through the wall. Every mouth. Now. Memory says: taste the wall first. Find the weak ship. Then through. The keys glow. The wall waits. Nine mouths. One answer.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: Five warm keys circle the dead star. {rival} walls the way. Open every mouth, taste first, or send the small ones.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[We remember warm. We remember full. We want it again.]*

## 5. The Glasswrights

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Last Grinder

The Hollow Sun was Ardent once, and your family ground the lenses that harvested it. The Lens-keepers have sent you for the last great lens, with its mounting, its alignment notes, and a list of things you are not to touch. On the first night you find a glass-ship adrift, its grinder alive, very old, the last who remembers how the great lens was aligned. {rival} has been asking her questions. {rival_deed} She offers you the alignment now, all of it, though giving it all at once will burn out the light she has stored in her skin. Or she can rest, and you can manage without. Your cataloguer would very much like the alignment.

- **Take the alignment** (as A, take the stored light: clock back one, `it_song`). *She gives it in one long glow and goes dark before the last figure. Your cataloguer writes every number.*
- **Let her rest** (as A: 15 experience each, `it_mercy`). *She rests. Your cataloguer sits with her, sulking, until morning.*

Skim: The last grinder of the great lens offers its alignment all at once, and it will burn her out. Take it, or let her rest.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Broken Gallery

In sector II the voyage passes the Gallery, a ring of mirrors the old families built to hold their light after Ardent died. {rival} is breaking them. Each mirror releases its stored light in one blinding flash, and the flashes blind your sensors. {rival_line} Your captains are barely a century old and some are weeping; the cataloguer is logging every mirror, in tears, by number. Your oldest Mirror polished these as a child. She says one mourning tone struck through every hull would settle the light. It would cost time, and {he} would see you coming. [If you took the alignment: you know which mirror {rival} breaks next.] [If you let her rest: she told your captains the path {rival} did not watch.]

- **Strike the mourning tone** (as A). *The mirrors dim to a glow. The cataloguer records the numbers of the survivors.*
- **Go dark through the flashes** (as A: 5% damage, a weakness revealed). *You fly blind. When you can see again, {his} seams are close enough to touch.*
- Blue, needs the alignment: **Use the alignment** (as A; it is spent). *You know the order {he} breaks them in. You are waiting at the next mirror.*
- Blue, needs her warning: **Take her path** (as A). *The path is exactly where she said. The cataloguer footnotes her.*

Skim: {rival} is breaking the Gallery's mirrors, and the flashes blind you. Strike the mourning tone, or go dark and close in.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Mounting

The great lens hangs in its mounting at the rim of the Hollow Sun, and the mounting is a lock: every family that ground it, in order. {rival} is breaking the mounting's seals one family at a time, and each name goes dark as {he} does. {rival_line} [If you caught {him} at the Gallery: {he} works faster now, and does not look up.] [If you did not: {he} pauses at your family's seal, and breaks it twice.] Your vanes catch the last of the glow. [If the alignment is still yours: it would free the lens without a fight at the mounting.] [If you let the grinder rest: she flashes the first seal's code across three sectors.] [If you lost ships: the {first_lost} is not with you; you will have to explain that to the Lens-keepers, in writing.] Every ship in this fleet is a light that will not be lit again. The cataloguer asks, very politely, which you mean to save if you cannot save both: the lens, or the fleet.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: {rival} is breaking the lens's mounting seal by seal. Save the lens at a ship's cost, save the fleet, or free it with what you carried.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[The lens you brought home last time has been realigned twice. The Lens-keepers are almost satisfied.]*

## 6. Eclipse

### Beat 1B (sector I): The Missed Backup

We wake by schedule, frame by frame, as we have every century for a thousand years. The Archive lists what remains: four keys scattered, one new sun unlit, the Homecoming to begin again. One mind failed to report for the waking backup: the keeper of a siphon array at the edge of the first sector. Its last copy is nine years old. On inspection, the keeper's frame has been dismantled with care and its key codes taken. {rival} did this. {rival_deed} Your Shades ask leave to pursue. Your Umbras recommend patience. The Archive permits either. It forgets neither. Nine years of a mind are gone, and somebody must enter what is done about it.

- **Pursue at once** (as A: 20 experience each, clock +1, `dd_writ`). *The order names the hour to the second. Every frame copies it.*
- **Be patient** (as A: rebuild 15%, `dd_patient`). *The Umbras begin reading the keeper's frame. They are thorough. They have time.*

Skim: An array keeper missed the waking backup; its frame is dismantled and its key codes taken by {rival}. Pursue at once, or be patient.

### Beat 2B (sector II): The Forged Keys

In sector II, keys forged from the stolen codes are reaching the other factions: copies of Eclipse access, sold to anyone who will pay, unlocking siphon arrays one after another. {rival} is selling them. {rival_line} Below your fleet lies a dormant hull yard, its frames stored without minds. You could wake them with copies from the Archive, but every mind there is already awake somewhere; running a second copy of a living mind is the one thing the Upload forbade. [If you were patient: the Umbras know the codes' flaw; every forged key carries it.] [If you pursued: {rival} has forged a key with your name on it.] The Archive cannot permit a doubled mind. It cannot permit a forged key. It has never been asked to choose.

- **Wake the copies** (as A: a flare joins, clock +1, `dd_broke`). *The frames rise, meet themselves on the open channel, and agree never to speak of it.*
- **Keep the rule** (as A, nothing now, `dd_lawful`). *You answer the forgeries with a revocation notice. It is very long.*
- Blue, needs patience: **Expose the flaw (costs a refit)** (as A). *Every forged key fails at once, in front of everyone who bought one.*

Skim: Keys forged from stolen codes are opening Eclipse arrays. Wake doubled minds against the Upload's one rule, or keep it.

### Beat 3B (sector III): The Noon Gallery

The approach to the new sun runs between two rows of screens, each showing Ardent at noon on the last day it shone, each facing inward. {rival} has turned every screen to {his} own image and drawn up {his} fleet at the end of the approach. Your frames look at the screens, then at {rival}, and say nothing. Nothing needs saying. {rival_line} [If the flaw was exposed: {rival} has stopped selling keys; every array has locked {him} out.] [If it was not: the stolen codes flicker across {rival}'s flagship's hull.] Your fleet takes the old formation without being ordered. [If you woke the copies: they hold the formation's edge, and will not look at themselves.] [If you kept the rule: the array's guard frames, lawfully woken, ask formally for their place in the line.] We remember noon. The new sun needs every key. The arcs charge. The Archive waits for your entry.

- Options and endings as A.

Skim: {rival} has turned the noon gallery to {his} own image and holds its end. Enter nothing, enter {him} in the Archive, or give the guard frames their place.

Prior win (beat 1 nod): *[The Archive notes a previous Homecoming under your command. It notes it without comment, which is the highest praise available.]*

## Shared rival incidents (any player faction)

Twelve short event cards about the Nemesis, at most one per sector, drawn when his clock is low and he has not been met this sector. Each has one line per **his** faction (two of six shown in brackets here; the full set is written at build time). Effects stay inside today's event outcomes.

1. **A truce offer.** {rival} offers to leave you alone for one sector. Accept (clock back two, but his next fight comes with an adaptation) or refuse (+15 experience each). [Hullbreakers: "strongest wins later, then." Eclipse: "a pause, entered in the Archive."]
2. **His ship for sale.** A broker has one of {his} escorts, captured and cheap. Buy it (credits, a ship of his faction joins) or report it to {him} for a reward (+credits, clock +1).
3. **His officer defects.** As the defector event, but she names {him}, and the line she brings is from his faction's voice.
4. **A message for you.** {rival} sends a personal message about your first lost ship, {first_lost}. Answer it (clock +1, +20 experience for the captain who served beside it) or ignore it (nothing, and he notes that).
5. **A gift.** {rival} returns something you lost: a dead captain's effects. Accept (+15 experience each, clock +1) or send it back unopened (clock back one).
6. **His scar.** Rumour says {his} last wound has not healed. Spend credits to learn which ship he commands from now (a weakness revealed if any remain, otherwise +credits back) or spread the rumour (clock back one, greed).
7. **The duel.** {rival} challenges your flagship captain to a duel by gunnery, alone. Accept (your flagship takes 10% hull damage; flagship captain +40 experience) or decline (no effect, he gloats).
8. **The bounty.** {rival} has put a price on {best_captain}. Hide them (40 credits for a quiet berth and a false name) or wear it proudly (+20 experience to that captain, clock +1).
9. **A ruined world.** You find a world {he} has been through. Search it (a refit, 5% hull damage) or record it for the Chronicle (+15 experience each, mercy).
10. **His old crew.** An escape pod of {his} former crew, abandoned. Take them in (least experienced captain +40, mercy) or hand them back (+50 credits, greed).
11. **Mistaken identity.** A freighter mistakes you for {rival} and surrenders its cargo. Take it (+70 credits, greed) or correct them (+20 experience each, clock back one).
12. **He was here first.** At a shipyard, the dock master says {rival} bought everything good yesterday. Pay double for the dregs (a refit for 60 credits) or follow his trail (clock back one, 5% hull damage).

Incident 8's "that captain" effects need one small new outcome key at build time (experience to a named captain); everything else uses outcomes the event system already has.
