# Pyre-Barge round 2, issue 111

Double-click `RUN_SURFACE_ROUND2.bat`. It queues GPU lane A through the existing PC watchdog. Free tools only: Blender 4.5.14, OPTIX on the RTX 5080, Godot 4.7.2, NumPy, and Pillow from ComfyUI's bundled Python. Nothing is downloaded. No live game asset is replaced.

The retained review is in [issue75b](../../../docs/shots/ship_pipeline/issue75b/README.md). Opus's full captures are also copied into `Downloads\voidsworn_test\board\issue-111\shots`. Intermediate models, maps, copies of the game, logs and import caches stay in `%TEMP%\voidsworn_issue111`.

`round2_build.py` first creates one shared eight-band Hullbreaker trim sheet. All three variants use its colour, roughness, metal, fine normals, vent and hazard trims. Four clean lofts follow the donor's carrier, port, starboard and upper captured-hull masses. Their bow faces are vertical, with armoured flat bellies and chines. Weighted normals, bevels, mounted equipment, radiators, forward/outward weapons and engine hardware replace the sparse round 1 kit. Surface plates use unequal sizes and staggered locations, with physical bolts, vents, hatches, conduits and registration markings. There is no concept-paint projection or colour sampled from the donor.

- `Aplus` adds the most authored surface clusters.
- `Bake` retains the same clean shell, then selected-to-active bakes the current donor's tangent normals, AO and geometry pointiness. Pointiness is Cycles' curvature estimate, baked through emission without light or albedo. A local cavity residual drives occlusion. The normal filter subtracts the low-frequency part of `normalize(raw) - (0,0,1)`, adds the flat tangent normal back, caps tilt, then renormalizes. The shared trim is resampled from UV1 into the unique UV0 atlas, so every exported texture uses one UV set reliably in Godot. It never recentres the full encoded map to grey.
- `Reuse` crops small machinery and sensor chunks from the current donor, welds coincident vertices, removes collapsed triangles and tiny loose islands, gives each an explicit flat bottom face and mounting skirt, and seats it on a raycast mount. Array-based cleaning avoids a corrupt donor edge loop that stalled BMesh traversal. Exposed side cuts still need the visual sweep. Chunks are limited to 0.062 donor units, about six percent of the ship length. No large hull section or source paint is retained.

`round2_probe.gd` inherits round 1's exact isolated lighting, camera and measurement loop. It records today plus the three variants at r=170, close zoom, top, side and below. It adds a separately labelled underside inspection light, then captures the same baked panel head-on and at a grazing angle with raw and filtered normals. Timings use the original battle lighting, not the inspection fill.

`round2_game.gd` loads this checkout's actual battle scene in a private TEMP copy, starts a seeded battle, and inserts a capital ShipView. The prototype is substituted only in that view's memory. The HUD, board, rules, sky, G4 look and game camera remain active. Nearby ships are moved away from the close crop for review; this is a visual integration test, not a played battle. APPDATA is redirected under the work folder. No player saves or settings are used as inputs.

The GPU benchmark keeps round 1's 100 warm-up and 360 measured frames, 1920x1080, Forward+, MSAA 4x, AgX and no vsync. Twenty shared copies use a 5 by 4 layout. It measures an isolated asset scene without battle rules, terrain, damage or effects. Frame p99 is a short sampled percentile, not a sustained 1% low.

`round2_run.py` verifies exports by reimport, triangle counts against Godot, finite positions, mounted root anchors, weapon directions, shared trim hashes, donor hashes, capture differences, filtered normal statistics and unchanged live ship/maps. The anchor check covers the raycast roots, not every child fitting. Visual sweeps remain necessary. `--worker --only Aplus|Bake|Reuse` supports a single build; `--capture-only`, `--game-only` and `--sheets` support review iterations through the watchdog. The BAT is the entry point for an owner-run rebuild.
