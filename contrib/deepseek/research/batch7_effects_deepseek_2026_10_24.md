## (a) SHIELD EFFECTS

Behavior spec: on hit, a ripple originates at the local-space impact point and expands; a brief inner flash plus outer blast wave reads as one layered event; hits feed a fixed 6-8 slot buffer (local position plus age plus power) that decays over time, merging same-area hits into one stronger ripple; a separate world-space particle burst fires at the impact point. At low health, noise-driven gaps (partial dissolve) beat uniform flicker; keep base color constant and encode urgency via pulse speed, gap density, or rim intensity. On break, drive a dissolve/sphere-mask outward from the killing hit point (optionally letting the final projectile pierce through), then fully hide the shield mesh.

## (c) WEAPON EFFECTS

Beams: thin high-contrast core, strong emitter flare, tight impact hot spot, subtle mid-path; additive blending only for the fringe, with a separate clamped core pass; a "minimal beam" variant (bright emitter, bright impact, faint mid-line) for massed fire. Projectiles/torpedoes: body silhouette must stay visible at gameplay distance; contrast guarantee (outline/rim/adaptive brightness); torpedo = bright core plus leading shock glow plus short ember tail; impact = spall burst of incandescent droplets. Capital-ship muzzle flashes: staged - brief charge/pre-flash, main flash clipped to emitter geometry, lingering barrel glow; real-time muzzle lights prioritized (capital, beams, small craft) and capped. Explosions: in vacuum prefer flash plus plasma/debris over dust shockwave; if a ring is used, thin, fast, additive, or a short low-amplitude distortion pulse - never a filled orange wash. Engine glows/trails: driven from turn-state parameters (throttle, damage, stance); tied to engine subsystem state (destroyed engines = no plume, no trail); fleet-wide trail brightness capped. Torpedo telegraphing: (1) predicted path line plus impact marker during the player's turn; (2) two-stage warning (long-lead incoming, short-lead predicted-hit); (3) brightness/color ramp toward white over the final about 800 ms, peaking at impact; (4) predicted-hit state distinct from "in flight" so a miss visibly follows from maneuver; (5) brief impact emphasis (slow-mo/camera push) at arrival.

## (d) PARTICLES AND JUICE

Starting values: hitstop 2-5 frames for most hits (16-83 ms at 60 fps), 8-12 frames for kills/criticals (133-200 ms), scaled by damage tier and target class. Screen shake: positional only, at most 0.25 s, exponential decay, global intensity slider; tactics-camera starting points light plus/minus 2-3 px, medium plus/minus 4-6 px, heavy plus/minus 8-10 px; avoid rotational shake during planning. Hit flash: localized only, never full-screen white or saturated red; at most 3 flashes/second, no sequence longer than 5 s; separate "disable damage flashes" toggle. Recoil: animate the firing ship/turret model, scaled to damage; anticipation 6-12 frames for heavy weapons/torpedoes, none for light fire. Audio: impacts built from at least 4 layers (sub, body, attack, tail); duck the sub layer during rapid light fire, restore for heavy/critical; separate whoosh/flight from impact for torpedoes. Failure modes: per-event juice on every hit (habituation, agency occlusion, unreadable importance); juice hiding information; VFX/SFX/UI/shake desync; long animation waits with no skip.

Findings:

- "Camera Shake That Adds Game Feel Without Motion Sickness" - strayspark.studio/blog: duration is the biggest offender (0.15 s = impact, 1.5 s = vestibular conflict); low-frequency/high-amplitude worse than fast small jitter; rotational shake (roll/pitch) more provocative than positional; no decay feels artificial; a global intensity multiplier is "non-negotiable." ADOPT - positional-only shake, at most 0.25 s, exponential decay, global intensity slider. Flag: tooling-studio blog; motion-sickness reasoning aligns with accessibility guidance.
- "Feedback Recipes - Trauma Model" - github.com/gamedev-skills (path truncated; not fully verified): single trauma value in [0,1]; shake amount = trauma squared or cubed; decay linearly; drive offset with noise/summed sines, never fresh rand() per frame; starting points max_offset 8-16 (or 6-10) px, max_roll 0.05-0.12 rad, decay 1.0-1.5 trauma/s, per-hit trauma 0.15 light (heavier truncated). ADOPT - trauma-based shake with squared output, per-hit trauma by damage, global intensity multiplier. Flag: community recipe doc; well-known model but not peer-reviewed.
- "Game Accessibility Guidelines" - gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-flickering-images-and-repetitive-patterns/: seizure risk - more than 3 flashes/second covering 25% plus of screen, or any flashing sequence over 5 seconds; repeated moving patterns over 25% plus also a risk; avoid flickering where possible, otherwise warn and provide a disable option. ADOPT - localized impact flashes, at most 3/second, plus a "disable damage flashes" setting. Flag: widely cited guidance; thresholds conservative, not a substitute for testing the project's own effects.
- "Xbox Accessibility Guidelines - XAG 118: Photosensitivity" - learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xag-version-history: luminance flash failure at more than 3/second covering about 20% of screen; lower-intensity flashing can fail if extended; saturated red flash (R/(R+G+B) at least 0.8) is more provocative; solutions: reduce contrast, frequency, size. - "Venture Accessibility Doc" - github.com/opd-ai/venture/blob/main/docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md: photosensitive players should be able to disable damage flashes; separate shake-intensity and flash toggles. ADOPT - separate toggles/sliders for screen shake, damage flashes, effect intensity. Flag: project-specific doc, not an industry standard.
- "Game Mechanics Optimizations - Anticipation Frames" - github.com/raduacg/game-mechanics-optimizations/blob/main/100_anticipation_frames.md: anticipation is 3-12 frames (0.05-0.2 s) of opposite motion before the primary action (anticipation, action, follow-through); without it, actions feel instant, robotic, weightless, with no telegraph; light 5 frames, heavy 12. ADOPT - anticipation charge-up on heavy weapons and torpedoes; none on light, frequent weapons. Flag: community guide; conventional ranges, not fleet-combat-specific.

Flag: comparative repo citing studies (Kao 2020, Kao et al. 2024); underlying studies not re-verified.
- "Juice is information, not decoration" - CodeCrunch lecture notes, github.com/CODECRUNCHWORLDWIDE (path truncated; not fully verified): every shake, burst, and thump must answer a question the player asked - what happened, how big, was it good or bad; juice that answers nothing is noise, and noise costs trust. ADOPT - every juice effect must have an information role; if it doesn't clarify hit/miss, damage magnitude, or threat, cut it. Flag: educational lecture notes, not a shipped-game source.
- "Effect synchronization tolerances" - Game Design AI Practice (same Chapter 14): when VFX, SFX, UI damage numbers, shake, and rumble all start on the same frame, the brain binds them into one event; 1-2 frame misalignment reads "off," 3-5 frames reads "like a bug." Tolerances: VFX plus/minus 0, SFX plus/minus 1 frame, UI damage numbers plus/minus 2 frames. ADOPT - define a sync spec per hit type and audit with frame captures. Flag: design conventions, plausible but not from a formal study.

## (e) LIGHTING AND POST

What carries the look: lighting first, materials second, post third - lighting establishes the light-dark structure, materials control how hulls respond, post carries polish. The Crying Suns case study shows that in a dark space scene, a depth-based post-process haze can be the decisive readability layer lifting dark hulls out of the void - but post cannot rescue a scene with no silhouette contrast. Recommended starting rig for 20 dark hulls: single directional "sun" (warm/neutral, casts shadows); fill 25-40% of key, cool, camera-relative or gradient ambient, no extra real-time fill lights; rim Fresnel-based in the hull material (masked via gloss/alpha), 50-80% of key luminance, cool color - the primary silhouette tool; fake bounce via screen-space depth haze/gradient fog instead of light probes for open space (reserve probes for stations); HDR bloom threshold about 1.0-1.2, emissive accents at 3-5x, engine/weapon cores above 10x, hull lighting below threshold; start with Neutral tonemapping for color fidelity (if ACES, rebalance brightness and reduce saturation; test AgX if available); teal-leaning background/neutral grade with warm orange accents for threats/impacts; confirm value separation in greyscale before grading for hue.

Findings:
- "Game Lighting Design - Patterns" - github.com/omer-metin/skills-for-antigravity (path truncated; not fully verified): adapted key/fill/rim - key brightest, defines shadow direction, shadows on this light only; fill 30-50% of key, cooler, no shadows; rim "especially important for readability in combat"; camera-relative fill, light layers, probe data for dynamic objects. Inference: in space, rim becomes the primary readability tool; fill can be almost entirely fake. ADOPT - key plus low fill (30-50%) plus strong rim as a gameplay readability layer. Flag: community reference doc; 30-50% is a starting range.
- "Three-Point Lighting" - courses.cs.washington.edu (course PDF): key-to-fill ratio 2:1 moderate, 4:1 typical/reasonable, 24:1 very high with undefined dark side; "too much fill light could compete with the shading." ADAPT - start 4:1 to 6:1 key-to-fill for space mood, but guarantee a rim cue so the dark side never becomes unreadable. Flag: film-cinematography text, not game-specific.
- "Lighting - Pro Knowledge Overview" - github.com/RobLe3/cc-blender-skill (path truncated): canonical setup - key 45 degrees front-right; fill opposite at 1/4-1/2 brightness (cool); rim behind at 1/2-equal key (cooler blue); example key 1000 W, fill about 1/3, rim about 60%. ADOPT - rim 50-80% of key, cool, consistent with system star; fill 25-40%. Flag: Blender-oriented; wattages not transferable.

ADAPT - if ACES, budget time to rebalance lights and saturation; not a drop-in. Flag: indie space-combat devlog; single-project account, directly relevant.

Flag: commercial/AI-prompt article; color-wheel reasoning standard, not a game-art source.

## (f) PERFORMANCE BUDGETS

What breaks first when 20 ships fire: (1) GPU fill rate / overdraw - first wall; transparent layers multiply fragment cost, and it's multiplicative and view-dependent (spikes exactly when the camera watches the cluster); (2) Render Thread draw calls / batching - per-effect draws accumulate, worse in engines that don't auto-batch dynamic particle draws; (3) CPU particle updates (Game Thread) - expensive per-particle logic spiking on a single frame, avoidable by staggering; (4) shader cost - subordinate to overdraw (layers multiply instruction cost), first wall only with very expensive shaders over large coverage; texture memory is rarely the first runtime bottleneck but is a build-time constraint. Mitigations in order: (1) merge effects, share materials - small master-material set (beam, trail, impact, shield, explosion), variation via material instances/parameters; (2) instance beams and trails - 20 beams can be one draw call with shared geometry/material (draw-call fix, not an overdraw fix); (3) stagger simultaneous effects across 0.3-0.8 s - cheapest mitigation for turn-based; (4) author cheap mass-fire variants for common effects, expensive ones for hero events; (5) cap simultaneous effects, pool decals, bake/merge older marks into damage masks at end of turn; (6) distance/apparent-size effect LOD; (7) budget by screen coverage, not particle count (e.g., hero at most 10%, common impact at most 2%, distant at most 0.5%); (8) CPU emitters for small/frequent effects, GPU emitters only for 1,000-plus particle hero explosions.

Findings:
- "Vfx Realtime - Sharp Edges (Overdraw Spiral)" - github.com/omer-metin/skills-for-antigravity (path truncated; not fully verified): 10 particles at 10% coverage = 100% fill rate per layer; three overlapping systems triple that; fire (50) plus smoke (100) plus sparks (200) at 5x overlap reaches about 1,750 fragment invocations/pixel; 200x200 px at 1080p is about 70M fragments; overdraw compounds exponentially; symptoms: GPU spikes when effects trigger, framerate tanking looking at certain areas, wild camera-angle variance. ADOPT - treat overdraw as the primary GPU budget; audit with overdraw visualization; cap counts, reduce lifetime, set fill-rate budgets per tier. Flag: reference doc; 1,750 figure illustrative.
- "Getting Results" - Unreal Engine 4.27 Documentation, dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/getting-results: overdraw = pixel shader cost = layers times pixels per layer times instructions per pixel; Cascade runs particle sim (Game Thread), draw-call finalization (Render Thread), visuals (GPU) in parallel - a slowdown in any one bottlenecks rendering; Shader Complexity mode: bright red about 300 instructions, pink about 600, white 900 plus; use near/far LODs. ADOPT - profile Game Thread, Render Thread, GPU separately per volley; stagger effects across frames. Flag: official Epic docs; UE4 Cascade architecture, engine-specific.
- "Optimising Ridiculous Space Battles" - positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2025/05/18/optimising-ridiculous-space-battles/: about 600 ships (20x20 grid) - cost "nicely distributed" across sprites, simulation, post-processing (mostly lightmaps), particles, hulks, bullets; surprise: lightmaps drawn for every laser bullet, engine glow, and ship sprite made the "matte" pass expensive; no single dominant system - many small per-effect draws accumulated. ADAPT - build beams/trails as single meshes or instanced draws, not sprite stacks; profile draw calls per volley, not just GPU time. Flag: 2D sprite game; accumulation pattern relevant, not a 3D benchmark.
- "Performance budget indicators" - PopcornFX Wiki, wiki.popcornfx.com/index.php?title=Performance_budget_indicators: three budget bars - particle count, memory per instance, overdraw estimate; green = hundreds of instances OK, red = simplify; primitive-vs-overdraw ratio: too many small particles, batch into larger ones, overdraw-dominant, particles too large; script budgets (Budget_ScriptSpawn, Budget_ScriptEvolve) cap CPU cost. ADOPT - explicit particle-count, overdraw, and script-CPU budgets per effect tier; use the ratio to choose "fewer, larger" vs. "more, smaller." Flag: PopcornFX v1 docs; editor estimates, not final-game measurements.
- Same Epic "Getting Results": any-scenario effects (e.g., weapon impacts) must be lighter than custom scenario-built effects, since they draw in fluctuating cost contexts; mitigations: reduce emission rates, reduce particle scale, near/far LODs. ADOPT - "common" impacts cheap and low-fill; "hero" effects (kills, shield breaks) heavier and staggered. Flag: official docs; no numeric budgets.
- "Profiling Tips" - realtimevfx.com/t/profiling-tips/5334/3: common offenders - too much fill ("7ish is fine, but sometimes 50, 60, 100 fullscreen particles..."), too many FX lights too big, too many physics particles (split so only 1/6th have physics), too many expensive particle types at once (a "spark fountain" computing full parabolas on spawn - 20 on the same frame spikes), too many trails (50 jets with dense trails needed a cheap version: fewer segments, shorter lifetime). ADOPT - author cheap mass-fire variants of torpedo trails, spark impacts, beam glows; reserve expensive variants for single dramatic events. Flag: forum post from an experienced VFX artist; production anecdotes, not controlled measurements.
- "Optimize VFX" - Adam Komarowski (Unreal Technical Art), adamkomarowski.github.io/unrealtechart/book/vfx/: Niagara uses dynamic draws - no auto-batching even with the same material; push particles into a single system via data channels; batching broken by static switches/bools, separate texture assets, different blend modes; scalar/vector parameters don't break batching; one master material per category (MFX_Sprite_Master, MFX_Ribbon_Master, MFX_Distortion), variation via dynamic material instances, lerp/if instead of static switch; about 1-5 emitters is 1-2 draw calls and 0.3-0.6 ms Niagara at 1,000-plus particles - below that, CPU emitters faster. ADOPT - one master material per effect category, variation via instances/parameters, merge emitters; CPU for small effects, GPU for 1k-plus hero explosions. Flag: technical-art book; approximate Niagara costs, order-of-magnitude.
- "SergeyG-SPACE" - itch.io/profile/sergeyg-space (community post): muzzle flash, beam core, impact spark, trail are "the same three or four material behaviours with different parameters"; one small shader set (additive beam with scrolling noise, radial flash with per-instance colour/lifetime, impact decal with a flipbook atlas) plus material instances turns "polish 11 weapons" into "author four materials and tune 11 parameter sets" - and keeps draw calls flat when six enemies and a rocket land in the same frame. ADOPT - material-instance-driven effects with a small master-material set; no per-weapon unique materials. Flag: community post from a co-development studio; practical experience, not a formal benchmark.
- "Vertex Formats and GPU Instancing" - LowDragMC Photon2 docs, low-drag-mc.github.io: instancing batches when geometry/material state matches; instance types TRAIL_INSTANCE, BEAM_INSTANCE, ARATRAIL_INSTANCE; batch breaks on geometry, material program, texture/sampler state, render layer, or layout differences; "for a handful of particles, setup cost may dominate"; transparent layers increase upload/sorting cost even at low instance counts. ADOPT - instance beams/trails with shared material; treat instancing as a draw-call fix, not a fill-rate fix. Flag: docs for a specific GPU particle system; batching rules standard.
- "Smoke Lighting and texture re-usability in Skull and Bones" - realtimevfx.com/t/smoke-lighting-and-texture-re-usability-in-skull-bones/: flipbooks popular for explosions; everything in the post (multiple explosion flipbooks, masks, noise) totaled 8.06 MB; more texture pairs = more variety, but memory grows per pair. ADOPT - small shared flipbook/noise library (a few MB, not hundreds); reuse via parameters and tinting. Flag: forum post; 8.06 MB is that game's set, not a general budget.

ADAPT - effect-category atlases (one explosion, one beam/noise) rather than per-effect textures; balance size vs. resolution. Flag: community tutorial; atlas-size trade-off not quantified.
- "Complete Guide to Niagara VFX Optimization" - MoreVFX Academy, morevfxacademy.com: PC/console standard = 512-1024 px individual textures with a shared master material (atlases "still make sense on mobile"); Texture Sample about 4-6 instructions; SceneColor plus 40-80; Refraction plus 20-40; DepthFade plus 15-30. ADOPT - 512-1024 px effect textures with shared master material; higher resolutions for hero close-ups only. Flag: commercial training article; practical, not a formal memory budget.

## (g) READABILITY RULES

Ten rules: (1) Clarity veto - any effect hiding the board, damage state, or attack source/target is cut or reduced. (2) Value/saturation discipline - reserve near-white and max saturation for critical events (kills, shield breaks); routine hits in lower bands. (3) Effect LOD by screen size, not just distance - turn off secondary particles at range but never LOD away the primary impact/damage signal; audit that LOD is actually enabled. (4) Parallelize independent hits, sequence important outcomes - resolve mechanics first, animate independent shots in overlapping windows, kills/shield breaks sequentially; break dependency cycles explicitly. (5) Every resolution skippable or accelerable - speed slider plus hold-to-accelerate plus skip; avoid a single binary fast mode. (6) No forced camera by default - cinematic/action cameras opt-in; default view is the tactical board. (7) "Animations off" is a first-class mode - with minimal board-level attack motion (turret flash, short beam line) so causality stays visible; game must be fully playable with effects disabled. (8) Separate toggles for speed, particles, and screen effects. (9) Frequency-based timing budget - routine impacts short; rare events allowed longer. (10) Pre-resolution transparency - show predicted damage, hit/miss, and torpedo impact during planning; the resolution confirms rather than surprises.

Findings:
- "VFX Design Principles" - gdad.wiki (Ulster University), gdad.wiki/wiki/game-art/vfx-design-principles: VFX is a gameplay-readability system first, celebration second; Riot's internal goal order: visual clarity, minimal clutter, thematic reinforcement, surprise/delight; every effect has a primary element (focal point, high value, clear silhouette, high opacity, strong contrast) and secondary elements (theme, lower value, blurrier); "scale of importance" - visual weight must match gameplay weight; visual extent must equal mechanical extent; three checks: Readable, Emphasis, Scale. Inference: the primary element of every weapon effect should be the hit/miss and damage signal, not theme particles. ADOPT - every effect gets a named primary (gameplay signal) and secondary (theme); primary must stay visible at gameplay distance. Flag: university wiki - secondary summary of Riot's style guide, not Riot's original document.
- "VFX Value and Colour" - gdad.wiki/wiki/game-art/vfx-value-and-colour: readability depends on partitioning value and saturation ranges between UI, character, VFX, environment; VFX occupies a wide high value/saturation band but should avoid 100% white/black and 100%/0% saturation (those belong to UI/environment); contrast, not absolute brightness, creates focal points - a mid-value effect against a dark background pops, while the same effect against a bright background does not. Inference: reserve the highest value for the most important event; if every weapon is max white, the fleet becomes equal-brightness blobs. ADOPT - value/saturation bands by event importance; near-white for critical events only. Flag: same university wiki; secondary synthesis.
- "Into the Breach dev on UI design" - staging.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-: the developers' stated principle is to sacrifice cool ideas for clarity every time; player-understanding requirements restricted design options considerably; unique weapons/firing patterns were cut because they weren't easy to convey; early prototypes had a mess of parabolic, shadow, and target indicators. ADOPT - clarity veto: any effect that hides the board or damage state is reduced or cut regardless of spectacle. Flag: games-press article; quotes direct, but a secondary summary of an RPS interview.

Inference: distance LOD is necessary but not sufficient; scale by apparent screen size (or distance plus importance) so a distant capital ship's damage state stays readable. Flag: official Unity docs; VFX Graph mesh-particle specific.
