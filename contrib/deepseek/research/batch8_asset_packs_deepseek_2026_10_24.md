## Pass 1: prebuilt ship and space asset packs (verdict: ADAPT)

Source facts. Unity Asset Store packs are generally delivered as Unity packages containing FBX meshes plus PBR materials, under the standard Unity EULA. Representative packs (all store listings are self-reported and marketing, hence weak sources):

- "Spaceship Gunboat Aurora": 135 modular FBX ship parts, landing gear, turrets, cargo doors, animated shields, sound effects, 3 colour variants; FBX with PBR (URP Metallic/Specular, HDRP), 4096 albedo/AO/smoothness/specular/normal maps, 4-7 LODs per component; realistic hard-sci-fi modular kitbash style; $9.99.
- "Spaceship Fleet III Collection II": 9 capital-class ships (battleships, carriers, cruisers, destroyers), modular weapons, torpedoes/missiles, decals, 6 texture sets; PBR specular, prefabs; LOD count not stated; $54.

Representative: "Spaceship Pack 3": 11 meshes (Commander, Destroyer, Dreadnought, Frigates), baked rocket-flare lighting, planet materials; 4096/2048/1024 textures; 3 LODs; Blueprint colour changer; semi-realistic/stylized. - CGTrader Royalty Free License permits commercial use only when the model is incorporated into a final product and cannot be extracted by third parties. Representative: Humble space ships pack $29.99 (PBR), Retro Spaceships Pack $10 (2048 PBR maps, FBX with LODs), "Spaceship pack60" $99.90; CGTrader also sells a $34.99/month subscription of royalty-free assets.

- Poly Haven is CC0 but catalogues HDRIs, PBR materials, and general props, not ship hulls (inference from catalogue search; confirm by browsing its catalogue).

Inference. Unity packs are the strongest single-vendor option for a realistic fleet, but they lock the buyer into a realistic hard-sci-fi style that is not stylistically compatible with the low-poly CC0 libraries. Mixing packs fights consistency in three ways: silhouette language (hard-sci-fi greebles vs. flat low-poly shapes), texel density, and material setup. Commit to ONE lane (a paid realistic fleet kit, or a free low-poly CC0 kit), not a marketplace collage. Aurora's animated shield component is notable for the Corsair (separate shield mesh is cyan-rule friendly). Cyan enforcement: prebuilt PBR assets often contain cyan in albedo/emissive/decals (engine glows); every incoming asset's texture set and material slots must be audited and remapped.

Verdict: category ADAPT. ADOPT (1) Quaternius Ultimate Spaceships Pack for prototyping/blockout, but NOTE the license correction: QAL v1.0, so keep it local/private if it must not be redistributed, or use Kenney instead for public-repo-safe blockout; (2) Kenney Modular Space Kit / Space Station Kit for stations/props/environment blockout (CC0, GLB, style-consistent with KayKit/Quaternius low-poly). ADAPT (3) Unity "Spaceship Gunboat Aurora" for the Corsair hero-ship kit (modular FBX, PBR, 4-7 LODs, separate shield/cargo/turret components), but do not buy fleet-wide until the style test passes; its realistic style does not blend with low-poly. SKIP: Sketchfab/CGTrader/ArtStation individual packs as a fleet-wide strategy (per-asset, style-fragmented, LODs/PBR not guaranteed); Poly Haven SKIP for ships (no hulls) but keep as CC0 lighting/material support library. Weak sources: Unity/Fab/Sketchfab/CGTrader/ArtStation product pages are store/marketing listings (self-reported); 3dmdb, stlfinder, assetfigures, ultimategameassets are secondary summaries, not authoritative for license/LOD claims; forums and community mirrors are weak for technical specs. Re-verify license terms and file contents on the primary vendor page before purchase.

## Pass 2: material packs for spaceship hulls (verdict: ADAPT)

Source facts. Poly Haven: all assets CC0, usable for any purpose including commercial work, no attribution required (only restrictions: no claiming authorship, no re-licensing). Its texture library contains seamless PBR materials including metal, painted metal, rust, industrial, scratched, and grunge surfaces; commonly 4K and 8K (minimum accepted submission resolution is 8K, downloads commonly 4K/8K); complete PBR map sets. Coverage for hulls is generic photoscanned/procedural surfaces, NOT spaceship-specific hull kits, decal sheets, or trim sheets. Formats typically JPG/PNG; expect separate AO/roughness/metallic maps and pack ORM yourself. Cost $0; CC0, safe to commit to a public repo. Style: photoreal surface detail; works as a base layer for realistic hard-sci-fi hulls. ambientCG: self-described as one of the largest sources of free PBR materials/HDRIs/models; all CC0, free even commercially; 2000+ materials including metal, painted metal, rust, grunge; JPG/PNG at 1K-8K; same caveats and same role as Poly Haven. Quixel Megascans (now on Fab): photoscanned surfaces, decals, 3D assets, plants; Bridge being replaced by Fab; new assets Fab-only; pricing per-listing on Fab (historically reported $0.99 individual / $4.99 kits / $24.99 packs at transition, secondary source, weak); Fab Standard License permits commercial/private use and private-repo sharing but prohibits standalone redistribution, so UNSAFE for a public repo; UE-oriented workflow. Adobe Substance 3D Assets: 15,000+ materials, decals, atlases, and 3D models (secondary sources weak on exact counts); pricing per official Adobe pricing-change FAQ (Feb 2025): Texturing US$24.99/month or US$249.88/year, Collection US$59.99/month or US$599.88/year, country-varying. Materials are typically .sbsar; textures exported via Substance apps to PNG/TGA/EXR; license permits use/modification/distribution only as incorporated into a Larger Work or Modified Work. Raw .sbsar files are not redistributable, so NOT safe for a public repo. ArtStation/Unity/Fab marketplace material packs: per-listing coverage (hull panels, painted metal, trim sheets, decals, grime); examples: "Sci-Fi Trim Sheet and Material Pack | Substance Designer + Blender Meshes" (ArtStation), "Procedural Sci-Fi PBR Materials" about $19.99 (Unity Asset Store), "Sci-Fi Material Pack - V1 - 4K" (Fab, 20 materials incl. walls/panels/shields). All store/marketing listings, weak for exact specs. Unity Asset Store assets may be used in other engines but cannot be redistributed as standalone items or in ways letting others extract raw files (Unity Support); ArtStation EULA: sellers choose Standard/Extended/Custom; Fab: Fab Standard License, no standalone redistribution.

Smart materials (Adobe's own docs, strong source): Smart Materials are presets for a whole layer stack, a folder of layers reusable as .spsm resources, created/used only in Substance 3D Painter. They can encode painted metal, edge wear, grime, rust streaks, panel-line dirt, chipped paint, ideal for iterating the Corsair's unified battle-barge look. They are authoring-only, not runtime assets.

Trim sheets (secondary sources: Meshy glossary, Frozenbyte wiki, Theseus thesis; definitionally consistent but weak/vendor-non-authoritative): a reusable texture atlas of strips, borders, panels, bevels, and surface detail, applied by precise UV0 layout (UV islands straightened and aligned to the trim strips). For about 20 ships, a 2-4 shared trim-sheet set (common hull-panel sheet; faction-accent sheet; wear/grime/decal sheet; shield/emissive mask kept separate) replaces 20 ships x 3-5 unique 4K texture sets, the primary texture-memory lever. Keep UV0 for trim sheets and UV2 for lightmaps/AO separate; do not bake per-ship AO into a shared trim sheet.

ORM packing (glTF 2.0 spec, strong): glTF's metallic-roughness model samples metallic from B, roughness from G, occlusion from R; when one image serves both, the packed layout is R=AO, G=Roughness, B=Metallic; values are linear, not sRGB. Engine expectations: Godot 4 ORMMaterial3D uses a single ORM texture; Babylon.js PBRMaterial exposes ormTexture and its glTF loader maps standard glTF ORM; Blender's glTF exporter can pack ORM on export; Substance Painter has a built-in "glTF PBR Metal Roughness" export preset. Poly Haven/ambientCG usually ship separate maps, so expect to pack ORM yourself (a pipeline step, not a problem). Conversion pitfalls (source-backed): ORM must be imported as linear, not sRGB, or roughness/metallic response is wrong (glTF spec); Godot's glTF importer may create StandardMaterial3D instead of ORMMaterial3D in some versions, verify after import (Godot issue #70979); Babylon's ormTexture must get the packed texture in the right slot/channel mapping (Babylon PBRMaterialProps docs). Inference pitfalls: Unity's metallic/smoothness packing and Unreal's common mask layouts differ from glTF ORM, so do not reuse _MetallicGlossMap as ORM without repacking; use PNG for packed ORM (JPEG compression bleeds channels and corrupts data); check normal-map green-channel convention (DirectX vs OpenGL) when moving assets between Unreal/Unity-authored packs and Godot/Blender; if a library's AO map is baked with a different UV set, packing it into ORM may misalign.

Public-git-repo rule: CC0 textures (Poly Haven, ambientCG) are safe to commit. Quixel/Fab, Unity Asset Store, Substance 3D Assets, and ArtStation source files are not safe: a public repo is redistribution. Practical discipline: keep paid source assets in a private repo/LFS or local library; commit only original derivative work (your own trim sheets baked from CC0 bases are your own work). This is not legal advice; review each asset's EULA before publishing.

Cyan = shields discipline (inference): material libraries often contain cyan in engine glows, screens, decals, or emissive masks. Remove/desaturate cyan from albedo/emissive/decal maps; author trim sheets with no cyan at all; put all shield glow in a separate material/emissive mask that is the only cyan in the game; do not rely on "fixing it in engine": cyan in a baked albedo still reads as cyan under lighting. Test this on the Corsair before fleet rollout.

Porting matrix: Poly Haven/ambientCG, clean in both Babylon.js and Godot 4 once ORM is packed (PNG/JPG, CC0); low-medium effort (channel packing only). Quixel, portable as image maps but Fab/UE-oriented with license friction; medium-high. Substance 3D Assets, baked textures via the glTF preset in both engines; .sbsar is not runtime; needs a subscription and baking; medium. ArtStation/Unity/Fab packs, depends on pack: GLB/FBX + PNG/TGA port; Unity shaders/materials do not; medium-high (inspect channel packing, shaders, license). Trim sheets, engine-agnostic textures on UV0; low effort; the best memory strategy.

Verdict: category ADAPT. ADOPT (1) Poly Haven + ambientCG CC0 PBR libraries: free, CC0, public-repo-safe, cover metals/painted metal/rust/grime at 4K-8K; need ORM packing and a no-cyan audit. ADAPT (2) Substance 3D Assets + Painter smart materials: the strongest authoring path for the Corsair's painted panels, edge wear, grime, and trim sheets; requires subscription, .sbsar not runtime, bake to PNG; best as the hero-ship authoring pipeline. ADAPT (3) one ArtStation/Fab sci-fi trim-sheet or hull-material pack for hull panel language and wear detail, only if the license allows and the trim sheet passes the cyan audit. SKIP: Quixel Megascans as a primary hull material source (per-asset paid, Fab/UE-oriented, not spaceship-specific, license friction). Weak sources flagged inline: store listings (marketing/self-reported); DevX, StraySpark, ToolTrim, AssetFigures, 3DTextures (secondary summaries); Blender StackExchange/Leadwerks/Epic forums (weak except confirming conventions documented by Khronos/Godot/Babylon/Adobe); Meshy glossary, Theseus thesis, Frozenbyte wiki (secondary for trim-sheet definitions); official vendor docs (Poly Haven FAQ, ambientCG license, Adobe help, Godot/Babylon docs, Khronos spec, Unity Support) are stronger. Still re-check pricing/EULA at purchase.

## Pass 3: marketplaces, licensing, AI-generated assets (verdicts: ADAPT marketplaces; SKIP AI for final fleet art, ADAPT for concept/blockout)

Central public-repo rule (source facts + inference): most paid marketplace licenses allow commercial use in a finished product but prohibit redistributing the source asset or letting third parties extract it. A public git repo containing the source FBX/GLB/textures IS redistribution. So paid marketplace source files are usually UNSAFE for a public repo even when commercial game use is allowed. CC0 is safe; CC BY is safe with attribution; paid-source files are generally private-only. Not legal advice; re-verify each asset's EULA before publishing.

Public repo: CC0 safe; CC BY safe with attribution; CC BY-SA CONDITIONAL (share-alike may affect derivatives); CC BY-NC/ND unsafe; Standard UNSAFE (no standalone redistribution). AI quality caveats (source facts): AI 3D tools produce detailed base meshes, but production pipelines still require retopology, UV unwrapping, PBR texturing, LOD generation, and engine QA. AI textures look convincing in isolation but produce seams, stretching, and incorrect material properties on messy UVs. Dense triangulated meshes and fragmented UVs are "invisible risk"; there is no standardized game-readiness benchmark covering topology validity, UV-seam density, PBR accuracy, and engine import success. Inference for the Corsair/fleet: expect dense triangulated non-quad meshes with poor edge flow, hence manual retopology x 20 ships; fragmented UVs fight the trim-sheet workflow (texel density consistency); PBR outputs are not authored for the ORM pipeline; each generation is independent, so style drift kills the one-unified-style requirement; AI models generate cyan engine glows/screens/emissives, audit every output. Budget: retopology, UV cleanup, ORM repacking, LOD creation, cyan removal. Do not commit AI outputs to the public repo unless you own the output and your input was not third-party IP.

## Pass 4: template and kit systems (verdicts: ADOPT asset-as-code + schema-validated templates; ADAPT kitbashing and procedural generation; SKIP engine-native prefabs as the canonical source)

1. Modular kitbashing sets (source facts). Kitbashing = assembling a model from pre-authored modular parts (panels, greebles, engines, cockpits, vents, weapons, hull shapes) instead of modeling each ship from scratch. ArtStation listings describe kits as "modular" collections for "fast blockouts, concept art, and early-stage sci-fi design", with some explicitly stating they are NOT final production assets: "No UVs, No materials, Messy topology (not intended for deformation, subdivision, or game-ready use)." Notable spaceship kits: ArtStation "Spaceship Bundle": 208 Starship Kit components (Panel, Square, Circle, Cylinder) + 128 Spaceship Kit components; FBX/OBJ; Starship Kit is UV'd, Spaceship Kit has no UVs; geometry not sub-D; no PBR mentioned; $79 Standard License (one commercial project, 2,000 sales / 20,000 views cap). ArtStation "152 Piece Hard Surface Kitbash Set": 152 hard-surface components; OBJ/FBX/.blend/.mtl; no UVs, no materials, messy topology, explicitly not game-ready; $9.99 Hobby / $11.99 Commercial / $44.99 Studios. ArtStation "Scifi Kitbash Kit Vol. 1": 93 modular sci-fi parts, Blender "clean ready-to-use basemesh" but no game-ready claims. Fab "Alien Sci-Fi Spaceship Proton Legacy": 40 sci-fi alien spaceships from modular parts; "not made to lock snap in position," "meant to be moved, scaled and rotated organically." Unity/Superhive/Gumroad kits: 150+ pieces (weapons, plates, greebles, vents, cockpits, engines, ports); FBX/OBJ/Blender; some use non-destructive modifiers. Team standardization guidance (a production spec guide, vendor tool page, not a formal standard, so guidance not gospel): the kit contract must define grid unit (e.g., 2 m grid pieces must not hide a 1.97 m corner), snap axis, allowed overhang, seam-hiding method, forbidden rotations, and whether sockets are centerline/face-aligned/pivot-aligned; Master Asset to Derived Asset ownership: every derivative declares what it inherits (pivot, scale, material slots, collision family, export folder); derived files may change silhouette/damage/trim but must not "invent new socket names." Inference: most marketplace kitbash kits are concept/blockout tools, not production fleet kits. Buy ONE kit for greeble vocabulary and silhouette language; then establish your own scale/origin/orientation/naming/socket conventions before the first part enters the shared library. The kit is not the standard; your spec is. Rebuild/clean approved parts into your own master template rather than assembling ships from raw marketplace pieces. Weak-source flag: ArtStation/Fab/Superhive listings are marketing with self-reported specs; "game-ready" claims must be verified by inspection.

2. Parametric ship builders (source facts). Blender Studio "Spaceship Generator" (Geometry Nodes): a free Blender file that takes an input collection of parts and instances them via Geometry Nodes (also a tutorial series). FI ShipKit (GitHub: savannah-i-g/fi-shipkit): five generator families authored entirely from Python-built geometry-node groups (stealth hull, war kit, fleet kit, station kit, building kit); every ship is "a handful of slider values + one headless build"; headless build/QA/export via `blender -b --python build_kit.py`, outputs a kit_contract.json; stated convention: nose = +X, up = +Z, true metres in Blender, glTF exporter converts to +X nose/+Y up/+Z starboard (pure rotation, no mirror), plus a chirality probe to verify export; uses linked kit files so rebuilding a kit propagates into every ship file on next open. Classic "SpaceshipGenerator" Blender script: random-seed procedural spaceships. Gumroad/Superhive "Spaceship Generator" (Geometry Nodes): "100% procedural geometry mesh and shader," Blender 3.6/4.0+. Houdini Digital Asset spaceship generators: e.g., an asset that "procedurally generates a spaceship according to user input." Inference: strongest for variant exploration, generating 5-10 silhouette candidates for the Corsair or a fleet class, but generated topology is rarely game-ready (clean quads the exception), UVs/PBR/LODs/sockets need manual cleanup, and style drift across parameters is hard to control ("unique" is not "unified"). Best use: parametric ideation to hand-authored master to kitbash/instancing for variants; not a fully parametric runtime fleet.

3. Procedural generation: Geometry Nodes, Houdini, code-driven (source facts + inference). Blender Geometry Nodes: node-based procedural modeling; theses/production write-ups report it saves time and enables varied repetitive assets "when the technique fits"; but Geometry Nodes do not fully survive export to game engines (FBX export supports only a subset of attributes, lossy; bake/export and clean before it becomes a template). Houdini: used for procedural detail (fuselage panels, pipes, modular repeats) layered over hand-sculpted hero assets; studios build procedural kit tools (e.g., "autokit"); HDAs can run inside a game engine, but HDA runtime requires Houdini Engine, not a plain GLB. Code-driven: Python/Blender scripting and headless build scripts rebuild kits deterministically and run QA (FI ShipKit pattern); imaginu compiles a JSON recipe into a deterministic GLB for Babylon.js (meshes, PBR vertex colors, named nodes). Comparison: Geometry Nodes, pros: fast variation, non-destructive, good for greebles/panels, free, scriptable headless; cons: lossy export, topology/UV cleanup, style drift, not a runtime format. Houdini HDA, pros: excellent hard-surface detail and fleet-scale variation, engine integration; cons: expensive/complex, needs Houdini Engine, overkill for a small team. Code-driven (Python/JSON to GLB), pros: deterministic, diffable, CI-friendly, version-controllable; cons: lower authoring expressiveness (you are building a tool, not just art), engine-import expectations to manage. Inference: code-driven generation is the most strategically aligned with a public repo and kit-first workflow (JSON/YAML ship definitions are text, diffable, and can drive builds). Houdini HDA runtime: SKIP for a small team unless a technical artist already owns it.

4. Asset-as-code approaches (source facts). Starsector .ship files: JSON-with-comments with hullId, hullName, hullSize, center, height, width, spriteName, style, weaponSlots, engineSlots, builtInWeapons. A Rebellion Ship Registry package: strict JSON Schema for "canonical ship visual identity" plus starter manifest and audit script; starter manifests stay "pending_review" until contact-sheet image, orientation, centering, and identity are checked. Other ship-definition examples: shipName.json plus icon and texture (size, maxLife, sprite references). imaginu: compiles a JSON recipe into a deterministic GLB for Babylon.js; the recipe is the asset definition. Inference: asset-as-code is the HIGHEST-LEVERAGE template system: diffable (PR review works), deterministic (same recipe + seed to same GLB, reproducible builds, CI validation), composable (parts/sockets/LOD/damage states declared declaratively), public-repo friendly (definitions and builder are source; generated GLBs can be regenerated instead of committed), schema-validatable (JSON Schema catches missing sockets, bad scale, duplicate hardpoint names before import). Trade-off: you must build and maintain the compiler/builder and ensure generated GLB matches engine import expectations (units, orientation, materials). ADOPT.

5. Prefab/variant systems (source facts). Godot: scenes compose/nest like prefabs; "New Inherited Scene" from a base .tscn creates a child that inherits nodes and can override properties or add nodes; base changes propagate. Unity: prefabs and prefab variants allow base-prefab overrides (binary). Unreal: Blueprints with class-based inheritance and child Blueprints (.uasset binaries, diff/review hard). Babylon.js: no built-in prefab asset type; equivalents are GLB loading + code composition (load/clone meshes, build TransformNode hierarchies); TransformNode is the standard non-rendered transform parent for grouping/attaching child meshes and hardpoints. Inference: for a public-repo, engine-agnostic pipeline, Godot's inherited scenes are the cleanest native prefab-variant system (text .tscn, diffable); Unity prefabs and Unreal Blueprints are binary and less public-repo friendly; Babylon.js has no native prefab, so the equivalent is (a) JSON ship definition + code composition or (b) a generated GLB (with Babylon.js, AssetContainer + clone/thin-instance). SKIP making any one engine's prefab format the canonical asset definition.

6. Socket and hardpoint standards (source facts). Stardock's official behind-the-scenes article on Sins of a Solar Empire II hardpointing: center the ship at X:0 Y:0 Z:0 before placing hardpoints; singular hardpoints (center, above, aura, bomb) need no numbering; serialized hardpoints use suffixes weapon.0/weapon.1/weapon.2, ability.0; banks group hardpoints firing the same weapon (weapon.0_topleft, weapon.0_bottomright; trailing -0 = firing order); turrets split into mount (yaw) and barrel (pitch), each with its own pivot (mount pivot at bottom center where it connects to the hull). glTF defines no "socket" type: engines use empty transform nodes/bones parented to the model; Godot BoneAttachment3D attaches props to a skeleton bone; Babylon.js TransformNode acts as non-rendered attachment parent. Inference: the fleet-wide socket spec should be engine-agnostic and build-time enforced: prefixes hp_weapon_*, hp_engine_*, hp_shield_*, hp_utility_*, hp_dock_*; serialized suffixes (.0, .1) and optional bank/location suffixes; explicit forward and up axis per socket (never rely on engine defaults); store sockets as empty nodes in the GLB (or a JSON socket list compiled into the GLB) so both engines read them without plugins; validate socket names against the schema; the JSON definition declares sockets and the builder injects TransformNode/empty nodes with correct names and transforms. (This is also the mechanical enforcement of "guns never face backward": a forward vector per weapon socket, rejected at build time if misaligned.)

7. Damage-state variants and LODs per template (source facts). Damage states are commonly named intact/damaged/broken/debris/disabled-collision; the spec defines which state owns collision, which owns VFX anchors, and whether damage swaps use mesh replacements or material toggles. LODs often encoded by suffix (.lod0/.lod1/.lod2; Bannerlord docs register .lod<n> as LOD). glTF KHR_materials_variants defines alternate material states per primitive; documented uses include healthy/damaged states, product configurators, night/day. It cannot change mesh geometry. LOD and damage state are orthogonal axes; destructible assets need shard count/minimum piece size/collision simplification defined in the spec, not left to a visual fracture tool. Inference: every fleet template ships with a named LOD chain (_lod0 to _lod3 at consistent triangle budgets and silhouette rules); damage states as either (a) separate meshes for large hull breaks or (b) KHR_materials_variants material toggles for scorch/glow/panel loss, not both mixed arbitrarily; a separate shield material/emissive mask that is the ONLY cyan element (so damage states cannot introduce cyan); debris as a separate prefab with its own LOD/collision, not a fractured copy of the hero mesh; the schema/builder rejects ship definitions lacking a complete LOD chain or defining undeclared states.

8. Keeping approved parts stable across re-exports (source facts). Git stores the entire binary again with every commit (.psd/.fbx), bloating repos. Git LFS replaces large binaries with pointer files; the binaries live on a separate LFS server; configure LFS BEFORE the first commit: retrofitting onto existing binary history is much harder. Perforce is built for binary assets with exclusive locks (two binary files cannot be merged); practical alternative to Git+LFS past about 10-15 people / 50 GB asset libraries. No VCS provides meaningful binary diffing; review workflows for art rely on manifests, contact sheets, and engine screenshots, not textual diffs. A production spec guide recommends a Delivery Package manifest (source file, exported meshes, texture folders, collision files, preview images, validation results). Godot inherited scenes propagate base changes to variants (unapproved upstream changes can leak in); FI ShipKit's linked kit files mean rebuilding a kit propagates into every ship file on next open. The same spec guide warns about "asset family drift" (reimport offsets, duplicate materials, variants that no longer snap, missing source files). Inference, a workable public-repo workflow: (1) text sources are the source of truth (ship definitions, socket specs, LOD rules, build scripts: Git, PR-reviewed); (2) generated binaries are build artifacts (GLB/FBX generated by CI from text sources; not committed, or committed only as release artifacts); (3) paid/third-party source art stays private (private LFS repo or local library; public repo holds only original/CC0/CC-BY work); (4) approved parts are frozen (Perforce locks, or CODEOWNERS/review gates on a small team); (5) review uses manifests and contact sheets (a PR changing a ship template must include a regenerated contact sheet, a diff of the JSON definition, and validation results); (6) CI validates the contract (schema validation, socket-name checks, LOD-chain completeness, triangle-budget checks). Weak-source flag: HN/Reddit threads on Git LFS vs Perforce are anecdotal and platform-specific; prefer Git LFS docs and Perforce game-dev documentation.

Verdict: category ADOPT (asset-as-code + schema-validated templates), ADAPT (kitbashing and procedural generation), SKIP (engine-native prefabs as the canonical asset definition; Houdini HDA runtime for a small team). Top approaches: (1) ADOPT: asset-as-code with JSON/YAML ship definitions + schema validation + deterministic GLB build (Starsector .ship precedent; Rebellion ship-manifest schema/audit; imaginu's JSON-to-GLB compiler); (2) ADAPT: modular kitbash + a written kit contract (buy ONE kit for greeble vocabulary; rebuild/clean approved parts into your own master); (3) ADAPT: parametric/procedural generation (Blender Geometry Nodes + headless Python build) for ideation and deterministic rebuilds, not for game-ready output. Weak sources: ArtStation/Fab/Superhive/Gumroad listings (marketing); forums/HN/Reddit (anecdotal); secondary blogs/theses on Geometry Nodes (not authoritative on export behavior; prefer Blender's own export docs/bug tracker); Houdini tutorials/Gumroad assets (vendor marketing; verify HDA claims against Houdini Engine docs); the "Asset Planning Production Spec Guide" (vendor tool page, not a formal standard).

## Pass 5: fit to this project, synthesis and final verdicts

1. Scale of asset effort for PC, about 20 ships (source facts). Babylon.js thin instances draw thousands of copies of a mesh with unique per-instance transforms using a single instanced draw call and no per-copy scene-graph object (regular InstancedMesh objects do carry a per-instance JavaScript-side cost). Godot MultiMeshInstance3D batches thousands of instances into one or a few draw calls per texture with per-instance transforms and custom data (documented drawback: all instances share one material per surface). Trim sheets let one texture set skin dozens of modular kit pieces, so a whole set's texture memory can be a fraction of unique texturing, with unique textures reserved for hero assets the camera studies closely; they reduce texture memory, improve batching through material reuse, and keep modular assets visually consistent. Babylon.js can progressively load LODs inside a GLB, but its documented support targets reducing time-to-first-render, not a full runtime LOD-management system. glTF KHR_materials_variants defines alternate material states per primitive and explicitly lists healthy/damaged states among its intended uses; it switches materials at runtime with low latency but cannot change mesh geometry. Inference: draw calls are NOT the bottleneck at 20 ships on PC; do not over-engineer for draw calls. The real constraints are texture memory, material count, authoring consistency, and iteration speed. Texture memory is the lever: 20 ships x unique 4K albedo/normal/ORM sets is wasteful and invites style drift. A 2-4 shared trim-sheet set (common hull panels, faction accent, wear/grime, shield mask) reused fleet-wide is the answer. LOD discipline: 2-3 rungs (lod0 hero close-up, lod1 mid, lod2 far/UI), not 5-7 per ship; authoring cost outweighs benefit at this scale. Instancing: use thin instances/MultiMesh for repeated SUB-PARTS (turrets, engines, missiles, debris, shield bubbles), not unique hulls. Shared materials: one hull material + one faction accent material + one shield material + one damage/decal overlay, shared across the fleet. Weak-source flag: the Meshy trim-sheet glossary is a secondary glossary, not a measured benchmark.

2. Colour law constraints (cyan = shields only). Source facts: prebuilt PBR assets frequently include cyan engine glows, screens, emissive decals, or UI panels authored for the vendor's look (Pass 1); KHR_materials_variants swaps materials for damage states but does not remove cyan from base albedo/emissive textures. Risk by category (inference): prebuilt ship packs, HIGH (engine glows, cockpit screens, shield effects, and decals are often cyan; note the Aurora pack's animated shield component may be cyan by design); material packs/PBR libraries, MEDIUM-HIGH (generic metal/paint/rust usually safe, but sci-fi trim sheets and decal packs often include emissive cyan); AI-generated meshes, HIGH (text-to-3D defaults to "sci-fi" cues: glowing cyan engines, visors, holograms, audit every output); trim sheets, CONTROLLABLE (author your own with no cyan; audit bought ones); shield effects, INTENTIONAL (the only allowed cyan). Enforcement pipeline (inference): (1) import audit gate: every incoming asset (bought, AI, or hand-authored) passes a texture audit of albedo, emissive, decal, and shield textures for cyan; (2) separate shield material: shields use a dedicated material (or KHR_materials_variants variant) whose emissive is the only cyan in the project; the hull palette is defined in a small palette file with cyan explicitly excluded; (3) schema rule: the asset-as-code schema rejects any ship definition whose material list includes a cyan-capable material outside the "shield" slot; (4) trim-sheet discipline: author trim sheets on a no-cyan palette; mask/repaint cyan regions of bought trim sheets in the source before packing; (5) damage states: KHR_materials_variants damage variants must not introduce cyan (scorch/damage emissive should be orange/white/red, never cyan).

3. Faction grammars and reuse. Source facts: modular kits with smaller granularity tend to be more flexible with greater visual variation at higher authoring cost (thesis repository, directional, not a vendor spec); faction differentiation via 9 colour variations is a colour-swap strategy, not a silhouette strategy (gamedev.tv kit listing, store page/marketing). Faction-by-faction (inference): Ithar (vertical-fin sails): hard-surface kits can supply fins, struts, and masts, but thin taut "sail" surfaces (possibly translucent) are not standard kit pieces; AI image-to-3D can ideate but not produce the grammar; the vertical-fin silhouette is the faction identity and must be hand-authored or adapted from a cloth/panel mesh. Do not rely on a bought kit for it. Reuse strategy: shared Ithar hull core + hand-authored fin/sail module + faction accent trim sheet. Chorus (grown flesh, never built things or familiar animal anatomy): no marketplace kit fits (hard-surface kits are the opposite); AI produces organic blobs but cannot guarantee "never familiar animal anatomy". Entirely hand-authored, sculpted/kitbashed from organic primitives, never from a hard-surface kit base. Deathless (machine intelligences, bone as metaphor only, no faces): hard-surface kits can supply mechanical greebles; bone-like motifs adapt from rib/strut shapes; "no faces" means avoiding cockpit canopies that read as eyes and humanoid masks, and auditing AI outputs for face-like symmetry. Partly hand-authored: kit-sourced mechanical base plus custom bone-metaphor language and no-face curation. Guns never face backward is a socket/orientation contract, not a mesh problem: every hp_weapon_* socket carries a forward vector validated at build time. Can ONE unified style come from bought assets? Inference: NO, not across these four grammars. A unified TECHNICAL style is achievable (texel density, panel scale, wear language, ORM pipeline, LOD rules) from bought assets and shared trim sheets; a unified silhouette grammar is not, because Ithar sails, Chorus flesh, Deathless bone-machines, and the Corsair battle-barge are deliberately distinct identities. The achievable target: one shared technical art bible + one shared hull-core kit language + faction-specific hand-authored modules. Bought assets supply the shared greeble vocabulary; they cannot supply the faction identities.

4. Kit-first workflow for the Corsair battle-barge, concrete sequence (inference anchored to Passes 1-4 facts). Step 0, write the contract BEFORE sourcing anything: a Corsair JSON definition + JSON Schema declaring hull class, dimensions, forward/up axes; material slots (hull, accent, trim, wear, shield, exactly one shield slot, cyan-only); socket list (hp_weapon_*, hp_engine_*, hp_shield_*, hp_utility_*, each with position, rotation, forward vector); LOD chain (lod0-lod2); damage states (intact, damaged, broken, with material-variant names). The schema is the gate: nothing enters the library unless it validates. This is the highest-leverage first action: it fixes scale/orientation/socket conventions before sourcing, enforces the colour law at the data level, makes the fleet reproducible and diffable in a public repo, keeps both engines interchangeable, and tells you exactly what to buy (and what not to buy). Step 1, mesh sourcing: decision point is buying a kit for greeble vocabulary vs. hand-authoring the master. Recommended: buy ONE hard-surface kit (e.g., an ArtStation or Fab kitbash set) for panel/greeble language, but do NOT assemble the Corsair from raw kit pieces: raw kits are often "no UVs, messy topology, not game-ready." Hand-author or clean the Corsair master hull (retopo, UV0 for trim sheets, UV2 for AO if needed). For the hero ship, consider a paid modular kit with LODs and PBR (e.g., the Unity Aurora pack with 4-7 LODs). Step 2, materials and trim sheets: source CC0 base materials from Poly Haven/ambientCG (bare metal, painted metal, rust, grime); author Corsair trim sheets (hull-panel, accent/faction-colour, wear/grime, plus a separate cyan-only shield mask). Decision point: Substance 3D Painter smart materials for the hero ship vs. hand-painting in Blender/Substance Designer. Step 3, ORM packing: R=AO, G=Roughness, B=Metallic, linear data, PNG not JPEG; Godot ORMMaterial3D, Babylon.js PBRMaterial.ormTexture; pitfall: do not reuse Unity metallic/smoothness maps as ORM without repacking, and check normal-map green-channel convention. Step 4, sockets, LODs, damage states: sockets as empty transform nodes in the GLB (or a JSON socket list compiled into the GLB); build lod0/lod1/lod2 to consistent triangle budgets; damage via KHR_materials_variants for scorch/panel-loss material swaps, separate meshes only for large hull breaks; cyan audit: the damaged variant must not introduce cyan. Step 5, schema + builder: write a small builder (Python/Node) that reads the Corsair JSON + part GLBs and emits a deterministic GLB with named nodes/materials; validate schema, socket names, LOD completeness, triangle budget, the cyan palette rule, and the weapon forward-vector rule. Step 6, engine import. Babylon.js path: load the generated GLB via SceneLoader/AssetContainer; map materials to PBRMaterial with ormTexture; parent weapons to TransformNode sockets; clone or thin-instance repeated turrets; use KHR_materials_variants if Babylon's loader exposes it, else handle damage via material swap in code. Godot path: import the GLB and verify the importer created ORMMaterial3D (some versions create StandardMaterial3D first); build a base Corsair.tscn and create inherited scenes for variants (damaged, faction trim); attach weapons via BoneAttachment3D or Node3D sockets; use MultiMeshInstance3D for repeated turrets/debris if needed. Decision point: committing to Godot makes inherited scenes the natural variant system; staying on Babylon.js keeps JSON + code composition.

5. Engine question: Babylon.js vs Godot. Source facts: GLB is the common denominator (Babylon SceneLoader/AssetContainer; Godot native glTF import); ORM supported in both (Godot ORMMaterial3D; Babylon PBRMaterial.ormTexture); Godot inherited scenes are a native prefab-variant system (child .tscn inherits a base scene; base changes propagate; a secondary blog comparison; Godot's own docs are stronger); Babylon.js has no native prefab asset type (composition is code/GLB-based; thin instances and TransformNode are the equivalents). Inference table: GLB workflow, low lock-in (portable). ORM, low (same packed texture). Prefab/variant, medium lock-in risk IF Godot scenes become the source of truth; keep JSON/GLB as the source and scenes as downstream consumers. Trim sheets, no lock-in (engine-agnostic textures, UV0). Instancing, low (thin instances vs MultiMeshInstance3D both solve repeated parts). Damage variants, low-medium if damage data stays in JSON. What actually locks you: making an engine-native asset format the canonical source (Godot inherited scenes, Unity prefab variants, or Babylon-specific material graphs). The safe architecture: JSON ship definitions + schema + builder to GLB + ORM + sockets + variants to thin engine import layer. Verdict for area 5: keep both open; do not lock.

Public-repo checklist (source-backed constraints: paid licenses allow commercial use in a product but prohibit source redistribution, Pass 3; CC0 allows copying, modification, and redistribution, Pass 3). JSON ship definitions, JSON Schema, builder scripts: public (text, diffable, original work). CC0 textures (Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney, KayKit): public. CC BY assets (Sketchfab CC BY, Fab CC-BY, Meshy free): public with ATTRIBUTION.md. Paid marketplace source FBX/GLB/textures (Unity, Fab Standard, CGTrader, ArtStation, Sketchfab Store): NOT public; private repo/LFS/local. Quaternius assets: NOT public (QAL prohibits redistributing assets as assets). Paid AI outputs (Tripo Pro, Meshy Pro, Luma Plus): public only if you own the output and the input was not third-party IP. Free AI outputs (Tripo free, Luma free): NOT public (non-commercial / watermark; Tripo retains rights). Meshy free outputs: public with attribution (CC BY 4.0). Generated GLB from your own JSON: public as a release artifact, but prefer CI generation over committed binaries. Git LFS: use for unavoidable binaries; configure before the first commit (retrofitting is painful); prefer generated artifacts over committed binaries. Weak-source flag: the Git LFS advice comes from Microsoft/Oracle documentation and a GitHub repo, moderate strength; verify LFS server visibility.

7. Final verdicts. (1) Prebuilt ship/space asset packs, ADAPT: useful for greeble vocabulary, hero-ship parts, and prototyping; not a turnkey 20-ship fleet; buy one lane, never a marketplace collage. (2) Material packs, ADAPT: CC0 base materials + self-authored trim sheets are the backbone; Substance is the strongest authoring path for the Corsair but not a runtime dependency. (3) Marketplaces and AI (licensing), ADAPT marketplaces; SKIP AI for final fleet art: marketplaces are use-licenses not redistribution licenses (only CC0/CC BY are public-repo-safe); AI fails topology/UV/style-consistency for a unified 20-ship fleet and needs retopology/UV cleanup/ORM repack/LOD creation/cyan removal per output. (4) Template/kit systems, ADOPT asset-as-code (JSON/YAML definitions + schema validation + deterministic GLB build); ADAPT kitbashing and procedural generation; SKIP engine-native prefabs as the source of truth. (5) Engine choice, keep both open; do not lock.

SINGLE HIGHEST-LEVERAGE STARTING POINT: write the Corsair JSON schema and contract first, before buying any mesh, material, or AI generation. Concretely: define the Corsair's dimensions, forward/up axes, material slots (hull, accent, trim, wear, shield), socket names (hp_weapon_*, hp_engine_*, hp_shield_*), LOD chain (lod0-lod2), damage-state names, and the cyan palette rule (only the shield slot may be cyan). Then write a validator that rejects any definition violating the contract. This single action fixes scale/orientation/socket conventions before sourcing, enforces the colour law at the data level, makes the fleet reproducible and diffable in a public repo, keeps Babylon.js and Godot interchangeable, and tells you exactly what to buy (and what not to buy). It is the cheapest possible first action and the one that prevents the most expensive rework.

SINGLE BIGGEST LICENSING OR CONSISTENCY TRAP: treating a paid marketplace asset as a public-repo asset. Paid licenses (Unity, Fab Standard, CGTrader, ArtStation, Sketchfab Store) allow commercial use in a product but prohibit redistributing the source asset; a public git repo containing the FBX/GLB/textures is redistribution, even if the game itself is legal, the repo is a license violation. This is the easiest mistake to make accidentally (a single committed FBX) and the hardest to undo (it lives in git history). The second-biggest trap is consistency: mixing marketplace packs across the Ithar/Chorus/Deathless/Corsair grammars produces a Franken-fleet: no marketplace contains vertical-fin sails, grown non-anatomical flesh, faceless bone-machines, and a battle-barge in one unified style.
