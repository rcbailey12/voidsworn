## (a) SHIP MOVEMENT SYSTEMS

1. Hex grids keep fleet tactics tractable - ADOPT. Source: "Benefits of a hex based grid in tactical combat?", r/StrategyRpg discussion via Libreddit mirror - https://libreddit.perennialte.ch/r/StrategyRpg/comments/w2cjqu/benefits_of_a_hex_based_grid_in_tactical_combat/igsgcyx/?context=3 - What it says (source): with ~20 ships, discrete hex positions keep range, facing, and AI pathing tractable and avoid diagonal-movement exploits. Why adopt: hexes give unambiguous adjacency and facing at fleet scale, which a free-floating plane cannot.

2. Conserve linear velocity, ignore residual spin (THANCS precedent) - ADAPT. Source: "THANCS - a Newtonian space TBT," Sufficient Velocity forum - https://forums.sufficientvelocity.com/threads/thancs-a-newtonian-space-tbt.5954/ - What it says (source): hex-based Newtonian tabletop system where ships keep their linear velocity from turn to turn. Why adapt: keep the drift but drop residual-spin bookkeeping; a 20-ship roguelite does not need rotational inertia.

3. Energy-cost movement (Relic Space model) - ADOPT the core cost model, adapted. Sources: GamesPress, "Starship Strategy RPG Relic Space Out Now on Steam Early Access" - https://www.gamespress.com/ru/Starship-Strategy-RPG-Relic-Space-Out-Now-on-Steam-Early-Access and "Open-World Space Game Relic Space Arriving on Steam Early Access in Q3" - https://www.gamespress.com/zh-TW/Open-World-Space-Game-Relic-Space-Arriving-on-Steam-Early-Access-in-Q3 - What it says (source): hex grid, one tile per turn, straight-line motion free, turning/starting/stopping costs energy. Why: a fleet-level energy/delta-v budget that makes turning and stopping the expensive choices gives inertia-like feel on a grid with simple rules.

4. Pure hexless free movement - SKIP for the base game; ADAPT vector plotting as an advanced option. Sources: Full Thrust discussion, Warhammer-Empire forum - https://warhammer-empire.com/theforum/index.php/topic,43215.msg907409.html and "Vector Movement Explained," BoardGameGeek - https://boardgamegeek.com/filepage/141873/vector-movement-explained - What it says (source): free vector plotting on an open table with ruler-measured drift. Why skip: ruler-style free movement is fiddly on screen at 20 ships and multiplies AI and UI complexity.

5. "Pivot, then fly straight" (A Billion Suns zones-of-control style) - SKIP as the physics model; ADAPT its readability. Source: "Zones Of Control," Planet Smasher Games (A Billion Suns) - https://planetsmashergames.com/a-billion-suns-blog/zones-of-control/ - What it says (source): ships turn to a new heading then translate in a straight line with no inertia. Why: no-inertia movement is highly readable, but skipping drift loses the space feel; use its clean telegraphing for previews.

6. Full 3D orientation without free 3D translation - ADAPT. Source: "Starlight Tactics," Steam DB - https://www.eprison.de/steamdb/app,354050.html - What it says (source): space fleet tactics marketed with yaw/pitch/roll ship orientation on a constrained tactical plane. Why adapt: orientation in three axes reads as "space" while keeping all movement on one plane keeps the UI and AI tractable.

7. Grid/hybrid to avoid UI fiddliness - ADOPT. Source: "Tactical RPG tactics," polygone.gamedev.net forum - https://polygone.gamedev.net/forums/topic/668841-tactical-rpg-tactics/5232392/ - What it says (source): free placement in tactical games creates constant small UI fights over exact positions. Why: 20 ships need discrete, unambiguous positions so orders are clicks, not pixel hunts.

8. Grid for rules, continuous spline for presentation - ADAPT. Source: "Wargaming in Both Rectilinear and Hexagonal Spaces," NASA Technical Reports Server via archive.org - https://archive.org/details/NASA_NTRS_Archive_20130008668 - What it says (source): rectilinear vs hexagonal adjudication with continuous motion presented on top. Why: adjudicate on the hex grid, then animate ships along smooth splines between hexes so the game feels continuous while the rules stay discrete.

9. "Coast + thrust delta" (Triplanetary vector model) - ADOPT in simplified form. Sources: Steve Jackson Games - https://www.sjgames.com/; BoardGameGeek movement thread - https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/printerfriendly/2020307; GitHub Triplanetary project - https://github.com/anicolao/triplanetary/tree/3c793eae0e907e6e4e6e661d9723daf58a4457a6 - What it says (source): default movement repeats last turn's vector; spending fuel shifts the destination by one hex. Why: one elegant rule produces believable drift and makes the delta-v budget visible as a destination offset.

10. Two-endpoint preview UI (from Mayday's three-counter inertia) - ADAPT. Source: Space Gamer magazine PDF via archive.org - https://ia800702.us.archive.org/20/items/space-gamer-18/Space_Gamer_18_text.pdf - What it says (source): Mayday tracked drift and thrust with multiple counters; the physical version was clumsy. Why: replace the counters with a digital two-endpoint preview - a coast-endpoint ghost and a draggable thrust-endpoint ghost - which keeps the inertia without the bookkeeping.

11. "Repeat last move, then spend movement points from the endpoint" - ADOPT (called the single most practical fleet-scale finding). Source: "Turn based tactical combat," Interstellar Space game forum - https://interstellarspacegame.com/forums/index.php?threads/turn-based-tactical-combat.51/ - What it says (source): each ship's default move repeats its previous displacement, and the player spends movement points from that endpoint. Why: it makes inertia the zero-effort default and thrust the active choice, which scales to 20 ships.

12. Full Attack Vector: Tactical fidelity - SKIP; ADAPT segmented thrust execution only. Sources: Ad Astra Games - https://www.adastragames.com/attack-vector-tactical/; Noble Knight - https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2148353677/; Wikipedia - https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/mobile-html/Attack_Vector:_Tactical - What it says (source): fuel fractions, declining mass, thermodynamics, and 3D attitude bookkeeping per ship. Why skip: wonderful simulation, unplayable bookkeeping at 20 ships; borrow only the idea of thrust applied in segments along the path.

13. Children of a Dead Earth as a movement model - SKIP; ADAPT only the delta-v cost display. Source: Steam store page - https://store.steampowered.com/app/476530/Children_of_a_Dead_Earth/ - What it says (source): hard orbital simulation with N-body physics, delta-v, and moment of inertia. Why skip: it is a physics simulator, not a fleet tactics game; take only the idea of exposing delta-v cost per maneuver so players feel fuel as a budget.

14. Persistent drift for capital ships - ADOPT, with strong preview/automation. Source: "Battlefleet Gothic: Armada" review, Hooked Gamers - https://mail.hookedgamers.com/pc/battlefleet_gothic_armada/review/article-1645.html - What it says (source): capital ships carry persistent drift that the player must plan around. Why: drift makes big ships feel massive; at 20 ships it only works with clear drift telegraphing and optional automation.

15. Delta-v vs travel-time tradeoff (Terra Invicta) - ADAPT to a fleet-level budget. Source: "Terra Invicta Dev Diary #17: Rocket Science," Pavonis Interactive forums - https://pavonisinteractive.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=56316 - What it says (source): spending more delta-v buys shorter travel time; the diary presents it as a strategic tradeoff. Why: a fleet-level maneuver budget creates the same tradeoff without per-thruster micromanagement.

16. Banking as animation only, not physics - ADAPT. Sources: Oolite forum - https://bb.oolite.space/viewtopic.php?p=248835; GameDev.net, "Space combat: No more turning battles?" - https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/54903-space-combat-no-more-turning-battles/?page=3 - What it says (source): in space, turning the nose does not change the travel vector; banking is a visual convention. Why: keep banking in the presentation layer so ships look alive, but never let the rules pretend a bank changes the vector.

17. Minimum move + delayed turn windows for heavy ships - ADOPT. Source: "Battlefleet Gothic" rules PDF via Tesera - https://tesera.ru/images/items/1815103/Battlefleet%20Gothic%20-%20Misc%20Box%20Pieces.pdf - What it says (source): ships move half to full speed and get one 45/90 degree turn; cruisers must move 10 cm before turning, battleships 15 cm. Why: delayed turning sells mass with one simple rule and differentiates ship classes at a glance.

18. Turn rate as a stat + emergency maneuvers; slow turning animation - ADOPT. Sources: "Battlefleet Gothic: Armada" review, MMGaming - https://mmgaming.net/2016/04/27/battlefleet-gothic-armada-review/; Computerbase review snippet - https://www.computerbase.de/2016-05/battlefleet-gothic-armada-test.1922/seite-2 - What it says (source): limited turn rates with special emergency maneuvers; slow turning reads as weight. Why: turn-rate-as-stat is cheap to implement and instantly communicates class; the "ponderous direction change" claim is a snippet only and could not be fully verified.

19. Ghost endpoint + facing ghost + path line preview; coast endpoint before thrust endpoint - ADOPT. Source: "Combat system update and prototype video," Predestination dev blog - https://predestinationgame.com/2012/07/16/combat-system-update-and-prototype-video/ - What it says (source): movement preview shows a ghost at the destination with facing, plus the path line. Why: with inertia, a plain path line hides drift; an explicit coast-endpoint ghost plus a thrust-endpoint ghost makes the preview honest.

20. Movement cones + "plot all, then execute" - ADOPT. Source: "Weekly Blog Post: Week 13 - Leading up to Softs," CMU Space Pirates dev blog - https://projects-old.etc.cmu.edu/space-pirates/?p=123 - What it says (source): each ship shows a movement cone of reachable positions; all orders are plotted, then the turn executes. Why: cones show the delta-v budget spatially; plotting all 20 ships before execution avoids 20 sequential turns.

21. End-of-turn position markers on projected move lines - ADAPT. Source: "Fleet Move Lines," FreeOrion forum - https://freeorion.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1896&view=print - What it says (source): a line alone does not show where each ship ends the turn; markers along the line fix that. Why: with drift, the line's shape and the endpoint are different information - show both.

22. Move trails during execution - ADOPT, adapted (bright for the selected ship, faint for the rest). Source: "Move Trails image," Concealed Intent on ModDB - https://www.moddb.com/games/concealed-intent/images/move-trails - What it says (source): persistent trails drawn during the movement phase. Why: trails make 20 simultaneous moves readable as a pattern instead of 20 independent motions.

23. Simultaneous (WEGO) execution after plotting - ADOPT. Sources: "Star Hammer: The Vanguard Prophecy," Slitherine - https://www6.slitherine.com/game/star-hammer-the-vanguard-prophecy/pc; WCCFtech review - https://wccftech.com/star-hammer-the-vanguard-prophecy-review/ - What it says (source): orders plotted for all units, then one simultaneous resolution phase. Why: avoids 20-ship i-go-you-go downtime and makes the fleet feel coordinated.

24. Full Star Fleet Battles impulse chart - SKIP for 20 ships; ADAPT speed-based sub-phases/initiative only. Sources: "Introduction to Star Fleet Battles," Amarillo Design Bureau PDF - http://www.starfleetgames.com/SFB_Cadet_Rulebook.pdf; Wikipedia, impulse-based turn system - https://encyclopedia.thefreedictionary.com/Impulse-based+turn+system - What it says (source): turns subdivided into many impulses with per-impulse movement. Why skip: full impulse bookkeeping at 20 ships is a spreadsheet, not a game; borrow only speed-based ordering.

FLAGGED UNVERIFIED (a): the BFG Armada "ponderous direction change" claim was a search snippet, not the full article; Star Hammer's "3D" movement is marketing language while a review snippet says ships move on two axes and 3D is camera-only; Children of a Dead Earth's turn-based vs real-time combat structure was not confirmed on the Steam page; Starlight Tactics' fleet-scale playability rests on store marketing only.
(a) bottom line: "grid for adjudication, continuous vectors for feel" is the strongest pattern; the main design risk is preview clarity and fleet-scale pacing, not physics fidelity.

## (b) PHYSICS PRESENTATION

THRUSTER PLUMES / ENGINE GLOW
1. Plume direction and shape should match the actual thrust vector and relative velocity - ADAPT (do not fully simulate Newtonian particles). Source: I-Novae Studios forum, "I-Novae: Engine Screenshot Thread!" - https://forums.inovaestudios.com/t/i-novae-engine-screenshot-thread/582/632 - Why: vector-reactive plumes sell thrust cheaply; full particle simulation is overkill for a turn-based game.
2. Plume quality is a major mass cue - ADOPT the principle; ADAPT the "space dust" halo only if it never uses cyan. Source: "Engine Effects (Quite Literally) news," IndieDB - https://www.indiedb.com/games/ggm-project1/news/engine-effects-quite-literally - Why: big soft plumes make big ships feel big; keep the halo white/warm so it never competes with shields.
3. Vacuum plume shape (wide, diffuse) - ADAPT. Source: "Thruster plumes - expansion in vacuum, contraction in atmosphere," Keen Software House support - https://support.keenswh.com - Why: tight jets read as atmospheric; wide diffuse plumes read as space and make thrust visible at fleet scale. SKIP atmospheric pressure variation - space-only combat never needs it.
4. Plume length/intensity proportional to acceleration - ADOPT; SKIP cockpit shake (fleet-scale camera has no cockpit). Source: GameDev StackExchange, "How to imply speed in space combat" - https://gamedev.stackexchange.com - Why: acceleration-rate cues make slow turn-based movement legible; camera shake belongs to a cockpit view.

WEAPON RECOIL
5. Visible impulse kick on the firing ship, but not atmospheric-gun recoil - ADAPT. Source: "Bringing Sci-Fi to Life: The Real Science Behind The Expanse: Osiris Reborn," GamesPress - https://www.gamespress.com/Bringing-Sci-Fi-to-Life-The-Real-Science-Behind-The-Expanse-Osiris-Reb - Why: a short scripted hull nudge/barrel flash reads as power without pretending space has air resistance.
6. Clear muzzle flash and impact feedback; small per-ship jolt, not full-screen shake - ADOPT; reserve screen shake for capital-ship explosions/heavy impacts. Source: "Post Processing and more juice! news - Absolute Territory," IndieDB - https://www.indiedb.com/games/ggm-project1/news/post-processing-and-more-juice - Why: the flash answers "who is shooting"; full-screen shake at 20 ships would be constant and nauseating.
7. Muzzle flash visibility - ADOPT as the functional "who is shooting" cue; SKIP missile smoke trails in space; ADAPT screen shake sparingly. Source: "Project Genesis - James," Steam store page - https://store.steampowered.com - Why: flash identifies the shooter, smoke has no physics justification in vacuum, and shake is a seasoning not a staple. Muzzle flashes warm/white/red - never cyan.

SHIELD HIT RIPPLES
8. Directional impact origin + wrap-around reveal (ripple spreading from the hit point across the shield volume) - ADOPT. Source: Positech Games blog, "Gratuitous Space Shooty Shield tweaks" - https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2023/10/23/gratuitous-space-shooty-shield-tweaks/ - Why: this is exactly the feedback that should own the cyan channel - a directional ripple answers "where did it hit" instantly.
9. Ripple-on-impact - ADOPT; SKIP full-shield noise; ADAPT shield-failure fragmentation (crack-and-shatter at the impact point, fading quickly). Source: I-Novae Studios forum, "Weekly Update #98" - https://forums.inovaestudios.com/t/weekly-update-98/6731/11 - Why: localized ripple is legible; full-shield noise at 20 ships is visual soup; shattering on failure sells the break.
10. Shield reacts on hit and shifts state - ADOPT, adapted so the shift stays within cyan/teal/white-cyan (dim/desaturate toward white as shields weaken), never green/red. Source: EVE Online, "New Visual Damage System on Singularity," EVE Search forum - https://eve-search.com/thread/457138-1 - Why: state change within one hue family preserves the cyan=shields contract while showing degradation.
11. "Blue/cyan flash = shield impact" as a player-learned convention - ADOPT (inference: players already associate cyan/blue flashes with shields, so the exclusivity rule reinforces expectation). Source: EVE University Wiki, "Visual effects" - https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Visual_effects - Why: the convention is free readability - do not spend it on engine glow.

HULL DAMAGE / DESTRUCTION / DEBRIS
12. Sectional damage visuals - ADOPT, adapted to 4-6 sections per ship (NGC-404 uses seven hull sections with per-section health, explosions from the damaged area, fire/smoke, scorched plating, and status callouts like "ENGINES DOWN"/"CORE BREACH"). Source: "NGC-404: Fractured Sector," Steam news - https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/ - Why: sectional damage tells the player where it hurts, which is the tactical half of damage feedback.
13. Full mesh-swap damage states - SKIP for 20 ships; ADAPT with decal-based scorch marks, emissive fire/smoke sprites, removable turret sub-meshes. Source: Roberts Space Industries / Star Citizen Wiki, "Design Notes: New Damage System" - https://robertsspaceindustries.com and https://starcitizen.tools - Why: multi-mesh damage states multiply memory and authoring cost by ship count; decals and sprites give 90% of the read at a fraction of the cost.
14. Fading debris, not fully persistent debris - ADAPT (Starsector debris disappears after a set time to cut clutter and cost; disabled ships drift off rather than exploding). Source: Fractal Softworks Forum (Starsector), "Debris disappears" - https://fractalsoftworks.com/forum/ - Why: persistent debris at 20 ships becomes visual noise and a perf cost; fading debris keeps the battlefield legible.
15. Scripted event-sequence explosions - ADOPT (GSB cruiser explosion = a scripted sequence of 17 distinct events); bright white glare is a useful non-cyan flash. Source: Positech Games blog, "Gratuitous Space Battles explosion breakdown" - https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/category/programming/page/41/ - Why: scripted sequences are controllable, repeatable, and sync cleanly with turn resolution; white/yellow glare keeps the color language clean.
16. Full wreckage fragmentation - SKIP; ADAPT with a single drifting non-collidable hulk that fades over time (players find "explode into nothing" unsatisfying; NEBULOUS reportedly said break-up wreckage is incompatible with its single-hidden-collider hit model). Source: Steam Community, "Too bad wrecks dont break up into debris - NEBULOUS: Fleet Command" - https://steamcommunity.com/app/887570/discussions/3/599659929547193446/ (same thread, second finding: ADOPT fade-out wreckage with fire/smoke; a player complained the main hulk "disappears into thin air" and suggested a simple fiery fade or pieces floating away) - Why: a fading hulk gives destruction weight without fragmentation physics.

SIM VS SCRIPTED
17. Scripted sequences for explosions and shield hits - ADOPT ("scripted does not mean cheap; it means controlled and repeatable"; easier to sync with turn resolution). Source: Frontier Forums, "The Star Citizen Thread v 3.0" - https://forums.frontier.co.uk/ - Why: turn-based resolution needs deterministic presentation; scripted events snap to the computed state cleanly.
18. Readability-first: physics as reference, not constraint - ADOPT (thruster/shield effects should be canned or parameter-driven, not fully simulated). Source: "Physics - Not a Dirty Word," Roberts Space Industries - https://robertsspaceindustries.com - Why: at 20 ships the player reads the battle, not the fluid dynamics.
19. Layered explosion structure (flash, shockwave, debris spawn, fading smoke) - ADOPT, adapted in fidelity, rather than a single particle burst. Source: "Realistic Explosions Pack - Unity VFX," Ultimate Game Assets - https://ultimategameassets.com - Why: layered structure reads at fleet distance where a single burst would vanish in one frame.
20. Cyan as the exclusive shield color - ADOPT; ADAPT health-state variation within cyan; never use cyan for engine glow, weapon impacts, or explosions. Sources: various VFX asset pages/community examples (Unity Asset Store, SummerEngine, StockCake) - no single URL was given for this finding. Why: one exclusive channel for the most tactically important binary state (shield up/down) is the cheapest readability win in the game.

FLAGGED UNVERIFIED (b): GSB explosion timing comes from a developer blog describing their own implementation (not independently verified); the Star Citizen "scripted" explosion claim comes from a forum post, not an official developer statement; EVE shield color behavior was described on a test server and in a wiki - live-game behavior may differ; the NEBULOUS technical limitation is community-reported, citing developer statements, with no primary developer source accessed.

## (c) GRAPHICS PIPELINE FOR HULLS

WEAR AND GRIME

Why: consistent edge wear across 20 ships with no per-frame shader cost. Inference: one curvature map per hull type beats per-frame procedural edge detection.
5. Curvature as a mask - ADOPT as mask, ADAPT with art direction. Source: "Curvature Map Guide: Bake Edge and Cavity Masks," CraftPBR - https://craftpbr.com/blog/curvature-map-guide - What it says (source): curvature maps convert surface-shape changes to grayscale - convex bright, concave dark. Why: drive edge wear and cavity grime from it, but do not let it generate uniform wear everywhere.
6. Godot built-in projected decals for localized wear/stains/battle scars - ADOPT; ADAPT the count (clustered decals drawn with the mesh, distance fade). Source: "Decal," Godot docs - https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.0/classes/class_decal.html - What it says (source): Decal node projects color/normal/ORM/emission textures onto meshes via clustered decals. Why: adds per-ship variety without unique textures. Inference: PC-only Forward+ is not bound by the 8-decal mobile limit, but fill-rate/sorting still matter.
7. Parameterized decals for wear variation - ADAPT; SKIP custom decal shaders unless necessary. Source: "Add support for custom decal materials," Godot proposal discussion - https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/4938 - What it says (source): decals with opacity masks and shader parameters create damage/wear variations without new textures. Why: a small decal set with adjustable opacity/scale/rotation covers many ships.

BAKED VS PROCEDURAL

Why: baked albedo/ORM/normal sets are cheaper and more predictable for 20 ships.
9. Procedural only for simple, animated, or highly repeated effects - ADAPT. Source: "Procedural vs Baked UV Images - Special Case," Soft8Soft forum - https://www.soft8soft.com/topic/procedural-vs-baked-uv-images-special-case/ - What it says (source): a simple procedural texture showed no observable slowdown vs baked; loading baked textures has its own cost. Why: procedural fits engine glow, shield ripples, simple panel patterns - not the main hull. Inference: anecdotal WebGL claims, not a benchmark.
10. Baked normal maps for panel lines and greebles - ADOPT. Source: "How to Properly Panel a Low Poly Space Ship," Blender StackExchange - https://blender.stackexchange.com/questions/258621/how-to-properly-panel-a-low-poly-space-ship - What it says (source): bake high-poly panel/greeble detail onto low-poly hulls. Why: the standard way to keep 20 ships cheap while reading as detailed metal.

ENGINE COMPARISON (c) verdicts: PBR - tie (both support the same texture pipeline); wear/decals - Godot advantage (built-in clustered Decal node); damage - tie (both do shader damage; Godot's decals make localized damage easier); lighting/IBL - Godot slightly easier for a unified environment, Babylon equally capable but more manual; post-processing - Babylon advantage for one-pipeline convenience, Godot advantage for AgX and built-in fog; instancing - tie (both more than enough for 20 ships; both better for debris than hero ships); 20-ship performance - tie (neither engine struggles at 20 ships; choose on workflow, not ship count); deployment - Godot advantage for native PC build and conventional editor, Babylon advantage for web deployment or existing Babylon code. Overall inference: neither engine's performance decides at 20 ships; Godot's built-in decals, unified Environment, native PC deployment, and AgX tonemapping fit a hull-heavy game, while Babylon's DefaultRenderingPipeline is convenient - the migration cost is the main argument for staying on Babylon.
FLAGGED UNVERIFIED (c): texture-baking benchmark numbers (1.36 ms, 4.5x, RTX 3060) are from a specific rock scene; "procedural is just as fast" claims are anecdotal; Star Citizen's 200-meshes/15-30-draw-call numbers are 2015-era and do not transfer directly; Babylon's decal support being "more manual" is an inference from documentation absence; ACES-vs-AgX cyan desaturation is inference with no tested source.
(c) bottom line: baked PBR pipeline with layered wear (base paint, exposed metal at edges via baked curvature/AO masks, grime at seams, decals for scars); shader-driven damage over mesh-state swaps at 20 ships; key/fill/rim rig + prefiltered environment map; emissive windows/running lights on a separate material and never cyan; bloom before tonemapping with ACES or AgX.

(e) synthesis: On weightless ships - Adopt: visible acceleration and alignment differences by mass; impact feedback on every hit (flash, ripple, debris, sound). Adapt: independent rotation only if the vector is always visible. Skip: relying on detailed hull art to convey mass - motion and feedback must do it. On effect spam - Adopt: focus-filtering of firing lines, nameplate LOD, low-VFX presets, faded beam/torpedo tails. Adapt: importance-based VFX intensity - routine fire dim, kills and shield breaks bright. Skip: persistent full-opacity shield bubbles or engine trails on every ship. On projectile readability - Adopt: a clear distinction between instant-hit beams and traveling torpedoes; focus-filtering for both. Adapt: torpedo flight time with visible target tether and predicted intercept; damage applied at impact, not launch. Skip: cosmetic-only travel time (damage on launch) - it makes the preview lie. On the torpedo question specifically: the source evidence gives a clear tradeoff - Instant hit: easy to read "who hit whom," but no counterplay window; can feel like a hitscan spreadsheet. High immediate impact, low anticipation. Best for beams, close-range direct fire. Flight time: requires visible target tether, ETA, and intercept preview, otherwise players lose track of "at whom." Creates anticipation, dodge/intercept decisions, and multi-turn commitment. Best for torpedoes, seeking missiles, anti-capital finishers. DeepSeek's recommendation (inference): give torpedoes real flight time with real impact-time damage, but make travel explicitly readable - a moving projectile with a target tether, a predicted impact turn, and a clear intercept/miss indicator; torpedoes become a deliberate, heavier commitment, not a slower version of the same thing. Reserve instant-hit torpedoes only if they are meant to be just another gun.
(e) bottom line: the strongest anti-patterns are not cosmetic - weightlessness comes from missing impact feedback and weak mass differentiation; VFX overload comes from persistent overlapping information (threat lines, nameplates, trails), not from one flashy explosion; projectile readability fails when the player cannot answer "who is firing at whom," and torpedo flight time worsens that failure unless target and ETA are explicitly shown; animation pacing fails when the player cannot skip, cannot predict, and cannot act during playback - shortening clips alone is not enough. Highest-leverage fixes: focus-filtering, impact confirmation, explicit torpedo tether/ETA previews, and dead-air elimination - not more VFX.
