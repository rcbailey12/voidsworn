## (a) BLENDER MCP SERVERS AND AGENT-DRIVEN BLENDER

No high-level modeling primitives. Decision: ADOPT as the baseline official channel - it is the maintained, first-party integration. Flag: none (primary source).

Sourced: The official server runs agent Python with no built-in guards; the only real safety boundary is a VM, consistent with the README's own warning that generated Python can delete or exfiltrate data. ## (b) SEPARABLE PARTS FOR RIGGING AND ANIMATION

(b)-1. Source: "Make-It-Poseable: Feed-forward Latent Posing Model for 3D Characters" - arXiv. URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16767v2. Sourced: Data-driven auto-rigging algorithms inherently struggle with AI-generated assets carrying topological flaws such as fused limbs; large deformations expose missing geometry. Decision: ADOPT the premise - treat part fusion as an expected failure mode, not an edge case. Flag: none (peer-reviewed/preprint research).

(b)-2. Source: "How to Prepare AI 3D Models for Animation" - Customuse blog. URL: https://customuse.com/learn/prepare-ai-3d-models-for-animation. Sourced: Pre-rigging checklist treats "independent movers separated and named" as a hard requirement; any "No" is a blocker for deforming assets. If limbs are fused, regenerating from better reference images is often faster than repairing. Decision: ADOPT as the rigging gate checklist. Flag: secondary (vendor blog), but concrete and production-derived.

(b)-3. Source: Blender Manual - "Separate". URL: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/editing/mesh/separate.html. Sourced: Three modes - Selection, By Material, By Loose Parts; By Loose Parts creates one object per disconnected piece. Decision: ADOPT - run a By-Loose-Parts separation count as the deterministic separability test before rigging. Flag: none (official docs).

(b)-4. Source: Customuse blog plus Make-It-Poseable paper. Sourced: Generate parts separately or use kit/part-based modeling with explicit part naming, then verify separability before committing to a skeleton. Decision: ADOPT for characters; ADAPT for ships (ship hulls/turrets/engines are naturally part-based - enforce the same separation discipline). Flag: mixed blog plus research paper.

(b)-5. Source: Customuse blog plus Blender Manual. Sourced: AI models often bake everything into one material/atlas; one material slot per logical surface helps and Separate-by-Material can split mechanically - but a hand and sword can share vertices at the grip while having separate materials, so material separation does not prove topological separation; the loose-parts check is still required. Decision: ADOPT as a secondary signal, never the sole check. Flag: blog (secondary) plus official manual (primary).

## (c) HUMANOID AND CHARACTER STEERING

(c)-1. Source: Tripo AI Blog - "GPT-6 Astra 3D Character Workflow: Rig and Animate from One Image". URL: https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/gpt-6-astra-3d-character-workflow. Sourced: Reference images "lock" the design rather than merely provide it; fix ambiguous areas in the references themselves before generation (bad cropping hides surfaces or leaves unwanted parts), since ambiguity propagates through the whole workflow. Decision: ADOPT - clean and lock references before any generation step. Flag: secondary (vendor blog), hands-on practice.

(c)-5. Source: same blender-ai-mcp TASK-138 document. (c)-6. Source: LinkedIn post - Charles Durham's AI 3D design practice reflection. ## Cross-cutting synthesis (leg's inference, not a new source)

For (a), the official Blender Lab MCP server is a thin Python-execution channel with doc resources and no modeling tools; community servers add structured tools and render feedback, but none remove the need for agent-authored bpy scripts when remodeling geometry - the fallback is disciplined script-and-verify loops with bounded mutations and screenshot/state inspection. For (b), fused limbs are a documented AI-mesh failure mode; prevention is part-based generation with explicit naming, detection is a loose-parts separation count plus a test-pose deformation check before rigging. For (c), steering means locking measurable constraints (isolated part references, coordinate measurement, staged anatomical phases, proportion metrics), not better descriptions; free generation fails in ways prompts cannot fix. For (d), externalize state to files, chunk into validated phases, and hand off to fresh sessions - conversation context alone is structurally fragile. For (e), Library Overrides plus BlenDiff snapshots give freeze plus diff review. For (f), glTF-Validator plus a game-readiness checklist (scale, pivot, tri/texture budgets, rig, face orientation, packed PBR channels) as a hard gate. For (g), plan mode as read-only checkpoint, Opus-review/smaller-model-execute delegation, named-critic screenshot loops, and handover/resume file-backed state. Transfer note for this project: the humanoid-specific findings apply by analogy to ships - hull/turret/engine as separately generated parts with locked references and per-part checkpoints; ~20-ship fleets make the headless CLI batch verification in (a)-11 and the hard export gate in (f) especially valuable.
