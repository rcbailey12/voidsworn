## Sheet 1: Story and career mode scope

**Question:** How much story and career should Ryan approve now?

**Story length**

- **Option 1, three beats per faction.** Ryan sees one scripted choice in sector 1, the consequence and a named rival in sector 2, and a rival flagship battle in sector 3, then a permanent faction reward. About 15 minutes of reading across a run. This is the plan B shape.
- **Option 2, five beats per faction.** Two extra choice and consequence steps. Arcs feel fuller, but the writing load grows. No new machinery is needed, so the cost is mostly text.
- **Option 3, seven or more beats across several runs.** Story carries over between runs. Ryan sees a longer campaign, but this needs new state and save work, delays A and B, and conflicts with the short arc described in BACKLOG 4b.

**Text volume**

- **Option 1, lean, about 80 to 100 words per beat.** Fast to write and easy to read. Choices are clear but characters stay thin.
- **Option 2, standard, about 100 to 150 words per beat.** Enough room for a choice to land and a rival to have a voice. This matches the pack's estimate.
- **Option 3, rich, about 200 to 300 words per beat.** Stronger flavour and more character, but more writing, more localisation, and a risk of reading fatigue in a roguelite.

**Voice or none**

- **Option 1, text only.** Ryan sees silent text in the patron quote style. Fastest and cheapest, and it fits the plan's assumption.
- **Option 2, short voice barks.** Ryan hears a selection line, a victory line, a defeat line, and a flagship sighted line for each admiral. Gives personality, but needs casting, recording, and budget.
- **Option 3, full voice acting for arcs.** Ryan hears every beat voiced. Expensive, slow, and high risk for a first story pass. Not recommended now.

**Portrait art style**

- **Option 1, patron-style glyph crests only.** Ryan sees clean faction symbols, no faces. Cheapest and fastest. Defers the portrait question until he chooses a style.
- **Option 2, painterly busts in the approved template.** Ryan sees a consistent gallery that matches Voice of the Deep v4. Needs 15 portraits plus Matriarch and Mending. Medium cost, exact days UNSURE.
- **Option 3, stylised emblem busts.** Cheaper than painterly, but may clash with the approved template and make characters less memorable.

**My picks:** story Option 1, text Option 2, voice Option 1, portraits Option 1.

**Overall pick: Option A first, then Option B.** A is days of work, it makes B's rivals audible, and its medals and records give B's payouts somewhere visible to land. B answers the most backlog bullets per unit of risk without touching battle mechanics, the node generator, or the save format's shape. C is not the next step. It is big, it touches the save layer, and flagship persistence works against the game's centre of gravity.

**What stays open for Ryan:** the four scope questions from BACKLOG line 64. If he wants longer arcs, B can extend to five beats per faction with writing only. Portrait style remains open until he picks one. All effort numbers here are pack INFERRED, not measured.

## Sheet 2: Music style for an adaptive score

**Question:** What should the adaptive score sound like?

### Direction 1: Choral steel

**What it sounds like:** a ship's chapel with a choir, low brass, timpani, and sustained strings. Serious, ceremonial, and expensive-sounding.

**Adaptive layers:** exploration is a low choir pad, a distant horn, and a slow string line. Battle adds drums, brass punches, a swelling choir, and metal hits. Victory opens into a full major chord with bells. Defeat drops the choir to a low hum and fades the strings.

**References:**

- BattleTech, "'Mech Bay Cantata", slow hymn-like chanting: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/3a1aOpWK9cQMQJMGRoiUR1).
- Rogue Trader, "The Imperium", gothic choral weight: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/6QBpYCAAYh8RmPvch8lIgK).
- Sins of a Solar Empire II, "The Final Push", heroic orchestral push: [Steam soundtrack page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3154090/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II__Soundtrack/).

### Direction 2: Void ritual

**What it sounds like:** a deep drone under everything, frame drums, slow metal scrapes, breath, and wordless chant. No clear melody. Strange, patient, and heavy.

**Adaptive layers:** exploration is sub bass, distant clangs, and a lone bone flute. Battle adds a frame drum pulse, a distorted horn, layered whispers, and rising dread. Victory lets the drone open into a warm chord and one voice. Defeat lets the drone swallow everything while a heartbeat fades.

**References:**

- Homeworld, "Turanic Raiders", dark ambient percussion: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/6xoXUJvBU9hKnM3bL1IqCZ).
- Stellaris, "Faster Than Light (Instrumental)", slow cosmic build: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/3Nx26K0HGFDzed0EVCcX48).
- FTL, "Void (Explore)", sparse and cold: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/7us4WRnwvqCE3cppHVNjIu).

### Direction 3: Machine pulse

**What it sounds like:** a reactor room. Pulsing analog bass, distorted drum machine, servo whines, radio static, and cold arpeggios. Mechanical and tense.

**Adaptive layers:** exploration is a ticking pulse, a low hum, and data blips. Battle adds a driving 4/4 beat, a distorted bass riff, and alarm stabs. Victory resolves the pulse into a clean major synth chord. Defeat is a power-down pitch fall and static.

**References:**

- FTL, "Rockmen (Battle)", driving synth battle: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/7us4WRnwvqCE3cppHVNjIu).
- Stellaris, "Battle For Supremacy", hybrid synth and orchestral pressure: [Spotify album page](https://open.spotify.com/embed/album/3Nx26K0HGFDzed0EVCcX48).
- Sins of a Solar Empire II, "Locked On", tight combat pulse: [Steam soundtrack page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3154090/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II__Soundtrack/).

**My pick: Void ritual.** It gives the game a signature that is not generic space orchestra, it leaves room for faction colours (bone, membrane, porcelain, machine), and it moves from quiet exploration to dread without becoming a wall of sound. Choral steel is the safe second layer for Hegemony and Voidsworn. Machine pulse can be the Deathless colour.

**Licensing:** all references above are for listening only. The backlog allows recorded sound packs, but a famous track is not something the game can ship without a licence. UNSURE on exact licence costs and on whether the team can record live choir or solo voices.

## Sheet 3: Admiral portraits direction

**Question:** What direction should Ryan approve for the 15 unmade portraits, plus Matriarch v3 and Mending?

**Fixed points:** the template is a three-quarter bust, lit from the left in faction glow, with a soft dark plate, painterly finish, and a thin frame. Voice of the Deep v4 is final. Matriarch v3 awaits Ryan's verdict. Mending v3 cannot be retried and needs a genuinely fresh direction. Chorus rules: grown flesh, membrane, chitin, bile, growth, gross; no human features, no Earth-animal anatomy, no buildings, instruments, or architecture. Portraits should reflect their Command.

### Option 1: Template completion

Ryan sees the 15 unmade portraits finished in the approved template, one consistent gallery. Matriarch v3 is either approved or held. Mending stays empty until Ryan gives a fresh direction.

**Trade-off:** fastest route to a complete set, but the Chorus risk is deferred and Mending remains a visible gap.

### Option 2: Chorus gate

Ryan sees no new portraits ship until he gives the Matriarch verdict and a fresh Mending direction. Then the Chorus is built first as the style test, and the other 14 follow.

**Trade-off:** highest coherence and least rework, but the slowest start. It forces the two open decisions before any art budget is spent.

### Option 3: Two-track

Ryan sees the 15 non-Chorus portraits move ahead in the template, while Matriarch and Mending get a separate Chorus design sprint. No Chorus portrait ships until Ryan signs off.

**Trade-off:** progress on most factions, Chorus held to a higher bar, and a slight risk that Chorus feels visually separate. That may be acceptable because the Chorus is meant to be alien.

**My pick: Option 3, two-track.** The template is already approved and the 15 portraits are not blocked. The only true blockers are Matriarch v3 and Mending. Two-track keeps the safe work moving and gives the Chorus attention without retrying a failed generation.

**What I need from Ryan:**

- **Matriarch v3:** approve it, or say what to change. If approved, the Spawning surge portrait can show brood structures releasing swarms.
- **Mending:** a genuinely fresh direction. Three fresh seeds, not retries: (a) "regrowth seam", a vertical seam of glossy new tissue closing over a cracked carapace, with repair tendrils; (b) "brood sac", a translucent sac of small repair organisms suspended in membrane; (c) "scar shell", a calcified shell that heals by growing bright scar tissue over breaches. Each keeps the no-face, no-human, no-Earth-animal rules and reflects Regrowth tide.
- **The 15:** confirm the existing painterly template is still the target, or choose a cheaper emblem style.

**What stays open:** Matriarch verdict, Mending direction, whether the 15 use painterly busts or emblem busts, and the faction order after the Chorus gate.
