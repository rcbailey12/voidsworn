Voidsworn campaign screen information audit, blind leg

Scope note: this review judges information design only. No screenshots exist, so beauty, layout feel and clutter as seen are out of scope. Anything that needs a screenshot is tagged UNSURE rather than guessed. Game claims cite the evidence pack sections or the attached code files; comparison-game claims cite the pages linked in the text.

1. Route planning

Verdict: PARTIAL

What the pack establishes. Before a jump, the 3D map gives the player a lot. Reachable nodes are shown three ways: lane colour and flow, a pulsing ring, and dashed lines on the flat map (pack s1; map3d.gd 419-429, 459; map_view.gd 41-50). Node type is visible for every node, with a legend naming every type (pack s1; campaign_ui.gd 381). Fight identity is on the map before commitment: encounter name, board name, and the node modifier's short name (pack s1; map_view.gd 56-59; map3d.gd 485, 516). Rewards are printed: patron sigil, glyph, short name and a rare-or-better star for boons, and plain labels for Refit, Credits and Repairs (pack s1; map3d.gd 513-519; campaign_ui.gd 432-439).

The hover card adds deep per-node detail and, on the 3D map only, a route comparison. Hovering a node lights the safest and richest routes from the current position through that node to the sector flagship, with running-total chips for fights and repair stops on every node of both routes (pack s1; map3d.gd 522-526, 597-604; map_intel.gd 115-127). The card prints both routes' totals: fight count with elite count, repair stops, shipyards, boons, refits, relics, and the worst fight's band name (pack s1; map3d.gd 597-604). Below the map, preview panels repeat the currently reachable fights with encounter, archetype, modifier, elite flag, board, descriptions and reward line, deduplicated among themselves (pack s1; campaign_ui.gd 384-406).

Where it falls short. The route planner is 3D-only. The flat map has no danger bands, no route comparison and no hover card; its tooltip gives node type, board, encounter, archetype and modifier, but no strength estimate and no route totals (pack s1; map_view.gd 103-114). Since the 3D map is the default (campaign_ui.gd 40), this is a fallback gap rather than the main experience, but it is still a whole-map information absence. The "richest" route is scored by an internal formula that weights boons at 3, relics at 4, refits at 2, credits at 1, shipyards at 2 and events at 1 (map_intel.gd path_stats, lines 87-92). The player sees the totals but not the weights, so the label "Richest" is asserted rather than explained. The planner also shows only two routes, safest and richest, not all routes, and the route comparison is reached by hovering a specific node, so comparing two different first jumps means hovering each in turn. The boss is excluded from route danger sums and worst-fight picks because it ends every route (pack s1; map_intel.gd 100), which is a reasonable design choice, but it means the route comparison does not tell the player how hard the boss itself is; the boss's own band is only on its node.

Comparison. BattleTech's star map lets the player browse systems with a base challenge level and then pick contracts from a board, with each contract carrying a skull rating; players report that contract skulls are relative and that the system's skull level is often the more reliable indicator (SpaceBattles forum discussion, lines 7-10, https://forums.spacebattles.com/threads/battletech-at-least-we-get-paid.356947/). That is a similar two-level structure to Voidsworn's node band plus route worst band, but BattleTech lets the player survey the whole neighbourhood before committing, while Voidsworn's route comparison is tied to hovering a node on the current map. Rogue Trader's galaxy map shows preset warp routes colour-coded green, yellow, orange and red by danger, and the player can spend Navigator's Insight to make a route safer before travelling (Gamer Guides, "Navigator's Insight and Charting Routes," lines 20-29, https://www.gamerguides.com/warhammer-40k-rogue-trader/guide/koronus-expanse-exploration/systems/navigators-insight-and-charting-routes-in-rogue-trader). That is a route-level risk readout, less granular than Voidsworn's per-node bands and route totals, but it is visible for every route without hovering a particular node. Sins of a Solar Empire 2 exposes phase-lane topology and lets the player zoom out to a galaxy view, with fleet supply and composition visible in the Military Command menu and on hover (StrategyWiki, "Sins of a Solar Empire II/Gameplay," lines 17-23, https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II/Gameplay; StrategyWiki, "Fleet supply," lines 20-24, https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II/Fleet_supply). Sins 2 is a real-time game, so its route planning is about lane topology and fleet positioning rather than jump-by-jump commitment, but it does show the player the whole lane graph and the fleet's supply cost at a glance.

Assessment. For the 3D map, the route-planning information is genuinely detailed and covers most of what the prompt asks: fights ahead, their estimated difficulty, repair and shipyard placement, rewards, and the identity of the next fights. The flat-map gap, the two-route limit, and the opaque "richest" score keep it from a PASS. PARTIAL is the right verdict.

2. Information at a glance

Verdict: PARTIAL

What the pack establishes. With nothing hovered and no screen opened, the map page shows a header with the sector number and region name, a Save and quit button, the 3D chart with the fleet token on the current node, and an intel strip of up to five boxes: Hostile presence, Rival raiders, Sector condition, Patrons watching, and the Nemesis box (pack s2; campaign_ui.gd 370, 410-437). The side panel, always on the map page and scrollable, shows Credits and Command points used out of cap as large readouts, admiral name and doctrine, the Nemesis card, the faction action panel, the full fleet list with per-ship hull numbers and a 20-segment bar, class, Mark, captain rank and name, readiness word, refit slots and promotion flags, a conditional fleet-hurt warning with average hull and a repair-stop message, a Manage fleet button, refits in the hold, the flagship's aspect name and description, every boon with its text, patrons this run, and every relic with its text (pack s2; campaign_ui.gd 462-514). Run progress is shown spatially: passed nodes go dark and grey, traversed lanes turn gilt, and the fleet token marks the current node (pack s2; map3d.gd 402-408; map_view.gd 47-53).

Where it falls short. The sector condition's effect is tooltip-only. The strip box prints the condition's name, and the description is in the tooltip (pack s2 and s5; campaign_ui.gd 424-425). The faction blurb, the rival-raiders explanation, and the patrons' sigil rule are also tooltip-only (pack s5). Heat rules are printed on the aspect screen but no fetched map-page code repeats them during a sector (pack s2; campaign_ui.gd 354-358). There is no numeric progress counter such as nodes cleared out of total or fights won this sector (pack s2, observed absence). The Nemesis card is always on screen, but as section 4 shows, it does not carry his behaviour text, persona or traits (pack s4). The side panel scrolls, so the lower sections, including boons and relics, are on-demand in practice even though they are on the page (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 466-468).

Comparison. BattleTech's star map surfaces system difficulty and available contracts at the map level, and the contract board exposes pay, salvage and skulls before acceptance; the player can see the neighbourhood's difficulty without opening a detail screen (SpaceBattles forum, lines 7-10; Steam Community, "Skulls Explained," lines 3-8, https://steamcommunity.com/app/637090/discussions/0/1696046342867748477/). Rogue Trader's galaxy map shows route colours directly on the chart, so route risk is always-on at the map level (Gamer Guides, "Koronus Expanse Resource Locations," lines 22-29, https://www.gamerguides.com/warhammer-40k-rogue-trader/guide/koronus-expanse-exploration/systems/koronus-expanse-resource-locations). Sins 2 shows fleet supply and composition in the Military Command menu, with a hover breakdown of how supply is distributed across ships (StrategyWiki, "Fleet supply," lines 20-24; Fandom, "Fleet Logistics," lines 17-21, https://sinsofasolarempire.fandom.com/wiki/Fleet_Logistics). Voidsworn's always-on set is broader than BattleTech's or Rogue Trader's in fleet and build detail, but it leaves some rules information one hover away and does not give a progress number.

Assessment. The player can see money, command points, fleet condition ship by ship, the build, and who is hunting them. The sector's special rules are only partly visible: the condition name is always on, but its effect is tooltip-only, and Heat rules are not on the map page at all. The lack of a progress counter is a smaller gap, but it matters for a run that crosses three sectors. PARTIAL.

3. Danger readability

Verdict: PARTIAL

What the pack establishes. Every battle, elite and boss node gets a danger estimate from the same budget numbers the game uses to build the enemy fleet, against the player's current fleet power (pack s3; map_intel.gd danger, lines 29-58; fleet_power, lines 17-27). The estimate returns a ratio and one of four bands: Easy under 0.8, Even under 1.05, Hard under 1.4, Deadly above (pack s3; map_intel.gd line 11). The file header is explicit that the fleet is rolled at the fight, so the band is expected strength, not exact strength (pack s3; map_intel.gd 5-9). The band appears as a coloured chip with the band name in capitals under every unpassed fight node on the 3D map, and in the hover card as "Danger: &lt;band&gt;" plus the ratio in words, "their strength about N% of your fleet's" (pack s3; map3d.gd 506-512, 580). Route totals name the worst band on each route (pack s1 and s3).

Elite marking uses three channels: a brighter red diamond with a gold crown ring, animated red hazard chevrons on lanes into elite and boss nodes, and the word "elite" in preview panels and route fight counts (pack s3; map3d.gd 268-276, 351, 70-73; campaign_ui.gd 393). The boss is the enemy flagship's hull model at larger scale, facing back down the sector, inside a red crown ring, labelled "Sector flagship" (pack s3; map3d.gd 277-288, 487). Rival nodes get an extra ring in the rival faction's colour, the hover card appends "(rival raiders)," and patrol ships drift around unpassed rival nodes (pack s3; map3d.gd 347-355, 574-576, 357-375). The Nemesis conversion is marked before committing with a red NEMESIS chip, his portrait, and his name on the hover card (pack s3 and s4; map3d.gd 492-499).

Where it falls short. The flat map has no danger bands at all. A player on the flat map gets encounter names and descriptions but no strength estimate anywhere (pack s3, observed gap; map_view.gd has no band code). The band is an estimate, which is the right call given that the enemy fleet is rolled at the fight; exact information would be false precision. Four bands are a defensible grain, but the hover card's ratio is the only explanation of why a fight sits in its band. Node modifiers like bounty and veteran, Heat flags, and for the Nemesis his scars and adaptation count, all feed the ratio, but the card does not itemise them (pack s3 and s4; map_intel.gd 48-54). The player sees the percentage but not the contributors. The band thresholds are as coded; whether they were tuned against play is UNSURE (pack s3).

Comparison. BattleTech uses skull ratings that approximate the tonnage of the initial enemy drop, but players report that mission type and system difficulty can make a skull rating misleading; a 3-skull mission in a low-skull system can be easier than a 3-skull mission in a higher-skull system (Steam Community, "Skulls Explained," lines 3-8 and 23-36; SpaceBattles forum, lines 7-10). Voidsworn's fleet-relative ratio is arguably more directly comparable to the player's actual fleet than a tonnage band, because it is computed against fleet power rather than raw enemy tonnage. Rogue Trader uses four route colours, green, yellow, orange and red, with qualitative risk from "small chance of ship damage" up to "deadly" (Gamer Guides, "Navigator's Insight and Charting Routes," lines 22-29; Steam Community discussion, lines 11-14, https://steamcommunity.com/app/2186680/discussions/0/4035855168797847606/). That is a route-level risk grade, not a per-fight strength estimate. Sins 2 does not present a per-fight danger band in the same way; it gives the player fleet supply and visible fleet composition, leaving the assessment to the player (StrategyWiki, "Fleet supply," lines 20-24; StrategyWiki, "Gameplay," lines 17-23). Voidsworn's four-band estimate is therefore a reasonable middle path: more tailored than a raw tonnage skull, more specific than a route colour.

Assessment. The scheme is sound and the elite, boss and rival markings are multi-channel and readable at the information level. The flat-map absence is a real decision gap, and the hover does not explain which factors produced the band. PARTIAL.

4. The Nemesis as information

Verdict: PARTIAL

What the pack establishes. The side-panel Nemesis card is always on the map page while he lives. It surfaces his full name with title and epithet, his faction, a portrait when the asset exists, a scar count and scar overlays, a "Hunting you:" clock of filled and open dots, a bold red strike warning when his strike flag is set ("His fleet is coming: your next fight is his."), a standing clock explanation, a "Weaknesses:" line with revealed names in gilt and unrevealed entries as "unknown," and an "Adapted:" line listing adaptation names in red (pack s4; campaign_ui.gd 516-540). On the map, an unpassed node he converts gets a red NEMESIS chip and his portrait, and the flat map prints "Nemesis" above it; the hover card names the fight as his (pack s4; map3d.gd 492-499; map_view.gd 60). The danger estimate treats his node as an elite fight and multiplies threat by 10 percent per scar and 6 percent per adaptation (pack s4; map_intel.gd 33-34, 54).

What is missing. The card never shows his faction behaviour text, so the player is never told on the campaign screens what his faction's strike actually does: the Corsair's credit or crippled-ship theft, the Deathless's return, or the other strikes (pack s4, observed absence). It never shows his persona or his strength traits (pack s4). It does not show the fed or riches counters (pack s4). A revived Deathless is indistinguishable from any other scarred Nemesis on the card (pack s4). Weaknesses show name only, never the weakness's effect text; adaptations show generic name only, and the effect and reason are explained only in the one-time sector-change modal (pack s4; campaign_ui.gd 897-899). Scar causes are not surfaced (pack s4).

Which missing item costs the most, and which surfaced item does the most work. The behaviour text is the costliest omission. The clock tells the player when the Nemesis will strike, but without behaviour text the player cannot prepare for what the strike will do. A Corsair strike that can take credits or a crippled ship calls for a different pre-strike posture than a Deathless strike that returns once, and the campaign screens never state the difference. The surfaced item that does the most work is the clock plus the strike warning. The dots and the red tail turn the Nemesis from a background threat into a countdown the player can plan around, and the node marking tells the player where the blow will land before they commit (pack s4).

Comparison. Shadow of Mordor's Nemesis system is the closest reference. New Nemeses begin as silhouettes with no information; the player gathers intel by interrogating Uruks or finding documents, and intel reveals strengths and weaknesses (IGN, "The Nemesis System," lines 30-33, https://www.ign.com/wikis/middle-earth-shadow-of-mordor/The_Nemesis_System; IGN, "Nemesis Traits," lines 2-3, https://www.ign.com/wikis/middle-earth-shadow-of-mordor/Nemesis_Traits). The game also presents personality, fighting style and relationships, not just a stat block (IGN, "The Nemesis System," lines 5-7, 10-35). Shadow of Mordor gates that information behind intel gathering, but once revealed it is specific and actionable: the player knows which strengths to avoid and which weaknesses to exploit. Voidsworn surfaces weakness names but not their effects, and never surfaces behaviour, persona or traits. It therefore gives the player less to act on than Shadow of Mordor does after intel is gathered. The comparison is not exact, because Voidsworn's Nemesis is a single persistent rival rather than a hierarchy, but the information principle is the same: a rival is only as interesting as what the player knows about how to fight him.

Assessment. Identity, portrait, scars, clock, strike warning and node marking are a solid core. The absence of behaviour text, persona, traits and the fed/riches counters, plus the name-only weakness and adaptation lines, leaves the player unable to plan against the rival's specific mechanics. PARTIAL.

5. Information layering and duplication risks at code level

Verdict: PARTIAL

What the pack establishes. The always-on map page stacks a header, an intel strip of up to five boxes, the 3D chart, a node-type legend, reachable-fight preview panels, a hint line, and the full side panel (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 410-437, 384-406, 462-514). The side panel alone stacks credits and command points, admiral line, Nemesis card, faction action, full fleet list with per-ship detail, a conditional fleet-hurt warning, the fleet button, hold count, flagship aspect, all boons, patrons line and all relics, and it scrolls (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 466-468). On the 3D chart, every unpassed node draws a name label, a NEMESIS chip and portrait when converted, a danger band chip for fights, a shape and modifier sub-line, a reward label or patron sigil, the board name, hazard chevrons on lanes into elites and the boss, and, while a node is hovered, route running-total chips on every node of both lit routes (pack s5; map3d.gd 473-527). The flat map draws a similar label stack minus bands, routes and chips (pack s5; map_view.gd 56-68).

Hover carries the deep per-node detail and the route comparison. The card follows the mouse and flips side near the panel (pack s5; map3d.gd 548-562). Tooltips carry several facts that appear nowhere else as text: the faction blurb, the rival-raiders explanation, the sector condition description, the patrons' sigil rule, the Nemesis clock explanation, and captain perk descriptions (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 607). On the strip boxes, the tooltip is the only place the sector condition's effect is written (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 424-425). Modals carry one-time announcements, and once dismissed most modal text has no home screen; the adaptation intelligence line is the clearest case (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 897-899).

Duplication is mostly consistent single-source reinforcement. The Nemesis clock appears in the strip box dots, the card dots, and the strike tail, all reading the same state (pack s5). Danger appears as node chips, hover-card text, and route worst-fight text, again from the same danger() source (pack s3 and s5). Reachable fights are described twice, on-node labels and chips and the preview panel row, but the previews are deduplicated among themselves (pack s1 and s5; campaign_ui.gd 384-406). The duplication does not create an obvious contradiction risk at code level, because the repeated facts share a source.

Where the layering is questionable. The always-on set is large for a map page. The side panel scrolls, so boons and relics are technically always on but practically on-demand. The hover card carries both deep node detail and the route comparison, which is a lot for one hover, and some critical rules text is tooltip-only. The preview panel row repeats the on-node fight labels, which is duplication that may cost space without adding decision value. The flat map's tooltip is thinner than the 3D hover card and lacks danger and routes, so the same game has two different information densities depending on which map is on (pack s5). Whether these choices produce visible clutter or an overloaded hover cannot be judged without screenshots, and that is UNSURE.

Assessment. The inventory shows a coherent layering logic: always-on state in the side panel and strip, per-node facts on the chart, deep detail and route planning on hover, and one-time announcements in modals. The problems are that the always-on set is broad, the hover is doing two jobs, and some rules facts are buried in tooltips. At the code level the duplication is reinforcing rather than contradictory. PARTIAL, with the visual-clutter question UNSURE.

(a) Ranked fixes or improvements, by decision improvement per unit of build effort

1. Add danger bands and a route summary to the flat map. Effort is low to medium, because the numbers already exist in map_intel.gd and the flat map already has node positions and tooltips. Impact is high for any player using the flat map, because at present that map gives no strength estimate and no route totals at all (pack s3, observed gap).

2. Put the sector condition's effect and the run's Heat rules on the map page, not only in tooltips or the aspect screen. Effort is low: read the existing strings and show them in the strip or side panel. Impact is broad, because these rules change how every fight and every route should be judged, and the pack shows the condition's effect is currently tooltip-only (pack s2 and s5; campaign_ui.gd 424-425, 354-358).

3. Surface the Nemesis behaviour text, and the effect text for weaknesses and adaptations, on the card or a dedicated intel panel. Effort is medium, because it needs new reads and layout, but the decision impact is high: the player currently cannot tell what the Nemesis strike will do, which is the costliest missing item in section 4 (pack s4, observed absences).

4. Add a short "why this band" breakdown to the danger hover, especially for Nemesis fights. Effort is low: the contributors are already read in danger() and can be listed as modifier names, scars and adaptations. Impact is medium, because it makes the estimate auditable and tells the player which factors to change (pack s3 and s4; map_intel.gd 48-54).

5. Add a numeric sector progress counter. Effort is low. Impact is modest but real for a three-sector run, because the pack shows progress is currently spatial only and there is no "nodes cleared of total" or "fights won this sector" figure (pack s2, observed absence).

6. Explain or expose the "Richest" route weights. Effort is low: a tooltip on the route label or a small legend would do. Impact is low to medium, because the player currently sees the totals but not why one route is labelled richer (map_intel.gd path_stats, lines 87-92).

7. Reduce the duplicate reachable-fight previews. Effort is medium, and the decision impact is low, because the preview row repeats on-node labels and is deduplicated among itself. This is more a layering simplification than a decision fix (pack s5; campaign_ui.gd 384-406).

(c) One-sentence verdict per area plus one overall verdict

1. Route planning: PARTIAL. The 3D map gives detailed per-node and route information before a jump, but the flat map has no planner, only two routes are compared, and the "Richest" score is opaque.
2. Information at a glance: PARTIAL. Money, command points, fleet condition and build are always visible, but the sector condition's effect is tooltip-only, Heat rules are absent from the map page, and there is no numeric progress counter.
3. Danger readability: PARTIAL. The fleet-relative four-band estimate is a sound response to rolled enemy fleets, and elite, boss and rival markings are multi-channel, but the flat map has no bands and the hover does not explain what drives the band.
4. The Nemesis as information: PARTIAL. Identity, portrait, scars, clock and strike warning are strong, but the absence of behaviour text, persona, traits and the fed and riches counters leaves the player unable to plan against the rival's specific mechanics.
5. Information layering and duplication: PARTIAL. The code-level inventory shows a coherent always-on and hover split with reinforcing duplication, but the always-on set is broad, hover carries two jobs, and some rules facts are buried in tooltips; clutter as seen is UNSURE.

Overall verdict: PARTIAL. Voidsworn's 3D campaign map has a genuinely strong information design for route comparison, danger estimation and the Nemesis clock, but the flat-map gap, the thin Nemesis behaviour information and several tooltip-only rules keep it below the completeness of the comparison games.
