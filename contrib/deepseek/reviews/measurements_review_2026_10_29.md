MEASUREMENTS OVERREACH

1. “KEEPING PACE, after one hard step. Not snowballing.” | The Nemesis verdict rests on 20 battles per cell, where one battle is 5 pp; only the sector-2 35 pp growth gap is near conventional significance (~2.4 SE), while the sector-3 25 pp gap (~1.6 SE) and the “not widening” inference are underpowered absence-of-evidence. The pack caveats this, but the headline still converts a noisy three-snapshot pattern into a run-level verdict. | Re-run with 100–200 battles per cell, report confidence intervals and a pre-registered primary contrast, and simulate full three-meeting trajectories rather than independent sector snapshots.

2. “base fleet cannot snowball by construction” | Indexing the base elite fleet to player fleetPower bounds only the base layer; the pack itself locates compounding in adaptations, traits, scars, Chorus faster cadence, fed stacks, and faction extras. Code-traced caps are not the same as measured behavior, and the Chorus/fed worst case is explicitly understated. | Simulate the actual faction cadences, adaptation choices, scars, and fed stacks through all three meetings, including worst-case caps, and compare enemy-vs-player power at each meeting.

3. “first adaptation landing before the sector 2 meeting is the single hard step” | This is inferred from one 20-battle cell; the plain-elite rates are nonmonotonic (65%, 80%, 60%), which is itself a noise warning. A single adaptation type may also drive the step, and the pack does not isolate adaptation type. | Larger repeated runs with CIs, plus separate arms for each adaptation effect and adaptation-choice distribution, would settle whether the step is robust and which adaptation causes it.

4. “weakness value: +20 pp in sector 2 and +10 pp in sector 3” | The +20 pp sector-2 weakness delta is about 1.3 SE at n=20 and is not statistically robust; the +10 pp sector-3 delta is plainly inside noise. The verdict uses these deltas to argue the intel loop “pays out on schedule,” but the numbers do not support a schedule. | More battles per cell, ideally paired by fleet/seed, with CIs on nem-minus-nemNoWeak in each sector.

5. “Nemesis fights also run long: 7.2 to 9.3 rounds” | No variance, CI, or per-battle distribution is given; the range overlaps the plain-elite range (6.4–7.8), so “run long” is not established as a distinct effect. | Report round-count distributions and CIs, and test the nem/nemNoWeak vs plain difference directly.

6. “THE TAX IS REAL, CONDITIONAL, AND WORST FOR THE MOST INVESTED PLAYERS.” | The offer-roll result is well sampled, but “worst for the most invested” and the 17% “expected effect” gap depend on uniform favour across patrons and on RARITY multipliers taken at face value; Legendary is multiplier 1, and boon text/tier effects are only approximated by mean m. A real uneven-favour profile does not see tier-3 tax on every offer. | Weight offers by realistic favour distributions, test uneven favour explicitly, and validate mean-m gaps against actual boon effect models or battle outcomes rather than rarity multipliers alone.

7. “duo boons … adjacent observation, same shape” | A duo pick hardcoded to Common is a separate defect: it is not a patron-vs-off-patron favour tax, does not depend on backfill, and may be intentional or may be better measured by duo effect than by rarity. Framing it as “same shape” risks folding a different bug into the tax verdict. | Code-intent review plus a separate duo-balance measurement: compare duo pick value at Common against rolled-rarity equivalents and decide whether the hardcoded rarity is intended.

8. “Backfill is a cliff, not a gradient” | The cliff is real for broad-fleet pool exhaustion, but the narrow-fleet results show kit-coverage backfill is a gradient (16.7% then 33.3% of offers), and “least choice” is proxied by held counts rather than measured choice. Legendary masking and partial kit coverage are acknowledged but not integrated into the headline. | Sample actual run offers across mixed favour and partial kit coverage, count real alternatives per offer, and report backfill frequency and tax by profile rather than bracketing with two extreme fleets.

Not checked: This pack-only review cannot verify the pinned code at main 264f441d, that the harness loader and sim3d.js were unmodified, that genEnemyFleet/rollBoonOffer behave as described, the RARITY multipliers or boon text effects, the real 80% loss rate, actual player arrival states, real favour distributions, bot-pilot representativeness, or Godot parity. It also cannot determine whether the duo Common rarity is intentional.
