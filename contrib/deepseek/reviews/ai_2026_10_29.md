Proof of reading: I read the EVIDENCE PACK title line, THE COMMISSIONER'S QUESTION, CHAIR CENSUS, WHAT TO JUDGE, the battle.js 131-165 slice (effArch, RAMP, genEnemyFleet), the battle.js 1311-1402 slice (aiTurn, aiAbilities, aiAfterFire, aiHurtW, fadeMove, aiUnit), and the ai_leg_prompt.md instructions.

Overall verdict: COMPETENT BUT HOLLOW - aiTurn has real positioning, threat avoidance, torpedo-lane awareness, and ability triggers, but fleet composition is randomized budget-filling and the shown AI lacks readable target priority or true disengagement.

Findings, ranked most severe first:

[BLOCKER] (READ + INFERRED) The archetype builder does not create compositional intent; it creates CP-shaped random draws. Swarm filters to light hulls, heavy forces one top big hull, carrier forces one carrier, but the final loop is still weighted random budget-filling. The pack says "archetypes reshape the same budget," and the code confirms no role composition, screening, escort logic, or anti-wing/anti-carrier structure. Fleets are stat soup at the right total with an archetype label.

[BLOCKER] (READ + INFERRED) RAMP.thin is defined but never read inside genEnemyFleet. The pack's comment says "per-ship toughness eases a little as numbers rise," not fewer/bigger ships; the builder slice uses grow, light, ceil, boss, std, and power feedback, but not thin. From the evidence, late-sector difficulty is not governed by the claimed thinning lever. If thin is applied elsewhere, it is outside this pack.

[FIX] (READ + INFERRED) The power feedback is an asymmetric rubber band, not an honest 80%-loss authoring tool. In sector 3, refP is 20; a fleetPower of 5 gives raw (5/20)^.6 ≈ .435, clamped to .75; a fleetPower of 40 gives 2^.6 ≈ 1.52, clamped to 1.3. It gives a battered fleet only 25% relief, taxes recovery as fleetPower rises, and caps how much a dominant fleet is challenged. It helps weak fleets somewhat and punishes strength, but it can flatten skill and reward keeping fleetPower low if fleetPower() tracks current strength.

[FIX] (READ) The AI has no true retreat. It only post-fire fades if a hex cuts threat by at least a quarter (if(!best||bv>here*.75) return false;) and phase/blinks away when hurt (u.hp<u.maxHp*.35&&evalThreat(u,foes)>u.hp*.3). Transports and hunt flagships are "runner" units only in the sense that they skip ram/board/fade; they do not disengage from battle. Sustained focus fire is therefore reliable.

[FIX] (READ + INFERRED) Standard AI targeting is aggregate, not prioritized. Movement scores evalOffense(v,ready,foes) minus threat, with a veteran-only sticky focus; the shown code never explicitly prioritizes carriers, transports, wounded ships, or launch hulls. Actual weapon-level target choice is in absent aiVolley, so the AI may still focus there, but the movement layer does not prove it. This is a target-range habit: it can spread damage across whatever maximizes aggregate expected offense.

[FIX] (READ) Ability use is condition-triggered and shallow. Launch fires before moving and sorts launch hexes only by distance to foes: launchHexes(u).sort((a,b)=>Math.min(...foes.map(f=>hdist(a,f)))-...); surge checks the nearest threat facing; focus/frenzy fire if any gun bears; reload can fire pre-move if any foe is within 6+(u.speed||2). There is no evidence of saving cooldowns for high-value turns or coordinating abilities across ships. The launch trigger is especially exploitable: bait the carrier's launch into a kill zone before it moves.

[FIX] (READ) Wing stances are effectively player-only in this pack. aiTurn processes squadrons through sqAct, and the comment says "squadrons follow the swarm rules," but there is no wing-stance logic in aiUnit or aiAbilities. Launch is used, torpedoes are partly respected through reload and torpThreat, and the gravity well is used through aiWellScore; wing stance management is not.

[NOTE] (READ) Standard difficulty keeps fixed speed order; veteran/Classic picks by opportunity. The comment says "standard keeps the fixed speed order." That makes standard activation order predictable and exploitable by killing or disabling fast ships before their turn.

[NOTE] (READ + INFERRED) Boss budget shrinks escorts after adding the boss: list.push({tpl:F.boss}); budget*=RAMP.boss[s];. Sector 3 boss ratio is .53, so the boss fight is boss plus roughly half a sector-3 budget. That is consistent with "bosses long rather than lethal" only if boss HP/abilities provide duration; the pack does not show boss stats or boss command depth.

[NOTE] (READ) effArch silently remaps carrier to heavy for factions with no launch hull: "a Deathless node never fields a carrier fleet." This is a known preview mismatch and means a whole archetype is absent for that faction.

Explicit checks:

COMPOSITION: genEnemyFleet is a budget builder with readable labels but weak internal intent. Swarm is legible as "light hulls"; heavy is legible as "one top big hull plus smalls"; carrier is legible as "one carrier plus random escorts." Beyond that, the final loop is weighted random CP filling, so fleets are not built around roles, screens, torpedo escorts, anti-wing coverage, or boss support. The thinning factor as described in the question - "later sectors field fewer, bigger ships" - is not supported by this pack: RAMP.thin is unused in the builder, and the comment defines it as per-ship toughness easing, not fewer/bigger ships. The right lever is not a global thin multiplier; it is role composition and escort/screen rules. As shown, late-sector fights become budget/light-hull pressure, not readable bigger-ship fights.

POWER FEEDBACK: The formula scales enemy budget by clamp(pow(max(.5,fleetPower())/refP[s], .6), .75, 1.3). It helps a weak fleet by reducing budget toward 75%, and punishes a strong fleet by raising it toward 130%. It does not "punish recovery" directly, but it taxes recovery because enemy budget rises as fleetPower rises. The clamp is asymmetric and not honest to a fixed 80% loss target: the lower bound still leaves a battered fleet facing three-quarters of budget, while the upper bound caps challenge against a dominant fleet. It is a defensible anti-snowball tool, but it makes difficulty relative rather than authored. If fleetPower() is current combat strength, a player can benefit from appearing weak; that is a real exploit risk.

BATTLE DECISIONS: Targeting rule: choose the movement hex maximizing expected offense minus weighted threat, with veteran sticky focus and a veteran death cliff; no explicit target-priority list in the shown code. Ability rule: pre/aim/escape triggers use abilities when simple conditions fire - launch if able, surge if facing shield under half and threat within 8, focus/frenzy if a gun bears, reload if a spent tube reaches, etc. Retreat rule: no disengage; post-fire fade if threat drops by ≥25%, blink escape at <35% HP when threat exceeds 30% HP, and brace when low/no-fire. Most exploitable habit: it launches before moving and sorts launch hexes only by distance to foes, so the launch can be baited into player guns. Smartest behavior: true pathfield movement around holes/rocks/wrecks when out of range (fieldDist(PF,v,hd)) plus torpedo-lane avoidance (score-=torpThreat(u,v)*.9); that is real tactical positioning.

WINGS AND SPECIALS: Launch is used, but not like a player would: it is pre-move, distance-sorted, and not screened. Wing stances are effectively player-only in this pack; sqAct is called, but no wing-stance logic appears. Torpedoes are partly used: reload and torpThreat appear, but aiVolley is absent, so torpedo target selection cannot be judged. The gravity well is used through aiWellScore. Effectively player-only: wing stance management, and possibly full launch/torpedo doctrine because the selection functions are absent.

DIFFICULTY TEXTURE: Sector 3 difficulty comes mainly from budget and hull count, not AI behavior. grow[3]=1.26, base budget 14, column multiplier 1.1, light[3]=5, and ceil[3]=20 minus live player ships. With a 4-7 ship player fleet, the enemy cap is roughly 13-16 ships, not 20 enemy ships; the soft ceiling is a total-board cap. Boss ratio .53 means boss plus reduced escorts. thin is unused in the builder, and the AI's threat-weighting, brace, and fade behavior tend to preserve ships rather than increase lethality. That source matches "bosses long rather than lethal" only if boss HP/abilities supply duration; the AI itself is not the lethal component.

Not checked: actual aiVolley, evalOffense, evalThreat, evalOffenseVet, evalThreatVet, sqAct, launchHexes, useAbility, fleetPower(), canOrder/giveOrder, boss templates/abilities, damage/HP tuning, full RAMP.thin usage outside genEnemyFleet, map-column generation, Heat/Nemesis details, and the campaign sim methodology. I also cannot verify whether standard aiPickActivation truly preserves fixed speed order or what its fallback is.

Questions for the designer:

2. What does fleetPower() count - current HP, roster CP, or something else - and can a player deliberately keep it low? Stakes: the .75 floor and .6 exponent determine whether the power feedback rewards staying weak.

3. What is aiVolley's target priority, and does it focus carriers, transports, launchers, or wounded ships? Stakes: without that, the AI cannot be judged as an opponent rather than a damage-spreading target range.
