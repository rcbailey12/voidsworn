The campaign's central rival changes mechanically and not narratively. Cost: the player is told the Nemesis learns, then hears the same voice regardless of what happened, which weakens the roguelite's signature fantasy more than any single bad line.

2. Severity high. OBSERVED. Beat 3 is the weakest beat in every faction arc, and the finales repeat reward shapes. The story review states "Beat 3 is the weakest beat in every arc" and counts that "three of the six finales are the identical pair". The campaign climax gets the least argued choice in each arc. Cost: the faction arcs build distinct voices and then end in generic resource trades, so the story's payoff does not match the run's length.

3. Severity high. OBSERVED. Beat 2 blue options stack rewards and remove costs, collapsing the dilemma. The review finds "Each blue combines the standard options' rewards while removing or reversing their costs". A blue should be a different kind of good, not a strictly better standard option. Cost: moral and tactical choices stop telling a story because one branch is obviously correct, which undercuts the campaign's choice architecture.

4. Severity high. OBSERVED. Beat 1 flags die, and Beat 2 choices have no named flags, so consequences cannot pay off. The review confirms "Six are never used again" and "No Beat 2 choice in any arc has a named flag". The fiction promises remembered choices while the draft cannot verify callbacks. Cost: the player's early decisions become local color, not story, and the rival's failure to remember is compounded by the campaign's own failure to remember.

5. Severity high. OBSERVED. The help screen teaches a false model of the game, and it is the first lore text a new player reads. The text synthesis finds the availability claim "is false against renown costs and LEGACY gates" and the shipyard promise "describes a flow afterBattle does not contain". This confirms the NOT READY verdict at the help surface. Cost: new players build their model of progression, unlocks and shipyard flow from text that contradicts the shipped systems, which is worse than a bad line because it misleads play.

The story review says the deeds "cannot be true of a Chorus swarm rival" and that "rival text across the beats hardcodes male pronouns". Two sample rivals are women. Cost: the antagonist's body and pronouns contradict the faction fiction and the rolled Nemesis gender, so scenes read as authored for one rival and pasted onto another.

Cost: the Chorus is one of the six voices the review calls distinct, and the arc's own prose breaks its declared rule, which weakens blind-identification and the faction's alien texture.

Cost: the player cannot trust the text on the surface where they spend resources, and the r8 rename lands in data tables but not around them.

9. Severity medium. OBSERVED. The Deathless Nemesis card contradicts behaviour and the hardcoded panel line bypasses the pronoun-safe path. The card says "He never retreats" against a behaviour object with no noFlee, and the panel line says "His fleet is coming" outside nemSay. Cost: the card lies about a known escape outcome and reintroduces a gender assumption the Nemesis roll does not guarantee.

Cost: the source of truth still carries a retired name, so future content can reintroduce it and the rename discipline loses authority at the exact place writers check names.

Verdict: The fiction and the mechanics are not telling the same story; the Nemesis adapts in systems but not in words, and that mute rival is the most important attack because it hollows out the campaign's core promise.
