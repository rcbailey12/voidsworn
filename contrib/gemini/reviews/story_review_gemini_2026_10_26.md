1. What Resonates in Comparable Games

Here is what works in the eight comparable games, why it works, and how those lessons apply to Voidsworn's 100 to 150 word text beats.

FTL: Faster Than Light ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light) and [https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options))

What works and why: FTL uses brief 80 to 120 word text cards that force a trade-off between two scarce resources (such as hull points versus scrap), while offering "blue options" (special choices unlocked only if you have a specific crew member, weapon, or system) that turn a risky gamble into a safe reward. This works because the player rarely has the exact tool needed for the blue option, so unlocking one feels like a clever payoff for earlier ship-building choices ([https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options)).

What transfers: The compact setup-plus-choice format transfers directly to Voidsworn's 100 to 150 word cards.

What does not transfer: In FTL, blue options are exciting because they depend on unpredictable equipment or crew found during a run ([https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options)). In the Voidsworn outlines, every Beat 2 blue option is unlocked simply by clicking Option 1 or Option 2 in Beat 1 of the exact same story arc. Because Beat 1 always offers that flag, the blue option in Beat 2 stops feeling like a rare discovery and turns Beat 1 into a solved puzzle where one button is strictly better across the two sectors.

Into the Breach ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach))

What works and why: Into the Breach establishes distinct faction leaders and time-traveling pilots using only one or two sentences at the start and end of islands ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach)). It works because each character has a rigid verbal tic (corporate jargon, military briskness, or guilt over lost timelines) and reacts specifically to whether you saved or failed island objectives ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach)).

What transfers: Giving each faction a strict set of vocabulary rules (such as Hegemony Navy's bureaucratic ledgers versus the Chorus's hunger) transfers completely to short text cards.

What does not transfer: Into the Breach relies heavily on mid-combat reactive barks when buildings are hit or pilots die ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach)). Voidsworn's outlines only include three sector-entry text cards and a single Beat 3 hail, win, and loss line, so the story cards must carry all of the rivalry's weight on their own.

Hades ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game)))

What works and why: Recurring bosses in Hades (like Megaera or Theseus) feel alive across dozens of runs because their pre-fight dialogue checks flags for what happened in your last encounter, which weapon you brought, or whether you used a specific keepsake ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game))).

What transfers: Using flags from earlier beats so the rival's dialogue in Beat 3 reflects how you handled them in Beat 1 and Beat 2 transfers directly.

What does not transfer: Hades relies on hundreds of voiced, run-over-run lines that advance a continuous story after every death ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game))). Voidsworn resets its Nemesis commander every run and has one 3-beat draft per faction with no voice acting, so it cannot rely on a multi-run conversation arc.

Darkest Dungeon ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon))

What works and why: Darkest Dungeon creates strong atmosphere in short curio interactions by pairing concrete nouns (iron maidens, rotting reliquaries, pitch torches) with immediate mechanical risks ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon)).

What transfers: Using sharp, physical details (like captains writing their names in chalk and ash in Voidsworn Crusade, Beat 3 "Relight the Hollow Sun") transfers very well to 150-word limits.

What does not transfer: Darkest Dungeon leans on an iconic voice actor to make purple prose work ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon)). Because Voidsworn is text-only with no voice acting, every sentence must remain clean and readable on screen.

BattleTech (2018) ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game)))

What works and why: BattleTech's shipboard event cards work because every narrative choice connects directly to existing management meters that the player already cares about: C-Bills (money), repair time, mechwarrior experience, and morale ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game))).

What transfers: Tying story choices strictly to existing campaign mechanics (credits, hull repairs, captain experience, relics, the Nemesis clock, and weaknesses) transfers directly and keeps scope tight.

What does not transfer: BattleTech's main campaign relies on long multi-character briefing conversations between named crew members with portraits ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BattleTech_(video_game))). Voidsworn uses crest symbols and no portraits, so the anonymous advisers in the outlines (quartermasters, bosuns, confessors) must stay brief.

Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Rogue_Trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Rogue_Trader))

What works and why: Rogue Trader frames choices around competing worldviews (Dogmatic zealotry, Iconoclast mercy, or Heretical ambition), ensuring that even when a player plays an authoritarian faction, they still have to choose which kind of leader they are ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Rogue_Trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Rogue_Trader)).

What transfers: Splitting a faction's identity into two competing internal values (like Hegemony Navy's strict obedience to orders versus saving fellow sailors in Beat 2 "Acceptable Limits") transfers very well to a two-button choice.

What does not transfer: Rogue Trader's deep branching trees and multi-page lore explanations do not fit a 3-card structure.

Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor))

What works and why: The Nemesis system works because procedural (randomly generated) enemies remember specific shared history: if you burn an orc or flee from him, he shows up later with burn scars or mocks your cowardice ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor)).

What transfers: Having {rival} react explicitly in Beat 2 and Beat 3 to the specific insult or injury you dealt them in Beat 1 (as seen in Ashfall Corsairs, Beat 2 "The Rattlehold Auction", where {rival} flinches at the memory of the bombed gift box) transfers directly.

What does not transfer: Shadow of Mordor relies on an open-world hierarchy of dozens of captains fighting each other ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth:_Shadow_of_Mordor)). Voidsworn rolls one single Nemesis per run across three fixed beats.

Stellaris ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game)))

What works and why: Stellaris tells 3-part anomaly and archaeology stories in 120-word popup windows, filtering the discovery through your empire's ethics ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game))).

What transfers: The 3-step structure (hook in Beat 1, complication in Beat 2, finale in Beat 3) is an exact match for Voidsworn's sector arcs.

What does not transfer: Stellaris events become automatic button-clicks once players memorize which option gives the best reward ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellaris_(video_game))). Without variation or balanced trade-offs on both paths, fixed 3-beat chains go stale after two or three runs.

2. Per-Faction Review

1. Voidsworn Crusade: "The Oath at the Hollow Sun"

Strongest Beat: Beat 2, "The Burning Choir". The image of the gutted hymn-ship Ashen Vigil looping a dead choir's recording as bait is vivid, and the choice presents a genuine religious dilemma between salvaging a sacred verse or striking immediately in righteous anger ("The oath allows no answer that is both swift and holy").

Weakest Beat: Beat 1, "The Saint's Hand". Having fanatical crusaders choose to "Sell it to the pilgrims" for 60 credits breaks the faction's zealous voice, turning holy crusaders into petty merchants in their very first scene. Worse, selling the hand locks you out of the Beat 2 blue option and is never referenced again, while carrying the hand gives you a relic in Beat 1 and unlocks the best option in Beat 2.

Single Biggest Fix: In Beat 1, "The Saint's Hand", replace "Sell it to the pilgrims" with a devout alternative (such as locking the hand inside the shrine-moon's vault under guard or melting the iron hand down to forge blessed shells), and let that second flag (vesper_sold, renamed appropriately) unlock its own blue option in Beat 3, "Relight the Hollow Sun".

Single Biggest Fix: Make Beat 3, "The Anchorage", check the hg_offbook and hg_orders flags from Beat 1, "The Listening Post", so that following orders to the letter unlocks an Admiralty fire-support or intel blue option in Beat 3, while going off the book changes the Admiralty's message into a threat of court-martial unless you win.

3. Cross-Cutting Issues
(a) Do the choices present real dilemmas, or is one option obviously best?

Right now, one path is obviously best in every arc because of how flags and blue options are wired:

Every Beat 1 has one live flag and one dead flag: In all six factions, Beat 1 sets one of two flags, but only one of those flags is ever checked in Beat 2 (vesper_hand, hg_offbook, cs_rigged, ch_silence, it_song, and dd_patient), while the other six flags (vesper_sold, hg_orders, cs_party, ch_ate, it_mercy, and dd_vengeance) do nothing for the rest of the run.

Every Beat 2 blue option removes the dilemma: Whenever you unlock the Beat 2 blue option, it gives you the best rewards of the standard options while deleting the penalty (no Nemesis clock increase in Voidsworn Crusade Beat 2 "The Burning Choir", no disobedience penalty in Hegemony Navy Beat 2 "Acceptable Limits", a 50-credit discount and no ambush in Ashfall Corsairs Beat 2 "The Rattlehold Auction", a clock reduction in Chorus Beat 2 "The Drowned Song" and Deathless Dynasty Beat 2 "A Crown on the Wrong Head", and zero hull damage in Ithar Beat 2 "The Glass Choir"). Once a player sees this on their first run, Beat 1 and Beat 2 become automatic clicks on every future run.

Every Beat 3 has no dilemma at all: All six Beat 3 cards end with two risk-free buff buttons (almost always +20 captain experience versus +20% hull repair or revealing a weakness) with no trade-off or flag check.

(b) Does each faction sound distinct?

Yes, the faction voices are the strongest part of the outlines. Even without crest symbols or headers, you can tell which faction a paragraph belongs to within two sentences:

Voidsworn Crusade sounds solemn and liturgical ("shrine-moon", "confessors", "chalk and ash" in Beat 1 "The Saint's Hand" and Beat 3 "Relight the Hollow Sun").

Hegemony Navy sounds clipped and bureaucratic ("appendix of nine pages", "stamped in triplicate", "0412 ship time" in Beat 1 "The Listening Post" and Beat 3 "The Anchorage").

Ashfall Corsairs sounds scrappy and superstitious ("Old Mott", "last fence", "three days and two fistfights" in Beat 1 "The Gift Box" and Beat 3 "Saints' Gold").

The Chorus uses short sentences and bodily, collective nouns ("every throat aches", "grinding teeth", "drifting spawn" in Beat 1 "The Silent Node" and Beat 2 "The Drowned Song").

The Ithar sounds mournful and ancient ("barely four centuries", "lamp-buoy", "black pearl" in Beat 1 "The Lamp-Buoy", Beat 2 "The Glass Choir", and Beat 3 "The Grave of Houses").

The Deathless Dynasty sounds arrogant and obsessed with legal order ("squatters", "calculated the insult to eleven decimal places", "corrected" in Beat 1 "The Opened Tomb", Beat 2 "A Crown on the Wrong Head", and Beat 3 "The Court Restored").

(d) Will the arcs hold up across many runs, or go stale fast?

They will go stale fast, likely after two runs per faction, for two reasons:

First, there is only one fixed 3-beat story per faction, so every single run as a given faction visits the exact same three places in the exact same order (for example, Hegemony Navy always starts with the Listening Post, always meets the eleven haulers and the 19-year-old lieutenant in Sector II, and always gets late authorisation in Sector III).

Second, because one Beat 1 choice strictly unlocks the superior Beat 2 blue option while the other Beat 1 choice unlocks nothing, players have no reason to experiment after they find the winning path once.

(e) Anything that reads too close to Warhammer 40k (names, phrases, concepts) and what to change

Two factions lean dangerously close to Warhammer 40,000, and two others have smaller overlaps ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000)):

The Deathless Dynasty reads like 40k Necrons ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrons)): In Beat 1, "The Opened Tomb", and Beat 2, "A Crown on the Wrong Head", you have ancient undying legions waking from a "long sleep" on a "tomb-world", ruled by a "Dynasty" and a "Dynast" ([Dynast Setekh the Patient] in Hegemony Beat 1, "The Listening Post"), calling younger species "squatters" on "crownworlds", led by "overseers" and "wardens", and fielding an Egyptian-themed "Deathless jackal" ship.

What to change: Cut "tomb-world", "crownworlds", "Dynast Setekh", and "jackal". Lean fully into their unique "census, archive, and ancient law" hook instead of Egyptian space tombs: call their sleeping worlds "Vault-Worlds" or "Ledger-Worlds", rename "Dynast" to "Justiciar" or "Censor", and rename the "Deathless jackal" hull to a legal/audit term like a "Deathless bailiff" or "warden-cutter".

Voidsworn Crusade reads like the 40k Imperium of Man / Adepta Sororitas ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_of_Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_of_Man)): Beat 1, "The Saint's Hand", and Beat 3, "Relight the Hollow Sun", pile up signature 40k words: "shrine-moon", "litanists", "confessors", "reliquary", "heresy", "heretic", and warships described as "cathedrals of iron".

What to change: Replace generic 40k gothic terms ("heresy", "heretic", "cathedrals of iron", "shrine-moon") with terms built around their specific Hollow Sun / dead-star fire motif. Call their enemies "oath-breakers" or "the Unlit" instead of "heretics", call the moon a "cinder-shrine" or "beacon-moon", and describe the ancient shipwrecks in Beat 3 as "furnaces of iron" or "great lanterns of iron" instead of "cathedrals of iron".

The Ithar and Ashfall Corsairs have minor 40k echoes: In Ithar Beat 1, "The Lamp-Buoy", and Beat 2, "The Glass Choir", an ancient dying race of "great houses" led by "elders" flying crystal "songships" echoes 40k Craftworld Eldar ([https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldar_(Warhammer_40,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldar_(Warhammer_40,000))), and the sample Corsair names [Warlord Grim Rattigan] (Voidsworn Beat 1, "The Saint's Hand") and [Warlord Bones Sharkey] (Ithar Beat 1, "The Lamp-Buoy") use the 40k Ork naming pattern of "Warlord + rough nickname".

What to change: In the Ithar beats, replace "elders" (which looks and sounds close to Eldar) with "Keepers" or "First Singers", and lean into their nautical "sails and lamp-buoys" vocabulary. For the sample Corsair names, replace "Warlord" with a nautical/pirate rank like "Commodore", "Reaver", or "Bosun-King".
