Sheet 1: Story and Career Mode Scope

The Question: How big should Career and Story Mode be on your screen, and where do you want to set the dial on story length, reading volume, voice audio, and portraits?

Part A: Overall Mode Scope (3 Options)

Option A (Small): Voices and Service Records (Effort: a few days, per pack)

What you will see: All 24 admirals finally speak in text boxes you already see during a run: picking your leader, entering a sector, spotting the enemy flagship, winning, or losing. You can rename your flagship, earn an admiral title based on how you fight, and open a new Career tab on the Progression screen showing per-admiral win records, living captain records, and a case of about 10 medals. You will NOT see story choices or rival storylines.

Option B (Medium): Faction Story Arcs via Events (Effort: about a week, mostly writing, per pack)

What you will see: Everything in Option A, plus a 3-part mini-campaign each time you pick a faction. In Sector 1, you get a faction story choice (like Corsairs choosing to rob a supply tender or escort it). In Sector 2, your choice comes back to bite or help you, and one of the enemy faction's named admirals shows up as your personal rival. In Sector 3, the final boss battle becomes a personal showdown with that rival's flagship. Winning unlocks a permanent shipyard hull variant or the rival's special Command ability, and records your choices in a Chronicle tab.

Option C (Big): The Long Career (Effort: several weeks, high save-file risk, per pack)

What you will see: You create one custom admiral at the start of the game and play connected tours of duty across a giant conquest map. Your flagship survives between runs (resetting to Mk I with battle scars), surviving captains retire to a veteran hiring pool, and beating all six factions unlocks a final endgame campaign. However, keeping your flagship forever takes away the sting of losing ships and risks breaking your save file.

Sheet 2: Music Style for an Adaptive Score

The Question: What should Voidsworn sound like as you move from quiet sector maps into heavy hex battles, and which reference style should guide sound-pack shopping or composer hiring?

Licensing note: All reference tracks below are copyrighted commercial game soundtracks. You can listen to them today to pick the vibe, but the game cannot ship them without a license. Once you pick a direction, you can buy royalty-free layered music packs or hire a composer to match that style (cost and effort UNSURE).

Option 1: Heavy Naval Brass and Tactical Percussion (The BattleTech & Into the Breach Feel)

What you will hear: Gritty low brass, tense chamber strings, metallic anvil hits, and submarine-style sonar pings. It sounds like heavy steel warships maneuvering into broadside range.

How it adapts in-game:

Exploration (Sector Map): Quiet cello notes, slow sonar pulses, and distant horn echoes.

Battle (Hex Grid): Marching snare drums, heavy brass chords, and a steady bass pulse kick in when weapons fire.

Victory / Defeat: A crisp brass salute on victory; a low, fading metallic groan and solo string note on defeat.

Verified Reference Tracks to Listen To:

Jon Everist, "March on Axylus" and "That's a Big 'Mech" on BATTLETECH (Original Soundtrack): [Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/1jsFEAfJaR7NMJvj8w5WoM)

Ben Prunty, "Old War Machines" and "Open a Breach" on Into the Breach Soundtrack: [Listen on Ben Prunty's Official Site](https://www.benpruntymusic.com/into-the-breach-soundtrack)

Option 2: Gothic Pipe Organ and Industrial Machine Synth (The Mechanicus Feel)

What you will hear: Huge cathedral pipe organs, distorted electronic bass, metallic clanks, and dark choir textures. It leans hard into sacred crusades, cold machine minds, and bio-horror.

How it adapts in-game:

Exploration (Sector Map): A low reactor hum, faint choir whispers, and ticking machine relays.

Battle (Hex Grid): Overdriven synth basslines and booming pipe organ chords drop in as shields break.

Victory / Defeat: A soaring church organ chord on victory; a flatlining machine power-down tone on defeat.

Verified Reference Tracks to Listen To:

Guillaume David, "Children of the Omnissiah" and "Noosphere" on Warhammer 40,000: Mechanicus (Original Soundtrack): [Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/2MorNFoa0orgkZEoX0FUcn)

Guillaume David, "Dark Age of Technology" on Warhammer 40,000: Mechanicus II (Complete Original Soundtrack): [Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/320VTnUEDoyIxreWdD4kGe)

Option 3: Deep-Space Synth Drone and Tribal Frame Drums (The Homeworld & FTL Feel)

What you will hear: Wide, echoing synthesizer pads paired with desert frame drums and pulsing electronic arpeggios. It feels lonely, ancient, and vast.

How it adapts in-game:

Exploration (Sector Map): Weightless ambient synth pads and soft radio static.

Battle (Hex Grid): Fast, rolling tom and frame drums layer on top of the synths, speeding up when hull damage gets critical.

Victory / Defeat: A warm, shimmering synth wash on victory; cold, empty wind static on defeat.

Verified Reference Tracks to Listen To:

Paul Ruskay, "Turanic Raiders" and "Imperial Battle - Original" on Homeworld 1 Remastered (Original Soundtrack): [Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/6xoXUJvBU9hKnM3bL1IqCZ)

Ben Prunty, "Space Cruise (Title)" and "MilkyWay (Explore)" on Ftl: Faster Than Light - Original Soundtrack: [Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/album/7us4WRnwvqCE3cppHVNjIu)

My Pick and Why: Pick Option 1 (Heavy Naval Brass and Tactical Percussion) as the main combat backbone, and splash in light organ or synth layers from Option 2 when fighting fanatical or machine factions (Voidsworn, Deathless, Chorus). Option 3 is too floaty for a heavy capital-ship tactics game, and pure Option 2 makes disciplined navies like the Hegemony or porcelain Ithar sound like a horror cult. Option 1 gives every turn real physical weight while letting you think during long turns without ear fatigue.
