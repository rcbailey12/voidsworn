2. Findings

[BLOCKER] Zero tutorial coverage outside tactical battle leaves the entire campaign layer untaught.

READ: tutorialCheck() immediately returns if !B or document.body.classList.contains('app-on'), and mapScreen() sets B=null.

READ: A first run includes "faction drives, the Nemesis... boons and patrons, modules, capture with an add/strip/ransom choice, Heat, and meta-progression".

INFERRED: Because tutorialCheck() is hard-gated to active combat (B present and app-on absent), a brand-new player receives zero tutorial tips during admiral/faction selection, sector map pathing, shipyard upgrades, patron boon selection, or post-battle capture decisions.

[BLOCKER] Turn 1 modal tip cascade trains players to click "Hide tips" and permanently disable onboarding.

READ: In tutorialCheck(), cond sets start:true and turns:true unconditionally, while select:!!s, target:OV.targets&&OV.targets.length>0, and objective:B.obj&&B.obj.type!=='rout' can also evaluate true at battle start.

READ: Clicking "Got it" (ACT.tipOk) calls setTimeout(tutorialCheck,400) to spawn the next tip 400ms later, and every tip card places <button data-act="tipOff">Hide tips</button> (o.tipsOff=true) directly beside "Got it".

INFERRED: Chaining multiple modal pop-ups 400ms apart at the start of Battle 1 creates immediate UI fatigue, baiting players into clicking "Hide tips" to play the game-which permanently suppresses mid-battle tips (threat, resolve, close) before their conditions ever occur.

[BLOCKER] Core ruled systems arrive with no tutorial triggers or help-card entries.

READ: tutorialCheck() only checks ten conditions: start, turns, threat, select, move, target, fired, resolve, close, and objective.

READ: ACT.stripHelp ("What the buttons do") only covers Done with ship, Ability, Command (gold), Ram / Board, Brace, and Launch.

INFERRED: Assuming no other tutorial system exists outside tutorialCheck() (as verified by the Chair Census: "The tutorial system is a set of contextual tip cards"), Faction Drives, Heat, Patrons/Boons, Modules, Hull Marks, Subsystem Aiming, Wing Stances, and the Nemesis's hidden weaknesses/adaptations are never taught by the tutorial system.

[BLOCKER] The Sector 1 Nemesis ambush and lineage debrief lack visible onboarding support in the code.

READ: The designer ruled a "forced meeting in sector 1's last columns" with a Nemesis that has "two hidden weaknesses, adaptations" in a run where "winning run 1 should be near impossible" and "its debrief is meant to teach the inheritance".

READ: The only code for the Nemesis in the pack is if(typeof newNemesis==='function') RUN.nem=newNemesis(); in newRun, with no tutorial condition in tutorialCheck() for Nemesis weaknesses or adaptations.

INFERRED: Forcing a new player into a designed loss in Sector 1 against an adapting boss with hidden weaknesses, without any combat tip explaining that weaknesses or adaptations exist, turns the climax of Run 1 into an arbitrary wall rather than a readable lesson.

[FIX] Map boon badges strip even the "Signal of" label, displaying only an unexplained patron name, glyph, and star.

READ: While rewardLabel(rw) returns 'Signal of '+PATRONS[rw.p].short for boons, rewardBadge() intercepts rw.k==='boon'&&PATRONS[rw.p] first and renders only ${P.glyph} and ${esc(P.short)}${rw.min?' ★':''}.

INFERRED: On the sector map (mapScreen), a Run 1 player does not even see the word "Boon" or "Signal of" on boon nodes-they see only an unfamiliar proper noun (P.short), an icon, and an unexplained ★ (rw.min), making informed pathing impossible.

[FIX] In-fiction reward and node terms (Refit, Marks, salvage) obscure basic progression mechanics.

READ: rewardLabel(rw) labels modules (rw.k==='mod') as 'Refit'; NODE_INFO.shipyard says 'Buy hulls and refits, repair, upgrade Marks.'; and NODE_INFO.elite says 'Drops salvage.'.

INFERRED: A new player cannot tell that "Refit" means an equippable ship module rather than hull repair, what "Marks" (f.mk) do compared to buying hulls, or what "salvage" is (since rewardLabel only defines boon, mod, cred, and repair).

[FIX] The ? Help Card (stripHelp) omits the Wings doctrine button sitting right on the action strip and omits Subsystem Aiming.

READ: actionStrip(u) renders a stance button (Wings: ${esc(WING_STANCES[u.stance||'strike'].n)} <span class="dim">(change)</span>) that cycles 'strike', 'screen', and 'hunt' (ACT.stance), and ui3d.js defines AIM_NAME={'':'Anywhere',prow:'Prow',mid:'Midships',eng:'Engines'}.

READ: ACT.stripHelp ("What the buttons do") documents six buttons on the strip but completely omits Wings stances and aimed shot locations (prow/mid/eng).

INFERRED: Even a diligent player who clicks ? on the action strip to read the manual is left guessing what switching wings between strike, screen, and hunt actually changes in combat, or why they should aim at Prow, Midships, or Engines instead of Anywhere.

[FIX] Boarding UI hides both the add/strip/ransom capture payoff and the Command Point (cpCap) constraint.

READ: actionStrip(u) shows Board <span class="dim">(${captureChance(u,bt[0])}% capture)</span>, #helpcard says "send crews into an adjacent enemy to capture it", and newRun enforces cpCap:10 (Math.max(RUN.cpCap, fleetCP()+3)).

INFERRED: During combat, nothing tells the player why capturing a ship matters (add/strip/ransom) or whether their current cpCap even allows adding the target ship to their fleet before they risk moving a ship into point-blank range.

[FIX] Close-quarters and Resolve tutorial tips fire reactively rather than proactively.

READ: cond.close fires only when !!s&&!s.isSq&&(!!ramInfo(s)||boardTargets(s).length>0), and cond.resolve fires only when canCommand('player') is already true.

INFERRED: Because setting up a ram or boarding action requires multi-turn positioning, waiting until a ship is already adjacent or lined up in a ram lane (ramInfo(s)) means a player who stays at firing range never learns boarding/ramming exists; similarly, waiting until Command is already charged misses teaching how waiting rounds or losing ships builds Command tiers (cmdTier()) beforehand.

[NOTE] Command button uses unexplained tier dots (●○○) and a 6-second confirm timer on the combat strip.

READ: The Command button renders ${'●'.repeat(cmdTier()+1)}${'○'.repeat(2-cmdTier())} and requires a confirming second tap within G.cmdConfirmMs||6000 ms ("Tap again (6s): ").

INFERRED: While the double-tap prevents accidental misfires of a once-per-battle ability, the three dots (●○○) on the button have no inline label indicating they represent tier scaling unless the player reads #helpcard.

2. THE TIP SYSTEM

Judgement as the main teacher: Contextual tips (tutorialCheck) are the wrong sole vehicle for a game combining BattleTech's tactical density with Hades's multi-layered campaign progression. First, tutorialCheck is disabled outside combat (!B or app-on), leaving the entire strategy layer unguided. Second, because five conditions (start, turns, objective, select, target) can all be true on Turn 1 and ACT.tipOk chains the next tip after 400ms (setTimeout(tutorialCheck,400)), the system front-loads a stack of blocking pop-ups before the player moves a single ship-while offering a global "Hide tips" kill-switch on every card.

The three tips that matter most and whether they fire early enough: (Note: the exact prose of TIPS is omitted from the pack, so tip topics are INFERRED from cond keys and #helpcard.)

turns (Alternating single-ship activations): Matters most for tactical pacing because players coming from standard turn-based games expect to move their whole fleet before the enemy reacts ("ends this ship's turn; the enemy then moves one of theirs"). Fires early enough? Technically yes (turns:true on Turn 1), but it is buried in the Turn 1 400ms pop-up cascade right behind start:true, where players are most likely to skim or click "Hide tips".

resolve (Admiral Command & Resolve scaling): Matters because Admiral Command is a once-per-battle swing power that scales up (cmdTier()) each round you wait and when you lose a ship. Fires early enough? No. resolve:canCommand('player') only triggers after Command becomes available. A player needs to know how Resolve builds and why holding the button increases its tier before losing ships or deciding when to pop Tier 1 vs. Tier 3.

close (Ramming and Boarding / Capture): Matters because boarding is the gateway to the game's capture economy (add/strip/ransom). Fires early enough? No. close:!!s&&!s.isSq&&(!!ramInfo(s)||boardTargets(s).length>0) only fires once a non-squadron ship has already lined up an active ram (ramInfo(s)) or moved adjacent to a boardable target (boardTargets(s)). Because closing distance in hex tactics takes multiple turns of deliberate movement, firing the tip only after adjacency is achieved is too late.

4. THE NEMESIS IN RUN 1

Verdict: A wall, not a lesson.

Justification: In Sector 1 of Run 1, a new player is still digesting alternating activations, hex movement, firing arcs, bracing, and carrier wings. Forcing a meeting in Sector 1's last columns with a rival who has two hidden weaknesses and adaptations-inside a run designed to be "near impossible" to win-violates fair tactical onboarding in three ways:

Unannounced rules: Nothing in tutorialCheck() or #helpcard tells the player that the Nemesis has hidden weaknesses to probe for or that it adapts to their tactics.

Punishing early competence: When a new player finds one working tactic in their first 30 minutes (e.g., bomber strikes or boarding) and the Nemesis adapts to shut it down during a forced loss, the game punishes the player for learning.

No persistence shown in newRun: In core.js, newRun calls RUN.nem=newNemesis(); // B4: one named enemy commander per run. (INFERRED: If newNemesis() generates a fresh Nemesis each run rather than carrying over the Sector 1 rival and the weaknesses the player died discovering, the Run 1 ambush teaches nothing actionable for Run 2.)

5. Questions for the Designer

Does a Sector 1 defeat in Run 1 guarantee enough progression to unlock at least one tangible new option (such as Admiral Mastery 1's altFleetPlan or a META.unlocked upgrade) before Run 2 begins?

Stakes: If the player dies to a forced Sector 1 loss and starts Run 2 with the exact same fleet, 10 CP cap, and 120 credits, the "lineage that learns" hook will feel like empty flavor text and cause immediate first-hour churn.

Does newNemesis() in newRun() generate a brand-new rival every run, or does the Sector 1 Nemesis-along with any hidden weaknesses the player died discovering-carry over into Run 2?

Stakes: If a new player loses Run 1 to a Nemesis with two hidden weaknesses and Run 2 replaces that Nemesis (or resets its weaknesses), the forced Sector 1 defeat teaches nothing that helps the player plan Run 2.

Why is tutorialCheck() hard-blocked when !B or app-on is active, leaving the map, shipyard, patron boon selection, and capture screens with zero tutorial hooks?

Stakes: Because Voidsworn's strategic layer uses fiction-first labels (Signal of..., Refit, Marks, salvage), leaving the campaign screens completely untaught forces new players to guess blindly at fleet-building before they even reach a battle.
