2. Findings

[BLOCKER] repairMult() is dead code; shipyard repairs ignore h_repair Heat and sector repair modifiers.

READ: core.js defines function repairMult(){ const c=sectorCond(); return (heatOn('h_repair')?.5:1)*((c&&c.repair)||1); }. However, campaign_run.js computes individual repair cost as rc=Math.ceil(miss/2*pm) and fleet repair as allCost=Math.ceil(allMiss/2*pm), where pm=priceMult(). ACT.repair and ACT.repairAll execute using priceMult().

INFERRED: repairMult() is never called in the shipyard slice. Neither the h_repair heat modifier nor sectorCond().repair has any effect on shipyard repairs, breaking sector condition and Heat design contracts. Furthermore, in repairMult(), heatOn('h_repair')?.5:1 multiplies by 0.5, which - if it were wired into a cost formula - would cut repair costs in half when Heat is enabled rather than penalizing the player (unless repairMult was meant to scale HP restored per credit).

[BLOCKER] Evidence pack omits HULLS, MODULES, and rollNodeReward, making exact HP/credit math impossible to verify from code.

READ: The data.js slice cuts off at line 544 (const cp=h.cp||1;), omitting the HULLS dictionary, base hull HP (t.hp), command points (t.cp), MODULES costs, and rollNodeReward(n.type). The Chair Census only states that hull costs are "roughly 60 per cp, rounded".

INFERRED: An economy audit asks to compare price per hit point (t.hp) and evaluate whether battle payouts (rollNodeReward) solve money by Sector 2, but the pack omits the exact numbers required to prove either point without estimation.

[FIX] Code and Chair Census contradict on whether "Add" (keeping captured prizes) is permitted.

READ: The Chair Census states: "Capture consequences are designer-ruled: strip and ransom only." Yet campaign_run.js lines 433-459 actively implement and default to 'add': "Default: Add a ship that fits with at least 40% hull" (dk=room&&(p.hpf||1)>=.4?'add':...) and renders the Add button in ACT.prizeStep.

INFERRED: Either the code in 264f441d violates the designer ruling by still letting players acquire free hulls via boarding (add), or the Chair Census misstated the ruling. If add is supposed to be cut, keeping it live destroys shipyard hull-buying demand because boarding yields free ships ("No upkeep for kept prizes").

[FIX] Ransom decay floor (0.4) is mathematically miscoded relative to the stated 55% base.

READ: Comments in campaign_run.js state: "ransom = 55% of that, scaled 60 to 100% by the hull left, and 15% less for each ransom already taken this run (never below 40%: owners learn)". The formula is Math.round(shipValue(p.tpl)*.55*(.6+.4*(p.hpf||1))*Math.max(.4,Math.pow(.85,n||0))).

INFERRED: Applying Math.max(.4, ...) inside the multiplier chain floors the decay factor at 0.40 (reached at n=6, where 0.85^6=0.377), which floors the actual ransom payout at 0.55x0.40=22% of shipValue (at 100% hull), not 40% of shipValue. If the designer intended the floor to be 40% of shipValue (down from 55%), the decay hits the floor after just two ransoms (0.55x0.85^2=39.7%).

[FIX] Shipyard repair cost (0.5 credits/HP) is so cheap it invalidates post-boss regroup tradeoffs (Q11) unless hulls have massive HP pools.

READ: The shipyard lede states: "Repairs cost 1 credit per 2 hull." (Math.ceil(miss/2*pm)). Designer ruling Q11 states: "after a boss, recovery is a regroup with tradeoffs (repair, upgrade, or buy), not a free restore".

INFERRED: Unless ship HP pools are in the hundreds per CP, paying 0.5 credits per missing HP is pocket change compared to hull prices (~60 per cp) and Mark upgrades (40 * cp * mk + 30). Repairing will never compete with upgrading or buying; it is an automatic tax paid before real spending begins.

[FIX] Prize valuation ignores priceMult(), creating arbitrage against the shipyard during high-cost sectors/Heat.

READ: prizeOffer and prizeRansom use shipValue(p.tpl), defined in comments as "shop price (or 40 per command point for hulls with no price, shipValue)". Meanwhile, shipyard hull purchases, repairs, Mark upgrades, and Class tracks all scale by priceMult().

INFERRED: Because prizeRansom and strip.cred (v * .15) do not scale with priceMult(), playing with h_coffers (1.25x prices) devalues capture payouts by 20% in real purchasing power, while unlocking META.unlocked.wrights (0.85x prices) inflates capture payouts relative to shop goods. Worse, if add remains legal, capturing a ship bypasses the 1.25x h_coffers surcharge entirely.

[FIX] Selling/decommissioning a ship in the shipyard also ignores priceMult(), enabling a buy-low/sell-high exploit or punishing h_coffers.

READ: In shipyardScreen, decommissioning pays sell=Math.round(HULLS[s.tpl].cost*.35), whereas buying costs Math.round(t.cost*pm).

INFERRED: While 0.35 is below 0.85 (so you cannot infinite-loop credits with wrights alone), decommissioning returns a fixed 35% of base cost regardless of whether you bought the ship at 0.85x or 1.25x, and ignores any Mark upgrades (s.mk) invested in the hull.

[FIX] Naming mismatch between "Eclipse" in prompt and ithar / deathless in FAC_PRICE.

READ: Question 2 asks: "Should price follow the faction-shape ruling that Eclipse is elite and Chorus is swarm?" In data.js, FAC_PRICE contains keys hegemony, voidsworn, corsair, chorus, ithar, and deathless.

INFERRED: "Eclipse" does not exist as a key in FAC_PRICE. Either ithar ("Glasswright") or deathless is the code identifier for Eclipse, or Eclipse is missing from FAC_PRICE entirely (defaulting to 1.0x across the board).

[NOTE] shipyardStock hardcodes Voidsworn hull IDs and translates via equivHull, restricting shop variety to 3 random classes.

READ: shipyardStock builds a pool from ['warden','harrow','penitent'] plus up to 3 meta-unlocked capital classes (reliquary, cathedral, ascendant), maps them via equivHull(h, RUN.fac), and takes .slice(0,3).

INFERRED: Unlocking Battleships (u_battleship) and Dreadnoughts (u_dread) in META dilutes the 3-slot shipyard pool, reducing the chance of seeing cheap Frigates/Destroyers in early sectors when RUN.cpCap or RUN.credits cannot support capital ships.

[NOTE] Deterministic module fallback in prizeOffer leaks predictable refits.

READ: In prizeOffer, if salvageYield returns no module (y.mod), the fallback is pool[Math.floor((v*7+(p.cp||1)*13)%pool.length)].

INFERRED: Because v (shipValue(p.tpl)) and p.cp are static per hull template and modulePool() only changes when fleet weapon/shield kits change, stripping a given enemy hull template will always yield the exact same fallback refit module for a given fleet kit.

Which faction's prices lie the most, and which way?

Conversely, because enemy shipValue uses shop price (t.cost), fighting Deathless or Ithar overpays the player in Ransom and Strip credits (1.3x-1.55x higher salvage payouts per CP defeated)!

2. FAC_PRICE AS IDENTITY

Chorus (Frigate: .65, Destroyer: .75, Cruiser: 1, Carrier: 1.2):

READ/INFERRED: Yes, this reads cleanly as swarm economics. Cheap small hulls (39 cr Frigates) encourage flooding the board early and treating small ships as expendable, while taxing Carriers (1.2x) discourages sitting back. However, because RUN.cpCap gates fleet size by Command Points (fleetCP() + t.cp <= RUN.cpCap), cheap credits alone do not let Chorus field more ships than cpCap allows - it just makes hitting cpCap trivial in Sector 1 and leaves Chorus with surplus credits for Mark/Class upgrades.

Corsair (Frigate: .8, Destroyer: .85, Cruiser: 1.05, Carrier: 1.15):

READ/INFERRED: Reads as a raiding/light-pack faction that favors small/medium hulls (0.8x/0.85x) and pays a mild premium for heavy ships. Matches their tow mechanic (w.cp <= 2 in campaign_run.js line 431).

Voidsworn (Carrier: 1.25, all else 1.0):

READ/INFERRED: No, a lone 1.25 multiplier on Carriers does not read as a cohesive faction identity. It reads like an isolated balance patch against a single overpowered hull (reliquary).

Hegemony ({} - all 1.0):

READ/INFERRED: Vanilla baseline. Appropriate for a standard/tutorial faction, though expressionless.

Ithar (Frigate: 1.3, Destroyer: 1.3, Cruiser: 1.15, Carrier: 1.1):

READ/INFERRED: Inverted curve. Taxing Frigates/Destroyers at 1.3x while discounting Carriers down to 1.1x pushes Ithar players away from fast small ships and toward capital ships, which clashes with a speed/shield fantasy unless their capitals are their true identity.

Deathless (Frigate: 1.45, Destroyer: 1.5, Cruiser: 1.55, no Carrier listed):

READ/INFERRED: Reads as an ultra-elite / undead juggernaut faction (likely what the prompt calls "Eclipse"). However, using shop price (FAC_PRICE) to express "elite vs. swarm" while keeping CP costs fixed creates a side effect: when the player fights Deathless/Ithar, shipValue(p.tpl) inherits FAC_PRICE, making elite factions loot piñatas for Ransom/Strip (+45% to +55% more credits) while Chorus enemies pay out starvation wages (0.65x on Frigates).

3. REPAIR ECONOMICS

Concrete Case (Cruiser at half hull after a boss):

READ: Sector 2+ starts at c===0 with a guaranteed 'shipyard' node (/* after a sector boss the fleet docks before its next fight */). Repair cost is Math.ceil(miss / 2 * pm) (0.5 credits per missing HP at pm = 1.0). Replacing a Cruiser costs ~180 * FAC_PRICE * pm (minus 0.35 * baseCost decommission = net ~117 credits for a standard 180-cr Cruiser, losing all Captain XP/ranks and Mark upgrades). Mark upgrades cost mkCost(s) = (40 * cp * mk + 30) * pm = 150 credits for Mk II on a 3-CP Cruiser, or 270 credits for Mk III. Class track level 1 costs 60 credits.

INFERRED (Assuming a 3-CP Cruiser has ~120 to 200 max HP):

At half hull, miss is 60 to 100 HP.

Repair cost (rc): 60 / 2 = 30 credits (or 100 / 2 = 50 credits).

Replace cost: Net ~117 credits (180 - 63), plus losing s.mk upgrades and captain c.rank/c.xp. Replacing a damaged ship is never right - repairing even from 1 HP (e.g., 199 missing HP = 100 credits) is cheaper than net replacement (117 credits) and preserves veteran captains and Marks.

Fight damaged vs. Repair: Paying 30-50 credits to restore 50% of a capital ship's HP is a no-brainer compared to risking losing a 180-credit hull + veteran captain + Mk upgrades in the next fight.

Trick with Mark Upgrades (changeShip): Note that in core.js line 224, changeShip heals the ship by the maxHP gained (ship.hp = clamp(ship.hp + (after - before), 1, after)), and shipyardScreen states "A Mark upgrade adds 20% hull and shields and 15% damage". Even so, paying 30-50 credits for a 50% hull heal is vastly cheaper than 150 credits for a 20% maxHP bump.

Does the post-boss regroup ruling (Q11) survive contact with these prices?

INFERRED: No. Because repairing half a Cruiser costs ~30-50 credits while a Cruiser Mk II upgrade costs 150 credits and a new Cruiser costs 180 credits, repair is too cheap to force a dilemma. The player will almost always click "Repair entire fleet" first as an automatic maintenance tax, and only then look at whether their remaining wallet reaches the 60 / 150 / 180 credit thresholds for Class Tracks, Marks, or Hulls.

4. THE CAPTURE TRIANGLE

Concrete Case (Mid-tier prize: 2-CP Standard Destroyer, shipValue = 120 credits, captured at hpf = 0.5 [50% hull], with a standard refit module worth MODULES[m].cost = 80 credits):

READ:

Add: Joins fleet for 0 credits at 50% hull (if fleetCP() + 2 <= RUN.cpCap and no factionGate). Repairing the remaining 50% hull at the next shipyard costs Math.ceil(miss / 2 * pm) (roughly 15-25 credits). Worth 120 credits in hull value (or 120 * 0.35 = 42 credits if immediately decommissioned at a shipyard!).

Strip: Yields 1 refit module (worth ~80 credits to buy, valued at half shop price = 40 credits by the default heuristic sv) + 15% of shipValue (Math.round(120 * 0.15) = 18 credits). Total heuristic value (sv): 18 + 40 = 58 credits (or 98 credits if you actually wanted to buy that module).

Ransom at Run Start (n = 0):

ransom(0)=round(120x0.55x(0.6+0.4x0.5)x0.85^0)=round(66x0.8x1.0)=53 credits

Ransom after Three Ransoms (n = 3):

ransom(3)=round(120x0.55x0.8x0.85^3)=round(52.8x0.614125)=32 credits

Does the right answer ever flip, and does the decay rule ever matter?

INFERRED:

When CP is open (room === true), Add crushes both Strip and Ransom: Adding gives a free 120-credit ship with no upkeep. Even if you don't want the ship, if you have open CP before a shipyard, Adding it and immediately clicking Decommission (+35%) at the shipyard yields 120 * 0.35 = 42 credits - which beats ransom(3) (32 credits) outright!

Between Strip and Ransom (when CP is full or gated), Strip almost always dominates Ransom on small/mid ships, and decay cements it: Even at n = 0 and 100% hull (hpf = 1.0), Ransom on a 120-cr Destroyer is 120 * 0.55 = 66 credits, whereas Strip gives 18 credits + a full refit module. Unless the module is complete junk for your build (and modulePool() already filters out unusable weapon/shield mods!), a module + 18 credits beats 53-66 raw credits.

When does decay flip the answer? Only on high-value ships (Cruisers/Carriers, v >= 180-240) or when MODULES[m].cost is low:

Take a 3-CP Cruiser (v = 180) at 75% hull (hpf = 0.75, hull factor = 0.9), with a 60-credit module (sv heuristic = 180 * 0.15 + 30 = 27 + 30 = 57).

At n = 0: Ransom = round(180x0.55x0.9x1.0)=89 credits (beats Strip's 27 cr + 60-cr mod).

At n = 3: Ransom = round(89.1x0.614)=55 credits (flips below Strip's 57 heuristic value and 87 total value!).

Thus, the 15% decay does flip Cruiser/Carrier captures from Ransom to Strip after 2-3 ransoms, forcing players to stop farming raw cash from boarding and take modules instead.

5. FLOW

READ (Sector structure from genSector):

Every sector has COLS = 7 columns (c = 0 to 6), meaning a player visits 7 nodes per sector:

c = 0: 'battle' in Sector 1; 'shipyard' in Sector 2+.

c = 1: 'battle' or 'event' (elites and drydocks are overwritten to 'battle').

c = 2: Mix of 'battle' (48%), 'elite' (13%), 'event' (17%), 'shipyard' (12%), or 'field'.

c = 3: Guaranteed to include at least one 'shipyard' (max 1 rest node per column).

c = 4 (COLS-3): Guaranteed to include at least one 'elite'.

c = 5 (COLS-2): Guaranteed rest stop ('shipyard', 'drydock', or 'field').

c = 6 (COLS-1): 'boss'.

Across a 7-node sector path, a typical player fights 4 to 5 combat nodes (including 1 elite and 1 boss) and visits 1 to 2 shipyards (up to 3 in Sector 2+ if hitting c=0, c=3, and c=5).

INFERRED (Income vs. Spending Trajectory):

Spending Sinks in a Run:

Expanding fleet to cpCap (say, adding 4-6 CP of ships across a run = ~240-360 credits, or 0 credits if boarded via Add!).

Upgrading 4-5 ships to Mk II (40 * cp * 1 + 30 = ~70-150 cr each, total ~450 credits) and Mk III (40 * cp * 2 + 30 = ~110-270 cr each, total ~750 credits).

Class Refit Tracks: 3 levels per class at 60, 110, 160 credits = 330 credits per ship class.

Refit Modules: 3 sold per shipyard.

Repairs: ~20-60 credits per sector (trivial at 0.5 cr/HP).

Where the Economy Pinches Hardest: Sector 1, Column 3 (cols[3] mid-sector shipyard). At c = 3, the player has only completed 2 to 3 fights (c = 0, 1, 2). Unless fight rewards + early ransoms exceed ~150 credits in 3 fights, the player arrives at the guaranteed c = 3 shipyard unable to afford a Cruiser (~180 cr) or multiple Mk II upgrades, forcing a sharp choice between a single Frigate/Destroyer, a Class Track level (60 cr), or saving for c = 5.

When a Competent Player Stops Making Credit Decisions: By mid-Sector 2 if boarding (Add + Strip), or Sector 3 otherwise. A competent player who boards enemy ships bypasses the two biggest credit sinks in the game: buying hulls (Add gives hulls for 0 credits, saving 240-400 credits) and buying refit modules (Strip gives modules plus 15% scrap credits). With repair costs negligible (0.5 cr/HP), a boarding-focused player funnels 100% of node rewards and ransoms straight into Mk II/III upgrades and Class Tracks (60/110/160 cr), solving the economy by Sector 2's mid-shipyard (c = 3).

5. Questions for the Designer

Is Add (keeping captured ships for free) supposed to be disabled per the Chair Census ("strip and ransom only"), or is campaign_run.js correct to allow it?

Stakes: As long as Add is active and upkeep-free, boarding completely bypasses shipyard hull prices (the largest credit sink in the game) and lets players farm free 35% decommission payouts.

Why is repairMult() defined in core.js if shipyardScreen, ACT.repair, and ACT.repairAll use priceMult() instead?

Stakes: Right now, the h_repair Heat toggle and sector repair modifiers are completely non-functional in shipyards, and base repairs (0.5 cr/HP) are too cheap to create the post-boss regroup tradeoff required by ruling Q11.

Should shipValue(p.tpl) for enemy prizes use the enemy faction's FAC_PRICE or the base CP cost?

Stakes: Tying shipValue to FAC_PRICE means fighting elite factions (deathless at 1.55x, ithar at 1.3x) showers the player in bonus ransom/strip credits, while fighting swarm factions (chorus at 0.65x) starves them.
