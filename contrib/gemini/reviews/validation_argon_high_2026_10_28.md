2. Findings

[BLOCKER] F2 (Hegemony) directly violates L7's ban on cross-sector FTL communication and duplicates F6's mechanism.

READ: F2 states that the Registry "ingests every task force's telemetry and reissues synchronized doctrine to all fleets at once" in a "die, upload, update everyone's doctrine overnight" software push.

READ: L7 states that "there is no instant communication across sectors except what a fleet carries or transmits at light speed with years of lag," granting exceptions only to a fleet's internal battle network and the Eclipse Archive.

INFERRED: A Hegemony task force destroyed up to three sectors away cannot upload telemetry "overnight" across light-years without violating L7. Furthermore, F2's telemetry-upload-and-software-push loop is mechanically identical to F6's Archive compilation, stripping F6 of its unique lore exemption under L7.

[BLOCKER] F5 (The Glasswrights) breaks gameplay tempo, A3's debrief loop, and the persistent Nemesis by severing Run N from Run N+1.

READ: F5 states that uncrewed gatherers recover glass shards "over years" across "deep time," so a new run is "the next casting in the queue, incorporating everything recovered by its launch, not the fate of the fleet just played".

READ: A3 frames the end-of-run screen as "the debrief the next attempt inherits: not only currency totals, but what this attempt taught," and the game features a "persistent Nemesis who adapts to the player's build".

INFERRED: In actual gameplay, the player immediately spends the renown, admiral XP, and core sync earned in Run N on Run N+1 while facing a Nemesis that adapted to Run N's fleet. Claiming that Run N+1 does not incorporate "the fate of the fleet just played" contradicts both the immediate mechanical inheritance of Run N's rewards and A3's debrief framing.

[BLOCKER] L3 ("Astra, the last core, caged and throttled") contradicts the 6-core Memory (Core Sync) system.

READ: F5 and L3 state that the Glasswrights "hold Astra, the last core, caged and throttled; when Astra is finally shut down, they end".

READ: The systems audit defines Core Sync around "6 patron cores," and A1 (Memory) states that "the patron cores remember the fleets that keep flying their protocols; sync tiers are a core gradually recognizing and trusting the player's line".

INFERRED: If Astra is "the last core" in existence and is held "caged and throttled" by a single faction, six independent patron cores cannot simultaneously exist across all factions to freely recognize, trust, and patronize player lines.

[BLOCKER] L7 (sub-light travel/lag) and P1 (80% defeat rate) leave F1, F3, F4, and A1 Doctrine with no plausible recovery mechanism when fleets wipe in deep space.

READ: P1 establishes that "about 80% of runs end in defeat" across three sectors where "every hull you lose stays lost", and L7 establishes that fleets move at "a few percent of light speed" with "years of lag" for light-speed transmissions.

READ: A1 funds Doctrine from "what lost attempts recovered" ("salvage endowment of dead fleets"), F1 studies "every fallen captain's record," F3 inherits physical "trophies, scar-stories, and grudges" by "next season," and F4 breeds out "whatever killed the last brood".

INFERRED: When a fleet is completely destroyed in Sector 2 or 3, there are no survivors to carry physical salvage, biological tissue, or trophies back at sub-light speeds, nor can biological broods (F4) or mammal packs (F3) beam telemetry home. Without survivor lifeboats, black-box relays, or scout screen retreats built into the fiction, defeated fleets cannot teach or fund the next run.

[BLOCKER] F1's generational cycle ("a new crusade every generation") breaks per-admiral Mastery continuity for unaugmented humans.

READ: F1 defines the Sunward Crusade as "unaugmented humans" who send "a new crusade every generation".

READ: Mastery tracks "per-admiral XP across 5 thresholds (0/6/16/30/50)," and losing a hull means "so does its captain".

INFERRED: If runs are separated by human generations (20-30 years) plus L7's multi-year sub-light transit times, a single unaugmented human Admiral cannot survive across enough runs to reach 50 XP, nor can an Admiral whose flagship died in Run 1 command Run 2 unless "Admiral" is explicitly redefined as an inherited office or dynastic seat rather than an individual commander.

[BLOCKER] A2 (horizontal-first scaling) and A4 (Run 5 and Run 10 milestones) fail the economy math and contradict P2 and P3.

READ: Total renown sinks equal 1,815 (635 Legacy, 930 Admirals, 250 Hull variants), while a win pays "roughly 100 renown; a mid-run death roughly 45; an early death under 10".

READ: P2 requires a "near impossible" Run 1 and a first win around "runs 4 to 6" built on cross-run progression; A2 claims "Horizontal first, vertical modest"; A4 claims at Run 5 "the first doctrine tier is complete and one or two lineages are deep," and at Run 10 the player reaches "full core sync".

Horizontal sidegrades cannot turn a "near impossible" Run 1 into a Run 4-6 win on 100-180 renown unless Legacy's vertical power (+4 command points, extra starting credits, veteran captains, carrier/battleship/dreadnought unlocks) does the actual heavy lifting. Moreover, A4's projections of completed doctrine tiers and two deep lineages by Run 5, or "full core sync" (25 protocols x 6 cores = 150 protocols) by Run 10, are mathematically unreachable at stated payout rates.

[FIX] A1's "institutional learning" frame fails to cover Memory (Core Sync) and mislabels vertical power in Doctrine and Lineage.

INFERRED: If a destroyed hull's mind is already safely in the Archive with immediate internal sharing (L7), the captain did not actually die, or else compiling "a new iteration" overwrites individual identity into a composite mind (violating L2). READ: L5 mandates that Crusade culture is "debt-keeping, stripped of church furniture: losses are recorded as debts owed," yet F1 uses the name "SUNWARD CRUSADE," "sends a new crusade," and archives records in "The Hall of the Fallen". INFERRED: F1 relies on martial-religious reverence ("Crusade," "Hall of the Fallen") instead of making debt-ledger settlement the engine of its cycle, while F4 requires a biological reskin of A1's bureaucratic terminology ("standing orders," "price cuts," "playbooks") to avoid contradicting L1.

3. Explicit Checks
a. THE FRAME

Does the learning frame cover doctrine, lineage, and memory equally, or does one track resist it?
The learning frame does not cover all three tracks equally; Memory (Core Sync) directly resists the frame, and Doctrine and Lineage only partially fit it.

Memory resists P4/A1: P4 requires that what persists is "what the player's faction has LEARNED from previous attempts". In Memory, the faction learns nothing; instead, an external patron core is "gradually recognizing and trusting the player's line" at 4, 12, and 25 protocols. That is a relationship/favour mechanic, not institutional learning (INFERRED).

Doctrine stretches learning over material capital: While price cuts and command points can pass as "institutional knowledge" and "staff lessons," extra starting credits ("salvage endowment of dead fleets"), starting relics, and unlocking carriers, battleships, and dreadnoughts are material and industrial escalations, not "standing orders the faction now teaches every fleet" (INFERRED).

Lineage contradicts its own non-vertical claim: Framing per-admiral XP as "annotated charts and inherited playbooks" works only if the "Admiral" represents a dynastic seat or staff lineage rather than a single mortal officer, and its Tier 5 reward ("a veteran flag captain with a boosted flagship") is an explicit vertical stat bump, contradicting A1's claim that Lineage grants "inherited options, not stat bumps" (READ/INFERRED).

b. THE SET

Are the six cycles genuinely six mechanisms? Name any two that collapse into the same mechanism under the fiction.
No; the six cycles reduce to three underlying mechanisms, with two explicit collapses (INFERRED):

F2 (Hegemony) and F6 (The Eclipse) collapse into the same mechanism (Automated Digital Telemetry & Software/Version Update): F2's "Registry ingests every task force's telemetry and reissues synchronized doctrine... Learning is a software push: die, upload, update" is functionally identical to F6's "a new iteration compiled with the prior iteration's combat records. Learning is versioning" (READ/INFERRED). Because L7 explicitly grants instant cross-sector sharing to the Eclipse Archive while denying it to the Hegemony, F2 is an illegal duplicate of F6 (INFERRED).

F1 (Sunward Crusade) and F3 (Ashfall Hullbreakers) collapse into the same mechanism (Generational Cultural/Archival Inheritance from the Dead): F1 ("sends a new crusade every generation... every fallen captain's record is studied by the next fleet") and F3 ("A new pack is raised on the last pack's kills, inheriting its trophies, scar-stories, and grudges") operate on the exact same loop - next generation raised on the recorded deeds of the slain prior generation - differing only in human archives vs. mammalian oral/trophy dressing (READ/INFERRED).

F4 (The Chorus) and F5 (The Glasswrights) share a Material/Somatic Re-casting mechanism, differentiated only by F5's queue lag: Both F4 ("Whatever killed the last brood is bred out or armored against") and F5 ("casts the next fleet from the recovered material with the killing flaw corrected") physically re-engineer the hulls/bodies of the next wave against the specific damage type that killed the prior wave, though F5 adds a lagged, non-serial queue (READ/INFERRED).

c. TEMPO

For each faction, does the cycle explain a player starting the next run immediately? Flag any cycle whose fiction implies waiting, scarcity of attempts, or a timeline the game cannot honor.

F1 (Sunward Crusade) - FLAGGED (Generational waiting & L7 lag): Sending "a new crusade every generation" implies a 20-to-30-year gap between runs, plus years of L7 light-speed lag for fallen captains' records to return. This timeline breaks per-admiral Mastery for "unaugmented humans" (who would age out or die across runs) and makes an adapting persistent Nemesis across generations implausible unless the Nemesis is immortal (INFERRED).

F2 (Hegemony) - FLAGGED (Violates L7 timeline): Claiming fleets "die, upload, update everyone's doctrine overnight" honors back-to-back player tempo only by directly violating L7's rule that cross-sector communication has "years of lag" (READ/INFERRED).

F3 (Ashfall Hullbreakers) - FLAGGED (Seasonal maturation & L7 recovery lag): Raising a new pack "suckled on the story of the pack you killed" to hunt "next season" implies generational maturation time and cannot explain how a pack wiped out in Sector 3 gets its physical "trophies" and oral "scar-stories" back to the den across light-years of vacuum (READ/INFERRED).

F4 (The Chorus) - FLAGGED (L7 biological feedback lag): Growing a new brood generation can happen quickly in fiction, but when a brood is exterminated in deep space, L7 forbids instant cross-sector signaling, leaving the hatchery with no biological sample or knowledge of "whatever killed the last brood" to breed against on the immediate next run (READ/INFERRED).

F5 (The Glasswrights) - FLAGGED (Explicitly breaks run-to-run continuity and scarcity): F5 attempts to solve L7's "over years" shard-recovery lag by making expeditions non-serial ("a new run is the next casting in the queue, incorporating everything recovered by its launch, not the fate of the fleet just played"). However, the game does immediately apply the renown, admiral XP, core sync, and Nemesis adaptation from the fleet just played to the very next run, so the game cannot honor F5's non-serial queue (READ/INFERRED).

F6 (The Eclipse) - PASSES TEMPO (via L7 exception): Because L7 explicitly exempts the Eclipse Archive ("whose internal sharing is immediate for minds already in it"), compiling a new iteration immediately upon fleet destruction matches back-to-back gameplay tempo without violating travel/communication lag (READ/INFERRED).

d. THE SHAPE

With horizontal-first scaling and the stated economy (about 100 renown per win, 45 per mid death, under 10 per early death, 1,815 total renown sinks, mastery capstone at 50 XP per admiral across 24 admirals, full sync at 25 protocols per core), trace runs 1, 5, and 10. Where does the player's power actually come from at each point, and does the first completion plausibly land around runs 4 to 6?

Run 1 Trace: The player enters with 0 renown, 0 Mastery XP, 0 Core Sync, 1 starting Admiral per faction, base starting credits, base command points, and basic hull classes (READ/INFERRED). Power comes purely from the base starting fleet and raw player tactics; assuming Run 1 ends in an early-to-mid death, it pays ~10 to 45 renown, ~1-4 protocols toward a core, and initial XP on one Admiral (INFERRED).

Run 5 Trace (after 4 losses: e.g., 1 early death at ~8 renown + 3 mid deaths at ~45 renown = ~143 cumulative renown):

Economy reality vs. A4: With ~143 total renown earned out of 1,815 (7.9% of total sinks), the player can afford only 2 to 3 cheap Legacy upgrades (out of 12 total costing 635 renown) OR 1-2 Admiral/Hull unlocks - nowhere near A4's claim that "the first doctrine tier is complete and one or two lineages are deep" (INFERRED).

Mastery & Sync reality: If the player stuck to a single Admiral across all 4 losses, that Admiral has likely crossed the 6-XP threshold (alternate starting fleet) and is approaching or just hitting 16 XP (4 aspect choices instead of 3); having two lineages "deep" (30 or 50 XP) by Run 5 is impossible unless losing runs award 15-25 XP each, which would trivialize the 50-XP cap in 2-3 runs (INFERRED). In Core Sync, focusing on one patron core across 4 runs (~2-3 protocols per run) reaches Tier 1 (4 protocols: improved offers) and nears Tier 2 (12 protocols: starting protocol) on a single core (INFERRED).

Where power actually comes from & Runs 4-6 Completion: Horizontal options (an alternate fleet or 4 aspect choices) do not make a "near impossible" Run 1 winnable by Run 4-6 unless Run 1's impossibility was caused by a hard vertical stat/resource check (INFERRED). For first completion to land reliably at Runs 4-6, the player's power must actually come from player pattern mastery plus cherry-picking Legacy's vertical upgrades (+2 command points, Tier 1 starting credits, starting relic, or first ship-class unlock) and Core Sync Tier 2 (a free starting protocol), which contradicts A2's claim that progression is "Horizontal first, vertical modest" (INFERRED).

Run 10 Trace (assuming 80% loss rate = 8 losses + 2 wins: e.g., 1 early death [~8] + 7 mid deaths [~315] + 2 wins [~200] + modest Heat bonuses [~20] = ~543 cumulative renown):

Economy reality vs. A4: At ~543 cumulative renown, the player has earned only 30% of the 1,815 total renown sink (not even enough to buy the 635-renown Legacy track alone if they bought zero Admirals or Hulls) (INFERRED).

Mastery & Sync reality: The player may have 1 Admiral at or near the 30-to-50 XP capstone (unlocking the second Command option and the vertical "veteran flag captain with a boosted flagship") and 1-2 cores at Tier 2 (12 protocols) or possibly 1 core reaching Tier 3 (25 protocols: guaranteed high rarity), while the other 22-23 Admirals and 4-5 cores remain largely untouched (INFERRED).

Where power actually comes from: By Run 10, power comes from stacking Legacy's vertical margin wideners (+4 total command points, extra starting credits, price cuts, capital ship unlocks, veteran captains), a Tier 4/5 main Admiral ("boosted flagship"), and a Tier 2/3 primary patron core (INFERRED). Reaching full completion of the 1,815-renown economy and 6-core sync takes 35 to 45 runs, not 10 (INFERRED).

e. DEFEAT

At an 80% loss rate, what does the debrief framing need to stay honest by run 4? Name the first thing that would start to feel false.

The first thing that will feel false: The debrief's claim to name "the specific unlock now affordable or the lesson a death bought" (READ/INFERRED). By Run 4, the cheap initial thresholds (6 Mastery XP, 4 Core Sync protocols, 30-renown Admiral) are behind the player, while the next milestones sit across wider gaps (16/30/50 XP, 12/25 protocols, ~50-80+ renown Legacy/Admiral costs), and early deaths pay "under 10" renown (READ/INFERRED). When a Run 4 loss yields +8 renown and +2 protocols without crossing a single threshold or affording any shop item, dressing up an unspent currency increment as a "named lesson" will feel like dishonest UI filler (INFERRED).

What the debrief needs to stay honest: Every defeat screen must surface concrete tactical or systemic carryover generated by that specific run's failure, even when no currency threshold is crossed - specifically: (1) logging the exact enemy/boss mechanic that destroyed the flagship as a named tactical entry or counter-hint for the next attempt, (2) explicitly showing how the persistent Nemesis adapted to the player's build, or (3) awarding a guaranteed single-run recovery/salvage modifier (e.g., a localized map reveal or salvage cache in the sector where the fleet fell) so every death leaves a tangible mark on the very next run (INFERRED).

They now know the early sector bosses, how to build around their favored core, and how their Nemesis counters their habits, though A4's promise of a "complete" first doctrine tier and "one or two" deep lineages remains out of reach mathematically (INFERRED). RUN 10
With roughly 8 losses and 1-2 wins behind them (~450-550 cumulative renown out of 1,815 total sinks), the player has unlocked roughly half of the Doctrine track (including command point boosts, price cuts, and mid-tier capital ship classes), pushed one primary Admiral to 30 or 50 XP (unlocking the second Command option and the boosted flagship), reached Sync Tier 2 or 3 on one or two patron cores, and unlocked only a handful of the 24 Admirals and 18 Hull variants (INFERRED). 6. Questions for the Designer

How do defeated fleets in F1, F2, F3, and F4 transmit combat lessons, salvage, trophies, or genetic adaptations back across sectors without violating L7's ban on FTL communication?

Stakes: Unless you establish a shared lore mechanism (such as automated light-speed black-box relays coupled with years-long campaign timelines, or surviving scout screens retreating from doomed flagships), four of your six faction cycles and A1's salvage-funded Doctrine collapse under L7 and P1's 80% loss rate (INFERRED).

If Astra is "the last core, caged and throttled" by the Glasswrights (L3), what are the "6 patron cores" in the Core Sync system, and why can all six factions freely sync with them?

Stakes: Right now L3 directly contradicts the existence and fiction of the 6-core Memory track; you must either redefine the 6 patron cores as lesser sub-cores/shards or rewrite L3 so Astra is not the sole surviving core (INFERRED).

Should the renown economy and Mastery/Sync thresholds be re-tuned to match A4's Run 5 and Run 10 milestones, or should A4 be rewritten to match the current ~40-run (1,815 renown) economy?

Stakes: At ~45 renown per mid-run loss, players at Run 5 and Run 10 will have less than a third of the progression A4 expects them to have, which will either starve Runs 4-6 of the power needed for P2's first win or make the progression curve feel three times slower than intended (INFERRED).
