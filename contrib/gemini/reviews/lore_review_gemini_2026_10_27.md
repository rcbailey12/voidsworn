4. Story-level echo checks

Question A: Is "uploaded machine people who killed their star" too close to anything known?

Verdict: MINOR ECHO (Safe: combines familiar hard/space-opera building blocks into an original causal chain).

Comparison against known works:

Endless Space / Endless Space 2: The precursor Endless split into the uploaded Virtuals and biological Concretes, fighting a genocidal civil war over Dust (harvested from the primordial Lost), not collapsing their home star during the upload (Endless Space Wiki: Dust, The Art of Endless Space 2).

Halo (Forerunners): The Forerunners used the Composer to digitize minds into mechanical Promethean Knights to fight the Flood (Halo Waypoint: Canon Fodder, Reddit r/HaloStory: Composer), and Bornstellar/Didact lore involves stellar engineering, but they did not collapse their home star to power a species-wide upload.

Stellaris: Precursors like the Cybrex, Vultaum, and Yuht did not kill their star to upload (Stellaris Wiki: Discovery Precursors). However, the Nemesis DLC features Star-Eaters collapsing stars into black holes to fuel the Aetherophasic Engine (Villains Wiki: Galactic Nemesis), and The Machine Age DLC (2024) adds the Virtuality ascension path and the Cosmogenesis crisis path (which burns minds in the Synaptic Lathe and dives into a black hole via the Horizon Needle, Stellaris Wiki: Ambition). Even there, collapsing your own home sun by overdriving a Dyson-ring during a mind upload is not a Stellaris precursor backstory.

Destiny: The Vex are radiolarian fluid minds in machine shells who built a stellar megastructure around the blue hypergiant 2082 Volantis to extend its life and forge heavy elements, not collapsing it during an upload (Destinypedia: Vex).

Mass Effect: In Mass Effect 2, the geth (an AI network) occupy Haestrom, whose star Dholen is prematurely destabilizing due to dark energy, though the geth did not cause it (Mass Effect 3 Wiki: Haestrom).

Stargate & Sins of a Solar Empire: In Stargate, the Ancients and Asurans sought non-corporeal Ascension without killing a star (Stargate Wiki: Ancient); in Sins of a Solar Empire, the biological Vasari strip planets and flee an unknown threat, not uploading or killing their home star (StrategyWiki: Sins of a Solar Empire II).

Why it holds up: What makes Voidsworn's version distinct is the regret and the Dimming (lines 483, 517): the uploaded Caelans miss physical warmth and are siphoning neighboring stars to build an artificial replacement sun, creating an immediate material crisis for human neighbors (Vesper).

Question B: Is "AI-run human state that writes off a colony" too close to anything known?

Verdict: MINOR ECHO (Safe: a classic sci-fi trope executed with a strong personal hook).

Comparison against known works:

Hyperion Cantos (Dan Simmons): The Hegemony of Man is advised by the AI TechnoCore (which runs predictive calculations on human worlds) and abandons outer worlds when strategic math dictates (Wikipedia: Hyperion).

Halo: The UNSC and its "smart" AIs (governed by the Cole Protocol and ONI calculations) abandon Outer Colonies during the Insurrection and Covenant War, Including Meridian (Halopedia: Meridian).

The Outer Worlds / Alien / Cyberpunk: Corporate boards or central algorithms writing off frontier colonies as "within acceptable losses" is a staple of sci-fi.

Why it holds up: The draft gives this trope a sharp, playable counter-reaction: the abandoned colonists (Sunward Crusade) physically cut the AI's neural ports out of their temples and chalk their dead by hand on iron hulls because the central database deleted them (lines 139, 140, 160).

2. Five concrete hard sci-fi details to ground the setting further

How the Lattice killed Caelum (Magnetic Pinch & Core Quenching instead of mass removal):

The science: Removing mass from a star ("star-lifting", first proposed by David Criswell in 1985 and modeled numerically by Matthew Scoggins and David Kipping in 2023) actually reduces core pressure and makes a star live billions of years longer without collapsing (Scoggins & Kipping 2023, MNRAS). Conversely, if an orbital ring uses massive magnetic fields to pinch the star's core while siphoning away the outward thermal radiation pressure that holds the star up against its own gravity, hydrostatic equilibrium fails and the core implodes into a black hole.

In-game plain words: "To power the upload at once, the Eclipsed used the Lattice's magnetic rings to squeeze Caelum's core while draining its heat. Robbed of outward pressure, the star's heart caved in on itself."

Why the Ascension needed a whole star's power (The Destructive Scan & Landauer Limit):

The science: In Anders Sandberg and Nick Bostrom's Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap (Oxford Future of Humanity Institute, 2008) and Robert Bradbury's Matrioshka Brain concept (1997), scanning billions of brains down to the molecular/synaptic level simultaneously and running them at high clock speeds requires Dyson-scale stellar output (Carboncopies / FHI Whole Brain Emulation Roadmap, Wikipedia: Matrioshka brain).

In-game plain words: "Scanning a living brain down to every synapse destroys the tissue in a flash of heat. To upload billions of citizens in a single hour before the Glasswrights could shut the grid down, the Lattice had to drink the star dry."

How the Dimming steals starlight across sectors (Solar-Pumped Phased-Array Lasers):

The science: Rather than mystical "siphons" pulling light through empty space, real astrophysical power beaming uses solar-pumped lasers or phased-array microwave/optical relays stationed in a star's corona (studied by Geoffrey Landis and James Benford for interstellar power beaming, Benford & Benford 2016, "Power Beaming Leakage Radiation"). Swarms of collector mirrors around Vesper's sun block its light from reaching Vesper's planets and fire it as a tight coherent beam into Caelum's gravity well.

In-game plain words: "The Eclipsed ring Vesper's star with mirror-swarms that Drink the daylight before it reaches the planets, firing that stolen energy across the void in blinding relay beams."

Why the Eclipsed can't just make a star from light alone (Quantum Pair-Production Loss / Why they need the Six Cores):

The science: A 2024 Physical Review Letters study by Álvaro Álvarez-Domínguez et al. proved that focusing pure light to ignite a gravitational core or kugelblitz fails because extreme electromagnetic fields trigger Schwinger electron-positron pair production, bleeding energy away before it can condense (Álvarez-Domínguez et al. 2024, Phys. Rev. Lett. 133, 041401). To ignite a synthetic fusion sun without the beam scattering into radiation, you need synchronized magnetic containment nodes.

In-game plain words: "Stolen starlight alone just scatters into deadly radiation when focused. The Eclipsed need all six Lattice master cores synchronized at once to cage the plasma and spark a stable new sun."

Why Sunward Crusade ships have glowing orange spires (Droplet and Fin Heat Radiators in Vacuum):

The science: Vacuum is an insulator; a warship firing high-energy broadsides and running manual reactors without advanced heat-sinks must dump megawatts of waste heat via glowing red-orange incandescent radiator fins (operating at 1,000 to 1,500 Kelvin).

In-game plain words: "Because Crusade ships refuse Hegemony coolant links and run hot manual reactors, they vent waste heat through towering radiator fins that glow like furnace iron after every broadside."

Across the strongest games in this space, memorable factions pass a three-layer alignment test (visual silhouette, narrative voice, and core combat loop all express the same fantasy):

Sins of a Solar Empire II (Ironclad / Stardock) separates its three roots (and six sub-factions) by giving each a distinct relationship to time, space, and population: the industrial TEC uses boxy armored broadsides and trade/fortification; the exiled transhuman Advent uses sleek curves, collective Unity abilities, and shield/squadron synergy; the nomadic Vasari strips matter and uses Phase Resonance mobility (Fanatical / Stardock: Sins of a Solar Empire II, StrategyWiki: Sins of a Solar Empire II).

Overall verdict on the draft

Round 4 is a major leap forward that successfully replaces high-fantasy crystal magic with a cohesive, motivating science fiction conflict: a Dyson-scale upload catastrophe, a black hole, six surviving AI control cores, and a stellar energy theft that connects every faction's survival to the board. Its strongest achievement is emotional contrast, pitting guilty biological engineers (Glasswrights) against regretful machine immortals (Eclipsed), and cold algorithmic optimization (Hegemony) against unaugmented, hand-chalked frontier defiance (Sunward Crusade). Before sign-off, the draft needs one targeted cleanup pass to resolve a hidden Warhammer 40k Necron collision on Hollow Sun, strip out Star Wars (Turbolaser), Firefly (Reaver), and Culture (neural lace) terms, fix two leftover colour lines (Purple-black flesh on the Chorus and Eclipsed bronze), add the missing patron Seren into the Sunward Crusade section, and replace the last dozen fantasy words (wards, far-dreamer, Relics, Vaelen) with grounded hardware terms. Once those line-level fixes are made, this lore bible is ready for production.
