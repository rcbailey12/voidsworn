1. Proof of reading

The system contains 12 fleet-wide relics (displayed to the player under the system name "salvage") that persist for the rest of a run and cannot be duplicated within the same run. Their effects split across two additive channels:

fx channel (5 relics): Stat modifier objects (shPct, hullRegen, speed, cdMod, armor) are pushed into fleetFxList alongside the admiral's fx and all held boons' fx, then summed additively by sumFx whenever ship stats are calculated.

run channel (7 relics): Run-level economy and combat-trigger keys (avenge, firstSure, revive, credPct, bloodied, salvage, xpPct) are summed additively across held relics by runFx (with the META salvagers unlock adding credPct +.2 on top) and consumed at specific battle or post-battle call sites.

For acquisition, winning an elite battle lets the player choose 1 of 2 random unheld relics (while a boss win offers 1 of 3, and both the Founder meta unlock and certain events grant 1 random unheld relic directly).

INFERRED: Because players cannot decline a relic reward, picking up a weak relic after a boss unintentionally shields earlier centerpiece relics from payRelic, whereas taking a build-defining boss relic exposes it as the mandatory sacrifice in the next payRelic event.

INFERRED: Using salvage simultaneously as the player-facing name for all relics, the internal ID of one specific relic, and a runFx property key invites confusion in UI text, debugging, and analytics.

b. The two revive systems (phylactery vs. Eclipse faction reanimate)

How they are wired (READ): phylactery (run:{revive:1}) is consumed at battle.js:936: on a player ship's destruction, if runFx().revive && !B.reviveUsed, the ship restarts at 25% hull and sets B.reviveUsed for that battle. The Eclipse faction reanimate mechanic is at battle.js:519 and is explicitly described as "Separate from the Eclipse faction reanimate mechanic (:519), which is a hull/faction system with its own conditions".

Do they stack, collide, or double-pay on the same death? (INFERRED):
Because B.reviveUsed belongs strictly to phylactery (:936) and :519 uses its own hull/faction conditions, the two mechanics do not share a state flag.
Whether they stack cleanly across separate deaths or collide/double-pay on the same death depends on whether the earlier check in the execution path short-circuits the destruction handler:
If :519 intercepts lethal damage before the destruction block at :936 runs (or if whichever runs first returns early): They stack sequentially-one lethal event consumes the first revive mechanism, and a later lethal event consumes the second.
If both execute during the same destruction event without an early return or ship.hp <= 0 guard: They collide/double-pay on the first Eclipse ship death-both :519 and :936 fire on the same fatal hit, burning B.reviveUsed redundantly on a ship that was already reanimating (and overwriting its post-revive hull percentage with whichever assignment runs second).
Interaction with avenge (:943): Because revive is at :936 and avenge is 7 lines below at :943, if :936 restarts the ship at 25% hull without returning out of the destruction handler before :943, a single death double-pays by both reviving the dead ship at 25% hull and healing all other ships for 15% hull.

What happens for an Eclipse fleet holding phylactery:
relicUseful does not exclude phylactery for Eclipse, and Eclipse renames it "Second Ignition". Under clean short-circuiting, it grants the Eclipse fleet one additional fleet-wide 25%-hull revive per battle on top of their native :519 reanimates; without short-circuiting, the first eligible Eclipse death consumes both at once.

What happens for a non-Eclipse fleet holding phylactery:
Non-Eclipse fleets have no :519 faction reanimate, so phylactery (displayed via fallback as "Eclipse backup frame") cleanly grants the fleet a once-per-battle 25%-hull revive on the first player ship destroyed at :936.

c. Avenge (martyr) trigger discipline vs. revive

Trigger discipline comparison:
revive (phylactery, :936) is strictly gated to once per battle by !B.reviveUsed.
avenge (martyr, :943) has no per-battle cap (on EVERY player ship destruction (no per-battle cap in this code)), healing every other living non-squadron player ship by 15% of its max hull each time a player ship dies.

Decision, snowball, or perverse incentive?
It is not a traditional win-more snowball in standard attrition, because each death reduces the player's remaining ship count (N - 1 surviving ships receive the heal).
However, it creates a perverse incentive (rewarding ship loss) in three specific cases:
Asymmetric hull values (sacrificing a small escort to heal heavy hulls): Because the heal is 15% of each surviving ship's max hull rather than 15% of the dying ship's max hull, letting a damaged low-hull frigate die while 3-4 damaged battleships survive restores 45%-60% of battleship max hull across the fleet-a net positive total hull gain that also saves post-battle repair credits if hull repair is priced per HP or if the escort reawakens.
Non-permanent deaths (phylactery at :936 and Eclipse reanimate at :519): If a ship's destruction triggers :943 (avenge) on the same death that revives it via phylactery or reanimates it via Eclipse's faction mechanic, intentionally letting a ship die heals the entire fleet by 15% max hull without losing a ship for the battle.
Squadron deaths as triggers (if unfiltered): While avenge explicitly excludes squadrons from receiving the heal (every other living non-squadron player ship), the trigger description (on EVERY player ship destruction) does not state that dying squadrons are excluded from triggering :943. If squadron deaths trigger avenge, losing disposable squadrons repeatedly heals the main fleet by 15% max hull with no per-battle cap.

d. Offer fairness and the usefulness filter (relicUseful)

Currently, relicUseful (core.js:239) excludes only lattice for a shieldless fleet. Auditing the other 11 relics for dead or near-dead fleet states:
coils (fx:{cdMod:1}): Strictly dead in a fleet whose ships only have passive abilities or abilities with 0-1 turn cooldowns that cannot be reduced further.
logbook (run:{xpPct:.3}): Strictly dead once all captains in the fleet have reached maximum level, or when offered at the end of a run with too few fights left to reach another level-up.
ledger (run:{credPct:.25}) & salvage (run:{salvage:1}): Strictly dead if offered on the final boss reward screen (if any step runs after it) or after the last shop/repair opportunity of the run.
martyr (run:{avenge:.15}): Strictly dead in a single-ship fleet (no "other" living ship exists to heal), and near-dead in a lossless/shield-tank fleet that never loses a hull.
cogitator (run:{firstSure:1}): Dead or near-dead in a fleet whose ships already reach ≥95 aim (or ≥75 when surging) through base stats, doctrines, or boons.
compass (fx:{speed:1}): Near-dead in stationary/artillery fleets or when +1 speed does not cross a turn-order or movement breakpoint.
banner (run:{bloodied:.15}): Near-dead in single-entity boss fights with no adds to kill, or in fleets that spread damage evenly so kills only occur on the final turn.
spore (fx:{hullRegen:3}): Near-dead in pure shield/evasion fleets that take no hull damage or in fast burst fights ending in 1-2 turns.
phylactery (run:{revive:1}): Near-dead in dominant fleets that never lose a ship, or partially redundant in Eclipse fleets if :519 and :936 collide.
armourweave (fx:{armor:.06}): Near-dead in shield/evasion fleets that avoid hull hits or against armor-bypassing damage.

Should the filter grow, or is a weak pick an acceptable cost of a random offer?
Grow relicUseful only for hard structural incompatibilities-specifically excluding coils if the fleet has zero cooldown-based abilities, logbook if all fleet captains are max level, and martyr if the fleet has only 1 non-squadron ship. Because the Founder meta unlock (core.js:328) and random relic events (campaign_run.js:356) grant 1 random relic with no choice, rolling a structurally non-functional relic there feels like a bug rather than a tactical trade-off.
Keep situational or build-dependent weakness (banner, spore, compass, armourweave, cogitator, phylactery) unfiltered. In a 1-of-2 (elite) or 1-of-3 (boss) draft, evaluating whether a situational relic fits the current fleet and upcoming bosses is the core player skill of the reward screen.

e. Acquisition and loss (2 on elite, 3 on boss, no decline, payRelic LIFO removal)

Elite offers 1 of 2 (campaign_run.js:410): Fine. With a small pool of 12 unique relics (core.js:240), a 2-option draft gives meaningful agency without letting the player deterministically force the exact same combo every run.
Boss offers 1 of 3 (campaign_run.js:410): Fine. Stepping up from 2 choices on elites to 3 on bosses properly rewards major milestone fights with higher selection quality.
No decline-for-credits option on the relic step (relicStep at :508-518): Real defect (inconsistency + payRelic interaction).
Boons already provide a decline-for-credits button (boons have one; relics do not), and events already define a credit equivalent (+80 credits) when the relic pool is empty (campaign_run.js:356).
Even though no relic has a direct stat downside, forcing the player to take a weak relic interacts perversely with payRelic (splice(RUN.relics.length-1,1)): taking a junk relic after an elite fight acts as an ablative buffer that protects an earlier core relic from payRelic, whereas if a player would rather have salvage credits than a dead relic (coils or max-XP logbook), they are barred from cashing it in.
payRelic removes the most recent relic (campaign_run.js:368, splice(RUN.relics.length-1,1)): Real defect.
Stripping the last element (LIFO) punishes the player based on hidden acquisition order: if a player defeats a boss and selects a build-defining relic, hitting a payRelic event next strips that exact boss reward, whereas picking up a minor relic later shields the boss relic.
payRelic should either let the player choose which held relic to sacrifice, remove a random relic (clearly labeled as a random gamble), or explicitly display the name of RUN.relics[RUN.relics.length-1] on the event choice button so the player makes an informed trade.

f. Naming coverage (RELIC_NAMES and base-name fallback)

The phylactery base-name defect:
The base name of phylactery in data.js:188-201 is "Eclipse backup frame"-which explicitly embeds the Eclipse faction's name.
In RELIC_NAMES (data.js:266-271), Eclipse is the only listed faction that overrides phylactery (renaming it to "Second Ignition"), while Hegemony, Hullbreakers, Chorus, and Glasswrights all omit phylactery (no phylactery) and fall back to "Eclipse backup frame".
As a result, every non-Eclipse faction calls the relic "Eclipse backup frame" while Eclipse itself calls it "Second Ignition". Either the base string and the Eclipse override were swapped during rename_map_final, or non-Eclipse factions lack a faction-appropriate name for a resurrection frame.
(A milder version occurs with "Founders' rangefinder" and "Founders' compass", which carry the "Founders'" proper noun into fallback, including Glasswrights falling back to "Founders' compass".)
General fallback coverage (8 to 11 of 12 per faction):
Outside of phylactery, the missing faction overrides are spore ("Tamed spore culture" - missing in Hegemony, Glasswrights, Eclipse), compass ("Founders' compass" - missing in Glasswrights), and salvage ("Scrap rig" - missing in Glasswrights), plus whatever coverage Sunward Crusade (voidsworn) has (its count is omitted from the pack's coverage list).
Having relicName fall back to neutral base names like "Tamed spore culture" or "Scrap rig" is mechanically fine as a defensive fallback, but represents a flavor opportunity-particularly for The Glasswrights, who only have 8 of 12 custom names (missing spore, phylactery, compass, and salvage) compared to 10-11 for the other factions.

g. The cogitator key collision (RELICS vs. MODULES)

Save validation (keep()):
READ: RELICS (data.js:188-201) and MODULES (data.js:166) are separate objects, and "keep() validation on saves checks relics against RELICS and inventory against MODULES independently (core.js:81)".
INFERRED: Save validation does not confuse the two keys because each array in the save state is validated against its own table.
Display (RELIC_NAMES vs. module names):
READ: Per-faction module display names live at data.js:261-265, while per-faction relic display names live at data.js:266-271 (RELIC_NAMES) and are resolved via relicName.
INFERRED: Display paths do not collide as long as relic UI always calls relicName and module UI always calls the module name helper; however, any generic helper (such as a unified run-summary screen, codex, or tooltip resolver) that looks up an untyped item ID across MODULES and RELICS will collide on "cogitator".
Offers:
READ: randomRelic and the reward choice filter against held relic IDs (core.js:240, campaign_run.js:410).
INFERRED: Offer generation does not confuse the two unless an event or shop script checks a merged ownership list by bare ID.
What would settle it:
Auditing every call site of RELICS, MODULES, relicName, and "cogitator" across the codebase to verify that no function resolves an untyped ID against both tables-or permanently eliminating the collision by renaming the relic key (e.g., rangefinder) with a 1-line save-migration alias in keep() (core.js:81).
