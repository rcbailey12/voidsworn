Overall Verdict: FRICTION - While the pre-shot hover math (attackTip, boonMods) is transparent and rich, overloading left-click on enemy ships to trigger immediate full-salvo fire, blind auto-movement (attackMovePlan), or inspection - combined with zero undo, auto-resetting single-weapon arming, and state bugs - creates severe misclick traps in a 20-ship tactics game.

2. Findings

1. [BLOCKER] Left-clicking an enemy ship conflates inspection, immediate salvo fire, and unpreviewed auto-movement (attackMovePlan) with zero undo

READ: In onBoardClick (ui3d.js 428-431), clicking an enemy while a usable friendly ship is selected (s&&s.side==='player'&&canUse(s)) immediately fires firingSet(s,u). If no weapon currently bears, it checks attackMovePlan(s,u) and immediately moves the ship to am.st and fires (moveUnit(s,am.st,{onLate:...})). If neither succeeds, the first click shows cantFireReason(s,u) and returns, requiring a second click within 3000 ms (G.lastWhy) to reach B.sel=null; B.inspect=u;. Meanwhile, attackTip(a,t) (ui3d.js 491-500) computes hdist(a,t), inArc(a,t,w.arc), and shieldQuad(t,a) from the ship's current hex a, so an out-of-range attack-move displays every weapon as out of range or out of arc, omits expected damage (n===0), and prints "Click to burn ${am.st.cost} movement into position and fire ${am.n} weapon".

INFERRED: In alternating activations (B.alt), guard(s) locks B.activeUnit=u on the first committed action (INFERRED). A player who clicks an enemy on the board intending to inspect its arcs or threat - as taught by "Click an enemy to see its weapons and firing arcs" (ui3d.js 127) - will instead irreversibly activate their selected ship, move it to an unshown hex am.st with unknown shield exposure and unknown hit odds, and dump all bearing weapons (INFERRED). Desktop tactics standards (BattleTech, Into the Breach, XCOM 2) never bind instant move-and-fire commitment to a single unconfirmed left-click on an enemy unit (INFERRED).

2. [BLOCKER] Single-weapon arming (B.armed) prohibits weapon grouping and silently resets to fire-all after one shot

READ: ACT.arm (ui3d.js 524) stores a single weapon index or null (B.armed=B.armed===+i?null:+i), and firingSet (ui3d.js 417) returns [B.armed] when armed or a.weapons.map((w,i)=>i) when null. Immediately after firing in onBoardClick (ui3d.js 428-429), the code runs if(B) B.armed=null;.

INFERRED: Voidsworn's combat mechanics make weapon sequencing critical: weapons carry w.ion ("shreds shields"), w.pierce, w.vsSq>1 ("anti-squadron"), w.cd ("reload"), and boons like s_ion, s_arc ("shorts the generator if this breaks the shield"), and e_kindle ("fire chance per hull hit"). Because B.armed only holds one index and resets to null after firing that weapon, a player who fires slot 1 (e.g., an ion cannon to strip shields) is silently returned to "fire everything" mode (INFERRED). Their next click on an enemy dumps all remaining weapons - wasting long-cooldown torpedoes or anti-squadron guns - unless they manually arm each remaining weapon one by one before every click (INFERRED). BattleTech avoids this by letting players toggle individual weapons on or off in a persistent checklist before committing the salvo (INFERRED).

3. [FIX] bestFacingReach and moveDmgLine auto-orient hexes by raw gun count against an unnamed target, ignoring B.armed and friendly shield facings

READ: bestFacingReach (ui3d.js 393-399) evaluates all states in R.states for each hex k, scoring only ready non-flak weapon count (const sc=n*1000-(st.cost||0)*10-(st.t||0)) and setting b.st.gunTarget to the enemy with the most bearing guns (if(c>tv){ tv=c; tgt=t; }). moveDmgLine (ui3d.js 466-469) calculates expected damage solely against const t=st.gunTarget and outputs "Expected: about ... shield and ... hull damage" without naming t. ACT.arm (ui3d.js 524) does not call updateReach().

INFERRED: This creates three tactical blind spots (INFERRED):

When multiple enemies are near a move hex, "Expected: about X shield and Y hull damage (likely kill)" does not tell the player which enemy ship is being targeted (INFERRED).

Arming a specific weapon (1-6) does not update hex auto-facings to bring that armed weapon to bear (INFERRED).

Scoring facings purely by offensive gun count (n*1000) ignores defensive shield quadrants, readily snapping the ghost ship to expose a collapsed shield or vulnerable rear quadrant ("Rear arc: armor halved, +15% crit") to the enemy fleet (INFERRED).

4. [FIX] striphint falsely announces "Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship." after moving 1 hex when no target is in range

READ: In actionStrip (ui3d.js 667-669), moved is evaluated as moved=u.mp<u.speed and canHit as OV.targets&&OV.targets.length>0. When canHit is false and moved is true, striphint displays 'Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship.'.

INFERRED: u.mp < u.speed is true the moment a ship spends 1 MP, even if it still has u.mp > 0 remaining (INFERRED). Furthermore, a ship that moved and cannot fire is the exact ship that should Brace ("Use it on a ship that can't shoot anything useful or is about to take a beating", ui3d.js 683) or use an Ability, Command, or Wings stance. Telling the player "Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship." actively misleads them into skipping a -40% hull damage reduction (Brace) and stranding remaining movement (INFERRED).

5. [FIX] B.wingSel is never cleared by cancelAction (Esc), selectUnit, or ACT.rosterPick, creating a sticky order trap

READ: Clicking a friendly squadron in onBoardClick (ui3d.js 426) sets B.wingSel=B.wingSel===u?null:u, and line 425 intercepts any subsequent board click on a non-squadron ship (u&&u!==w&&!(u.side==='player'&&u.isSq)) to assign w.wingOrder (strike or guard) and return early. Neither cancelAction (ui3d.js 402), selectUnit (ui3d.js 391), nor ACT.rosterPick (ui3d.js 112) resets B.wingSel=null.

INFERRED: If a player selects a wing on the board, changes their mind, and presses Esc (or clicks a friendly ship in the roster), B.wingSel remains armed (INFERRED). Their next click on a friendly ship on the board to select it - or on an enemy ship to fire - is hijacked by if(B.wingSel) at line 425, reassigning the wing's target instead of executing the ship selection or attack (INFERRED).

6. [FIX] Turning in place (Q/E) immediately commits the ship's activation (guard(s)) without a ghost preview and lacks mouse controls on actionStrip

READ: In onKey (ui3d.js 518-519), pressing Q or E executes if(guard(s)) act(()=>rotateUnit(s,-1)) (or +1), where guard(u) (ui3d.js 401) locks B.activeUnit=u in alternating mode. Although ACT.rot is defined (ui3d.js 525), actionStrip (ui3d.js 665-670) contains no turn buttons.

INFERRED: A player cannot preview whether turning 60 degrees in place will bring an enemy into a broadside or prow arc without pressing Q/E, which immediately spends the turn step and locks B.activeUnit (INFERRED). Additionally, mouse-centric players have no way to rotate in place from actionStrip (INFERRED). In Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader and Battlestar Galactica Deadlock, rotation and movement are previewed as a ghost state before execution (INFERRED).

7. [FIX] Done with ship and Enter end activations immediately without warning when a stationary ship hasn't braced or ready weapons haven't fired

READ: TIPS (ui3d.js 645) states "a ship that ends its activation in the same hex is easier to hit (enemies get +10)" and "Bracing cancels the sitting-still penalty". Done with ship is the first .primary button on actionStrip (ui3d.js 670), and Enter triggers ACT.endTurn() directly (ui3d.js 514) with no confirmation.

INFERRED: While Admiral Command uses a 6-second two-tap confirmation (B.cmdArm, ui3d.js 671) to prevent misclicks, End activation (Enter / Done with ship) has zero guardrails even when the active ship is sitting still without bracing (incurring +10 enemy hit chance for zero benefit) or still has bearing weapons ready to fire (INFERRED). Both Into the Breach and BattleTech warn or prompt when ending a unit's turn with unused actions (INFERRED).

8. [FIX] Camera save/restore (camKeep/camRestore) and camShow snap coordinates instantly, while Tab cycles ships in unsorted array order

READ: camShow (ui3d.js 680) teleports the camera target 75% toward off-screen enemies (G.ctl.t=new V3(t.x+(p.x-t.x)*.75,0,t.z+(p.z-t.z)*.75); G.ctl.attract=false;), and camRestore (ui3d.js 679) overwrites G.ctl.t, G.ctl.r, G.ctl.a, and G.ctl.b directly. nextShip (ui3d.js 523) iterates alive('player') in array order and calls G.ctl.focus(uWorld(pool[i])).

INFERRED: In a 20-ship alternating-activation battle, snapping the camera to off-screen enemies and snapping back to camKeepView on every single activation produces up to 40 hard camera cuts per round (INFERRED). Meanwhile, pressing Tab jumps the camera across the map in array/spawn order rather than spatial hex order (INFERRED).

9. [NOTE] The Threat Map (R) and enemy threat text hide per-enemy attribution and activation state

READ: The threat map (R, ui3d.js 650) shades hexes enemy guns can reach next turn, "the darker the hex, the more damage is waiting there". Inspecting an enemy ship adds "Next round it" or "Its next move can bring guns to bear on..." (ui3d.js 130).

INFERRED: Aggregate hex darkness does not tell the player which enemies threaten a hex, whether those enemies have already activated this round (!u.activated vs u.activated in B.alt), or which shield facing of the player's ghost ship will take that incoming fire (INFERRED). Into the Breach and XCOM 2 make threat readable by showing explicit target lines/icons per enemy (INFERRED).

10. [NOTE] Half of the keyboard bindings are missing from keysHelp() and actionStrip, while a PC-only game hardcodes mobile "Tap" copy

READ: The Commissioner states "Voidsworn is a desktop-only (PC) roguelite fleet-tactics game". Yet onBoardClick (ui3d.js 425-426), striphint (ui3d.js 669), and stripHelp (ui3d.js 683) use "Tap a gold hex", "Tap a red-ringed enemy", and "Tap Done with ship". Meanwhile, onKey (ui3d.js 506-517) binds R (threat), X (ram), G (board), F (focus), T (top view), and Home (reset), none of which appear in keysHelp() (ui3d.js 161) or on actionStrip buttons (ui3d.js 670). (Note: the Chair Census claims R threat is in keysHelp, line 161, but line 161 omits R.)

INFERRED: Desktop tactics players expect "Click" terminology and inline <kbd> badges on action buttons (just as weaponLine renders <kbd>${i+1}</kbd> at line 124) (INFERRED). Omitting <kbd>X</kbd>, <kbd>G</kbd>, <kbd>B</kbd>, <kbd>Enter</kbd>, and <kbd>R</kbd> from the strip and help bar hides core shortcuts (INFERRED).

3. Explicit checks

1. SELECTION MODEL

One-ship selection and click-enemy-to-fire-everything at 20 ships:

One-ship-at-a-time selection (selectUnit, ui3d.js 391) is the right foundation for alternating single-ship activations, matching BattleTech, Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader, and XCOM 2 (INFERRED).

However, click-enemy-to-fire-everything (and click-enemy-to-attack-move-and-fire) is the wrong default for a 20-ship hex tactics game with no undo (INFERRED). In real-time-with-pause fleet RTS games like Sins of a Solar Empire 2, right-clicking an enemy to focus-fire or auto-move works because movement is continuous, reversible at any frame, and individual weapons auto-cycle on independent cooldowns (INFERRED). In a turn-based hex game where activations lock in via guard(s) (ui3d.js 401), firing locks out Brace (ui3d.js 683), stationary ships suffer +10 enemy hit chance (ui3d.js 645), and shield-stripping order matters (w.ion, s_arc), binding an irreversible move-and-alpha-strike to a single left-click on an enemy invites catastrophic misclicks (INFERRED).

The inspect-vs-fire split:

Making the exact same left-click on an enemy ship mean Fire, Auto-Move + Fire, Error Toast (cantFireReason), or Inspect depending on B.sel, canUse(B.sel), firingSet, attackMovePlan, and a 3-second G.lastWhy timer (ui3d.js 428-431) fails basic modal clarity (INFERRED).

Even worse, clicking an enemy in the roster (ACT.rosterPick, ui3d.js 112) unconditionally deselects your ship and inspects the enemy (B.sel=null; B.inspect=u), whereas clicking that same enemy on the 3D board fires or auto-moves (ui3d.js 428-429).

In BattleTech, XCOM 2, and Rogue Trader, inspecting an enemy or previewing a target never fires a shot until the player confirms the attack or clicks an explicit weapon/ability icon (INFERRED).

2. ARMING

Arm-one-weapon (1-6, weapon rows) vs. fire-all toggle:

Voidsworn's B.armed model (ui3d.js 417, 524) is an all-or-one toggle that resets to null (all) after every shot (ui3d.js 428-429).

Compared with the field, this is strictly worse than both BattleTech's salvo checklist and Rogue Trader's / FTL's per-weapon targeting (INFERRED):

In BattleTech (2018), all ready weapons default to enabled (like Voidsworn's fire-all convenience), but clicking weapon rows or pressing number keys toggles individual weapons on or off within the current salvo before clicking a dedicated Fire button; called shots and multi-target splits are explicit modes (INFERRED).

In Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader (voidship battles) and FTL: Faster Than Light, each weapon or battery slot is activated individually (1-6) and aimed at its target, because firing order (shield-breaker macro-cannons/ions before lance crits/beams) is the heart of combat (INFERRED).

Voidsworn tries to combine Sins 2 one-click alpha-strikes with FTL/Rogue Trader shield-gate synergies (w.ion, s_ion, s_arc, e_kindle), resulting in a UI where firing two shield-breakers followed by a hull-breaker requires arming slot 1 -> clicking target -> re-arming slot 2 (because B.armed reset to null) -> clicking target -> clicking target for remaining weapons (INFERRED). Replacing B.armed (single integer) with a persistent boolean mask of armed weapons - or keeping B.armed selected until all weapons are spent or Esc is pressed - would fix this immediately (INFERRED).

3. INFORMATION

Comparison against Into the Breach, BattleTech, and FTL:

Voidsworn's hover telemetry (attackTip and boonMods, ui3d.js 475-500) is closest to BattleTech's odds breakdown enhanced with expected-value math (INFERRED). Showing exact hit percentage (hitChance), shots x damage, which target shield quadrant is struck (QUAD_NAME[quad]), which hull section lies underneath (SEC_NAME[SEC_OF_QUAD[quad]]), active roguelite boons (boonMods), and expected shield/hull damage (expectedVs) with a Likely kill flag is genuinely superior to BattleTech, which hides expected damage math and active perk modifiers (INFERRED).

What is still hidden that the comparators show:

Attack-move preview (attackMovePlan): When clicking an out-of-range enemy triggers attackMovePlan (ui3d.js 429, 500), the UI hides the destination hex am.st, the resulting ship facing, the per-weapon hit chances from am.st, and the expected damage, whereas XCOM 2, BattleTech, and Star Wars: Zero Company always preview the exact firing tile and shot odds before committing a move-and-shoot order (INFERRED).

Target identity on move hover (moveDmgLine): Hovering a gold move hex displays "Expected: about X shield and Y hull damage" (ui3d.js 468) without naming st.gunTarget or drawing a target line on the board, unlike BattleTech and Into the Breach, which draw explicit targeting arcs/lines from the ghost position to every valid target (INFERRED).

Enemy intent and activation order (Into the Breach / XCOM 2 standard): While Voidsworn marks a single B.aiFocus ship ("The enemy fleet is focusing its fire on this ship", ui3d.js 119, 130) and shades aggregate threat hexes (R), it hides which unactivated enemy ships can hit the hovered ghost hex and which shield quadrant of your ghost ship they will strike (INFERRED).

Enemy weapon cooldowns and sub-system status on the board (FTL standard): While unitTip (ui3d.js 472-474) shows enemy hull, 4 shield facings, armor, evasion, and section HP on hover, seeing an enemy's ready vs. reloading weapons requires inspecting the enemy (refreshPanel, ui3d.js 122-125) - which is blocked on the board whenever a usable friendly ship is selected (ui3d.js 428-431) (INFERRED).

4. ERROR RECOVERY

Judging no-undo, Esc layering, two-tap Command confirm, and Tab cycling:

No-undo on movement (moveUnit) and rotation (Q/E): In deterministic or RNG tactics games (BattleTech, Into the Breach, Rogue Trader), movement orders either use a two-step preview-then-confirm (BattleTech lets you click a move hex and rotate your ghost facing freely before clicking Confirm Move) or allow free movement undo until a weapon is fired or new information is revealed (Into the Breach) (INFERRED). Voidsworn has neither: a single click on a hex (or pressing Q/E) immediately executes an irreversible move/turn animation and locks B.activeUnit (ui3d.js 401, 518-519) (INFERRED).

Esc layering (cancelAction, ui3d.js 402): Layering B.mode -> B.armed -> B.sel/B.inspect is clean in principle, but incomplete in code: cancelAction() fails to clear B.wingSel (ui3d.js 425-426) and B.cmdArm (ui3d.js 671). Furthermore, the Chair Census mentions "right-click cancel" (WHAT TO JUDGE item 4), which is absent from the provided ui3d.js slices; if right-click only cancels or orbits the camera, it is not being used to separate movement/attack commands from left-click selection (INFERRED).

Two-tap Command confirm (ui3d.js 671): Using an inline 6-second countdown button ("Tap again (6s): ...") for a once-per-battle ability is sleek and avoids a blocking modal, though Esc and ship deselection should disarm B.cmdArm (INFERRED).

Tab cycling (nextShip, ui3d.js 523): Filtering pool to ready (!isDone(u)) ships first is smart, except when B.alt && B.activeUnit is already locked - at which point cycling Tab to other ready ships that guard(u) will reject ("Finish the active ship first", ui3d.js 401) adds friction unless the UI clearly distinguishes the locked activeUnit (INFERRED).

What to adopt vs. refuse:

Adopt: Two-step move/heading confirmation (click hex to place ghost and adjust facing with mouse/Q/E, click again or press Confirm to move - just like BattleTech), OR free undo of movement/rotation before firing a weapon (since Voidsworn has no fog of war mentioned in the pack) (INFERRED). Also adopt a confirmation/auto-brace prompt when clicking Done with ship / Enter on an unbraced stationary ship (INFERRED).

Refuse: Never allow undo after a weapon fires (fireWeapon) or a boarding/ram roll resolves, preserving roguelite shot commitment (INFERRED).

5. CAMERA AND SCALE

Judging camKeep/camRestore, camShow (pan-without-zoom), and opening zoom against Sins 2 and BSG Deadlock at 20 ships + wings:

What works: The principle "never zoom on its own, only pan when something important is off screen, and hand your view back" (ui3d.js 677) and respecting manual camera movement during the enemy turn (if((G.camMoved||0)>v.at) return;, ui3d.js 679) is far better than XCOM 2's or BattleTech's intrusive action cameras that hijack zoom and pitch on every shot (INFERRED). The opening full-board zoom-out-then-in establishes fleet context well.

Where it fights selection at 20 ships + wings:

Instant snaps instead of smooth transitions: camShow (ui3d.js 680) and camRestore (ui3d.js 679) assign G.ctl.t directly with G.ctl.attract=false. Across 20 alternating activations, snapping back and forth between the player's saved view and off-screen enemy activations causes spatial disorientation (INFERRED).

Lack of Sins 2 cursor-anchored zoom and tactical icon scaling (as far as shown): At 20 capital ships plus fighter/bomber squadrons (ws=squadronsOf(u)), Sins of a Solar Empire 2 and BSG Deadlock keep the board readable by letting the camera stay zoomed out while projecting crisp floating ship class icons, health/shield pips, and order lines in screen space (INFERRED). In Voidsworn, ACT.rosterPick (ui3d.js 112), nextShip (ui3d.js 523), and ACT.center (ui3d.js 681) pull the camera in (G.ctl.focus(uWorld(u), Math.min(G.ctl.r, 150))), forcing the player into a local view where off-screen enemy shots require constant camShow jumps (INFERRED).

6. COMPARATOR VERDICTS

BattleTech (2018): Steal its two-step move+facing preview (place ghost on hex, then set exact facing while seeing target lines to every enemy in arc) and its per-weapon salvo toggle checklist with a dedicated Fire button; avoid its sluggish post-action camera pauses and hidden active-modifier math (INFERRED).

Into the Breach: Steal its pre-shot movement undo (reset move before firing) and explicit enemy-to-target threat lines showing which enemy attacks where next; avoid assuming its 3-unit, zero-RNG minimalism scales directly to 20 multi-weapon ships without weapon-group controls (INFERRED).

FTL: Faster Than Light: Steal its persistent per-weapon arming/queuing (1-6 stay armed or queued until explicitly disarmed so shield-breaker-then-hull-breaker timing is effortless) and floating target subsystem/shield readouts; avoid its lack of spatial movement previews (INFERRED).

Battlestar Galactica Deadlock: Steal its 3D floating munition/threat vectors, firing-arc cones projected from the ghost ship during rotation preview, and clean fleet roster status pips at 20-ship scale; avoid its simultaneous we-go timeline scrubbing, which does not fit alternating single-ship activations (INFERRED).

Sins of a Solar Empire 2: Steal its effortless zoomed-out fleet readability (crisp screen-space tactical icons, shield/hull bars, and cursor-centered zoom so you rarely need the camera to auto-pan); avoid its RTS single-click-enemy-to-auto-move-and-fire binding, which causes fatal misclicks in a no-undo turn-based hex game (INFERRED).

Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader (voidship battles): Steal its arc-based weapon slot bar where clicking a weapon highlights only its valid firing arc and targets, plus its clear preview of which of your own 4 shield facings will face the enemy at a destination hex; avoid its bloated ability bar clutter of minor crew passives (INFERRED).

Star Wars: Zero Company: Steal its modern turn-based tactics standard of previewing the complete move-and-attack outcome (destination tile, cover/shield exposure, and shot percentages) before committing an attack-move; avoid tight cinematic over-the-shoulder cameras that obscure surrounding hex threat in a 20-unit battle (INFERRED).

XCOM 2: Steal its target-cycling heads-up icons (Tab or clickable enemy target portraits showing hit % from the currently hovered move hex) and its explicit warning popup when ending a turn with unspent actions; avoid its notorious camera rubber-banding and lack of a pre-shot move undo (INFERRED).

The single change that would most improve this game's battle UI:
Decouple inspection/targeting from instant execution and remove unpreviewed attackMovePlan on enemy click (INFERRED). Clicking an enemy should target/inspect it (locking the attackTip comparison panel and allowing the player to toggle which weapons 1-6 are included in the salvo without auto-resetting after one gun), with a dedicated confirm action (second click on the locked target, Fire button, or Space/F) to fire - and require players to click a gold move hex (where they can see their ghost facing and expected damage) rather than silently auto-moving via attackMovePlan when clicking an out-of-range enemy (INFERRED).

The one thing Voidsworn already does better than the field:
Pre-shot causal math transparency (attackTip + boonMods, ui3d.js 475-500) (INFERRED). No comparator in this set combines exact hit odds, per-facing shield soak vs. hull bleed-through, underlying hull section hit (Prow/Midships/Engines), terrain/motion modifiers (Sitting still, Raking pass, nebula), expected shield/hull damage (expectedVs), and a live list of every triggered roguelite patron boon (boonMods) in a single, scannable hover card (INFERRED).

5. Questions for the designer

Is attackMovePlan (ui3d.js 429) meant to be a convenience shortcut, or a core mechanic ("fire on the way" / raking pass)?

Stakes: If it is just a shortcut, removing it (or making it preview the destination hex first) eliminates the #1 misclick trap in the game; if mid-move firing requires attackMovePlan, then hovering an out-of-range enemy must render the am.st ghost path and real expectedVs odds before the click commits.

In alternating mode (B.alt), why does moving or turning in place (Q/E) immediately lock B.activeUnit without a ghost preview or pre-shot undo?

Stakes: If no hidden information is revealed by moving or rotating a ship, allowing the player to preview facing changes - or undo movement before firing a weapon - removes all positioning misclicks without weakening tactical commitment.

How often do players need to sequence weapons (e.g., w.ion / s_ion shield-breakers before e_kindle / crit hull weapons) or split fire across two targets in a single activation?

Stakes: If weapon sequencing and target splitting are central to high-level play, the current single-slot B.armed integer that resets to null after each shot (ui3d.js 428, 524) fights the player on every salvo and should be replaced with a multi-weapon toggle checklist.
