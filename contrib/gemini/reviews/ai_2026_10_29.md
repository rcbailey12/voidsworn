2. Findings

[BLOCKER] midFire (onLate) short-circuits aiUnit, making the veteran pre-fire brace check dead code whenever an enemy ship moves

READ: In aiUnit (battle.js 1380-1382), whenever best && best.path.length is truthy and midFire is true (!u.isSq&&!runner&&!(u.ab==='nova'&&canAbility(u)) and not NOMIDFIRE), moveUnit executes onLate, which assigns volleyP (running aiAbilities(u,live,'aim') and aiVolley(u,live)), awaits volleyP and aiAfterFire, and executes return; on line 1382.

READ: The veteran defensive brace decision (if(aiVet()&&!u.isSq&&!u.t.boss&&canOrder(u,'brace')) when "thr>u.hp*.45&&myOff<thr*.35&&u.hp<u.maxHp*.7") sits on line 1395, after line 1382's return;.

INFERRED: In live browser play (where NOMIDFIRE is not set), any veteran ship that moves even 1 hex and has no adjacent boarding target fires its volley mid-move via onLate and exits on line 1382, never reaching line 1395. Even the fallback brace inside line 1382 ("if(!fired && !u.dead && u.hp<u.maxHp*.5...") is blocked if aiVolley fires a single scratch shot. Moreover, because process.env.NOMIDFIRE disables midFire in headless simulation runs, simulation win-rate data tests a different AI control flow than the shipped browser build.

[BLOCKER] The shared board ceiling (RAMP.ceil) collides with swarm and RAMP.light weights to silently discard enemy budget against 6-7 ship player fleets

READ: genEnemyFleet scales budget up to RAMP.pwMax (1.3) when player fleetPower() is high (battle.js 135, 144), then computes the enemy ship cap as const ceil=RAMP.ceil[s]-((typeof RUN!=='undefined'&&RUN&&RUN.fleet)?Math.min(7,RUN.fleet.filter(sh=>sh.hp>0).length):4); and halts purchasing when list.length<Math.max(4,ceil) (battle.js 135, 150, 155-156).

INFERRED: In a swarm node (only 1-2 CP hulls) or a default node heavily weighted toward 1-2 CP hulls, the while loop hits list.length === Math.max(4, ceil) after buying 4 to 7 cheap hulls and terminates with substantial unspent budget left on the table. Expanding the player's fleet count actively starves enemy swarm and light-weighted nodes of their budget by consuming the shared board cap.

[FIX] Bidirectional power feedback (B2, 0.75 to 1.3) directly contradicts fixed-column progression and taxes player recovery

READ: Line 140 states "each map column has a fixed difficulty, so a fleet that grows can outgrow it (the point of buying that cruiser)", while lines 143-144 immediately scale budget by Math.max(RAMP.pwMin,Math.min(RAMP.pwMax,Math.pow(Math.max(.5,fleetPower())/RAMP.refP[s],RAMP.pw))) with pw:.6, pwMin:.75, pwMax:1.3.

INFERRED: Assuming fleetPower() reflects current surviving/upgraded fleet strength, a battered player at ≤62% of refP (0.619^0.6 = 0.75) receives a 25% budget discount, but spending scrap to repair or buy a cruiser can swing the next node's budget multiplier from 0.75 up to 1.3 - up to a +73.3% jump in enemy budget that taxes away 60% of incremental power gains (pw: 0.6) and punishes spending resources before a fight.

[FIX] The off <= 0 movement fallback (battle.js 1372) overrides caution (aiHurtW) and forces disarmed or retreating ships to charge toward minR

READ: Line 1359 sets const minR=Math.min(...u.weapons.map(w=>w.opt)); and line 1372 applies if(off<=0){ ... score-=Math.max(0,nd-minR)*4; }.

Because -4 per hex outweighs threat * tw in most low-to-moderate threat gradients, a ship whose guns are reloading or whose firing arcs face forward cannot kite or retreat; it is pulled toward minR.

3. Explicit Checks

1. COMPOSITION

Budget builder & archetype readability: genEnemyFleet produces readable intent for heavy and swarm, but fails on carrier and default (null) fleets.

swarm (battle.js 150) filters roster to x.cp<=mn+1&&!x.car (only the lightest 1-2 CP non-carrier hulls), producing a clean light swarm - unless the player has 6-7 ships in Sector 1-2, where ceil (RAMP.ceil[s] - playerShips) prematurely cuts off the loop at 4-7 ships and discards the rest of the budget.

heavy (battle.js 151, 158) seeds one highest-CP hull (top), strips all but one <2 CP hull from roster, and weights subsequent picks by Math.pow(x.cp, 1.6), which reliably builds a top-heavy line.

carrier (battle.js 152) buys only one carrier hull (cr), deletes all other carriers from roster (roster=roster.filter(x=>!x.car)), and hands the remaining budget to the default weight formula (x.cp===1?1.1:Math.min(2,budget/(x.cp*2))+.35)*(x.cp<=2&&arch!=='swarm'?RAMP.light[s]:1) (battle.js 158). Because RAMP.light[s] multiplies 1-2 CP hull weights by 4 (Sectors 1-2) or 5 (Sector 3), both carrier escorts and un-archetyped fleets degenerate into 1-2 CP light-hull soup.

The thinning factor (RAMP.thin): As noted in Finding 10, RAMP.thin:[0,.73,.675,.62] does not mean "fewer, bigger ships". Per battle.js 131-135, RAMP fields more, lighter ships (ceil:[0,10,14,20], light:[0,4,4,5]) while thin reduces per-ship toughness to 73%, 67.5%, and 62% across Sectors 1-3 so total EHP tracks the old curve. It is the wrong lever for late-game tactics: flooding Sector 3 with up to 13-16 softened 1-2 CP hulls dilutes hull identity, turns late fights into cleanup chores, and crowds the hex board rather than presenting distinct capital-ship threats.

2. POWER FEEDBACK

Who it helps and who it punishes: The B2 power feedback (clamp((Math.max(.5,fleetPower())/RAMP.refP[s])^0.6, 0.75, 1.3), battle.js 135, 144) helps a fleet sitting near 62% of RAMP.refP[s] ([0, 7, 12, 20]), where (0.619)^0.6 = 0.75 grants the full 25% enemy budget discount while the fleet is still functional. It punishes:

Players who upgrade or snow-ball ahead of the curve (up to 1.55 * refP, where 1.548^0.6 = 1.3), taxing 60% of their power growth by inflating enemy budgets - including boss escort budgets - by up to +30%, directly violating line 140's stated goal ("each map column has a fixed difficulty, so a fleet that grows can outgrow it (the point of buying that cruiser)").

Players recovering from a bad fight (going from 0.75x when battered back to 1.0x-1.3x after repairing/buying hulls) and truly crippled players below 50% of refP, for whom the 0.75 floor stops scaling down while their fleet cannot beat even a 0.75x node.

Is the clamp range (0.75 to 1.3) honest? No. A 0.75 to 1.3 band is a 1.73x total swing (1.3 / 0.75) hidden behind the map. In a roguelite designed for an 80% loss rate where Run 1 should be near impossible, scaling enemy budgets up to 1.3x when the player builds a strong fleet turns earned power spikes into longer wars of attrition, while the 0.75x pity discount masks early mistakes until the player hits a boss base hull (F.boss is added before budget scaling on line 142).

3. BATTLE DECISIONS

One-line rules from aiTurn (battle.js 1311-1402):

Targeting rule: In aiUnit, movement scores candidate (hex, facing) states using evalOffenseVet(v,ready,foes,focus) (with sticky fleet focus focus0 = aiRefreshFocus() in Veteran mode) or evalOffense(v,ready,foes) (Standard), falls back to BFS travel distance (pathField) toward the nearest non-squadron foe when off <= 0, and delegates actual weapon fire to aiVolley(u,live) (plus tacticRam, tacticBoard, and nova when net AoE score bv >= 40).

Ability rule: aiAbilities(u,live,mode) triggers abilities greedily in three passes - 'pre' before moving (launch, surge, evasive/phase, reload, regrow, reclaim, ionpulse, overburn, blink), 'aim' after moving right before firing (focus/frenzy when a ready gun can fire, and reload when a spent torpedo tube has a target in reach), and 'escape' after firing (blink when HP < 35% and threat > 30% HP) - plus nova and brace in aiUnit.

Retreat rule: Non-objective ships never retreat off-board; instead, they increase threat aversion tw as HP drops from 60% to 30% via a smoothstep curve with hysteresis up to 45% HP (aiHurtW, lines 1339-1340, Veteran non-flagships only), apply a -25 score cliff when threat >= u.hp (line 1367), step to a lower-threat hex post-fire via fadeMove if threat >= 5 can be cut by ≥25% (lines 1342-1345), or blink away in 'escape' mode when < 35% HP (line 1335).

Most exploitable habit: The off <= 0 approach leash combined with midFire skipping Veteran brace (plus squadron cooldown baiting).

Proof: On line 1372 (if(off<=0){ ... score-=Math.max(0,nd-minR)*4; }), any ship whose weapons are on cooldown (ready empty ⟹ off=0) or whose firing arcs point away when turning to flee gets hit with a -4 per hex penalty for moving farther than minR (Math.min(...u.weapons.map(w=>w.opt)), line 1359), forcing it to close toward the player instead of kiting or retreating. Compounding this, line 1331 (if(pre&&(u.ab==='evasive'||u.ab==='phase') && canAbility(u) && foes.some(f=>hdist(u,f)<=5))) omits !f.isSq, letting a player squadron within 5 hexes bait out evasive/phase before move planning, and lines 1380-1382 (if(volleyP){ ... return; }) exit before the Veteran brace check on line 1395 whenever the ship moves at least 1 hex.

Smartest behavior: Obstacle-aware multi-factor hex-state scoring (pathField + states loop) paired with hysteresis caution (aiHurtW), lethal-hex cliff, torpedo-lane evasion, and fadeMove.

Proof: Lines 1319-1322 (pathField / fieldDist) run a true BFS around terrain obstacles (blocks(terr(n.q,n.r))) so ships are not baited into rock walls or black holes. Lines 1339-1340 (aiHurtW) use smoothstep interpolation (x=k*k*(3-2*k)) with a 30%/45% HP latch (if(f<.3) u.aiHurt=true; else if(f>.45) u.aiHurt=false;) so damaged ships do not oscillate between charging and fleeing after minor heals/regen. Lines 1363-1375 combine offense, threat, a -25 lethal-damage cliff (if(vet&&!u.isSq&&threat>=u.hp) score-=25;), torpedo lane avoidance (score-=torpThreat(u,v)*.9;), and flagship escort spacing (escIn>=2?.6:2 and -1 for adjacent stacking, lines 1361, 1370), followed by fadeMove (lines 1342-1345) stepping out of incoming fire/torpedo lanes after shooting if threat drops by ≥25% (bv<=here*.75).

4. WINGS AND SPECIALS

Launch: Used automatically and reflexively. In aiAbilities 'pre' (line 1326), a carrier with canAbility(u) always launches immediately into the valid launch hex closest to any player unit (launchHexes(u).sort((a,b)=>Math.min(...foes.map(f=>hdist(a,f)))-Math.min(...foes.map(f=>hdist(b,f))))[0]), without checking threat to the spawned wing or waiting for the squadron phase (which already ran on line 1313).

Wing stances (and ship stances/orders other than brace): Effectively player-only in aiTurn. Despite the Chair Census stating aiTurn drives "stance", launch passes only {hex:hs[0]} (battle.js 1326), squadron activations immediately exit to await sqAct(u); return; (battle.js 1348), and the only order aiUnit ever issues to any ship is giveOrder(u,'brace') (battle.js 1382, 1395, 1399). Unless sqAct (outside this pack) switches wing stances internally, wing stances and all non-brace ship orders are unused by aiTurn.

Torpedoes: Used defensively like a veteran, offensively like a blunt instrument. Defensively, the AI actively dodges player torpedo lanes during both main movement (score-=torpThreat(u,v)*.9, line 1374) and post-fire fadeMove (evalThreat(v,foes)+torpThreat(u,v), lines 1343-1344). Offensively, aiUnit has no torpedo-lane setup or lead-positioning logic in its movement loop, and its 'aim' torpedo reload check (w.k==='torp'&&w.off===0&&!weaponReady(w)&&foes.some(f=>!f.isSq&&inReach(u,w,f)), line 1332) is preempted by the 'pre' reload check firing on raw distance (<=6+(u.speed||2)) before moving.

Gravity well: Used only passively. The AI routes around impassable hole hexes via pathField (blocks(terr(n.q,n.r)), line 1320) and adds score+=aiWellScore(u,v); (line 1376) when choosing its own destination hex, but has zero logic to knock, ram, or herd player ships into the gravity well or to account for gravity-well pull when launching wings (line 1326) or firing.

5. DIFFICULTY TEXTURE

Where Sector 3 difficulty actually comes from: From RAMP (battle.js 135, 141-162), Sector 3 difficulty comes overwhelmingly from board-filling light-hull volume (escort/chaff bloat) rather than individual ship quality or sharper AI lethality:

Base budget rises from 4.2 + 0.9*col (S1) to 14 + 1.1*col (S3), while RAMP.grow barely changes across sectors (1.2 → 1.24 → 1.26, only a +5% multiplier increase from S1 to S3).

Meanwhile, RAMP.ceil doubles from 10 (S1) to 20 (S3), RAMP.light[3] weights 1-2 CP hulls by 5x, RAMP.boss[3] gives the Sector 3 boss an escort budget equal to 53% of a full Sector 3 node (scaled up to +30% higher by B2 if the player is strong), and RAMP.thin[3] cuts per-ship toughness to 0.62.

Why this produces bosses that measure "long rather than lethal": In Sector 3 boss fights, the player faces a boss hull (F.boss) surrounded by up to 12-15 thinned (0.62), light (RAMP.light[3] = 5) escort ships that ring the boss (escIn within 3 hexes, lines 1361, 1370), soak player action economy and torpedo lanes, and trigger defensive survival tools (aiHurtW caution, -25 death cliff, fadeMove, surge, regrow, reclaim, brace) as they take damage. Because the Oct 27 tuning (battle.js 135) cut Sector 3 grow (1.35 → 1.26), thin (.64 → .62), and boss escorts (.65 → .53), the escort swarm lacks the concentrated firepower to kill a prepared player fleet quickly, but still has enough hull count and defensive AI caution to drag the fight out for many turns.

5. Questions for the Designer

Was midFire (onLate in battle.js 1380-1382) intended to skip the Veteran pre-fire brace check on line 1395, and do your campaign simulations run with process.env.NOMIDFIRE enabled?

Stakes: If sims run with NOMIDFIRE=1 while browser players run with midFire=true, your 20% win-rate tuning was calibrated against an AI that braces under heavy fire in sims but almost never does so in the actual game.

When a strong 6-7 ship player fleet shrinks ceil (RAMP.ceil[s] - playerShips), should genEnemyFleet upgrade remaining picks to higher-CP hulls instead of exiting the while loop with unspent budget?

Stakes: Right now, B2 increases the enemy's paper budget against a 7-ship player fleet while ceil silently throws that budget away on swarm and light-weighted nodes, inverting the intended difficulty curve.

Why does Sector 3 scale difficulty via RAMP.light[3] = 5, RAMP.ceil[3] = 20, and RAMP.thin[3] = 0.62 (more, weaker 1-2 CP hulls) instead of lowering RAMP.light so late-sector budgets buy cruisers and battlecruisers?

Stakes: Shifting Sector 3 (and carrier escorts) from 5x-weighted 1-2 CP chaff toward fewer, full-strength capital hulls would directly fix bosses feeling "long rather than lethal" without lowering the 80% loss target.
