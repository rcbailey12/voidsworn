## The pick: B, unanimous

Every seated leg picked B, including the blind leg written before
any model ran, and B is also the builder's pick. The second
opinion confirms it, with changes below that all four legs
converged on in some form.

## 1. Why B (chaired)

B is the only option with an element that means boarding
specifically: the three amber lines tying the attacker's hatch to
the breach. A's bigger breach, ring and plume make the moment
visible but keep it in the hit and explosion category; a player
who learns A learns "something struck that ship". C's extra
ingredient, pops spreading through the hull, slides the read back
toward internal damage and costs the longest hold for the least
legible element. B's connection between the two ships can be
learned once and never misread, and amber is already boarding's
colour (the target ring), so the eye groups the lines correctly.

## 2. Does each read as boarding at camera 170?

- r0 (today): No, unanimous. A pinprick dot and a glow; on the
  real-ship capture the crew numbers do all the storytelling.
  Reads as a minor hit, and the ship carries no mark afterwards.
- A: No (blind Rey, Hydrogen), Unsure (DeepSeek, Qwen). The ring
  and burst are clear at 170, but with no tether to the attacker
  the first glance is a heavy shell or torpedo hit.
- B: Yes, unanimous.
- C: Split. DeepSeek and Qwen say yes on the strength of the
  tether, with the pops reading as damage or sparkle noise;
  blind Rey and Hydrogen say unsure, because the pops pull the
  read toward "boarding, then something blew up inside". Chaired:
  C reads as boarding only because it contains B; its own
  addition argues against it.

## 3. Holds

B's 1.6 s is right (blind Rey, DeepSeek, Qwen). Hydrogen would
trim to 1.2 to 1.4 s on the ground that B's story is told by
+0.8 s; the chair's answer is that this is true only because the
lines currently fade early, and with the lines kept alive for
the whole hold (change 1 below) the 1.6 s earns its time. A's
1.4 s is acceptable. C's 1.9 s is too long, unanimous: for an
event that fires a few times a battle, the extra third of a
second buys the least legible part of any option.

## 4. Changes to B the council converged on

1. **Keep the lines alive and moving for the whole hold.** On the
   cam 170 sheet they have faded by +1.6 s, leaving only the ring
   and vent, which is A's hit language again. Stagger the lines
   in (about 0.2 s apart, Qwen), pulse them once mid-hold so they
   read as crew crossing, not a static tether (all four legs).
2. **Fan the lines out.** Three lines converging on one dot, or
   bunched into one thick stub across the short gap, can read as
   a beam or cutting lance. Land them at three points around the
   breach, spread along the facing hull edges, so they read as
   grapples securing the two ships (blind Rey, Hydrogen).
3. **Sharpen the breach, and let it stay.** On light hulls the
   warm glow and pale vent wash the hatch mark out (Hydrogen,
   Qwen: darken the centre, scorched high-contrast rim). The
   blind leg's stronger version, which the chair adopts: after
   the hold, the pried hatch stays on the target's flank for the
   rest of the battle, built from the part kit per house rules.
   The original complaint was that nothing stays clear on the
   target's side; this fixes the aftermath and marks which ship
   is being fought over.
4. **Keep the target's ring constant and amber through the
   hold.** The sheets show the target ring changing state by
   +0.8 s; at 170 the ring plus the tether is the only pairing
   cue (Qwen).
5. **Small colour and source hygiene.** The inside light should
   be amber, not standard explosion orange (DeepSeek), and a
   brief flash on the attacker's hatch at +0.3 s marks where the
   action comes from (DeepSeek). Cheap, and both reduce
   hit-confusion.

## 5. Clashes with RAM and CMD (principle; no captures in pack)

- Ram: the danger is proximity, not graphics. A ram ends with
  hulls touching and an impact flash, and the sheets' own +1.6 s
  frames show the attacker's hull sliding across the target's
  hex; if boarding's hold releases into that without the tethers
  still visible, the two beats blur (Hydrogen). Guard, from
  Qwen: keep the ram beat ring-free and line-free, and never
  overlap the two windows. DeepSeek adds: spawn the lines only
  once the ships are fully adjacent, so they never read as a
  tractor tether mid-approach.
- Command: the live risk, raised by all four legs. CMD moments
  draw in the same shared line style; if a command beat ever
  draws amber lines or rings between ships, amber stops meaning
  boarding. Guards: boarding lines always originate at the ship
  hatch, never the centre (DeepSeek), and the travelling pulse
  in change 1 doubles as the differentiator from static command
  lines (Hydrogen). Whoever builds the pick should confirm amber
  stays boarding's colour alone.

## Where the blind leg agreed and disagreed

Agreed with the council on: the pick; A reading as a hit; C's
pops reading as damage; 1.9 s too long; the lines fading by
+1.6 s; the CMD amber risk. Differences: the blind leg rated C
"unsure" on the boarding read where DeepSeek and Qwen said yes
on the tether's strength (chaired above, the difference is
whether C gets credit for containing B); the blind leg's
persistent hatch mark goes further than any model leg, though
Hydrogen and Qwen independently support its contrast half; and
the blind leg missed the target ring's mid-hold state change,
which was Qwen's catch.
