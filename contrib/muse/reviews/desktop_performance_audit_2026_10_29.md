# Desktop performance audit (chair, code reads only), Oct 29, 2026

Scope: battle.js (1,402 lines), scene3d.js (686), fx3d.js (633), vfx3d.js (219), perf3d.js (113), plus main3d.js (20) for boot behaviour. Frame of reference: the PC-only ruling (Oct 24, 2026), desktop GPU budget as the design floor, battles at about 20 ships total plus launched wings. No runtime profiling was done; this is a static read. The built-in profiler exists for the runtime half (perf3d.js:40-52, PERFCAP, Babylon perf collector with a per-battle CSV).

## Findings

1. [FIX] The mobile layer can claim a real PC, and it costs that PC its settings. OBSERVED: G.mobile is computed once at load as coarse pointer AND min(innerWidth, innerHeight) under 900 (scene3d.js:4), and nothing in the game reassigns it afterwards. A touchscreen 2-in-1, a small snapped window, or a tablet-class device at boot therefore boots as a phone: main3d.js:11 forces quality to medium when no saved quality exists, and forces fxReduced to false in the same branch, which also discards the prefers-reduced-motion default set at scene3d.js:4. The downstream effects are all OBSERVED: the render loop skips frames when the board is still, rendering every second frame and every fourth when idle (scene3d.js:52); the shadow map drops to 1024 from 2048 (scene3d.js:92); hull and rock art loads from the low-resolution directories (scene3d.js:417, scene3d.js:447); bursts spawn at 0.4 strength (fx3d.js:295); cloud count drops from 34 to 14 with no animated nebula materials (scene3d.js:81-82); the decorative showcase battle shrinks (campaign3d.js:36); audio voices cap at 20 instead of 34 (sfx.js:117). Under the PC-only ruling this layer is dead weight whose only remaining function is misclassifying desktops. Recommend Opus gate the whole branch behind an explicit phone build flag or delete it at the Godot cutover, and in the browser build at minimum stop it from overriding fxReduced and quality for a device that also has a fine pointer.

2. [FIX] Nav lights force a full world-matrix recompute for every ship, every frame, before the render does it again. OBSERVED: fxPoolTick ends by calling navLights for every view in G.views (vfx3d.js:163), and navLights calls v.root.computeWorldMatrix(true) on each one (vfx3d.js:166), the force flag recomputing the ship's whole subtree, then transforms each nav light position through it. Babylon then computes the same matrices again during scene.render. The cost is linear in total hull part count but with the matrix work doubled, and it runs even when nothing moved. INFERRED: at 20 ships with kit-built hulls this is one of the largest avoidable CPU costs in the frame. A dirty check (only recompute when the view moved this frame) or dropping the force flag would remove most of it.

3. [NOTE] Per-frame allocation churn in updateWorld is steady, small, and everywhere. OBSERVED, per unit per rendered frame: hexWorld allocates a new Vector3 (fx3d.js:122 calling scene3d.js:6); coastTick returns a fresh offset object for every non-squadron ship (fx3d.js:102-105); the nameplate key is rebuilt as a mapped, joined string for every unit before being compared to the cached key (fx3d.js:171), which allocates even on frames where nothing changed; animateFactionMats allocates new Color3 values via scale() for up to three colours per faction material set (fx3d.js:85-93); the skyline traffic update allocates a scaled vector per traffic ship (scene3d.js:49). INFERRED: at 20 to 40 units and 60 fps this is on the order of a few thousand small allocations per second. Not a frame-time blocker on a desktop, but it is constant GC pressure that a handful of scratch objects would erase, and it scales linearly with unit count, wings included.

4. [NOTE] Desktop default is Ultra, the full post stack runs every frame, and there is no adaptive step-down. OBSERVED: quality defaults to ultra (scene3d.js:4). The base pipeline is MSAA 4x with bloom, ACES tone mapping, vignette and sharpen (scene3d.js:36-46). Ultra adds volumetric god rays (scene3d.js:109) and allows TAA when opted in (scene3d.js:105); SSAO2 is attached on High as well as Ultra for non-mobile (scene3d.js:113); Ultra supersamples 1.25x on displays under DPR 1.5 (scene3d.js:116). setQuality only changes when the player cycles the setting (ui3d.js:530); nothing in the audited files watches frame time and steps quality down. INFERRED: this is correct against the ruled floor (a discrete desktop GPU), but an integrated-GPU desktop has no safety valve between a manual settings trip and a choppy battle.

5. [NOTE] Effects volume at 20+ ships is pooled and hard-capped; the ceiling is known and bounded. OBSERVED: all small effects go through four SpriteManager pools (one draw call each) capped on desktop at 1,800 additive, 420 fire, 640 smoke and 180 chunk sprites (vfx3d.js:139); when a pool is full the oldest live sprite is recycled rather than allocating (vfx3d.js:150). Per volley, decorative bolts cap at 42 on Ultra and 30 on High (fx3d.js:377); dynamic flash lights pool at 3 on Ultra and 2 on High (fx3d.js:319); debris batches cap at 320 items on desktop (fx3d.js:525); death sequences use fixed desktop counts (90 sparks and a 50-particle smoke burst, fx3d.js:480-484). INFERRED: a worst-case exchange, every ship firing at once with several deaths, saturates the additive pool first, and the failure mode is older effects vanishing early, not unbounded growth. Wings add units rather than effects systems: each wing is one unit with three craft instances (fx3d.js:71-75), plus a TrailMesh per craft on High and Ultra (vfx3d.js:112).

6. [NOTE] Engine glow lights multiply with the fleet, but their reach is fenced. OBSERVED: on High and Ultra, every opening-fleet ship with size at least 0.6 gets its own PointLight parented to its root (fx3d.js:74), so a 20-ship battle carries up to about 20 extra point lights on top of the three scene lights (scene3d.js:24-28). The fence is includedOnlyMeshes limited to the owning ship's meshes (fx3d.js:74), so each light shades only its own hull. INFERRED: the cost lands as per-fragment light evaluation on hull pixels, worst on the large capital hulls that fill the screen at battle zoom; it does not grow with the rest of the scene.

7. [NOTE] The expensive passes are actively dieted; shadow and geometry-buffer costs do not run at full rate by default. OBSERVED: the cascaded shadow generator is 2048 with 3 cascades on desktop (scene3d.js:92), but perf3d sets its refresh rate to render-once and only refreshes on camera movement, every fourth frame, or while ships are moving (perf3d.js:92-97). Decorative rocks below the board are frozen outright and the rest spin a quarter per frame (perf3d.js:90, perf3d.js:98-100). The SSAO geometry buffer has a render-list predicate excluding the far decorative field, sky props and billboards (perf3d.js:104-108). The glow layer's per-frame mesh test was patched to a Set lookup after the exclusion list was found growing across battles (perf3d.js:84-88). This file is the reason findings 4 and 6 stay notes rather than fixes.

8. [NOTE] Sprite expiry uses splice inside the pool tick. OBSERVED: fxPoolTick walks each pool backwards and splices out expired sprites one at a time (vfx3d.js:154-157). INFERRED: a mass expiry in one frame (a big explosion decaying all at once, or battle-end cleanup) makes that frame's tick quadratic in the number expiring, bounded by the pool caps in finding 5. Worst case is a single-frame hitch at effect peaks, not sustained cost. A swap-remove would make it linear if Opus ever sees the hitch in a PERFCAP capture.

9. [NOTE] battle.js contributes nothing per frame; its scaling cost sits in activations, not frame rate. OBSERVED: battle.js has no render loop; its only timer is stallGuard's interval during AI waits (battle.js:1058). Its unit scans are per event: occMap rebuilds a full occupancy Map from B.units on every call (battle.js:94), deployment and stance passes filter the whole unit list (battle.js:1152, battle.js:370), and the Chorus feed pass scans all units per kill (battle.js:504). INFERRED: because targeting and AI evaluation call these scans repeatedly per activation, a full AI turn's cost grows faster than the unit count (roughly quadratically at the top end), which presents as a pause on enemy activations at 20+ ships rather than as frame drops. Worth one PERFCAP-style timing capture on a veteran AI turn before treating it as more than a note.

10. [NOTE] Quality and fxReduced are genuinely reachable on desktop, and fxReduced is a real performance lever, not just an accessibility one. OBSERVED: the HUD cycles Graphics ultra, high, medium (ui3d.js:530) and toggles Reduce flashing (ui3d.js:529), mirrored in the campaign Settings rows (campaign3d.js:116, campaign3d.js:119), and both persist (ui3d.js:543). fxReduced shrinks and dims flashes (fx3d.js:293), caps volley bolts at 6 (fx3d.js:377), zeroes the flash light pool (fx3d.js:319), stops ambient skyline battle flashes (vfx3d.js:184), hulk cook-offs (vfx3d.js:83) and venting effects (fx3d.js:504). Medium additionally turns off SSAO (scene3d.js:113), god rays (Ultra only, scene3d.js:109), engine lights (fx3d.js:74) and shadow casting quality steps down (scene3d.js:106). A player on weak hardware has a working ladder; it is just entirely manual (see finding 4).

## Not checked

Mesh and draw-call counts per hull in ships3d.js (so the absolute draw-call total at 20 ships is inferred from pass structure, not counted); wfx3d.js bolt, beam and torpedo tick internals beyond the pool caps; gfx3d.js shield and dome effects; CamCtl update cost; the campaign and title screens; actual frame times on any hardware (no runtime capture was run; PERFCAP at perf3d.js:40-52 is the tool for that); the Godot build, which this audit does not cover.
