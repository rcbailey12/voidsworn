# Campaign screen information audit, synthesis, 2026-10-26

Chair synthesis of four blind inputs on the information design of Voidsworn's Godot campaign screens: DeepSeek leg (contrib/deepseek/reviews/campaign_map_review_deepseek_2026_10_26.md), Qwen leg (contrib/qwen/reviews/campaign_map_review_qwen_2026_10_26.md), Argon leg (contrib/gemini/reviews/campaign_map_review_gemini_2026_10_26.md), Rey audit (contrib/muse/reviews/campaign_map_review_rey.md). All four worked from the same evidence pack built from the fetched campaign files (campaign_ui.gd, map3d.gd, map_intel.gd, map_view.gd, campaign_host.gd, campaign_fallback_ui.gd, choice_button.gd).

Scope limit, stated plainly: this is the information half of 26c Job 4 only. No PC screenshots of the campaign screens exist, so every judgement about visual legibility, label-stack clutter as seen, hover card occlusion, and side panel density as experienced remains open. Job 4's visual verdicts stay blocked until screenshots exist. Nothing below should be read as a visual verdict.

Overall verdict: PARTIAL. The campaign screens do the hard work well (a real route planner, an honest self-derived danger estimate, a complete build and fleet panel) and stand up against the comparison games. What holds the layer back is concentrated and cheap to fix: the game's signature system, the Nemesis, is the least fully explained thing on his own home screen, and a handful of decision-shaped facts live only in tooltips, one-time modals, or the 3D map alone.

## Calibration: the legs agree on facts and split on labels

All four inputs agree on every material fact in this synthesis. They diverge on verdict labels in a consistent pattern: Qwen scores PASS on four of five areas, judging the default 3D experience and treating the flat-map absence and tooltip-only rules as noted gaps; DeepSeek and Rey score PARTIAL across the board, counting those absences against each area; Argon splits (PASS for danger on the 3D map, PARTIAL overall, and the audit's only FAIL, for the Nemesis area). The chair sets its bar explicitly: an area passes only if a player on either shipped map, without hovering tooltips or having memorised a one-time modal, can act on the information the area exists to provide. Under that bar the chair scores PARTIAL on areas 1, 2, 3 and 5, and FAIL on area 4, adopting Argon's reasoning there: the Nemesis card succeeds as a schedule board and fails at the area's actual purpose, rival intelligence for counter-play, which is the benchmark the commissioning brief names (Shadow of War's intel screen states weaknesses as exploitable effects; Voidsworn's card prints names only).

## Area verdicts (chair)

1. Route planning: PARTIAL. The 3D planner is real and strong: safest and richest routes lit through any hovered node, running-total chips per node, full route totals in the hover card, and a conditional warning when a hurt fleet's route has no repair stop before the flagship (map3d.gd route block; map_intel.gd:66-127). Three limits, all four legs in substance: the two lenses are fixed and their scoring is invisible (weights in map_intel.gd path_stats: boons 3, relics 4, refits 2, credits 1, shipyards 2, events 1; the hover card does not even print the credits and events counts that feed the Richest score, Argon's pin); the boss is excluded from route danger sums by construction (map_intel.gd:100); and the whole planner is 3D only. Argon adds a fourth: danger() scales budget by column but divides by fleet power now, so late-column bands on a full-sector route read worse than they will when reached.
2. Information at a glance: PARTIAL. The side panel is a genuine build summary (credits, command points, per-ship hull numbers and segmented bars, readiness words with plain thresholds, captain and refit state, promotion flags, aspect, every boon and relic in full text, a fleet-condition warning that answers the repair question instead of just stating damage). The misses are specific omissions from a strong surface: no numeric sector progress anywhere; the sector condition's effect exists only in its strip box tooltip (campaign_ui.gd:424-425); the run's Heat rules print on the aspect screen and are never repeated on the map page, even though Heat flags feed the danger estimate the player is reading.
3. Danger readability: PARTIAL (PASS on the 3D map). The estimate is derived from the same budget numbers the game builds fleets with, divided by current fleet power, in four bands (Easy under 0.8, Even under 1.05, Hard under 1.4, Deadly above; map_intel.gd:11, 29-58), shown as on-node chips, an exact percentage in the hover card, and a worst-fight summary per route. All three channels read one function, so they cannot contradict. Special fights are marked on multiple channels (elite diamond, crown ring and lane chevrons; the boss as the flagship's own hull at scale). Two defects keep it at PARTIAL: the flat map shows no danger information at all (a drawing omission, since map_intel.gd computes bands independently of which map draws them), and the hover card never itemises why a number is what it is (scar and adaptation multipliers, bounty and veteran modifiers, Heat flags are all folded in silently). Band calibration against actual play outcomes is unproven in every input (UNSURE).
4. The Nemesis as information: FAIL (see the bar above). This also settles Job 2's open question 7 in full. Surfaced, always on the side-panel card while he lives (campaign_ui.gd:516-540): full name with title and epithet, faction, portrait with drawn scars, scar count, the clock as dots in two places, a bold red strike warning when the flag is set, his converted node marked before the player commits (NEMESIS chip and portrait on the 3D map, red text on the flat map), weaknesses by name when revealed ("unknown" when not), adaptations by generic name. His full adaptation explanation (name, why, effect) appears exactly once, in the sector-change modal (campaign_ui.gd:899). Never surfaced in any fetched campaign file (grep-confirmed by two legs independently): his faction behaviour text, so the same clock dots mean "lose 25% of credits or a crippled ship" against a Corsair and "a duel bonus between flagships" against a Voidsworn with no way to price the difference; his persona and traits; scar causes; revive state; and the fed and riches counters, which have no reader in the campaign files at all. Fed does not even enter the danger estimate (map_intel.gd:54 prices scars at x1.10 and adaptations at x1.06 only), so the estimate silently understates exactly the rival who grows. Weaknesses are the Shadow of War gap in miniature: revealed shows the NAME, never the effect text the same table carries.
5. Layering and duplication: PARTIAL. The skeleton is sound (always-on state, hover depth, modal announcements) and duplication is mostly single-source and reinforcing (the clock in two places, danger in three channels from one function). Three structural leaks: rules text that exists only in tooltips (sector condition effect, rival-raiders rate, patron sigil rule); permanent state announced only in a one-time modal (the adaptation line); and priority inversion in always-on space, where full reachable-fight descriptions sit permanently below the chart (duplicating the hover card and node labels) while active rules hide. Two contradiction risks are live: the card and modal use generic NEM_ADAPT names while NEM_ADAPT_FAC faction names exist unused by this layer, so another surface can call the same adaptation by a different name; and the Richest ranking can visibly prefer a route whose printed totals look worse, because the scoring rule is hidden.

## Ranked fixes (chair order, decision value per effort)

1. Print his behaviour line on the Nemesis card (one label from the data the game already carries). Closes the audit's largest gap and makes the clock's urgency faction-shaped.
2. Give the adaptation line a permanent home on the card (effect text plus the why string per adaptation), and print revealed weaknesses with their effect text, not names alone. The strings exist in the tables the card already reads.
3. Surface fed and riches on the card for the factions that have them, show revive state for Deathless, and wire fed and riches into map_intel.gd danger() so the estimate stops understating him.
4. Promote the sector condition's effect from tooltip to text, repeat the active Heat rules on the map page, and add a numeric sector progress readout. All four are facts the game already knows, on a panel that already carries denser material.
5. Itemise the danger number in the hover card (a short breakdown: scars, adaptations, modifiers, Heat), print the credits and events counts in route summaries, and state what Safest and Richest optimise in one line. Removes the one live contradiction between channels.
6. Draw danger bands (and ideally route summaries) on the flat map. map_intel.gd already computes them map-independently; map_view.gd never asks.
7. Replace the intel strip's duplicate Nemesis box (its tooltip is word-for-word the card's text) with the Heat rules or progress counter from fix 4, and make the bottom reachable-fight preview panels collapsible where the hover card already carries the same text.
8. Announce weakness reveals and non-sector-change adaptations (Chorus adapts after every meeting) with a toast reusing the modal's sentence, if campaign.gd does not already. Partly UNSURE (see below).
