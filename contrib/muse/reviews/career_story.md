Its five asks: progression that feels rewarding; an admiral per faction with personality and goals, plus recurring rival admirals; personalisation (name your admiral and flagship, paint, crest, earned titles); a short story arc per faction with choices and consequences; and a career that carries over between runs, with a career screen. ## What the code already gives you (OBSERVED)

The bones are better than the backlog implies:

- **24 admirals already exist, four per faction**, each with a name, a doctrine, a mechanical identity (fx), a starting fleet, a renown cost and a unique Command (data.js:202, data.js:208 and the Object.assign block that follows). What they do not have is a single line of dialogue or a goal of their own. Personality today is a stat block.
- **The voice pattern already exists, one system over.** Patrons carry a quote field and speak it in the boon modal (data.js:388). Nothing stops admirals using the same field shape.
- **Per-faction storytelling already exists, one system over.** Events support per-faction variants: eventScreen merges `ev.v[RUN.fac]` over the base event, with the comment "each faction tells it in its own words" (campaign_run.js:197). Two of the eleven events use it. Events can also start fights (campaign_run.js:226) and set the ambush flag, which converts the next battle node into a Hegemony elite ambush (campaign_run.js:77). Choices with consequences are therefore already possible; they are just not chained into an arc.
- **Cross-run persistence already works and is already narrative-shaped.** endRun banks renown scaled by Heat (campaign_meta.js:187), grants admiral mastery, and pushes the run's dead captains into META.fallen, capped at 80 (campaign_meta.js:184), rendered as the Hall of the Fallen tab on the Progression screen (campaign_meta.js:113, 124). Per-faction victory flavour exists (WIN_TITLE, campaign_meta.js:171), the recap already computes a standout ship from per-ship career stats (campaign_meta.js:177), and the end screen already steers the player toward a faction they have not won with (campaign_meta.js:180).
- **The unlock rails exist.** Hull variants are bought once with renown and then stocked in shipyards (data.js:332), mastery has an XP table and four perk tiers (data.js:348), and factions already flavour everything from module names to what a run is called (data.js:270).

What is missing (OBSERVED, by absence in the four files read): no admiral dialogue anywhere, no arc or quest state in RUN or META, no medals, no per-admiral win/loss record (META.stats is global, mastery is the only per-admiral number), no captain ledger beyond the Fallen, and no way to rename anything. Ship and captain names are drawn from per-faction pools (data.js:312-318), including Deathless captain epithets, which is personalisation of flavour, not of identity.

## Option A (small): Voices and Records

**What the player sees.** Admirals talk. Each of the 24 gets a small voice block (selection line, sector intro line, victory line, defeat line, flagship-sighted line), shown on surfaces that already exist: one line on the admiral card, one in the sector intro modal, one on the end screen, which already names the admiral in its subtitle. A new Career tab joins the Progression screen's existing five tabs (campaign_meta.js:113). On it: per-admiral service records (runs, wins, best sector, mastery), a medals case (about ten medals derived from run stats the game already counts: flagship kills, a no-loss sector, 100 career kills, a win on Heat), and captains' service records, the living counterpart of the Hall of the Fallen. Finally, two naming hooks: rename your flagship on the fleet screen (the name is a plain data field rendered everywhere already), and an earned title for your admiral, awarded at endRun from your dominant playstyle stat (ram-heavy careers earn "the Breaker", carrier careers "the Beekeeper" in Corsair words, and so on), displayed beside the admiral's name wherever the map sidebar shows it today.

**What it reuses.** The PATRONS quote field shape and its rendering in the boon modal; the Progression tab machinery; endRun's existing "Carried forward" panel as the place medals are awarded; runRecap's standout-ship math, which already reads per-ship dealt/kills/battles; META.fallen's persistence pattern for the captain ledger; per-faction name pools for title flavour.

**What is new.** Voice data (24 admirals x about 4 short lines, writing only), a medals table in data.js (about 10 rows), additive META fields (career records per admiral, medals, captain ledger, admiral title), one Career tab renderer, one rename input, one title-derivation function at endRun.

**Rough effort (INFERRED).** Small: a few days. No battle code, no RUN changes, save impact limited to additive META fields of the kind migrateMeta already backfills. It delivers the backlog's "ranks and medals" and "clearer reward moments", plus a first coat of admiral personality. It does not deliver story, rivals or consequences.

## Option B (medium): Faction arcs, told through the event system

**What the player sees.** Picking a faction starts its arc: three beats, one per sector, on top of the normal run. Beat 1 (sector 1) is a scripted, faction-specific event with a real choice: the Voidsworn choose whether to tithe a refit to the shrine-fleet or keep it; the Corsairs choose whether to rob the tender or escort it. The choice sets a flag the run remembers. Beat 2 (sector 2) is where the consequence lands and the rival arrives: the robbed tender's escort is now an ambush with a named commander (the ambush machinery at campaign_run.js:77, given a name and a line), or the escorted tender's faction sells to you at a discount for the rest of the run. The rival is drawn from the enemy faction's own four admirals, so a Hegemony arc faces a named Corsair warlord, not a generic elite. Beat 3 (sector 3) is the reckoning: the sector flagship battle is framed as the rival's flagship, with their intro line before the fight and the arc's resolution text after it. Win the run with the arc complete and the faction pays out a permanent reward: an arc-exclusive hull variant in your shipyards (the variant rails at data.js:332) for the modest version, or the rival's own Command as an alternate Command unlock for the generous one. Arc state shows as one line on the map sidebar ("The Tithe: the shrine-fleet waits for your answer in sector II") and completed arcs collect on a Chronicle section of the Progression screen, one entry per faction, with the choices you made recorded.

**What it reuses.** The event system end to end: per-faction variants (campaign_run.js:197) become per-faction arc events, the seen-events gating keeps beats from repeating, the event fight path (campaign_run.js:226) stages beat battles without touching the node generator, and the ambush flag is the consequence engine. endRun is the resolution point and already computes everything an arc payout needs. The rival needs no new character system: the enemy factions' ADMIRALS entries already carry names, doctrines and Commands. WIN_TITLE (campaign_meta.js:171) is the template for arc ending text.

**What is new.** Arc data: 6 factions x 3 beats, each beat an event-shaped object with two choices and a consequence flag (the writing is the bulk of the work, roughly 100 to 150 words per beat). A small arc state machine: current beat per faction in META (so an arc can span runs if a run dies early, which suits a roguelite), beat delivery (one guaranteed event per sector is steered to the arc beat, using the existing event node flow), rival naming on the ambush and flagship, the Chronicle renderer, and the payout hook in endRun. No new battle mechanics.

**Rough effort (INFERRED).** Medium: about a week, most of it writing. Code risk is low because every delivery mechanism already exists; the one genuinely new mechanism is guaranteeing one arc beat per sector, and the event-fight path shows the codebase already accepts scripted battles outside the node economy.

## Option C (big): The Long Career

**What the player sees.** You do not pick an admiral per run; you are one. At career start you create your admiral (name, faction, a crest assembled from a glyph and colours, the same visual language patrons already use with glyph and col at data.js:388, since the four files contain no portrait art pipeline, OBSERVED). Runs become tours of duty in one career. Your flagship persists between tours: same name, same captain if she lives, same aspect family, reset to Mk I with its scars (battles fought and damage dealt are already tracked per ship inside a run). Captains who survive a won run retire to a roster and can be rehired as veteran starting captains on later tours (the lifepod reserve system, generalised across runs). The career screen becomes the game's front door: service record, tours, medals, the roster, the Hall of the Fallen and a Hall of Legends beside it. Over the career, a conquest layer tracks which factions you have beaten and which arcs (Option B) you have closed; closing all six opens a finale tour, a harder run against a combined fleet, with the career's ending text. Renown splits into spendable renown (as today) and career standing, which only grows and gates the finale and the best roster hires.

**What it reuses.** The Persist and migration machinery (additive META growth is the established pattern), mastery, favour and Heat as the career's XP spines, the Fallen hall, the variants shop, per-ship career stats, and nextFacPick's existing steer toward unwon factions (campaign_meta.js:180), which becomes the conquest map's logic.

**What is new.** A career object and its save migration (the highest-risk piece: the save layer was visibly hardened in v53, with sanity checks and normalisation, so a new persistent structure has to be born inside that discipline), flagship persistence rules, a cross-run captain roster and hire economy, the conquest/finale layer, a crest generator, and a career screen that reorganises the Progression screen. It also changes the game's contract: permadeath and the clean run reset are what make the lifepod and the Fallen hurt, and a persistent flagship dilutes both.

**Rough effort (INFERRED).** Big: several weeks, touching run lifecycle (newRun and endRun), the save format, and every progression surface, plus finale content. Highest ceiling, highest risk, and it partially spends the game's best emotional asset (loss) to buy continuity.

## The pick: Option B, with Option A as its first step

Build A first because it is days of work and it makes B's characters audible: an arc whose rival has no voice is a fetch quest, and A's medals and records give B's payouts somewhere visible to land. Then build B as the mode itself. B is the option that answers the most backlog bullets per unit of risk: story arc per faction (its whole structure), choices with consequences (flags, ambush, discount), recurring rival admirals (named, from the existing rosters), rewarding progression (arc payouts on the existing variant rails), and admiral personality (each faction's beats are written in its admirals' voices, reusing A's voice blocks). It does so without touching battle mechanics, the node generator or the save format's shape, because the event, ambush and persistence systems were already built for exactly this and are currently underused (two faction-variant events out of eleven; one ambush source in the whole game).

C is the wrong next step for two reasons. First, its riskiest component (a new persistent structure in a save system that has already needed v53 hardening) buys the one thing the game does not lack, which is continuity systems: mastery, favour, Heat, variants and the Fallen already carry the player between runs. Second, its flagship persistence works against the design's centre of gravity. The lifepod toast and the Hall of the Fallen land because ships stay lost; a flagship that always comes back makes every other hull feel more disposable, not less. If B ships and players ask for more, C's career screen can still be built later as a view over the META data A and B accumulate, minus flagship persistence, and lose very little.

On the owner's open scope questions (BACKLOG.md:64), this pick assumes: story length of three beats per faction (about 15 minutes of reading across a whole run), text only with no voice acting, and portraits deferred in favour of patron-style glyph crests until the portrait art style is chosen. If the owner wants longer arcs, B's beat structure extends to five beats per faction without new machinery; the cost is purely writing.
