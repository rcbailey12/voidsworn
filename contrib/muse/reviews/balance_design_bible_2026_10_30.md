## 1. Game shape

- A run is three sectors. Each sector ends in a boss; a Nemesis meeting is mandatory per sector (sector 1 meeting in the last columns), and the Nemesis clock is an accelerant on that schedule, not the schedule itself (source: Oct 28 battle-layer program, Nemesis synthesis; daily log Oct 29, 2026, Argon first-run screening).
- Battle density target: about 20 ships total on the board as a typical battle, ruled Oct 24, 2026 and clarified Oct 29, 2026: 20 is a leaning typical target, never a hard cap, especially for swarm factions (source: Ryan ruling, recorded in MEMORY.md and the red team core pack).
- Difficulty ramps by elite ships punching above their weight class, not by raw ship count: Ryan rejected dial-invariance and the more-ships ramp on Oct 24, 2026 (source: Ryan ruling, Oct 24, 2026).
- Run 1 difficulty ruling (Ryan, Oct 28, 2026): winning run 1 should be near impossible. The first completion is a multi-run achievement built on accumulated generational learning, not a skill-only possibility. This is a design stance and it stands. The empirical premise originally attached to it (an approximately 80 percent loss rate) does NOT reproduce and is retired; see section 8, item 1 (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md, Validation section).
- The learning frame (ruled Oct 28, 2026): meta-progression is wrapped in per-faction learning fiction. Every faction's next fleet learns from the last in its own idiom. The Glasswrights are the exception in shape: a parallel procession, not a serial loop (optical memory hulls, slow gatherer hulls recovering shard fields, the forge recasting the next fleet; hundreds of staggered expeditions). All cores are equal; Astra is a peer core the Glasswrights caged, not a superior (source: progression synthesis addenda, Oct 28, 2026; lore bible r8).
- Measured run shape at the pin, fresh-profile bot pilot, full campaign loop (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md): 12,600 runs, 140,979 battles, 0 harness errors. Overall win rate 66.0 percent [65.0, 67.0]; Veteran 63.0 percent [63.0, 64.0]; Standard 80.0 percent [79.0, 82.0]. Sector reach: sector 2 reached by 84.0 percent, sector 3 by 71.0 percent. These are bot-relative fresh-profile floors: real players accumulate mastery and favour across runs, and a first-time human is far weaker than this pilot. Any win-rate claim that does not name its pilot and campaign policy is not a property of the game.
- Battle classes (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md): bosses are the longest fights in every sector (5.20 to 6.66 mean rounds) and among the least lethal per fight (94 to 97 percent battle win rate). The attrition peak is sector 2 elites and sector 2 Nemesis fights, tied at 0.74 and 0.75 ships lost per fight.

## 3. Economy

- Credit flow, measured (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md): mean credits 120 at run start, 405.1 [402.0, 408.2] at end of sector 1, 501.0 [496.7, 505.3] at end of sector 2. Starting credits 120 (source: economy chair screening, core.js:191 note, Oct 29, 2026).
- Income shape: node rewards pay almost no credits directly (normal battles: boon 62 percent, module 16 percent, credit node 12 percent, repair 10 percent; elite and boss always a boon at minimum tier). Credit income arrives via battle salvage and prize money, event credit rewards (+30 to +80), ransom and scrap (55 percent decaying, 35 percent), and sector-condition credit bonuses (wreck +15 percent, war +20 percent, scarce +25 percent on battle pay, data.js:605-607) (source: economy synthesis chair screening, Oct 29, 2026).
- Shipyard spending across 47,625 visits: hulls 70 percent, modules 21 percent, Mk upgrades 5 percent, repairs 4 percent (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md).
- Price system: explicit hull costs run about 65 to 70 credits per CP (warden 70 at cp1, harrow 130 at cp2, penitent 200 at cp3, reliquary 260 at cp4, cathedral 340 at cp5); the fallback formula is round((60 x cp + 10) / 5) x 5 at data.js:545, then the FAC_PRICE class multiplier applies (source: economy Qwen leg chair verification, Oct 29, 2026). FAC_PRICE state at the pin: deathless {Frigate 1.45, Destroyer 1.5, Cruiser 1.55} with no Battleship key; voidsworn has a single Carrier 1.25 key; hegemony is an empty object (sources: battleship_entry_2026_10_29.md; economy synthesis, Oct 29, 2026). The Battleship gap inverts pricing: a cp6 Eclipse battleship priced by the fallback lands at 370, below the cp4 zenith cruiser at 390 after its 1.55 multiplier (source: factionshape Rey leg, Oct 29, 2026). The filed battleship entry's fix (Battleship:1.6, price 590) is part of an entry whose ENTRY SOUND verdict is overturned; see sections 8 and 9.
- Price multiplier stacking is bounded in shipped data: SECTOR_CONDS (data.js:602-607) has exactly one price field, scarce at 1.2, so the real stack range is about +50 percent worst case and minus 15 percent best, uncapped in code (source: economy Qwen leg chair verification, Oct 29, 2026).
- Repair ruling (Ryan, Oct 29, 2026, reaffirmed since): repair should always beat scrap-and-replace per ship by design; the real tradeoff is budget allocation at the regroup; ACCEPT AS IS FOR NOW, leave it generous, revisit only with actual play data. Evidence under two independent methods: the Oct 29 model found the repair-versus-buy tension binding in 0.0 percent of 60,000 modeled regroups; the real campaign Monte Carlo found binding in 0.42 percent of 47,625 shipyard visits (199 visits), with median worst-ship damage at arrival of 0 HP and median purse 378 credits (sources: sims_cp_repair_2026_10_29.md; montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md). Per-ship math: the penitent cruiser (hp 120, cost 200, data.js:65) repairs from half hull for 30 credits, 15 percent of replacement (source: economy Qwen leg, chair verified, Oct 29, 2026).
- The regroup is a ruled game beat (Oct 28, 2026, Q11): after a boss, recovery is a regroup with tradeoffs and cost (choose among repair, upgrade, or buying ships), not a free full restore. The shipped healing stack is why it does not bind: post-win patch 15 percent of max hull free (finishBattle), post-boss transit patch 40 percent free (campaign_run.js:536), drydock full-fleet free repair, field repair nodes 20 percent free (source: economy Qwen leg chair verification, Oct 29, 2026).
- Healing lever values, measured for later use (source: tuning_grids_2026_10_30.md, grid b; Sunward Crusade Veteran baseline 50.0 percent): deleting the post-win patch is the strongest single lever (win rate 40.7 percent [35.3, 46.3]) and still lifts binding only to 1.25 percent; transit patch to 0 percent: 45.0 percent; battle pay x0.7: 45.3 percent; battle pay x1.5: 53.7 percent. No single lever makes the tradeoff bind; a binding regroup takes combined cuts, priced in the grid.
- repairMult defect (verified): repairMult's exactly four call sites all scale FREE healing (campaign3d.js:41, campaign_run.js:207, :408, :536); the paid shipyard price never passes through it, so the h_repair doctrine at 0.5 halves free healing while wearing a discount's name. It is not dead code (an Argon economy claim to that effect is refuted, Oct 29, 2026). Remedy is final synthesis action 14: route it to the paid price or rename the doctrine (sources: economy Argon leg chair correction; redteam_final_synthesis_2026_10_30.md).
- Capture economics (ruled Oct 24, 2026): fleet maxed means extra captured ships auto-scrap; not maxed means an option to add. Consequences for ships not kept are strip plus ransom only. Measured: add-then-decommission at a flat 35 percent beats a late-decay ransom; ransom decay floors at a 0.4 factor from about the sixth ransom (sources: economy Argon leg chair correction, Oct 29, 2026; redteam Argon player leg, Oct 30, 2026). Salvage tow is nearly dead in play: 109 of 131,579 salvage picks (0.08 percent) (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md).
- Faction price/CP grid verdicts (source: tuning_grids_2026_10_30.md, grid a; 300 runs per row, Veteran): Chorus prices x0.8 plus CP cap +4: 91.3 percent [87.6, 94.0], 4.53 ships at end of sector 1, the only Chorus row that clearly works, and it works by hull count as the ruling intended. Neither knob alone moves reliably (CP +4 alone 87.0 percent; prices x0.8 alone 88.0 percent). Eclipse prices x1.25 COLLAPSES the faction: 32.3 percent [27.3, 37.8], fleet about 2.3 ships at end of sector 1. The expensive half of the Oct 29 principle cannot go in price-first at that size; a +10 percent row is the unrun next cell.

## 4. Progression

- The spine (ruled Oct 28, 2026): admiral leveling with skill choices in a small tree per admiral, light RPG elements in the Hades and BattleTech register, explicitly so progression keeps scaling at run 5, run 10, run 15, and beyond. Admirals are early style choices the player then levels; locking or gating stays possible in some form. Run 10 is a rough milestone, not a design target; tuning is not hard-coded around run counts (sources: progression synthesis addenda, Oct 28, 2026).
- Tree shape (source: skill_tree_proposal_final_2026_10_28.md and the Oct 29 assemblies): 24 admirals, 216 assembled texts across tiers 1 to 3 (tier 1: 24 per-admiral plus 24 shared; tiers 2 and 3 assembled Oct 29, 2026). Design rules binding all tree drafting: (1) a skill must change a decision, pure numeric only at a 15 percent floor; (2) at most 1 in 3 pure numeric, numerics only on Branch C; (3) faction learning idiom; (4) no duplication of the 12 LEGACY doctrine effects; (5) nothing rewards losing, dying, or retreating (catastrophe conversion is acceptable); (6) tier 3 must not restate a capstone at lower power. Loss XP is stated at 10; pick traces land pick 1 at about run 5, pick 3 at about run 10, capstone at about run 14 to 15 (source: Oct 28 tree validation note).
- Composition warning (red team gamedev seat, Oct 30, 2026): everything folds into one uncapped additive pool (sumFx at core.js:171; derive() at core.js:207), so the 216 tree texts inherit the duplication verdicts of section 6 unless the fold gains caps, diminishing returns, or exclusivity before tree content lands on it. This is final synthesis action 10.
- The boot grant (ruled Oct 28, 2026): the full unlock at boot is a confirmed leftover from testing (main3d.js:11 grants all 12 LEGACY items, 635 renown, and all 24 admirals, 930 renown: 1,565 of 1,815 total renown sinks, 86.2 percent, granted before the first battle). Restoring the as-designed renown ladder is NOT ruled; Ryan is open to a rewrite (source: progression_synthesis_2026_10_28.md). The grant persists into saved META, which matters for the cutover importer (section 9).
- Renown (ruled Oct 29, 2026, battle-layer Q14): renown's role is now respec and experimentation; new doctrine content goes to the backlog. A winning run pays about 103 renown; a sector 2 death about 44; post-redesign sinks were honestly costed at 885 with respec as the standing sink (sources: progression_synthesis_2026_10_28.md; Oct 28 tree validation note). Run-end renown formula as shipped: battles x2 + elites x4 + bosses x15 + kills/4 + (sector minus 1) x5, plus 10 percent per Heat level, paid on any run end, loss included (campaign3d.js:30; source: Oct 29 chair screening of the Argon first-run leg).
- Heat (ruled Oct 29, 2026, battle-layer Q13, closing the parked progression Q3): Heat pays as composed in the final tree proposal: +2 XP per Heat point to the flown admiral, +1 to the faction shadow pool, overflow at cap routes to the line's shadow XP (source: Ryan ruling, Oct 29, 2026, recorded in the daily log and the final tree proposal addenda).
- Favour and the boon tax (measured; sources: measurements_2026_10_29.md, boon_favour_sweep_2026_10_29.md, measurements_addendum_2026_10_29.md, tuning_grids_2026_10_30.md grid c). Rarity roll as shipped: Common m=1, Rare m=1.5, Epic m=2, Legendary flat (Legendary multiplier is 1 at data.js:620). At sector 1 with no favour an offer rolls 70 percent Common, 24 percent Rare, 6 percent Epic. The favour tax on off-patron backfill picks is a two-step staircase in rollRarity (core.js:177): no tax at tier 0; about 15 percentage points of Rare-or-better share at tiers 1 and 2 (favour 4, 8, and 15 return literally identical distributions, because tier 2's perk is a starting protocol, not rarity); about 52 points at tier 3 (favour 25), where patron picks are 100.0 percent Rare-or-better against about 48 percent for fillers. Backfill incidence is favour-independent: 0.0 percent while the patron fields 3 or more useful boons, then exactly 1 filler per offer at 4 held and 2 at 5 held, with kit coverage as a second, graded trigger. Sampling scale: 184,800 offers in the original measurement, 237,600 in the favour sweep, 900,000 per variant in grid c, agreeing within noise.
- Q7 (ruled Oct 28, 2026): off-patron spillover offers stay, but off-patron picks must retain real value; the hidden rarity tax on fillers is the defect. The shipped code violates the ruling twice over: backfill-only access (off-patron boons enter only when the patron pool runs short) plus favour-free rarity rolls for off-patron picks (source: boons_synthesis_2026_10_29.md). Measured fix: passing the patron argument to the backfill rarity roll (fix C1) closes the tax to within 1 point at every favour tier (source: tuning_grids_2026_10_30.md, grid c). The fix spec is ruled_fixes_spec_2026_10_29.md; it must name the duo defect separately (next item).
- Duo-at-Common is a SEPARATE defect from the tax (reclassified Oct 30, 2026): duo boons enter offers at hardcoded Common (r:0), a skipped rarity roll, not a missing argument (source: measurements_addendum_2026_10_29.md). Measured on the grid c sampler: duo incidence is about 30 percent of eligible offers; rolling duo rarity like patron picks makes the duo distribution the patron distribution by construction. Wave 3's reassurance does not cover this layer: duo boons survived 0 of 300 sampled offers in the Argon QA leg's reward probes (source: redteam Argon qa leg, Oct 30, 2026; see section 8, item 9).
- Legendary access (ruled Oct 28, 2026, Q6): legendary stays engineerable but resolves by weighted roll against the duo for the contested slot. Measured: with two of a patron's boons held and minR at least 1, the legendary takes a slot in about 62 percent of offers (source: tuning_grids_2026_10_30.md, grid c). Rule-changing protocols get numeric rarity scaling (ruled Oct 28, 2026, Q4).
- Core sync fiction (ruled Oct 28, 2026, Q19, Option A): the faction's crews are the learners; the core's recognition is the visible sign. The debrief is tied to progression, including core sync, and to Nemesis adaptation, with the no-fake-lesson honesty clause (sources: progression synthesis addenda, Oct 28, 2026).

## 5. Nemesis system

- Shape: one Nemesis per run, generated fresh at newRun (core.js:339 calls newNemesis, campaign_run.js:247), with exactly two weaknesses per Nemesis life, filtered against its traits; successors may re-roll (ruled Oct 29, 2026). Meetings are mandatory, one per sector; the clock (nemClockAdd, nemStrike) accelerates meetings and can force an early extra adaptation (0.21 adaptations carried into meeting 1 on average) (sources: Oct 28 Nemesis synthesis; montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md).
- Adaptation visibility (ruled Oct 28, 2026, Q9): adaptation is visible on the debrief (learn and attribute), hidden in-run.
- Q4 clash rule (ruled Oct 29, 2026): an adaptation may not directly counter a revealed weakness. Enforcement caveat: the filed telemetry spec's clash-audit join was defective for weaknesses inherited across runs via the Q5 loss debrief; the telemetry addendum (telemetry_spec_addendum_2026_10_30.md) fixes the join with a nemesis_lineage key. See section 8, item 15.
- Q5 loss reveal (ruled Oct 29, 2026): a lost Nemesis meeting still teaches a weakness, revealed on the debrief. As shipped, the reveal call (nemReveal) exists only on the win branch; the loss branch at campaign_run.js:309 pays nothing. The fix spec (ruled_fixes_spec_2026_10_29.md) builds it on the loss branch. Chair-flagged cap, neither vetoed nor confirmed by Ryan: at most one new reveal per lost meeting (section 9).
- Base premium (powered rerun, 3,930 battles; source: measurements_nemesis_rerun_2026_10_29.md and measurements_addendum_2026_10_29.md): the base fleet is power-indexed at about 1.3x flat (live ratios 1.36 / 1.33 / 1.27 by sector). It cannot run away; all compounding is the capped adaptation layer (cap: sector minus 1, hard cap 6).
- Growth cost at full strength (same sources): sector 1 full package +12.3pp [+1.7, +22.9] (the trait package alone is +8.5pp; the first meeting is not free). Sector 2 first-adaptation step +14.6pp [+3.2, +26.0], decomposing into the trait package (about 8.5pp) plus one adaptation (about 2 to 7pp): a slope, not a cliff. Sector 3 growth cost +27.7pp [+16.4, +39.0]. Single adaptations in isolation: armor largest at +12.5pp, borderline significant; aim +10.8pp; evasion, point defense, shields not significant; no single adaptation drives the step, so there is no one-line tuning fix.
- Battered-fleet caveat (same sources; this caveat governs tuning, because battered arrivals are the typical case under the ruled loss rate): the sector 2 growth cost widens from +14.6pp at full strength to +28.1pp at 65 percent hull and +38.1pp at 40 percent hull. Sector 3 stays flat because fleetPower indexing collapses the enemy CP ratio for battered fleets (1.27 down to 0.92 at 40 percent).
- Weakness value (same sources): measured payback is +3.8pp in both sector 2 and sector 3, not significant at the rerun's power. The earlier +20pp and +10pp payback figures are WITHDRAWN (section 8, item 6). Honest claim: weaknesses help a little, plausibly; the size is unmeasured.
- Campaign-scale verdict (source: montecarlo_evidence_2026_10_30.md): NO SNOWBALL. Player win rate against the Nemesis rises by meeting (88.0 percent, 91.0 percent, 95.0 percent) and by adaptation count carried (87.0 percent at 0, 90.0 at 1, 94.0 at 2 or more), a progress confound: player power growth outruns the capped adaptation count (2.30 carried into the final meeting on average). Weakness reveals are front-loaded: 1.40 gained at meeting 1, 0.41 at meeting 2, 0.02 at meeting 3. Meetings per run across 12,600 runs: 12,006 first, 9,494 second, 4,575 third.
- The one snowball-shaped exception: the Chorus fast adaptation cadence costs +19.2pp [+6.9, +31.4] at sector 2 and +30.8pp [+19.8, +41.9] at sector 3 over Chorus at standard cadence, and the sector 3 fast-cadence meeting sits at a 15.8 percent bot win rate (source: measurements_addendum_2026_10_29.md). It is the first tuning target; the sector 2 battered-arrival width is the second.
- Unresolved at measurement level: the second adaptation's cost (two arms disagree, bounded at about 12pp) (source: measurements_addendum_2026_10_29.md). A real clock-summoned hunter Nemesis is backlogged by ruling (Oct 29, 2026), decided after playtest.

## 6. Content pools and duplication maps

Master mechanism (chair-verified at the pin): aggregation is purely additive. One fold (sumFx, core.js:171) sums relics, admiral effects, and boons into a single effect object; no caps, no overwrites. Duplicates therefore stack rather than substitute: the pool problem is what a slot buys, not whether it works (source: boons_synthesis_2026_10_29.md).

### Weapons: DUPLICATES WITH PAINT (4 of 4) (source: weapons_synthesis_2026_10_29.md)

- Literal duplicate: flak (data.js:4) and gsentry (data.js:22) are field-identical, including kind: {shots:3, dmg:3, r:2, opt:2, acc:80, fall:10, vsSq:3, vsCap:.6}. Mount counts diverge (11 flak mounts, 1 gsentry), so the table pays a design slot for zero mechanical difference. Eclipse effectively lists the same point-defense gun twice (gsentry plus its own flak rename, "Null shredder").
- Kind-system consequence: canFire returns false for kind flak, so flak-kind mounts never fire offensively; intercepts is a boolean some() at battle.js:352 (any one flak-kind mount grants 2 intercepts; a second mount grants nothing). Two PD mounts on one hull are one working mount plus decoration (source: redteam gamedev adjudication, Oct 30, 2026).
- The synthesis also records two statistical clones and a rename layer that sometimes carries a trait and sometimes only paint (full pairs in weapons_synthesis_2026_10_29.md). Open hygiene choice for Opus: give gsentry a real delta or delete it and let the rename carry the slot.

### Squadrons: DUPLICATES WITH PAINT (substance agreed by all four prongs) (source: squadrons_synthesis_2026_10_29.md)

- The spore wing is a bomber reskin (4 of 4, chair verified): typed sq:'bomber', identical speed and evasion, near-identical hull (26 vs 28). Its weapon sporeb vs bombs differs by +1 damage, +0.1 vsSq, minus 2 crit on the same shots, range, accuracy, and falloff, with the same pierce. No different delivery mechanism.
- The carrier archetype is one carrier hull plus generic fill: the generator seeds a single carrier, then fills budget with the same weighted draw every archetype uses; no escort, screen, or formation shaping. The carrier branch is skipped entirely for boss fights.
- Torpedo screen math (settled from code): one roll per wing per salvo at .45 (.65 with pickets); each wing stops after its first live torpedo, so per-wing kills cap at one per salvo while stacking compounds geometrically across wings. The cap is not stated in the stance text.
- Wing veterancy perks on the 3/8/15 XP ladder are random grants: a player can feed a wing XP but cannot steer what it becomes. Enemy wings cannot reach the ladder at all.
- Carrier value points: best value b_brood (3 hangars at cp5); worst dedicated carrier the reliquary (cp4, hangar 2, hp 130 against the Chorus hulk's cp4, hangar 2, hp 170); worst hangars per CP h_dominion (1 hangar at cp6).
- Carrier preview defect, narrowed form: effArch at battle.js:138 remaps a carrier roll to heavy for launch-less factions before generation, so no wingless carrier fleet is fielded; the preview layer still advises anti-wing preparation against fleets that will not have wings.

### Modules: MOSTLY DISTINCT JOBS, NO BUILD LAYER (3 of 4 on grade; substance agreed by all four) (source: modules_synthesis_2026_10_29.md)

- The 13 modules mostly do separable jobs. The structural defect is around the catalog: fitting is free, instant, and fully reversible with no cost, cooldown, or location restriction, so the module layer is a pre-battle checklist, not a build system. Whether fitting should gain friction is an open Ryan decision (section 9); it was never formally put to him during the Oct 29 program.
- Five modules duplicate five captain perks effect-for-effect: gunnery/auspex (+10 aim), killer/cogitator (+10 percent crit), ironhull/prow (+15 percent hull, prow adds +5 percent armor), helm/drive (+1 speed), deflector/regen (shield regeneration pair). Three-system stack: drive, the helm perk, and the compass relic all grant +1 speed into the same additive pool.
- Key collision: MODULES.cogitator and RELICS.cogitator share a key in different tables; every consumer is namespace-explicit today, so it is isolated, but it is a standing confusion risk (sources: modules_synthesis_2026_10_29.md; relics_synthesis_2026_10_29.md).
