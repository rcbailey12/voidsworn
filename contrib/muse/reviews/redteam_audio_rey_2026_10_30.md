# Red team audit: Audio designer leg (Rey, blind)

Seat: Audio designer (music and sound). Target: Voidsworn at the audit pin, browser build as the playable truth, Godot tree as the future build. Method: the evidence pack first, then direct reads of sfx.js, sfx_data.js structure, and the godot/music and godot/audio folders in the local clone to test the pack's claims. Findings are marked OBSERVED (seen in pack evidence or in a cited file) or INFERRED (reasoning from what was seen). Quotes are exact and under 20 words.

A note before the findings: the SFX engine itself is well built. Buses with reverb sends, a compressor and brickwall limiter on the master chain, voice stealing that fades instead of cutting, salvo gain compensation, ambient ducking under stings, positional pan and attenuation, and a round-robin picker that never repeats a take twice in a row are all in sfx.js as shipped. Nothing below attacks the engineering. Everything below attacks coverage: what the engine is given to play, where, and for how long.

## Findings

1. **The shipped game has no music at all. (OBSERVED)** A grep for "music" across every .js and .html file in the browser build returns no file. The only continuous sound on the campaign layer is a synthesized ambient bed plus one looping recorded roomtone in sfx.js (lines 184-196), faded in at gain .032. The pack's own core states the browser build is the playable truth today and is frozen to break fixes, so this gap persists until the Godot cutover. Cost: the game's stated feel benchmark is cinematic, in the register of a Star Wars movie and EVE Online, and the version a player can actually play today meets that benchmark with silence where the score should be. Twenty-four finished, licensed, loudness-levelled tracks exist in godot/music/tracks/ and none of them can be heard in the shipped game.

2. **No credits surface exists in either build, and eight tracks legally need one. (OBSERVED)** godot/music/CREDITS.md is complete and accurate: I verified the 24 credited titles match the 24 files in godot/music/tracks/ exactly, and music.json carries each track's attribution line. But a grep for "attribution" and "CREDITS" across every .gd and .tscn in the Godot tree returns nothing, and the browser build has no credits screen either. Eight of the 24 tracks are CC BY 3.0 or 4.0 (map_drone, calm_tactical, calm_darktimes, hot_heat, fac_voidsworn, sting_victory, sting_champion, sting_lose), licences that require attribution as a condition of use. The music README describes the stored attribution line as "ready for a credits screen" (godot/music/README.md), which is an admission that the screen does not exist. This is the concrete answer to the program brief's open music credits question: the data work is done, the presentation is missing, and the same is true for the SFX clips, whose only credit is a line-1 code comment in sfx_data.js asserting the Sonniss bundle is "royalty free, no attribution required". Cost: a compliance defect waiting for the Steam release the project has deferred, not a taste issue.

3. **The sounds the player hears most have the least variation. (OBSERVED)** sfx_data.js holds 27 recorded keys, and 16 of them have exactly one variant, including every UI clip (uiClick, uiConfirm, uiDeny, uiSelect, uiPanel), yourTurn, enemyTurn, victory, impactChord, whoosh, launch, and scrape. The post-MAP loop in sfx.js sets UI cue pitch jitter to .02, two percent. The broadside cannon, which the cue table itself calls the house sound ("kinetic broadside cannon is the house sound", SFX.CUES comment in sfx.js), has four offline-rendered synth takes and two boomLow clips behind it, recycled across fleets of up to 20 ships with a six-voice cap. Cost: this is a roguelite built for repeated long sessions, and ear fatigue lands first and hardest on the UI layer and the main gun, the two sounds with the thinnest material.

4. **The entire campaign layer rests on one ambient loop in the shipped build. (OBSERVED)** The recorded ambient in sfx_data.js is a single variant (ambient: 1), looped from a random offset over a thinner synth bed (sfx.js lines 195-196). Node choices, events, shops, regroups, and the Nemesis clock all play over that one roomtone. Cost (INFERRED from the single-variant fact): hours of campaign time share one acoustic room, which flattens the sector-to-sector and faction-to-faction contrast the rest of the game works to build. The Godot music set solves this on paper with six map tracks, which only sharpens finding 1.

5. **Faction identity barely reaches the battle soundscape. (OBSERVED)** In the cue dispatch code in evidence, only two weapon families vary by faction: lance() picks lance_dark for Eclipse and lance_choir for Glasswrights, and torp() picks a variant for four factions. Guns, hits, shields, and explosions, the bulk of every battle's sound, use one shared palette for all six factions. The Godot music does carry faction flavour tracks (fac_ files, one per faction), but battle SFX identity does not follow. Cost: six factions with distinct visual silhouettes, lore voices, and now distinct music themes all shoot and explode in the same voice, and the Chorus and Eclipse in particular have sound identities in fiction (song, machine) that the SFX layer never expresses.

6. **This seat had nothing to overturn, because audio was never audited. (OBSERVED)** The pack states it plainly: the claims register contains no audio-domain bullet, and a search of the filed FX implementation review for audio, sound, music, and sfx returns no matches. Ten Oct 29 syntheses, an FX review, and a full audit program shipped without one leg listening to the game. Cost: findings 1 through 5 survived every prior gate not because they were weighed and accepted, but because no process step owned them. Any future audit program that does not name an audio owner will reproduce this.

7. **Retired mobile assumptions still set the voice budget. (OBSERVED)** sfx.js caps voices with "maxVoices(){ return G.mobile?20:34; }", and setOn carries the comment "a muted game costs no audio CPU on phones", in a game whose phone target was dropped and which is PC only. The claims register separately records that the retired mobile layer misclassifies real PCs (the drift verdict). INFERRED cost: on any desktop the mobile check misreads, the densest broadsides in the game get the 20-voice budget instead of 34, thinning exactly the moments the salvo system was built to thicken. Minor on its own; it is the audio instance of a drift problem already ruled real elsewhere.

8. **Battle sound triggers are keyed to display strings. (OBSERVED)** The banner() function in sfx.js decides which sting plays by matching the banner's text and color: "if(s==='Your turn')", "if(color==='#ff8a6a')", "if(s==='Sector flagship')". Any rename of a banner string, or a color tweak by the VFX seat, silently removes or misassigns its sound, with no error anywhere. Cost: a fragile coupling between the text layer and the audio layer that will break quietly during exactly the kind of polish pass this audit is meant to precede. The Godot SFX port (godot/audio/sfx.gd, cues.json) is the chance to key cues to events instead of captions.

9. **The browser build gives the player one knob for all sound. (OBSERVED)** All output runs through a single volume (META.opts.sfxVol) and a single on/off (G.sfxOn). There is no way to tame UI clicks relative to weapons, or ambience relative to impacts. The Godot build adds a separate music volume (settings in godot/main/main.gd line 20, slider in godot/main/title.gd line 200), which proves the need was felt, but SFX sub-mixes remain one knob there too on the evidence read. Cost: players who find one layer fatiguing (see finding 3) can only turn everything down, including the cues that carry game state.

10. **The synth library is rendered at 32 kHz. (OBSERVED)** sfx.js sets SR:32000 and renders every synthesized cue offline at that rate. INFERRED cost: a 16 kHz ceiling on all synthesized material, which caps the air and edge available to the laser, plasma, and shield cues that live in the top octave. This is the smallest finding here and the cheapest to change at the Godot port, where synth takes are already being re-rendered to files under godot/audio/.

## Credit where due

The Godot audio work is genuinely strong and mostly integrated, not vaporware: the music player is instantiated in godot/main/main.gd (lines 91-95), battle start calls play_battle (godot/scenes/battle/battle.gd line 502), intensity is fed live combat stats (line 642), and victory and defeat stings are wired (lines 1687-1689). The adaptive design uses a calm-to-intense handover band (0.38 to 0.62) with slow smoothing in music_player.gd, which is the right shape for a tactics game. The Godot SFX port renders the cue table to files with multiple takes per clip (hullHit alone has six variants in godot/audio/clips/, against the same six in the browser data). The problem is not that the future build lacks audio. The problem is that the present build does, and that nobody's checklist includes the credits screen or the campaign's single roomtone.

## Verdict

NOT READY on sound: a well-engineered SFX engine playing to an empty hall. The single most important attack is finding 1: the shipped, playable game has no music whatsoever, while 24 finished tracks sit unheard in the migration tree, so the game's loudest promise, cinematic fleet battles, is currently made in silence.

## Not checked

- The mix by ear: relative loudness, fatigue over a real hour of play, and whether the -18 LUFS loop levelling sits right under the SFX. No listening is possible from a pack or a code read.
- The Sonniss GDC 2026 bundle's actual licence terms. The sfx_data.js comment asserts no attribution is required; the bundle's own licence text was not in evidence and I did not verify it.
- The Godot music check (godot/music/test/music_check.gd) and its PASS claim; I did not run Godot headless.
- Which cue each Eclipse and Chorus weapon in data.js actually triggers in battle; the dispatch functions were in evidence, the full weapon-to-cue mapping was not traced.
- Whether the Godot build's adaptive intensity feels right in play, as opposed to reading correctly in code.
- Hearing accessibility beyond the banner duplication visible in banner(): whether every audio-carried state change (boss arrival, adaptation, weakness reveal) has a visual equivalent.
- Audio CPU and memory cost on the target PC hardware, and behaviour under the browser's autoplay and suspend policies beyond what the code comments describe.
