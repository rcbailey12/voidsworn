## The one-line state of the sky

Everything around the sky got its V2 (planets, asteroids, stations, ships, the on-board nebula) and the sky itself did not: the starfield is still the pre-1766 snow, the bake is still 8-bit at 4096x2048, two faction nebula palettes still break RULEBOOK 10.9, and the galactic band is still multiplied down to 0.06. Issue 1766 was approved Nov 22 (R419, R421) and is now in build with updated points; this leg judges what is on main today and whether that build still aims at the right sky.

## Findings

1. Star snow, unchanged. OBSERVED. godot/world/world_sky.gd, the SKY shader's star line: "star_layer(d, 120.0, 0.955, 1.0, bd * 0.03) + star_layer(d, 300.0, 0.985, 0.55, bd * 0.012) + star_layer(d, 650.0, 0.994, 0.35, bd * 0.004)". Three grid layers at thresholds 0.955/0.985/0.994 put on the order of 85,000 stars on the sphere (the plan doc's count), thousands inside one 45-degree battle frame, against RULEBOOK 10.11's "About 400 star pinpoints in a 45-degree battle frame". Worse than the count is the floor inside star_layer: "acc += temp_col(...) * exp(...) * gain * (0.15 + 2.6 * m)", so even the faintest kept star renders at 15 percent of full gain, and sig = px * (0.65 + 0.9 * m) makes them soft blobs rather than pinpoints. The result is a near-uniform grey-white noise floor behind every battle, which is exactly what Cohesion Law 1 Band 1 forbids ("0.00 to 0.015 (near-black)", "Majority of the sky frame"). Fix: the 1766 build's cut (about 400 per 45-degree view, band-clustered, steep magnitude curve, no flat floor, sig tightened but kept at or above about half a pixel at 1080p so faint stars do not shimmer when the camera orbits).

2. Dust lanes do not black out stars. OBSERVED. godot/world/world.gd:524: "m.set_shader_parameter("star_floor", float(sf) if sf != "" else 0.15)" and the shader default at world_sky.gd (SKY): "uniform float star_floor = 0.15;". Stars seen through the baked nebula are mixed at star_floor (sky line: "col += st * star_k * mix(star_floor, 1.0, T)"), so 15 percent of the starfield punches through even fully opaque dust. RULEBOOK 10.11 sets star_floor = 0.0 with the VS_STAR_FLOOR override kept. Until this flips, the dark rifts the bake works hard to carve (the alpha channel is literally "how much starlight gets through") can never read as black. Fix: default 0.0 in both places, keep the env override.

3. The galactic band is built, then dimmed to near-invisibility. OBSERVED. world_sky.gd, galaxy_band(): "return col * lum * band * 0.06". The band has a core, a wide skirt, a warm galactic centre and a real rift term ("rift = 1.0 - exp(-yr * yr) * smoothstep(0.35, 0.6, rn) * 0.85 - ..."), and then the whole thing is scaled by 0.06, so the sky's one natural landmark contributes almost nothing and the frame's depth cue defaults back to the star noise. With the stars cut (finding 1), the band has to become the depth anchor, and it should get its drama from the rift contrast, not from raw brightness: the cohesion budget puts landmarks at 0.03 to 0.25 linear (Band 2), so a core peak held under roughly 0.18 linear with darker rifts is the right shape. Fix: raise the band into Band 2 by darkening the rift side (let rift approach 0 in the lanes) rather than only multiplying the core up.

4. Two faction nebula palettes still break the no-purple, no-mustard rules, and they feed every sky those factions touch. OBSERVED. godot/fx/faction_looks.json on main: deathless nebula is [[0.1, 0.04, 0.16], [0.34, 0.14, 0.52], [1.0, 0.84, 0.55]], a true purple mid colour, against RULEBOOK 10.9 ("no indigo to purple gradient") and 10.11 ("No mustard fog and no purple Eclipse nebula"); chorus nebula carries [0.55, 0.38, 0.12] and [0.8, 0.9, 0.35], the mustard-olive pair the crystal-belt complaint is about. These colours become c1/c2/c3 in world_sky.gd setting() for every battle in those factions' sectors, and the rival mood lerps other skies toward them too. There is also a generator-level hazard in setting(): when a faction's two gas colours sit close in hue, c2 is pushed toward a channel-swapped complement ("c2 = c2.lerp(Vector3(c1.z, c1.x * 0.8, c1.y) * 0.6, 0.55)"), which can manufacture hues nobody picked, including purples, from compliant inputs. Fix: re-palette the two faction entries (Eclipse to dark ash-obsidian with an ember rim, Chorus to cold slate dust with mineral cyan-amber), and clamp the complement swap so its result stays inside the cohesion hue rules.

5. The regional sky nebula is brightest exactly where the V2 foreground needs calm. OBSERVED values, INFERRED consequence. world_sky.gd REGION table (line 36): the "nebula" region runs neb 1.0 and cover 0.72, frontier neb 0.55 cover 0.5, and the bake then pushes saturation ("col = max(mix(vec3(lum), col, 1.45), vec3(0.0))") and a luminance gain on top. Since 1766 was scoped, the foreground changed underneath this: the on-board nebula is now one rose colour (10.9), storms are cyan, planets and ships went V2 with measured value bands. A faction-coloured sky nebula covering 72 percent of the frame at full gain competes with all of it and breaks Band 1's "majority of the sky frame" near-black rule in the very region named for nebulae. The sky gas should sit behind and below the board (the setting already places its brightest feature below the board ahead, feature y about -0.42) and read as distant weather, not as a second subject. Fix: pull neb and cover down for the regional sky (deepest cut in the nebula region), keep the drama in the band and the rifts, and let the on-board rose nebula be the only loud gas in frame.

6. The bake is still 8-bit, and size is being spent where the eye cannot use it. OBSERVED. world_sky.gd BAKE_BODY: "uniform vec2 full_size = vec2(4096.0, 2048.0)" and the output is gamma-encoded into an 8-bit image with a dither ("g += (h3(...) - 0.5) / 255.0"). Issue 1502 exists because dark gradients band at 8-bit; the dither hides it at rest but the sky is read through a 2.2 power in the frame shader and mood gains, which re-stretches the dark steps. Meanwhile 4096 wide is spent on a backdrop whose finest detail is gas folds, while the per-pixel stars (the only truly sharp element) are already drawn in the frame shader, not the bake. Fix (with 1502): bake 2048x1024 in 16-bit half float, linear, no mipmaps on neb_tex; spend the saved bake time on more raymarch steps if anything.

7. Small depth-killers behind the board. OBSERVED. world.gd dust() seeds 1,300 to 2,600 dust specks "through the space round and below the board" (belt 2600, graveyard 2000, storm 1600, default 1300), and the bake scatters five far galaxies (galaxies(), sizes 0.004 to 0.012 rad) plus six lamp stars with wide halo terms in the frame shader ("exp(-a * a / 0.00006) * 0.25 + 0.0006 / (0.0004 + a * a) * 0.08"). Each is defensible alone; together with findings 1 and 5 they keep the mid-ground busy at exactly the depths where ship silhouettes should be alone. The far galaxies are the worst offenders per pixel: soft bright ellipses parked behind ships read as smudges, not depth. Fix: thin the dust counts, shrink or drop the far galaxies behind the board's hemisphere, and keep lamp halos tight.

## Is the 1766 build still aimed right?

Mostly yes, and the newer foreground work strengthens rather than weakens it. Planets V2, Asteroids V2 and Ship Exteriors V2 all moved toward measured value bands and grounded palettes; a near-black, band-anchored sky is the only backdrop those pieces can all sit in front of without a value fight (Cohesion Law 1 is explicit that Band 1 is the majority of the frame). Two adjustments from this leg: (a) sequence the regional nebula pull-down (finding 5) inside the same build, since the star cut alone will make the sky gas relatively louder; (b) treat the faction palette fixes (finding 4) as part of the sky build, not a separate faction pass, because the sky is where those colours cover the most pixels. The Dead Star seam work is already scoped in the 1766 build brief against the real depth cause; this leg did not re-derive it and does not second-guess it.

## Top 5 upgrades, ranked (what Ryan would see)

1. The star cut with star_floor 0.0 (findings 1 and 2). The battle frame goes from TV static to deep space: ships, beams and hex lines sit on near-black, dust lanes carve real black shapes, and a few hero stars gleam by temperature colour. This is the single biggest visual change available in the game right now.
2. The band as the landmark (finding 3). A tilted Milky Way with dark rifts crossing it gives every battle a horizon and a sense of place, different per sector because band_n is seeded per setting, without raising the frame's average brightness.
3. Calm the regional sky gas (finding 5). The rose on-board nebula, cyan storms, comets and V2 planets stop competing with a second, faction-coloured sky nebula; nebula regions still feel gaseous, but distant and below the board.
4. Palette compliance (finding 4). Eclipse skies stop being purple (ash-obsidian and ember instead, which also matches Eclipse's own ships), Chorus skies lose the mustard, and no generator path can reintroduce either.
5. The 16-bit bake at half size (finding 6). Dark gradients stop banding when the camera moves or a mood gain lifts them, and sector loads get faster, not slower.

## References, and what to take from each

- The Expanse (TV): near-black space, sparse hard stars, ships lit by one key source. Take: darkness is the default, light is information.
- Homeworld (games, esp. the remasters): the skybox is a painted landmark (one strong nebula gesture per map) behind clean silhouettes, never noise. Take: one gesture per sky, placed with intent (this game's feature direction already does this; protect it).
- EVE Online: regional colour identity in the sky, but the brightest gas sits low and far, and the tactical foreground is dark. Take: region identity through the band and rift tints, not through cover.
- Sins of a Solar Empire: the star and its planets carry the frame; the starfield itself is restrained. Take: the anchor planet plus band is composition enough.
- Battlestar Galactica (2004): almost empty black around the fleet, which makes every light on a hull read. Take: confidence to leave most of the frame empty.
- Elite Dangerous: a real galactic plane with a dust-lane rift you can orient by. Take: the rift is the landmark, not the glow.
- Interstellar and 2001 (film): the dead star should be a black disc with a thin bright rim against near-nothing, not a textured ball. Take: for the Dead Star board specifically, restraint around the hole sells scale.
