## Sheet 1: story and career mode scope

What Ryan will see under the pick: every admiral speaks in text
boxes that already exist (picking a leader, entering a sector,
spotting the flagship, winning, losing). He can rename his
flagship and earn a title from how he fights. A Career tab shows
per-admiral records and a medals case. Then, per faction, a
3-beat arc: a real choice in sector 1, the consequence plus a
named rival admiral in sector 2, and a flagship showdown with
that rival in sector 3. Finishing an arc pays a hull variant or
the rival's Command, recorded in a Chronicle tab.

Why this pick: all three legs agree on it exactly, and it matches
the approved plan (B5: A first, then B) and the career review's
own pick. A gives B's rivals a voice and gives B's payouts a
visible place to land. C is rejected by all three legs for the
same two reasons: it touches the save layer (the highest-risk
piece) and a flagship that never truly dies undercuts the
permadeath tension that makes the Hall of the Fallen matter.

The four dials, decided:
- Story length: 3 beats per faction, one per sector. Five beats
  is possible later with writing only, no new machinery; it is
  not the launch scope.
- Text volume: 100 to 150 words per beat. Lean enough for one
  event card, roomy enough for a choice to land.
- Voice: text only for launch. Short radio stingers (Argon) and
  recorded barks (Qwen) are post-launch add-ons that do not touch
  the story system; starting with voice would lock text that is
  still being revised.
- Portraits in UI: glyph crests now, paintings later per Sheet 3.
  Nothing in career mode waits on a painting.

What stays open for Ryan: confirm the four dials, then read and
approve the 6 faction story outlines before Option B ships
(plan B5's exit gate).

## Sheet 2: music style for an adaptive score

Chair pick: brass and tactical percussion backbone for battle,
synth drone for exploration, organ and machine layers only as
faction colour.

What Ryan will hear under the pick: on the sector map, quiet
drone and sparse horn, the lonely-vast feel. In battle, drums
and brass take over and swell as capital ships trade fire, with
strings tightening as hull damage mounts. Victory gets a brass
fanfare; defeat drops to slowed strings. Faction colour rides on
top of that one system: strict march snare for Hegemony, rougher
percussion for Corsairs, chant and drone for Voidsworn, wet
organic percussion for Chorus, metallic drone instead of brass
for Deathless. Organ and machine-synth layers appear only for
the fanatical and machine factions, never as the sound of the
whole game.

Why this pick, and what happened to DeepSeek's pick: Qwen and
Argon both land on brass for battle weight, and that majority
holds because the game's core feel is tactical weight on a hex
grid, not atmosphere alone. DeepSeek's Void ritual (drone, chant,
dread) is not thrown out; its vocabulary becomes the
exploration layer and the Chorus and Deathless colour, where
dread belongs. Pure ritual as the main direction would make
every faction sound the same and would under-sell the weight of
a capital-ship exchange. Pure organ (Argon's Option 2) would
make disciplined factions sound like a horror cult, which is
Argon's own warning and the chair agrees.

## Sheet 3: admiral portraits direction

Chair pick: finish Chorus, then 1 poster admiral per remaining
faction, glyph crests for the rest.

What Ryan will see under the pick: the Chorus set closed
(Voice v4 is already final; Matriarch v3 gets his verdict; a
fresh Mending Choir gets generated), then one painted flagship
face per other faction for run selection and for Option B's
rival boss fights, with clean glyph crests everywhere else
until a portrait screen exists and proves where paintings sit.

Why this pick: it is Argon's Option 1, and it beats both other
legs' shapes. DeepSeek's two-track keeps non-Chorus painting
moving in parallel, which still spends art review time on
portraits for a screen the game does not have yet. Qwen's
Chorus-first-then-batch has no stopping point once batching
starts. The poster cap gives a defined next step: 6 paintings
(the fresh Mending plus 5 posters), with Matriarch v3 as a
verdict, not a painting. Every faction gets a face where a face
matters most (selection and rival fights) for a third of the
review work of painting everything.

Template rules, unchanged: three-quarter bust, lit from the
left in faction glow, dark faction plate, painterly finish,
thin faction frame, no text, no insignia lettering, and every
portrait reflects its admiral's Command. Chorus modifiers stay:
grown flesh, membrane, chitin, bile, growth, gross; no human
features, no recognizable Earth-animal anatomy, no buildings,
instruments, or architecture.

Mending Choir, fresh direction (not a v3 retry, in any form):
- Chair lean: Argon's "Living Suture-Hive": a pulsing
  biological cocoon-graft, translucent amber nursing sacs and
  curved chitin bone-needles stitching glowing pink membrane
  webbing across shattered dark carapace, lit from the left in
  sickly Chorus glow. It reads as Regrowth tide directly (every
  ship regrows 25% hull and rebuilds destroyed sections), with
  zero human, animal, or architectural shapes. When briefed for
  generation, say carapace, not hull plate, so it does not read
  as a built thing.
- Alternates, from DeepSeek (all fresh seeds, explicitly not
  retries): regrowth seam, brood sac, scar shell.
- Always open, from Qwen: Ryan names a fresh direction in his
  own words, starting clean without referencing the v3 attempt.

What stays open for Ryan, in order: (1) Matriarch v3 verdict,
  thumbs up or down, tested on whether it reads as Spawning
  surge brood-sac swarms; (2) Mending direction: approve the
  Suture-Hive, pick a DeepSeek seed, or name his own; (3) poster
  batch: approve the 1-per-faction cap, or overrule toward
  painting all of them now or pausing after Chorus.

Portrait count note: this synthesis uses the pack's framing
("the 15 unmade portraits" plus the two open Chorus questions)
and does not assert an inventory total. The three legs disagree
on whether 15 includes the Chorus trio, and no portrait
inventory was in the pack. The exact count stays pack-framed
until it is confirmed against the inventory.
