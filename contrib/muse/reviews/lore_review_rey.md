# Faction lore review, round 4: Rey audit leg (blind)

Reviewer: Rey. This is an independent audit leg, written from the brief, the evidence pack and lore_bible_r4.md only. Line numbers count from the bible file's first line ("# Lore bible, round 4") as line 1, and were checked against the file line by line. Outside claims carry a source link; anything I could not source is tagged UNSURE. Settled decisions in the brief are not relitigated; where a locked name has a problem I flag the problem and leave the call to Ryan.

Already handled by the project, recorded here so they are not mistaken for new findings: "the Umbral" (rejected, Endless Space 2's Umbral Choir) and "the Halo" (rejected, too close to Halo). On "Sunward": I agree with the project's acceptable call. Sunward is the title of an Eclipse Phase supplement about the inner Solar System, a different sense and a different medium shelf (https://pandemoniumbooks.com/products/eclipse-phase-sunward).

## Job 1: originality and collision check

### Real collision (one item, two names)

**Turbolaser** and **Heavy turbolaser** (Hegemony weapons, line 248). REAL COLLISION. "Turbolaser" is a coined Star Wars word: it was first uttered in Episode IV, and heavy turbolaser batteries are the signature armament of Star Destroyers, listed at sixty per ship in the Star Destroyer armament tables (https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Heavy_turbolaser_turret). This is the same sense, ship guns on a space navy, and it collides with the draft's own naming rule 1, which bars names lifted from Star Wars (section 1.4). It is also the kind of word rule 1's own examples were written to catch. Alternatives in keeping with the draft's rules: **beam cannon** and **heavy beam cannon** (plain, technical, no owner), or **ray lance** if a second lance family is wanted next to the plasma lances.

### Minor echoes

Each of these has a prior use a well read player may notice. None blocks use on its own; together they are the draft's remaining echo load.

- **Glasswrights** (locked name). MINOR ECHO, and the one locked name with a real prior coined use: it is the name of the guild at the centre of Mindy Klasky's five novel fantasy series, The Glasswrights' Apprentice and its sequels, published 2000 to 2004 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindy_L._Klasky). Different sense (a medieval guild, not starship engineers) and a small audience, but it sits in exactly the genre Ryan rejected in rounds 2 and 3. Flagged, not relitigated: the name is locked.
- **Corran** (Eclipsed admiral Corran of the Long Dusk, lines 528 and 529). MINOR ECHO. Corran Horn is a major Star Wars character, a Rogue Squadron pilot who becomes a Jedi (https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Corran_Horn/Legends). One letter is not much distance for a named admiral. Cheap fix inside the pool: respell or swap the name (Corvane works); Corin is not available as the replacement, since it already sits in the Glasswright pool (Job 2, item 6).
- **Sabine** (Eclipsed name pool, line 528). MINOR ECHO. Sabine Wren is a headline Star Wars character from Rebels and Ahsoka (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_Wren). A background pool name, so the echo is faint, but Star Wars is the franchise this draft works hardest to avoid.
- **Mallory Nine-Fingers** (Hullbreakers, line 300). MINOR ECHO. Logen Ninefingers, called the Bloody-Nine, is Joe Abercrombie's most famous character, named for his missing finger (https://uploads.strikinglycdn.com/files/e0ff22a8-b13c-457b-a63a-a8f1911bd07b/7609684401.pdf). A maimed fighter with a finger counting name is the same joke in the same register.
- **The Ascension** (the setting's central event, line 16 and throughout). MINOR ECHO. Ascension is the title of the 2008 Mass Effect novel by Drew Karpyshyn, and "ascension" language for beings who upload or transcend is well worn in science fiction generally (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect:_Ascension). See also Job 3: the word is doing religious work the draft says it does not want.
- **Astra** (patron, lines 51 and 123). MINOR ECHO. Astra is the first word of Astra Militarum, the Imperial Guard's official name in Warhammer 40k since 6th edition (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/mediawiki/index.php?title=Astra_Militarum&oldid=615411). "Astra" alone is a Latin word for stars and no one owns it, but in a draft with a standing no 40k rule it is worth a conscious keep.
- **Hegemony** (faction name). MINOR ECHO. Dan Simmons' Hyperion books centre on the Hegemony of Man, a human interstellar state quietly guided and manipulated by the TechnoCore, a civilisation of AIs (https://www.theastromech.com/2025/06/the-hyperion-cantos-chronological-guide.html). "Hegemony" is a common English word and the draft's rule 1 allows common words; what makes this an echo is the pairing, a Hegemony plus a guiding machine intelligence. The draft's Registry rules openly rather than secretly, which is real distance. Keep with eyes open.
- **Kharon** (patron, lines 48 and 120). MINOR ECHO. Kharon is one letter from Charon, and in Mass Effect the Charon Relay, Pluto's ice covered moon, is the door into the relay network, activated in 2149 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Effect:_Revelation?oldid=437186325). Kharon is also simply the Greek ferryman's name in an older spelling, which is presumably the intent. Faint; keep.
- **Vyre** (patron, lines 49 and 121). MINOR ECHO. House Vyre is one of the great houses of the Retribution of Scyrah in Warmachine, with its own line of warjacks (https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2147684902/Harpy---Vyre-Light-Warjack). Tabletop niche, different sense. Faint; keep.
- **Seren** (patron, lines 53 and 125). MINOR ECHO. Seren is RuneScape's crystalline goddess of the elves and divine aspect of light, set against her brother Zaros, who is darkness (https://runescape.wiki/w/Seren). A light goddess named Seren is an awkward neighbour for a patron who shapes the unaugmented humans, and it double books the light side of the setting's light and dark pairing. Worth a look precisely because the pairing is similar.
- **Brood-node Vesk** (Chorus ship name, line 379). MINOR ECHO. The vesk are one of Starfinder's core species, seven foot militant lizardfolk of the Veskarium, a Paizo headline race (https://paizo.com/community/blog/v5748dyo5ljo0&page=last?Unveiling-a-New-Starfinder-Race-The-Vesk). One background ship name; dropping or respelling it (Vesq, Bresk) costs nothing.
- **The Hunger Below** (patron, lines 50 and 122). MINOR ECHO. Sea of Thieves' first big content update was The Hungering Deep (2018), a campaign about summoning and killing a giant sea beast (https://seaofthieves.fandom.com/wiki/The_Hungering_Deep). Different medium and sense; the shared shape is "the hunger" plus a depth word. Faint; keep.
- **Reaver, Reaver-captain, Reaver-king** (Hullbreaker class and titles, lines 306 and 310). MINOR ECHO. In Firefly, Reavers are the savage cannibal raiders of the edge of space (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaver_(Firefly)). "Reaver" is an old English word for a raider, so this is register overlap rather than a lift, but the register is identical: brutal space raiders. Keep, knowingly.
- **Marauder** (Hullbreaker class, line 310). MINOR ECHO. The Marauder is one of BattleTech's signature machines, described by Sarna as arguably the most iconic early BattleMech (https://www.sarna.net/wiki/index.php?title=Marauder&direction=next&oldid=391088). A generic raiding word used by many games; faint.
- **Colossus class** (the Ninefold Maw's class, line 388). MINOR ECHO. In Stellaris, the Colossus is the planet killer weapon added by the Apocalypse expansion (https://www.xbox.com/en-SG/games/store/stellaris-apocalypse/9PF99B4WXVV7). A size word, not a name lift; faint.
- **Second Dawn** (Eclipsed relic, line 536). MINOR ECHO, plus an internal problem: Second Dawn is the name of the doomsday cult in The 100 (https://www.denofgeek.com/?p=599602). Worse, "dawn" belongs to the Sunward Crusade by the motif table (line 103), so an Eclipsed relic is wearing the Crusade's word. Renaming this relic fixes both problems at once: candidates, Second Dark, Long Memory.
- **Nyssa** (Glasswright pool, line 464). MINOR ECHO. Nyssa of Traken was a Doctor Who companion across 1981 to 1983 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyssa_(Doctor_Who)). Faint on its own; it matters as part of the pool pattern in Job 3.
- **Corin** (Glasswright pool, line 464). MINOR ECHO. Corin is the Prince of Archenland in C. S. Lewis's The Horse and His Boy (https://pressbooks.library.upei.ca/artsreview-x/chapter/identity-environment-and-escape-in-the-horse-and-his-boy-and-the-tombs-of-atuan/). Faint; the bigger problem is internal (Job 2, item 6).
- **Vaelen** (Glasswright pool, line 464). MINOR ECHO. One letter from Valen, Babylon 5's revered Minbari religious leader and prophet (https://En-Academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/428419). Faint; again, the pool pattern matters more (Job 3).
- **Voice of the Deep** (Chorus admiral, line 343). MINOR ECHO. In Destiny, the Darkness is called "the Deep" throughout the Books of Sorrow (https://gamerant.com/destiny-book-sorry-oryx-dead-vex/). A descriptive title rather than a name; faint.
- **Malek Draven** (Crusade name being replaced, line 191). MINOR ECHO, UNSURE on weight. The sound sits between Malekith (Warhammer and Marvel) and Draven (a League of Legends champion). The draft already retires this name to Oath-Captain, so no action is needed; noting it so the replacement is not reversed later.
- **The Chorus** (locked name). MINOR ECHO at most. The draft itself handles this at section 5.14 (line 400): a chorus in a play speaks as one "we", and the 2021 shooter Chorus is a game title, not a faction. I agree with the draft's reasoning. No action.

### Story level questions

**Is "uploaded machine people who killed their star" too close to anything known? Verdict: MINOR ECHO overall, no single work matches the whole shape.** The nearest neighbours are partial. Stellaris lets a crisis empire build Star Eaters that detonate stars into black holes to fuel an ascension engine, which is the same two beats (star into black hole, in service of an ascension) reached from the villain's chair rather than from history (https://www.cbr.com/stellaris-nemesis-dlc-playing/). Sins of a Solar Empire's Advent are the closest story neighbour for a different faction: humans exiled by the Traders who return transformed and vengeful (see Job 4). The Necron silhouette (ancient machine people, undying) is the obvious visual neighbour, and the draft's own avoid list in section 7.2 works hard against it. Mass Effect's Reapers, Stargate's ascended beings, Halo's Forerunners and Destiny's Vex each share one element (machine gods, ascension, dead precursors, machine swarms) and none shares the combination of a self made black hole, a surviving machine people who regret it, and a plan to build a replacement sun. The regret is the draft's own twist and it carries the distance.

**Is "AI run human state that writes off a colony" too close to anything known? Verdict: MINOR ECHO, a broad trope with one named neighbour.** The named neighbour is Hyperion again: a Hegemony whose infrastructure is run by AIs that guide it in secret (source above). The draft's Registry differs in the way that matters: it rules openly, in the open vocabulary of projections and acceptable losses, and the horror is procedural rather than conspiratorial. The "crew expendable" moral shape is older than any one franchise (the Weyland-Yutani parallel is real but UNSURE as a citable claim here, so I note it without a source and without weight). No collision; the written off colony as the founding wound of a breakaway faction is the draft's own move.

## Job 2: internal consistency

Contradictions and strains, each with line numbers, the passages in conflict, and a one line fix. Numbering is mine.

1. **The Chorus colour fix is only half applied.** Lines 38 and 345 say the Chorus is "Moving off purple" and that violet "now belongs to the Eclipsed", but the ships line still reads "Purple-black flesh" (line 344). Fix: change line 344 to "Olive-black flesh" so all three colour statements agree.
2. **Motif clash: grief is assigned to the Glasswrights but spent by two other factions.** The motif table gives "Light, glass, reflections, grief, what was taken" to the Glasswrights (line 99), yet the Eclipsed voice is "quietly grieving" (line 487) and the Chorus name pools include "Grieving Tide" (line 378) and "Mournful Gut" (line 379). Fix: either narrow line 99 to light and glass, or rename the Chorus pair (Starving Tide, Empty Gut) and let the Eclipsed keep one grief word as the siblings' shared wound, stated as an exception.
3. **Motif clash: dawn, dusk, noon, cold and burning are assigned to the Crusade but spent by the Eclipsed and the Glasswrights.** Line 103 gives "Oath, chalk names, dawn and dusk, burning, cold, brands" to the Sunward Crusade. Against that: the Eclipsed are "Cold, exact" (line 487), field "Corona Vessane the Burning Hand" (line 529), "Corran of the Long Dusk" (lines 528 and 529), the relic "Second Dawn" (line 536), the saying "We remember noon" (line 524) and the epithet "of the Last Noon" (line 528), while a Glasswright ship is "Held Noon" (line 466). Some of this is defensible (the Eclipsed remember a sun, so noon is memory, and line 101 gives them "the lost sun, warmth remembered"), but the table as written bans it. Fix: amend line 103 to give the Crusade dawn and dusk as lived time, and add one line to section 1.3 allowing the Eclipsed noon and cold as remembered things only.
4. **One name, one owner (rule 3, line 110) is strained inside two factions.** In the Eclipsed, Zenith is a rank (line 534), an admiral's name (Zenith Aurex, line 529) and a ship class (line 535); Corona is a rank (line 534), an admiral's name (line 529) and the Corona arc weapon (line 536); The Unsetting is both Aurex's epithet (line 529) and the flagship's name (line 535). In the Glasswrights, Facet is a title (line 465), a rank (line 470) and a ship class (line 471). "Keeper" titles appear in three factions: Keeper of the Oath (line 191), Keeper of Astra (line 465), Keeper of the Archive (line 534). Fix: rename the ship classes so ranks and admirals keep the words (for example zenith class to pinnacle class, facet class to lens class), give the flagship a name of its own instead of the epithet, and drop one of the three Keeper titles.
5. **The draft breaks its own rule 4, "No apostrophes inside names" (line 111), with the Crusade ship name "Vesper's Debt" (line 192).** Fix: "Vespers Debt" reads badly; use "The Debt of Vesper" or fold it into the existing pattern as "Vesper Debt".
6. **Near name pairs across factions will be misread as the same person.** Glasswright pool "Corin" (line 464) against Eclipsed admiral "Corran" (lines 528 and 529); Crusade pool "Serafine" (line 190) against patron "Seren" (lines 53 and 125). Fix: change one name in each pair; the pairs cannot both stay. The replacement picks are a taste call, so no specific names are recommended here.
7. **The Eclipsed's patron carries the Crusade's signature phrase.** Kharon's boons are "Armour, holding the line" (line 48) and the Eclipsed "fight the way it taught them, enduring" (line 120), while the Crusade's own fight line is "They brace and hold the line" (line 171). Fix: give Kharon a different verb in both places ("armour, outlasting" or "armour, standing fast"), leaving hold the line to the Crusade alone.
8. **How patrons are accessed is stated two ways that do not quite meet.** Line 118 says a core "chooses which fleets to help by how they fight" and line 127 says "Any fleet can draw on any core", yet Vyre is physically captured and reverse engineered by the Hegemony (lines 49, 121 and 222) and Kharon is held by the Eclipsed (line 500), who call the cores "our keys" (line 118). The draft never says whether capture blocks other fleets from drawing on a core. Fix: add one sentence to section 1.5 stating the rule (for example: capture gives the holder first call and its tech, but a core can still answer any fleet that fights its way).
9. **The cores are scattered across systems with no line explaining how they travelled.** The Lattice "tore apart" around one star (line 16), yet Seren was "Salvaged from Vesper's outer belt" (line 53; also line 125) and the Hunger Below "crashed into that ocean moon" in another system (lines 50, 122 and 333). Fix: add one line to section 1.5 (the collapse flung the cores out on long orbits, or fleets carried them off as salvage in the dark years), whichever the project prefers.
10. **The timeline has exactly one hard number.** The Eclipsed have lived in the dark "for a thousand years" (lines 483 and 499). Nothing dates the Dimming's start or Vesper's secession, although the Crusade oath has been sworn "every generation since" (line 135) and the Hullbreakers call this war "the biggest in memory" (line 257). A thousand years of Eclipsed dark against a human colony's generations of oath swearing needs the Dimming to be recent, and the draft never says so. Fix: add a three line timeline (collapse, Dimming begins, Vesper secedes) to section 1.1.
11. **The collapse mechanism points the wrong way as written.** Line 16: they were "pulling mass and energy out of the star faster than it could bear. The star's core failed and it collapsed into a black hole". Star lifting, the real concept the draft is using, removes mass from a star to optimise its output and extend its lifespan, which pushes away from collapse, not toward it (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_lifting). A black hole forms when a remnant core exceeds the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit, roughly 2.2 to 2.9 solar masses (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff_limit). Within soft sci fi this is a one sentence repair, not a rebuild. Fix: make Caelum's star massive and old, its core already near the limit, and let the overdrive break the Lattice's containment so lifted mass fell back onto the core at the worst moment.
12. **Two opinion lines blame the Lattice for the Dimming, but the Dimming is the siphon arrays.** Crusade on Glasswrights: "They built the machine that does this" (line 178). Glasswrights on Crusade: "Their star is dying because of what we built" (line 449). What the Glasswrights built is the Lattice, which is wreckage (line 471 and section 6.10); the Dimming is the Eclipsed "fed by energy siphoned from neighbouring stars" (line 483) through "siphon arrays" (line 500). Fix: repoint both lines at the siphons and the brothers who run them ("They built the machine their brothers now run" and "because of what our brothers built from our work").
13. **The Eclipsed call the Chorus's data "our archive", but the Archive is their own system.** Line 516: "It holds our archive in its throat." The Archive is where "every uploaded mind is backed up" (line 499). What the Chorus holds is Caelan data stored inside the Hunger Below core, which became its second voice, Memory (line 333). Fix: "It holds our dead in its throat" keeps the force and removes the clash.
14. **The Glasswrights' want and their behaviour disagree about Astra.** They want the cores "recovered and shut down for good" (lines 21 and 418) and to "recover all six cores, starting with Astra, which they already hold, and shut them down" (line 436), yet they hold Astra (lines 51 and 123) and their whole fighting style is built around its gifts of lances and precision. Fix: add one line in section 6.4 stating the terms on which they use it (caged and throttled, flown only to hunt the other cores, and the last one they will shut down), or state that they refuse its boons.
15. **Palette clash the brief did not name: Crusade ships against Hullbreaker ships.** Crusade: "black iron hulls, rust-red trim, ember-orange windows and engines" (line 161). Hullbreakers: "soot black and rust, dried-blood red trim, dirty red-orange engines" (line 266). Two factions, black hulls with red to red orange accents; at board distance they read as one family with different silhouettes. Fix: shift one palette (my recommendation: Hullbreakers toward rust brown and their trophy colours, which line 266 already sets up, and let the Crusade keep black iron and ember orange alone).
16. **"Bile plasma" spends the Hegemony's signature word.** The Chorus base weapon is "Bile plasma (was Bio-plasma)" (line 392), while plasma is the Hegemony's identity weapon family, light blue by ruling (line 248). Fix: rename to "Bile spray" or "Bile jets"; the Chorus has no need of the word plasma.
17. **"Phase" is a signature word for two factions at once.** Glasswrights make "phase jumps" (line 440); the Eclipsed have a "phase drive" (line 494) and a "Phase engine" relic (line 536). Fix: the Eclipsed keep phase (it matches passing through wrecks); the Glasswrights' movement becomes "skip jumps" or "short jumps", which also fits the locked word jump.

## Job 3: sci-fi grounding pass

The good news first: the core concept is already grounded. Section 1.5 describes the embers as "a ship-sized power core still holding a vast charge of stellar energy, plus a damaged fragment of the Lattice's control intelligence" and favour as "the core's model of you getting better" (line 118). That is hardware and software, not magic. The fantasy drift that remains lives in names, titles and furniture, listed below.

1. **Line 85, "wards" as the Sunward Crusade's word for shields.** A ward is a magic guard; it is a fantasy word doing daily work in the shared vocabulary table. Alternative: "screens" or plain "shields" for everyone, with the factions differing elsewhere. **MATTERS**: this is the one vocabulary word every player will read constantly, and it smuggles the fantasy register into the faction that is supposed to be the grounded one.
2. **Line 86, "far-dreamer" for long range messengers.** Mystical on its face, and it reads as a psyker in costume, in the very table whose "never say" column bans astropath and psyker on the same line. Alternative: "relay officer", or let everyone use "signals officer" as the table already allows. **MATTERS**.
3. **"The Ascension" as the name of the upload event (line 16 and throughout).** It is the most religious word in the book, applied to a technical act the draft itself describes in plain words on the same line: "uploading every mind into a machine body". Alternative: "the Upload", which matches the draft's own verb, or "the Transfer". **MATTERS**: this word names the setting's central event on nearly every page, and its register fights the round 4 brief. See also Job 1.
4. **Line 464, the Glasswright name pool (Vaelen, Nyssa, Corin) reads elvish.** Soft two syllable names with ae and y spellings are the exact register Ryan rejected in rounds 2 and 3, and the Glasswrights' own family lines (of the Third Mirror, of Long Focus) already show the harder, craft based alternative the pool could follow. Alternative: build given names from the same optics and craft stock as the family lines, shorter and harder. **MATTERS**: names are what players say out loud, and this pool is the single most fantasy reading list in the book.
5. **Line 150, "redeemed hulls".** Redeemed is a religious word, and it undercuts the draft's own insistence that "They are a crusade, not a church" (line 135). Alternative: "reclaimed hulls" or "stripped hulls". **COSMETIC** on its own; it matters only as part of the pattern in flags 1, 2 and 6.
6. **Line 183, the Crusade crest, "a half sun over a sword".** A medieval sword at the centre of the most tech grounded faction's badge. Alternative: a half sun over a radiator spire, or over a chalk line, both already Crusade furniture (lines 161 and 135). **COSMETIC** as text; it becomes the faction's most seen symbol in art, so the call is Ryan's (Job 5).
7. **Lines 49 and 121, Vyre's epithet "the Storm Queen".** A fantasy monarch's title on a fragment of machine intelligence. Alternative: "the Storm Core", or drop the epithet to "Vyre, the Storm". **COSMETIC**.
8. **"Patrons" as the umbrella word for the cores (section 1.5).** Patron carries saint and patronage resonance. The draft mostly uses "cores" and "embers", which are both grounded; the fix is consistency, not a rename. Alternative: use "embers" as the only umbrella word. **COSMETIC**.
9. **Line 535, the flagship class "Sun-ark".** Ark carries the biblical boat. Alternative: "Sun-barge" or "eclipse hull". **COSMETIC**.
10. **Line 216, the prefix "HNS", and line 249, "the Anchorage".** HNS mimics the naval HMS, and an anchorage is a sea word; both rub against the house rule that this is space, not sea. Alternative: registry codes (REG Steadfast) or no prefix; "the Anchorage" to "the Muster" or "Meridian yards". **COSMETIC**. (The class words picket and cordon on line 247 are border and security words as much as naval ones; I would leave them.)
11. **Lines 440 and 472, "star torpedo", "starling wings", "Mirror veil".** A fairy tale shimmer rather than fantasy proper. Alternatives: "long torpedo", "wing pairs", "mirror screen". **COSMETIC**.

### Five hard sci-fi details to add

1. **Give the Lattice a visible mechanism: magnetic nozzles.** Real star lifting proposals steer plasma flows off a star with magnetic fields (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_lifting). One line in section 1.1 saying the Lattice lifted plasma on magnetic rails over the star's poles grounds the megastructure, and it sets up the Job 2 item 11 fix: the disaster becomes a containment failure in a system the reader can picture.
2. **Say how big Caelum's star was.** A black hole is what is left when a core passes the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit, roughly 2.2 to 2.9 solar masses (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff_limit). One clause, "a big old star, nine times the Sun's mass" or similar, makes the collapse feel engineered rather than wished. No jargon reaches the player; the number can live in the bible only.
3. **Beamed power needs a receiver, and beams drift.** The Lattice beaming power system wide is real physics at demonstrator scale: in 2023 Caltech's SSPD-1 MAPLE experiment beamed detectable power from orbit to a receiver on Earth (https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-beam-solar-power-to-earth-from-space-for-first-time-ever/). A bible line that Lattice beams need receiving arrays, and that wrecked receivers leave beams wandering, makes the wreckage dangerous for a reason and gives the Dimming's siphon beams a visible cost.
4. **A restored mind is only as new as its last backup.** Mind uploading has a standard technical framing, scan, translate, run, set out in Sandberg and Bostrom's 2008 roadmap from Oxford's Future of Humanity Institute (https://www.eurozine.com/afterlife-freeze-and-upload/). One rule for the Archive, backups happen at intervals, so a restored Eclipsed mind loses everything since its last sync, turns revival from resurrection into engineering and gives "gone dark" (section 7.6) a harder edge: some deaths come back wrong by hours or years.
5. **Heat has to go somewhere.** UNSURE on a citable source for this one, so it is offered as basic physics rather than a sourced claim: ships glow because they must dump heat, and near the Hollow Sun the disc's light is the local daylight. The Crusade's radiator spires (line 161) and the Eclipsed's sunshade hulls (line 494) already imply this; one bible sentence naming radiators as the reason both factions look the way they do ties look to physics for free.

## Job 4: does each faction feel distinct and appealing?

### The comparison standard

The strong games differentiate on what a faction does, not on what it is called. Sins of a Solar Empire 2 fields three races, each split into two sub factions with different play styles: the TEC lean on trade and armour, the Advent on shields, psionics and their Unity, the Vasari on phase mobility (https://www.gamesmarket.global/rts-x-4x-sins-of-a-solar-empire-ii-898359d944d49ffef575c92c3a6833d6/ and the official gameplay page, https://www.sinsofasolarempire2.com/gameplay). Endless Space 2 builds factions around distinct mechanics rather than stat bonuses, and its creative director said the team would rather ship "an unbalanced game with crazy faction all being different, than to have a perfectly balanced game with all the factions looking alike" (https://www.pcgamer.com/endless-space-2-takes-endless-legends-quests-and-varied-factions-to-the-stars/). Stellaris assembles identity from ethics and civics, with civics described in guides as the playstyle defining layer (https://roguewiki.com/stellaris/guides/stellaris-origins-guide). Battlefleet Gothic Armada 2 shipped all twelve tabletop factions differentiated by "unique ways of moving through space or weird, alien weaponry and ships", including differences in what near identical factions can field (https://store.steampowered.com/oldnews/?l=latam&feed=pcgamer&appids=573100&enddate=1641024000&appgroupname=Battlefleet). BattleTech's Great Houses share one technology base and differ by doctrine, economy and character, which is the proof that shared hardware is fine when doctrine differs (house summaries at https://battleint.net/index.php/M%C3%A4chte_der_Inneren_Sph%C3%A4re). Homeworld differentiates through story stance and one persistent fleet that carries over from mission to mission (https://h2g2.com/entry/A798492), which is the closest cousin to what Voidsworn's run structure and Nemesis can do.

Measured against that standard, this draft is in good shape: every faction has at least one rule bending mechanic and a voice rule, which is the Endless Space 2 test. The findings below are about where look, voice and mechanics stop agreeing.

### The Sunward Crusade

- **One line fantasy:** be the written off colony that holds the line, pays its debts in names, and makes the machine that abandoned it watch.
- **What is unique:** chalked names as a direct answer to being deleted (line 135 and section 2.2), the debt of days levy, defectors who cut out their own implants and are welcomed but watched (section 2.3). No other faction's identity is built out of bookkeeping and refusal.
- **What might feel generic:** space knights. Oaths, a sword crest (line 183), dawn language and brands can slide toward Warhammer zealotry, which is why Job 3 flags 1, 2, 5 and 6 matter for this faction most.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Mostly yes. Black iron and ember orange, short imperative call and response, broadsides that hit harder after moving, brace and hold, a Nemesis who never flees and duels your flagship (line 171): all one statement. The sword crest and "wards" are the two notes off key.

### Hegemony Navy

- **One line fantasy:** be the calm machine state that has already calculated your defeat and budgeted for it.
- **What is unique:** the Registry's voice, polite, timestamped, with losses "within acceptable limits" as both motto and horror (sections 3.1 and 3.2), and the one human detail in the book that no other faction has: commanders switch off their links for one minute before a decisive battle, "the only thing the Registry never logs" (section 3.6).
- **What might feel generic:** the stock evil AI empire. The draft knows it; its own avoid list (Skynet, Cylons, the geth, section 3.8) shows the work. The politeness and the bookkeeping are the distance, and they hold.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Yes, strongly. Pale grey and steel blue, drone escorts, formation sync around the flagship, ion stripping shields, reinforcements arriving in round 3 as the Registry reallocates (section 3.5): the network is visible, audible and mechanical at once.

### The Ashfall Hullbreakers

- **One line fantasy:** take the strongest ship in the dark by beating its captain, and wear the wrecks of everyone you have beaten.
- **What is unique:** the challenge rule (anyone can fight the captain for the ship, section 4.8), trophy plates cut from other factions' hulls and worn in their old colours (line 266), and a Nemesis whose stolen ship comes back to you when he dies (line 276), which turns theft into a loan with teeth.
- **What might feel generic:** orks and pirates. The draft's guardrail is tone: "Cold and disciplined in their cruelty, not comic" (section 4.2). That sentence has to survive every later writing pass, because the silhouette (scrap, rams, boarding) is the most borrowed in the book.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Yes. Soot and rust, short brutal boasts, speed, thin shields, rockets, rams, boarding and no lances (section 4.5) all say the same thing. Their one problem is not internal; it is the palette overlap with the Crusade (Job 2, item 15).

### The Chorus

- **One line fantasy:** be the appetite that learns the taste of your fleet and remembers the names of the dead it never met.
- **What is unique:** two throats arguing inside one voice, Hunger against Memory (line 333), a nine word sentence cap as a hard voice rule (section 5.2), and Memory holding the Caelan dead's faces and names, which makes the swarm the setting's accidental archive. No comparison game gives its locusts a conscience made of stolen data.
- **What might feel generic:** space locusts, the Tyranid silhouette. The draft's anti Tyranid avoid list (section 5.2) plus Memory are the mitigation, and they work on paper.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Yes, with one exception the draft itself raises: the name is a song word for a faction that never sings, defended well at section 5.14 (line 400) and locked besides. Olive flesh, bile glow, no shields, regrowth, boarding tendrils and a free first turn (line 354) all agree.

### The Glasswrights

- **One line fantasy:** be the engineers who built the light, now making amends with beauty as a weapon and guilt as fuel.
- **What is unique:** they are the only faction whose want is to switch the prize off (lines 21, 418 and 436). Everyone else wants the cores; the Glasswrights want them dead. That inversion is a real differentiator, and their funeral rite, burning their dead in focused stored light (section 6.6), is the book's best single custom.
- **What might feel generic:** space elves. Tall, slender, black eyed, beautiful, grieving, crystal and light: this is the most familiar silhouette in the draft, and the name pool (Job 3, flag 4) leans into it instead of against it. The draft's "Not Aeldari" list (section 6.2) fights the furniture while the names and the look keep the shape.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Mechanics yes: blinding speed, heavy shields, no brace, prism lance, a duelist Nemesis who returns with the same four ships upgraded (line 440). Voice mostly: quiet, precise, apologetic. The strain is Job 2 item 14: a faction defined by refusing the cores should not be quietly built around one.

### The Eclipsed

- **One line fantasy:** be the people who traded their sun for forever, and have had a thousand years to wonder if it was worth it.
- **What is unique:** doubt as the faction's engine. They caused the setting, they power the Dimming, and they are "no longer sure the Ascension was worth it" (line 483). Their only death is a mind that cannot be restored going "dark" (section 7.6), and their captives are scanned into the Archive and released, "a kindness" (section 7.6) that is more chilling than any threat in the book.
- **What might feel generic:** the Necron silhouette, ancient undying machine people. The avoid list in section 7.2 is thorough, and the doubt plus the new sun plan are genuine distance: Necrons want their old empire back; the Eclipsed want a star they have never seen.
- **Do look, voice and mechanics agree?** Yes, the best in the book. Violet black plates and a white gold corona, a voice that counts years and bodies ("This is my ninth body", line 487), no shields, heavy armour, Archive revival once, a patient Nemesis at half clock speed (line 504): every layer says the same sentence.

### The two human factions

**Yes: a player can tell them apart at a glance and in a paragraph, and the draft's answer is enough.** At a glance: black iron, ember orange and frost scarred unaugmented faces against pale grey, steel blue and blue implant lights (sections 2.5 and 3.3); there is no screenshot in which these two fleets blur. In a paragraph: centre against frontier, augmented against unaugmented, calculation against oath is a complete triangle, and the draft sharpens it with the best line in either section, "One trusts the calculation; the other is what the calculation left behind" (section: Why two human factions). The at a glance risk in this draft is not Crusade against Hegemony. It is Crusade against Hullbreakers (Job 2, item 15).

### Weakest and strongest

- **Weakest: the Glasswrights.** Not because the concept is weak; guilt driven engineers are a strong idea. They are weakest because three separate strains land on the same faction: the most borrowed silhouette (space elves), the most fantasy reading name pool (Job 3, flag 4), and the one want against behaviour contradiction (Job 2, item 14). Each is a small fix; together they are why this faction would read softest to a new player.
- **Strongest: the Eclipsed.** They are the setting's cause, its engine (the Dimming), its title image (the eclipse), and its only faction with an internal argument. Look, voice and mechanics agree completely, and their Nemesis behaviour (patient, half speed clock, returns once in sector 3) is the most distinctive in the game. If a player remembers one faction a week later, it is this one.

## Overall verdict

Round 4 does the job it was set: the setting now stands on a megastructure, a black hole, AI cores and mind uploads, and the fantasy Ryan rejected survives only in furniture, a sword on a crest, a handful of words like "wards" and "far-dreamer", and one elvish name pool. The collision check is nearly clean, with exactly one real collision (turbolaser) and a tail of minor echoes, the worst of which sits on a locked name (Glasswrights) and can only be flagged. The internal problems are all small and specific: one stale colour line, a motif table its own name pools keep breaking, a timeline with one number in it, and a collapse mechanism that needs one sentence of physics to point the right way. The factions pass the hard test, each carrying a rule bending mechanic and a voice rule that agree with its look, with the Eclipsed the strongest design in the book and the Glasswrights the one faction where silhouette, names and behaviour all need the same small push. Nothing here needs a round 5; it needs one editing pass with the six answers above.
