# Red team audit: UI/UX designer leg (Rey, blind)

Seat: interface, information and commitment layers, input flow, onboarding, and accessibility as a named sub-lens. Evidence: the UI/UX pack (core pack, battle UI synthesis, HUD synthesis, text synthesis, accessibility synthesis, firstrun synthesis, tutorial assembly, ui3d.js excerpts) plus firsthand checks in the clone's ui3d.js and CSS at the audit pin, cited by file and line.

## Findings, ranked by severity for this seat

### 1. One click can spend a ship's position, and the preview stops exactly where the risk starts (OBSERVED)

The blocker complex in the battle UI synthesis stands, and firsthand code reading sharpens rather than softens it. The hover tooltip for an out-of-range enemy does disclose the move in words: "Click to burn ${am.st.cost} movement into position" and fire on the way (ui3d.js:499, verified). What it never shows is the destination hex, the arrival facing, or the odds from that hex, because hover odds are computed from the ship's current position and attackMovePlan picks the destination for the player (ui3d.js:412, 429, verified). So the disclosure is honest about the cost and silent about the consequence. The player's mental model is "shoot that"; the shipped behavior is "relocate, then shoot, from a hex and facing you did not choose, at odds you did not see." Fire-all on click is likewise irreversible, a ship that fired cannot brace, and the only two-tap confirm in the battle guards the admiral Command, the rarest click available. Protection is allocated backwards. Cost: the worst misclick in the game is not a wasted shot, it is an unplanned move into a threat field the player was never shown. Phased activation is the ruled fix and this leg does not relitigate it; the attack is that at this pin the ruled direction is unbuilt and the shipped gesture remains live in the only playable build.

### 2. The teaching system is structurally incapable of teaching half the game (OBSERVED)

The firstrun verdict, ONE SYSTEMIC TEACHING FAILURE, stands on the code, not on taste. tutorialCheck requires a battle object and returns when the app shell is up (ui3d.js, tutorialCheck, in pack), and the TIPS table it serves contains ten tips, every one of them about battle: selection, turns, facing, shields, firing, Command, ramming, threat, objectives. Not one tip states the game's actual contract: runs are generational, loss is the curriculum, the first run is near-impossible by design. The campaign layer, where routing, patrons, command points, and the Nemesis clock are decided, gets zero contextual teaching by construction. The assembled campaign tip set (16 tips, filed) is copy in search of a surface; nothing in the pack evidences a campaign-layer hint surface for it to land on. Cost: a new player is taught how to fire and never taught what game they are playing, in a design whose entire retention bet is that the player understands losing correctly.

### 3. The battle opens without the enemy in it, against the ruled framing (OBSERVED in pack, INFERRED as to the playable build)

The HUD synthesis verdict NOT READY stands on its convergent core. In the nine captures, the enemy is not visible in the start state or the threat view, nameplates merge into garbled strings in the carrier cluster, the Engagement legend is collapsed and unlabeled in all nine images, and the tutorial card teaches a cue the screen contradicts (it says Command glows gold; Command is dark and Pass is gold). Ryan ruled on Oct 28 that battles start zoomed out showing the whole battlefield and the enemy fleet. Fairness note the synthesis itself records: all nine captures are Godot G2 builds, so this evidence convicts the Godot surface directly and the browser build not at all; there is no pack evidence that either build implements the ruled opening framing today. Cost: the awareness layer (seeing the enemy, reading the board's colours, trusting the tutorial) is weaker than the command layer in a game where the first decision of every battle is positional. A first battle that teaches a wrong cue and hides the enemy is an onboarding defect, not a polish item.

### 4. Inspect and fire share one gesture, and the game's own empty panel teaches the model that gets betrayed (OBSERVED)

Verified at ui3d.js:128: with nothing selected, the panel instructs "Click an enemy to see its weapons and firing arcs." With one of your ships selected and the enemy in range, the same click fires every bearing weapon. The synthesis records all four prior prongs agreeing on this, and the code confirms both halves: the teaching text at line 128 and the fire path through the tooltip's "Click to fire" line at line 499. Cost: the exact click a new player was taught to use for information becomes, one selection later, the most committal click in the game. This is the human-factors root of finding 1: the gesture's meaning flips on selection state that a first-run player does not yet track.

### 5. Text is small, fixed, and unscalable in both builds; the line that teaches the controls is among the worst offenders (OBSERVED)

The accessibility synthesis measured the Godot build FAIL: battle HUD body text at 14 to 15 px against an 18 px PC floor, a scaling floor that takes the help line to about 7 px, no user text-size control, and the controls help line at roughly 3.1:1 to 4.2:1 contrast against a 4.5:1 bar. The browser build shares the disease on firsthand evidence: its CSS body sizes run 13 px and 15 px (head.html and voidsworn-3d.html), and a search of every JS and HTML file at the pin finds no text-scale, text-size, or font-scale setting of any kind (verified). The browser HUD markup leans on muted and dim classes for exactly the lines a new player reads most (buildHud and refreshTop, in pack). Cost: readability is a fixed property of the developer's monitor, not the player's; aging eyes and small laptop screens get a smaller game with no remedy, and the Godot migration is carrying the same sizes across instead of fixing them at the port, which was the cheap moment.

### 6. Keyboard control exists but cannot be discovered from any single surface (OBSERVED)

Verified at ui3d.js:161: keysHelp lists Tab, Q, E, 1 to 6, B, V, Esc, Space, and Enter. It omits R (threat), X (ram), G (board), F (focus), T (top-down), and Home, several of them core combat inputs that the game's own tips and button titles reference. The camera hint bar covers a different subset (drag, WASD, wheel, F, T) and none of the combat keys. So the full keyboard surface exists only as the union of two partial lists in two places, one of which (the panel) is visible mainly when nothing is selected. Cost: mouse-only play by default, not by player choice; the tactics-game audience that reaches for hotkeys finds an undocumented subset and reasonably concludes the rest does not exist. For accessibility this is also the input-alternatives story: the alternatives exist and are hidden.

### 7. A sticky wing selection escapes the standard cancel paths (OBSERVED)

The battle UI synthesis records, chair-verified, that Esc, selectUnit, and rosterPick never clear wingSel, so after selecting a carrier wing the next board click assigns a wing order instead of selecting or firing. Firsthand reading adds the mitigations: the mode does clear once an order is assigned (ui3d.js:425), clicking empty space clears it (line 425), and while the mode is live the hint line says so and names the cancel ("Wing selected: tap an enemy ship to strike it," line 669). Cost: this is a classic mode error with a partial safety net; the player most likely to hit it is one who pressed Esc, the universal cancel, believed the mode was gone, and then clicked an enemy to inspect it, issuing a strike order instead. Mode state that survives the cancel key is a defect even when a hint line confesses it.

### 8. The hint line can declare a ship finished while it still has options (OBSERVED)

Verified verbatim at ui3d.js:669: after any movement with nothing currently hittable, the hint reads "Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship." regardless of remaining movement points or an unspent Brace. The same hint line is, fairly, the best micro-teacher in the battle UI (the firstrun synthesis says so, and this leg agrees), which is exactly why its one false statement matters: the surface players learn to trust teaches them to end activations early and to never discover Brace as a fallback. Cost: a quiet, repeated skill tax on every new player, delivered by the one voice they were told to believe.

### 9. Board meaning rides on hue pairs, with second channels present but collapsed or hover-gated (OBSERVED)

Objective zones in the browser build are a red-green pair carrying opposite meanings: exit is #7dff9b green, transport exit and station perimeter are #e07a70 red (ui3d.js:101, verified), and the Godot accessibility review measured the same pair at about 1.46:1 and 1.23:1 composited, hue alone separating "leave here" from "this kills the station." The mitigations are real and this leg credits them: hovering a zone hex prints its name and effect (ui3d.js:455), and the legend, when expanded, lists every zone with its name and full effect text (ui3d.js:711). But the legend's default state is collapsed to colour swatches whose names live only in hover tooltips (ui3d.js:713), and targetability itself is signalled by a pulsing red ring while your own selected ship wears a green one. The threat view, to its credit, grades by luminance ("the darker the hex, the more damage is waiting there," threat tip, in pack), which is the pattern the rest of the board should copy. Cost: a red-green colour-blind player gets the collapsed, colour-only version of the board by default, and must discover the expanded legend to recover information sighted players get at a glance.

### 10. The settings surface is a drift museum, and the primary button will not sit still (OBSERVED)

buildHud (in pack) packs roughly twenty toggles into one settings panel, several explained only by title-attribute prose, including graphics tiers, temporal AA, concept-art test hulls, diagnostics, and a "Copy report" debug affordance addressed to the developer, all at the same level as sound and animation speed. The quality tooltip still promises tiers "Medium: tuned for phones" in a PC-only product, and refreshTop still branches its turn label on G.mobile ("Your move" versus "Your activation"), so the retired mobile layer the drift synthesis graded ROT is not retired at all; it is writing live strings in the shipped HUD. The same refresh pass cycles the primary button through four labels with four meanings: End turn, End activation, Pass, Continue. Cost: a settings panel that reads as a debug console erodes trust in the few options that matter (reduce flashing, reduce shake, follow activations are in there, buried), and a primary action whose label changes meaning by mode forces a re-read on the most-pressed button in the game.

## Verdict

FRICTION and NOT READY both stand: the pre-commit information layer (per-weapon odds by shield facing, expected damage, likely-kill flag, ghost preview) is genuinely best in class, and the game still lets one click spend a ship's position without showing the destination. The single most important attack: finding 1, the attack-move fires from a hex the player never saw, at odds computed for a position the ship does not occupy, and no undo exists; the tooltip's honest cost disclosure makes the missing destination preview a design choice, not an oversight.
