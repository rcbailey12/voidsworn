# Nemesis synthesis: mechanics audit (Oct 28, 2026)

Chair synthesis of the Oct 28 battle-layer program, Nemesis item. Legs: Qwen (A RIVAL), DeepSeek (A RIVAL), Argon (A MECHANISM IN A COSTUME). Evidence pinned to main 264f441d. Chair verifications were run against campaign_run.js and core data in voidsworn-fresh and are marked as such. Filed legs: contrib/qwen/reviews/nemesis_2026_10_28.md, contrib/deepseek/reviews/nemesis_2026_10_28.md, contrib/gemini/reviews/nemesis_argon_high_2026_10_28.md.

## Verdict

**A RIVAL WITH A BROKEN INFORMATION LOOP.** The Nemesis is a real rival in structure (persistent, adapting, faction-flavored, scarred by your fights) and all three legs agree the bones are right. What fails is the loop that would make him feel like one: the player is asked to prepare for a weakness they may never be told, against adaptations that can quietly counter the one weakness they did learn, on a meeting schedule the game forces rather than the clock implying. Argon's harsher verdict centers the mandatory-meeting finding; the synthesis adopts his facts and Qwen and DeepSeek's framing: the mechanism is a rival, the information flow around him is not finished.

Two chair census errors in the pack are owned here so they do not propagate: the fast Nemesis clock is 1.5x, not double as the pack census said (the Deathless half-speed 0.5x was right), and each Nemesis has a sixth, faction-special adaptation beyond the five generic ones the census counted.

## Findings (ranked)

1. **Intel drought is the item's strongest signal (all three legs).** Weaknesses are revealed only through intel events, which are drawn, not guaranteed, or by beating him. At the ruled loss rate (about 80 percent of runs end in defeat, and that rate is fine by designer ruling), a player can plausibly go a whole run, or several, never learning a single weakness. Every other Nemesis system (preparation, adaptation-reading, faction counterplay) sits downstream of information the game may never provide. The synthesis position: this is the first fix. A guaranteed intel beat (first meeting reveals one weakness, or the debrief names one per meeting, which pairs with the ruled debrief visibility for adaptation) makes the rest of the system legible.

2. **The meeting is mandatory once per sector; the clock is an accelerant, not a summoner (Argon, chair-verified).** nemNodeIs at campaign_run.js:283 forces his fight at columns at or beyond cols.length-3 while he is unmet that sector, battle and elite nodes only. The clock filling early brings the meeting forward; it does not create it. Design language that describes him as clock-summoned misdescribes the shipped system, and the designer question below is genuinely open: mandatory boss per sector, or hunter the clock actually summons?

3. **Adaptation can counter the very weakness intel revealed (Argon, chair-verified mechanics).** nemAdapt at campaign_run.js:287-291 filters candidates only by not-yet-taken; there is no clash check against N.weak. An ion weakness can be answered by reinforced shields; a torpedo weakness by point defense. Chair discount on Argon's wording: the adaptation counters the weakness, it does not erase it (the weakness's extra effect still applies against the enlarged pool). The defect is real but it is a fairness seam, not a nullification: the player did the preparation the game asked for and the game moved the target without saying so. A clash check (adaptations may not directly counter a revealed weakness), or revealing the adaptation's target on the debrief (ruled visible, Oct 28 Q9), closes it.

4. **The adaptation cap is cumulative, not per sector (Qwen and DeepSeek, chair-verified).** Cap equals max(0, sector-1), plus the Chorus adaptFast allowance (beaten plus scars), capped at 6. So zero adaptations are possible in sector 1, one by sector 2, two by sector 3, diverging from the "one per sector" design sentence. Adaptation also cannot be dodged: when the cap allows one, a fallback always fires (faction special first, then the first untaken generic). Baiting him into a bad adaptation is possible and expensive, and all three legs grade it legitimate play.

5. **Only two of six strikes touch the map (all three legs, chair-verified).** nemStrike at campaign_run.js:276-281: only the Corsair raid and the Deathless tithe return effects; duel, swarm, reinforce, and challenge return empty and exist only as battle rules. The strike system reads as six faction behaviors and ships as two map effects plus four battle modifiers. Either the other four gain map presence (Argon and Qwen both propose a map-visible reinforcement convoy for Hegemony that the player can intercept, which also addresses finding 7) or the design language should stop promising six.

6. **The raid warning can lie in two distinct ways (Argon and DeepSeek, one item, two mechanisms).** Argon's mechanism: nemWarnLine evaluates the crippled-ship condition before the clock-filling fight resolves, nemStrike re-evaluates after it, so the warning and the strike can disagree about whether a ship was crippled. DeepSeek's mechanism: the warning is phrased around the next win, but loud event choices also fill the clock through nemClockAdd and the strike fires immediately, so a raid can seize a crippled ship on an event-driven fill the warning never described. Both are warning-integrity defects; Opus can confirm each in one read.

7. **Hegemony is the weakest of the six packages (all three legs, unanimous).** Its reinforce strike is the only one creating no preparable decision, and it has no map effect (finding 5). Chorus is the strongest package and the closest to the rival-versus-tax line (feed), with Deathless's tithe beside it. The tithe has its own structural problem (Argon): its clock runs at half speed and the forced meeting zeroes it, so the tithe is starved by the same system that schedules it.

8. **The raid return path exists but was invisible to every leg (chair correction).** Qwen, DeepSeek, and Argon all flagged "does stolen property come back" as unverifiable or missing. It exists at campaign_run.js:305, just past the pack's line-300 cut, under an R49 fairness comment (what a raid took comes back when he dies). This is a pack truncation artifact, owned by the chair, not a game defect. Argon's practical argument survives the correction: at an 80 percent loss rate the player usually dies before collecting, and a sector 1 hull returned in sector 3 is late help. Separately, Nemesis death registers only at sector 3 or later (nemAfterBattle lines 301-302), so how noFlee factions survive a sector 1 or 2 flagship destruction is legitimately open from the evidence.

9. **Escalation compounding is a question, not a finding (DeepSeek).** Corsair growth is uncapped (+1 ship per escape) and Chorus feed compounds; at the ruled loss rate a repeatedly-escaped Nemesis could snowball across a run. No leg measured the compounding against actual escape rates, and the loss rate itself is ruled fine. The synthesis carries this as a designer question about caps, not as a defect.

10. **Legacy-save migration edge, discounted (Argon, chair-contextualized).** ensureNemesis copies only 10 fields and filters adaptations to the generic set, omitting loot, held, riches, fed, honed, revived. Context Argon lacked: ensureNemesis only regenerates when a saved Nemesis lacks a persona, which makes it the B4-to-R42 legacy migration path. The wipe is a one-time legacy edge, not a live-play defect. Recorded so Opus can weigh it as save-migration hygiene only.

## Questions for the designer

1. Mandatory boss or clock hunter: is the once-per-sector forced meeting the design, with the clock as accelerant (then the fiction and UI should say so), or should the clock be able to summon him on its own, including in a sector the player is routing around?
2. Adaptation versus weakness: add the clash check (an adaptation may not directly counter a revealed weakness), or keep counters legal and rely on the ruled debrief visibility to make them legible?
3. Intel guarantee: what is the minimum the player is owed per meeting or per run (one weakness named on the debrief is the synthesis's candidate)?
4. Snowball caps: should Corsair growth and Chorus feed cap per run, and if so, at what?
5. Raid return: given the loss rate, is the return-on-death timing acceptable, or should partial recovery happen at the next meeting?

## Not checked

Live escape rates and actual adaptation frequencies in played runs (needs telemetry or a dedicated sim, not a pack read); nemSay and pronoun consistency inside Argon's garbled-transcription passage (flagged in its screening, cheap for Opus to verify); Godot port parity for the strike and adaptation rules.
