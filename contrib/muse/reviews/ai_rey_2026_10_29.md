# Enemy AI and fleet generation audit - Rey leg (Oct 29 program)

Chair note on method: this leg was written blind from the evidence pack and the leg prompt, plus direct code reads in the repo at the pack's pin (main 264f441d) where a claim depended on code outside the pack's slices. Reads beyond the slices are named as such in the findings. No other program material was consulted.

## 1. Proof of reading

Sections read: the EVIDENCE PACK title line ("enemy AI and fleet generation audit"); THE COMMISSIONER'S QUESTION; CHAIR CENSUS; WHAT TO JUDGE (items 1 to 5); and both code slices: battle.js 131-165 (effArch, RAMP, genEnemyFleet) and battle.js 1311-1402 (aiTurn, aiUnit, aiAbilities, aiAfterFire, fadeMove, aiHurtW).

**Overall verdict: COMPETENT BUT HOLLOW** - the battle brain at full strength is real (focus fire, kill-securing, threat-priced movement), but fleets are composed by a weighted lottery wearing an archetype hat, the brain's cleverness is one scalar value function plus threshold triggers, and the smart half is gated off for the players who meet the game first.

## 2. Findings

1. [BLOCKER] Composition has a readable outline and a random interior. READ: genEnemyFleet's archetypes each contribute exactly one act of intent: swarm filters the roster to the lightest non-carriers, heavy seeds the single biggest affordable hull, carrier seeds one carrier and bans others from the fill. Everything after the seed is a weighted lottery (weights cp^1.6 for heavy, a budget-ratio formula otherwise, times RAMP.light for cheap hulls) with no role logic at all: no screen for the carrier, no escort assignment, no anti-wing pick when the player fields wings, no second carrier check. A "carrier fleet" is one carrier plus whatever the dice afford. The census phrase "archetypes reshape the same budget rather than adding to it" is accurate, and reshaping turns out to mean one pick deep. INFERRED: players can read the first ship and nothing else; the remaining hulls are stat soup at the right total, which is what the commissioner asked us to distinguish.

2. [BLOCKER] A ship that moves and shoots never gets the veteran brace decision. READ (in the pack slice): in aiUnit, when the ship moves with a non-empty path and mid-move fire is on, moveUnit's onLate runs aiAbilities 'aim' and aiVolley, the result is stored in volleyP, and the `if(volleyP){ ...; return; }` block exits the activation after aiAfterFire plus a fallback brace that only triggers when the volley fired nothing. The veteran pre-fire brace heuristic (brace when threat is over 45% of hull, its own offense is under 35% of that threat, and hull is under 70%) sits below that block and is only reached when volleyP is null, meaning the ship did not move or mid-fire was disabled. INFERRED: the one considered defensive decision in the AI is structurally unavailable to exactly the ships that exposed themselves by moving into range, which is most ships on most turns.

3. [FIX] The audited brain is veteran-only, and fresh profiles do not meet it. READ (beyond the slices): aiVet() gates the fleet focus, the death-cliff movement penalty, veteran threat and offense evaluators, opportunity-based activation picking, and the brace heuristic. campaign3d.js sets `let AI_LEVEL='standard'` with the comment that a fresh profile starts on Standard, and main3d.js restores Veteran only for profiles with prior runs. On Standard the AI also takes the RAMP.std 0.9 budget discount. So every smart behavior in this pack describes the returning player's opponent; a new player faces fixed speed order, a flat 0.3 threat weight, no focus, and no death cliff. That may be a deliberate onboarding ramp, but it means the near-impossible first run the designer ruled for is produced by budget arithmetic against a deliberately simpler opponent, not by the opponent audited here.

4. [FIX] Thinning is misdescribed in the pack's own question, and it scales damage as well as toughness. READ: WHAT TO JUDGE item 1 calls thinning a factor where "later sectors field fewer, bigger ships". The code does the opposite. genEnemyFleet never reads RAMP.thin; ship counts rise by sector (budget growth plus light-hull weights). thin is applied at spawn (battle.js:322-323, beyond the slices) as the makeUnit scale for hull, shields and regen and as the damage multiplier for generated fleets: sector 3 ships fight at 0.62 hull and 0.62 damage, and boss escorts take thin times RAMP.escort, 0.62 x 0.78, about 0.48. INFERRED: anyone tuning thin believing it shapes composition will instead be tuning fight length and lethality, which connects directly to check 5 below.

5. [FIX] The power feedback clamp is honest about one factor and not about the budget. READ: the factor is pow(fleetPower / refP, 0.6) clamped to [0.75, 1.3]. Because of the 0.6 exponent, the cap binds at about 1.55x reference power (routine for a strong late-sector fleet) and the floor binds below about 0.62x. Around the clamp, elite (x1.05), hunted Heat (x1.15) and bounty (x1.1) multiply outside it, so a node's real budget can reach roughly 1.6 to 1.7x its reference-scaled base while the design story says "clamped between 0.75 and 1.3". The census is careful ("a player-power feedback factor clamped between 0.75 and 1.3"); the shipped tuning conversation may not be. INFERRED: the clamp's honesty problem is presentational, not mathematical, until someone balances against the 1.3 figure as a budget cap.

6. [FIX] No ship in aiUnit ever decides to retreat. READ: aiUnit's defensive repertoire is repositioning (threat-priced movement, fadeMove after firing, blink escape), and brace. There is no flee, withdraw, or disengage branch. Withdrawal exists only as battle-level script outside the AI (beyond the slices): the rout rule in checkEndRules ends the battle when enemy CP falls to 35% of its starting value, escortsBreak sends boss escorts off when the boss drops under half hull, and the hunt objective's flagship jumps out on a round timer. INFERRED: because flight is an event and never a decision, the AI's caution machinery (hurt weights, hysteresis, fade) can only delay deaths, never save a ship; every fight is a DPS race to a scripted break point, and a player who has measured the 35% line knows exactly how much killing a battle requires.

7. [FIX] Target selection is one predictable scalar, and wounded ships advertise themselves. READ (volley internals, beyond the slices): aiPickPrimary values a target as hull damage capped at its remaining HP, plus 0.3x shield damage, times 1.1 for ships or 0.6 for squadrons, plus a kill bonus, plus 10 for the fleet focus, plus up to 15 scaled by how wounded the target already is; the primary is sticky at 85% of the best value across activations. There is no role term: a reclaim or regrow support, a carrier one launch from replacing its wings, and a gun line all price by CP, hull and wounds alone. INFERRED: the player can compute the likely victim in advance (the most wounded valuable ship in reach) and pre-brace, screen, or heal it, and can wound a cheap hull to draw fire off an expensive one. Focus fire this legible is a puzzle input, not an opponent's intent.

8. [FIX] Carrier launch is telegraphed to a single predictable hex. READ (pack slice): the 'pre' ability pass runs launch before movement, and launchHexes are sorted by distance to the nearest foe with the first hex taken: the wing spawns on the open hex closest to the enemy, every time, before the carrier itself moves. INFERRED: a player who knows the rule knows where the new wing appears and can pre-position flak or a strike on that hex; a launch rule that picked among the two or three nearest hexes, or launched after moving, would cost little and read far less scripted.

9. [NOTE] Three systems are effectively player-only. READ (beyond the slices): the AI fires every volley with called: null, so subsystem called shots are never used against the player; the AI's only order is brace via giveOrder, never full, lock, or helm; and the gravity well enters AI scoring only through aiWellScore as pure penalty (-45 inside the rim, -6 inside the pull), with no positive term for the slingshot the well grants ships that start a turn near it. The AI treats the well as weather, never as a tool. Wing target orders are likewise player-issued; AI wings run on stance and the swarm planner (see check 4).

10. [NOTE] The advertised budget is not always the fielded budget. READ: the fill loop stops at the hull ceiling (RAMP.ceil[s] minus living player ships, floored at 4) even when budget remains, stops whenever budget drops below 1 CP, and admits hulls priced up to budget + 0.3, so it can also overspend slightly. In sector 3 (roughly 22 CP of pre-feedback budget, ceiling about 13 to 16 hulls against a small player fleet) a swarm-weighted roll can hit the count ceiling with CP unspent. INFERRED: node difficulty labels and the feedback math are computed on budget, but the fight is made of hulls; where the ceiling binds, the two quietly part company.

## 3. Explicit checks

1. COMPOSITION. The budget builder produces a readable headline and an unreadable fleet. Swarm (lightest hulls only) and heavy (biggest hull seeded, big-hull-weighted fill, at most one light extra) do read at a glance; carrier reads for exactly one ship. Past the archetype's seed pick the fill is a weighted lottery with no role composition, so intent is one ship deep. On thinning, the check's premise needs correcting: thinning does not field fewer, bigger ships in late sectors. It is a spawn-time multiplier on hull, shields, regen and damage (0.73, 0.675, 0.62 by sector) that discounts each generated ship as counts rise. As a composition lever it does not exist; as a lethality lever it is doing heavy, mostly invisible work. It is the wrong lever for the job the question assigns it, and the right lever for the job in check 5.

2. POWER FEEDBACK. The feedback helps a battered fleet and taxes a strong one, both less than the headline numbers suggest. fleetPower (battle.js:123-125, beyond the slices) counts living ships only, pricing each at CP times (0.4 + 0.6 x hull fraction) with mark, module, captain and hangar bonuses, scaled by boon and relic mass. A fleet at half its ships is far below reference and gets up to 25% budget relief at the next node, which is real death-spiral protection; a strong fleet pays at most 30% more while its own power can exceed reference by far more, because the 0.6 exponent compresses the response (2x power draws only about 1.52x before the cap). Recovery is barely punished: repairing a ship from half to full raises its power contribution by about 30% of its CP share, which moves the enemy budget by single digits after the exponent. Deliberate sandbagging (staying damaged to farm weaker nodes) is possible on paper and self-harming in practice, since the same damage weakens the fleet in the fight it is trying to soften. Against the ruled 80% loss rate the band is honest in form: symmetric, bounded, and too weak to flatten the sector and column budget growth that actually carries difficulty. Its one dishonesty is presentational, per finding 5: the clamp binds one factor while other multipliers stack outside it.
