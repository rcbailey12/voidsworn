## Job 1: originality and collision check

### Real collisions the chair adopts

**Turbolaser and Heavy turbolaser** (Hegemony weapons, line 248). CONFIRMED BY 2+ LEGS (all four). The word was coined for Star Wars and heavy turbolasers are the signature guns of Star Destroyers; the draft's own naming rule 1 (section 1.4) bars names lifted from Star Wars. Source: https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Turbolaser . Rename candidates from the legs: Coherent beam (Qwen), Pulse laser (Argon), Beam cannon (DeepSeek, Rey). Any of them fits the Hegemony's clean technical voice.

**The Hollow Sun** (the dead star's in-world name, lines 16, 57, 76 and throughout). SINGLE LEG (Argon called it a real collision; DeepSeek rated the phrase a minor echo off a different, weaker source, and Rey and Qwen rated it clear). CHAIR VERIFIED, and Argon is right: in Warhammer 40,000, the Hollow Sun is the crownworld of the Necron Suhbekhar Dynasty, sourced to the Deathwatch: The Outer Reach RPG book. Sources: https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Suhbekhar_Dynasty and https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Suhbekhar_Dynasty . That is the exact pairing ruling R44 bans: a Necron name tied to a star, in a draft whose machine faction already works hard to avoid Necron tells (section 7.2). Argon's secondary source also checks out: The Hollow Sun is a 2023 science fiction novel by Jake Theriault, where the name belongs to a cargo ship, not a star (https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/222617278-the-hollow-sun). All four legs agree the full five-word title "Embers of the Hollow Sun" is used by nobody else and is distinct; the problem is only the in-world name. Ryan's options are Job 5 question 1.

**Neural lace** (Hegemony implant, lines 36, 139, 215). SINGLE LEG (Argon). Neural lace is Iain M. Banks's term from the Culture novels for a brain-computer mesh, and the Hegemony matches the source's shape closely: an AI-governed civilization whose citizens carry the implant. The other legs did not flag it; that is silence, not disagreement. Chair confidence: high, this is a well-known coined term. Rename candidates from Argon: Cortex thread, Link-weave, Synapse tap.

**The Ascension** (the upload event, line 16 and throughout). Argon rated it a real collision; DeepSeek, Qwen and Rey all rated it a minor echo, so the existence of the echo is CONFIRMED BY 2+ LEGS and only its severity splits. The sources: Ascension is the central proper noun for transcendence in Stargate, and Stellaris uses Synthetic and Virtuality Ascension for whole-civilization machine uploads. The draft's own section 7.2 (line 489) already warns against "the Ascended" for Stargate reasons, which makes keeping "the Ascension" hard to defend internally. Alternatives from the legs: the Upload (DeepSeek, Rey), the Transference or the Casting (Argon), the Translation (DeepSeek). Ryan's call, Job 5 question 3.

**Oath-keeper** (Crusade title, line 191). SINGLE LEG (Argon, real collision on real-world grounds: the Oath Keepers are a real American paramilitary organization whose leaders were convicted of seditious conspiracy, and Oathkeeper is also a named sword in Game of Thrones). The other three legs list the title as clear. The chair carries it because the argument is not about fiction at all: pairing the word "Crusade" with "Oath-keeper" invites an association no other name in the draft risks. Alternatives from Argon: Oath-warden, Vow-bearer, Roll-keeper. Ryan's call; it can ride along with Job 5 question 11.

**Reaver, Reaver-captain, Reaver-king** (Hullbreaker class, rank and Nemesis title, lines 306, 310). CONFIRMED BY 2+ LEGS as at least an echo: Argon rated it a real collision, DeepSeek and Rey rated it a minor echo, Qwen rated it clear. In Firefly, Reavers are savage space raiders who strip wrecks and bolt trophies to their hulls, which is the Hullbreakers' exact visual and behavioural niche (lines 257, 265, 266); that niche match is Argon's argument and it is the strongest form of the point. Alternatives from Argon: Wrecker or Gut-ship for the class, Breaker-king for the title.

### The physics fix (all four legs converge)

The draft says the star collapsed because the Lattice was "pulling mass and energy out of the star faster than it could bear" (line 16). CONFIRMED BY 2+ LEGS (all four): removing mass from a star lowers its core pressure and makes collapse less likely, not more; star lifting extends a star's life. The legs' fixes stack into one sentence the chair recommends whole: Caelum was a massive old star; to power the upload at once, the Lattice's magnetic rings squeezed the core while draining its heat, the core lost pressure support and caved in past the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit (roughly 2.2 to 2.9 solar masses for a remnant core). Components: thermal drain and pressure loss (Qwen, DeepSeek), magnetic pinch (Argon, citing Scoggins and Kipping 2023, "Lazarus Stars", MNRAS, on why lifted stars do not collapse), containment failure with mass falling back (Rey). Ryan's call, Job 5 question 5.

### Grounding details worth adopting (merged from all four legs' sets)

1. Magnetic nozzles: real star-lifting proposals steer plasma off a star with magnetic fields (Rey, DeepSeek). One bible line makes the Lattice a machine the reader can picture. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_lifting .
2. The upload as a destructive scan: mapping a connectome and running it costs stellar-scale power, which is why the Ascension needed the star at all (Qwen, Argon, DeepSeek converge; Argon cites Sandberg and Bostrom's Whole Brain Emulation roadmap, Oxford, 2008).
3. Beamed power needs receivers, and beams drift: the Lattice's system-wide beaming and the Dimming's siphon beams both gain a visible mechanism and a visible cost (Rey; Argon's phased-array version cites Benford and Benford on power beaming). Caltech's MAPLE experiment beamed detectable power from orbit in 2023 (Rey's source: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-beam-solar-power-to-earth-from-space-for-first-time-ever/).
4. A restored mind is only as new as its last backup: give the Archive an interval or an error-correction threshold, past which a mind has "gone dark" (Rey and Qwen converge independently; this turns revival from resurrection into engineering).
5. Heat has to go somewhere: the Crusade's glowing radiator spires (line 161) are what a hot manual reactor in vacuum requires (Argon; radiators at 1,000 to 1,500 Kelvin glow furnace orange, which is also a free explanation of the faction's look). SINGLE LEG but cheap and load-bearing for the art.
6. Why the six cores are needed together: focused light alone scatters into pair production before it can ignite a core, so a new sun needs all six cores as synchronized containment (SINGLE LEG, Argon, citing Alvarez-Dominguez et al. 2024, Physical Review Letters). This one upgrades the Eclipsed's whole plan from wish to engineering problem.
