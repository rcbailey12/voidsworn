# Outside review: storm bolts (PR 1299) and hazard outline (PR 1307)

Review of two backdrop changes: the ion storm bolts recoloured
cyan-white with the violet halo removed (PR 1299, issue 1269, branch
opus/issue-1269 at 2a375335), and the faint outline added to hazard
hexes at gain 0.25 (PR 1307, issue 1267, branch opus/issue-1267-edges
at a4721d1a). Frames judged: the two BD1269 storm sheets (c170, c26)
and the six BD1267 edge sheets (nebula and world skies, cine and grid
views, c170 and c26).

Council: blind Rey leg first, then DeepSeek (DeepThink on, Search off)
and Qwen (Qwen3.8-Max, gate verified both ends). Grok was blocked by
the cloud bot wall before the brief reached it. The Gemini (Flash) seat
did not run: its Google sign-in was still waiting on the phone approval
when this review was chaired. Both seats are recorded empty.

## PR 1299, storm bolts: KEEP (unanimous)

The after bolts read as lightning at both distances. The forked,
branched silhouette carries at c170, where the white core is the
brightest thing in its quadrant, and at c26 the after bolt is a crisp
white fork where the before frame was a glow blob with a streak buried
in it. Removing the violet halo also removes the muddy purple-teal
smear the halo made against the storm cloud.

On clash, the strongest argument in the council is Qwen's: the enemy
indicators in these captures are magenta and violet, so the old violet
bolts sat in the enemy marking family. Cyan-white removes that rhyme.
Friendly indicators are mint-teal ellipses; the bolts are jagged, thick
and high luminance, and nothing in the frames confuses the two. One
honest limit: no weapon fire appears in any of the eight sheets, so
bolt-versus-weapon-arc confusion is judged from shape language and hue
separation only, and a busy combat frame remains the real test.

One disagreement, recorded: the blind leg found the after bolt thin
and low on energy at c26, nearer a scratch than a discharge, where
DeepSeek and Qwen read the same frame as crisper than before. The
chair keeps the concern as a note rather than a condition, because
both readings agree on the fix direction: energy belongs in the core,
not in a restored halo.

Adopted tweak: desaturate the bolt's cyan edge toward ice-white (or
halve its alpha) and keep the core pure white, so the bolt separates
from the teal cloud by luminance instead of rhyming with it by hue.
DeepSeek's addition, a brief white core flash at the strike moment, is
the motion check to run alongside it.

## PR 1307, hazard outline: KEEP (unanimous), with a distance fade

In the cine view at c170 the outline does its job on both skies: a
faint pale hex boundary traces the hazard cloud where the before frames
show only soft cloud, at low contrast, with ships and selection rings
still reading first. No clutter added. That is the view the change was
for, and it works.

In the grid view the council splits on value, not on harm. The blind
leg found before and after hard to tell apart, since the grid already
edges the hazard hexes; DeepSeek and Qwen both see the hazard rims
separating from the neutral grid. The chair's finding: no harm in the
grid view and a modest separation benefit, but the real gain is the
cine view. DeepSeek's suggestion to raise the outline opacity in the
grid view is not adopted; the grid view was never the problem.

At c26 the outline is mildly too strong, two legs to one: the hex
borders become large, bright, slab like lines at UI-overlay strength
over the soft cloud (clearest on the world cine c26 sheet). It does
not clutter, and the hazard extent is unmistakable, but at close zoom
0.25 no longer reads as faint.

Adopted tweak: scale the outline alpha with camera distance. Hold
0.25 at battle and cine range; fade toward 0.12 to 0.15 at c26-range
zoom, so the boundary survives close-up without the slab feel.
