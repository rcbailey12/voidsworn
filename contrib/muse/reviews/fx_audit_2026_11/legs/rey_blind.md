# Blind Rey leg (writer): red team of the effects, animation, and sound stacks

Evidence: PACK.md only. No other sources were used.

## 1. Verdict

THE STACKS HOLD ONLY WITH REPLACEMENTS.

The three stacks fail in three different shapes, and the tally in the
pack shows each one. Effects has the graphics disease in its classic
form: of 34 items, 23 are ASSUMED and 3 are ANCHORED, and the anchors
sit underneath whole families of later numbers. Animation has almost
no numbers at all: 19 of 22 items are ASSUMED, with zero DERIVED and
zero ANCHORED, and the stack's content is a pile of absolute feel
rulings that no builder can be checked against, which has already
produced serial re-rulings of the same movement model. Sound has the
fewest bad numbers and the worst verification state: of its 6
MEASURED items, two are defects found by reading data and code, one
is the verified absence of the stack's own quality gate, and every
tuning number in the section predates anyone hearing anything.

The verdict most depends on three items:

- E31 (weapon timing SPEC pins). The Oct 25 engine test scene's demo
  loop choreography, carried into the A5 specification as centres for
  testing ranges, in a pass that was never scheduled. This is the
  stage 5 pattern from the worked example repeating exactly: one
  incumbent scene's designed property, promoted by citation into the
  standard for all seven weapon kinds. If E31 were sound, the
  effects stack's centre would be sound. It is an anchor.
- A12 (the glide rule, third movement statement in a week). A8, A10,
  A12, and A13 are four overlapping movement authorities with zero
  stated parameters between them, and A22's hurry rule still points
  at a pace target retired by the same record. Animation's problem
  is not a wrong number, it is that no number or consolidated spec
  exists that two rulings could be compared against. If a
  consolidated, parameterised movement specification existed, most
  of the A section's dispositions would collapse into it.
- S15 (the listen pass has never run). Every tuning number in the
  sound stack (S6, S7, S11, S18) is a pre-listening number, and the
  one event that would convert them into judgements has been
  outstanding since the intake merged. Until S15 executes, the sound
  stack is not a standard at all, it is a draft awaiting its first
  review round.

Two items sit just below those three in weight and are named here so
the chair does not lose them: E8 (the single largest nebula payoff
assigned to the one tier whose cost has never been measured, on a
tier structure that has no measured frame cost anywhere), and S2
(the Sonniss exception, the only item in the pack whose cost of
being wrong is legal and reputational rather than aesthetic).

## 2. Game dev lens

Effects, E1 to E34:

frame budget allocation, no minimum spec decision, and no measured

backed by nothing; if real battles cannot hold it, every tier rule
below is calibrated to a fiction, and if they easily hold it, effects
are underbuilt against the AAA objective.

wrong split spends the burst budget where the eye is not, or starves
the break and the first tumble, the segment the synthesis itself

A13 [OBSERVED, pack section 4]: The motion class split states a
