## Verdict for Ryan

Your instinct was right: the star cut never went in, and the sky is now the oldest-looking layer in the game. Everything in front of it (planets, asteroids, stations, ships, the on-board nebula) has had a V2 with measured value bands; behind them still sits the pre-1766 sky: thousands of soft star blobs per frame, dust lanes that cannot go black, a galactic band turned down to a whisper, and two faction palettes that break your own no-purple and no-mustard rules. The good news is the fix is already designed and in build as issue 1766 with 1502, and the council is unanimous that the direction is still right, in fact more right now that the foreground is brighter and denser. What this review adds is scope discipline: the star cut alone is not the job. Palette governance, a luminance cap on the mood multipliers, and one resolution trap in the 16-bit downsize all need to ride with it, or the new sky will still break 10.11 in boss fights and Eclipse sectors.

## Findings

1. Star snow: the count, the floor and the size are all wrong at once. OBSERVED. godot/world/world_sky.gd, SKY shader star line: "star_layer(d, 120.0, 0.955, 1.0, bd * 0.03) + star_layer(d, 300.0, 0.985, 0.55, bd * 0.012) + star_layer(d, 650.0, 0.994, 0.35, bd * 0.004)". Three layers at thresholds 0.955, 0.985 and 0.994 put roughly 6,800 to 11,600 stars inside one 45-degree battle frame (about 85,000 on the sphere, per the plan doc), against RULEBOOK 10.11's "About 400 star pinpoints in a 45-degree battle frame". Inside star_layer the brightness is "gain * (0.15 + 2.6 * m)", a flat floor that keeps the faintest star at 15 percent of full gain, and the size "sig = px * (0.65 + 0.9 * m)" renders them as soft blobs. The frame's negative space is therefore filled with mid-bright noise, which is what Cohesion Law 1 Band 1 exists to prevent ("0.00 to 0.015 (near-black)", the majority of the sky frame). Unanimous across all four legs. Fix: land the 1766 field, about 400 pinpoints per 45-degree view gathered on the band, a steep zero-floor magnitude curve (pow(m, 3.0) or steeper), pinpoints tightened but kept at or above about half a pixel at 1080p so faint stars do not shimmer under camera orbit, and only a handful of hero stars (legs suggest 5 to 15) at full gleam.

2. Dust lanes cannot black out stars. OBSERVED. godot/world/world.gd:524, "m.set_shader_parameter("star_floor", float(sf) if sf != "" else 0.15)", with the same 0.15 default in the SKY shader (world_sky.gd, "uniform float star_floor = 0.15;"). Stars mix through the baked nebula at star_floor, so 15 percent of the field punches through fully opaque dust; rifts read as grey gauze. RULEBOOK 10.11 sets star_floor = 0.0 with the VS_STAR_FLOOR override kept for tuning sheets. Fix: default 0.0 in both places (Qwen's caution stands: fixing only the shader default leaves world.gd reintroducing 0.15 on every battle).

3. Two faction palettes break the colour rules, and the sky is where they cover the most pixels. OBSERVED. godot/fx/faction_looks.json: deathless nebula mid "[0.34, 0.14, 0.52]" is a true purple and chorus carries "[0.55, 0.38, 0.12]" with "[0.8, 0.9, 0.35]", the mustard-olive pair; RULEBOOK 10.11: "No mustard fog and no purple Eclipse nebula". These entries become c1, c2 and c3 for every sky in those factions' sectors via world_sky.gd setting(), and the rival mood lerps other skies toward them. Fix: re-palette both entries inside the sky work (Eclipse to dark ash-obsidian with its existing gold bright kept as the warm accent; Chorus to cold slate dust with muted mineral cyan-amber, per the 1766 brief's crystal-belt language), not as a separate later faction pass.

4. A generator path can manufacture banned hues from compliant inputs. OBSERVED code, INFERRED reach. godot/world/world_sky.gd setting(): "c2 = c2.lerp(Vector3(c1.z, c1.x * 0.8, c1.y) * 0.6, 0.55)" fires when a faction's two gas colours sit close in hue, pushing the shadow colour toward a channel-swapped complement nobody picked; for blue factions (hegemony, ithar) that path leans magenta-purple. Fix: replace the swap with a sanctioned shadow construction (a cooler, desaturated, darker tone of c1, as Gemini suggests), or clamp its output against the same hue rules the palettes follow.

5. The regional sky gas and the mood stack can overwhelm the V2 foreground. OBSERVED values, INFERRED totals. world_sky.gd REGION table (line 36): nebula region "neb 1.0, cover 0.72"; STOP_MOOD boss multiplies neb by 1.6 and cover by 1.25 (cover clamps at 1.0 in stop_mood, so a nebula-region boss fight runs cover 0.90), and the bake itself pushes saturation ("col = max(mix(vec3(lum), col, 1.45), vec3(0.0))") plus a luminance gain. Against that, the on-board rose nebula (10.9), the cyan storms, comets, V2 planets and V2 ships all have to read. The sky gas is a second subject competing with the first. Fix: pull the regional neb and cover down (deepest cut in the nebula region), cap post-mood gas luminance inside Band 2 (legs converge on a peak around 0.12 to 0.25 linear, with the majority of the frame in Band 1), and let boss intensity come from coverage and contrast rather than raw gain.

6. The galactic band is the right landmark, turned down too far. OBSERVED. world_sky.gd galaxy_band(): "return col * lum * band * 0.06". The band has a core, a wide skirt, a warm centre and a genuine rift term, then gives back 94 percent of itself. Once the stars are cut, the band is the sky's depth anchor and its per-sector identity (band_n is seeded per setting). DeepSeek's caution is adopted: stronger must mean more structured, not brighter as a ribbon. Fix: raise the core into Band 2 (legs suggest a 0.10 to 0.18 linear peak) with a low skirt (under about 0.03), and get the drama from the rift: let the rift lanes approach true black so the band reads as a spine cut by dust, the Elite Dangerous lesson.

7. The 8-bit bake, and a resolution trap inside the 1502 fix. OBSERVED. world_sky.gd BAKE_BODY: "uniform vec2 full_size = vec2(4096.0, 2048.0)", output gamma-encoded into 8 bits with a half-step dither ("g += (h3(vec3(px * 1.37, seed + 7.0)) - 0.5) / 255.0"). Dark gradients band at 8-bit (issue 1502), and the frame shader's 2.2 power and mood gains re-stretch the dark steps. The 1502 direction (2048x1024, 16-bit half float, linear, no mipmaps on neb_tex) is endorsed by every leg, with one trap Qwen caught and the chair confirms by arithmetic: the five far galaxies are baked into the same picture at "sizes 0.004 to 0.012 radians"; at 2048 texels around the equator one texel spans about 0.176 degrees, so the smallest galaxy (about 0.23 degrees) covers roughly 1.3 texels and its spiral shading smears. Fix: land 1502 with 1766 (sequencing matters: once the snow is gone, banding becomes the most visible defect in the frame), and move the far galaxies into the frame shader beside the lamp stars, or keep them on a separate high-resolution layer, before the gas bake drops to 2048.

8. Small depth-killers: lamp halos and dust specks. OBSERVED counts, INFERRED weight. The frame shader draws six lamp stars with wide halo terms ("exp(-a * a / 0.00006) * 0.25 + 0.0006 / (0.0004 + a * a) * 0.08"), and world.gd dust() seeds 1,300 to 2,600 specks around and below the board. Each is defensible; together with findings 1 and 5 they busy the exact depths where silhouettes should be alone. One correction to two legs: the salvage mood's 2.6 dust multiplier scales the bake's dust parameter (the gas lanes), not the 3D speck count, which is fixed per map kind in dust(); the speck counts do not multiply to 5,200. The underlying point survives in weaker form: after the star cut, the specks become the most visible particulate in frame. Fix: tighten the lamp halo falloff, and thin the default speck counts modestly, keeping dust as the parallax cue rather than a texture.

## Is the 1766 direction still right?

Yes, unanimously, and the foreground V2 work strengthens it. Planets, asteroids, stations and ships all moved toward measured bands and grounded palettes; a near-black, band-anchored sky is the only backdrop all of them can sit in front of without a value fight. The council's adjustments, which the chair adopts:

- Scope: findings 3, 4 and 5 (faction palettes, the complement swap, the mood luminance cap) must be explicitly in the sky build or a named fast follow. Qwen's objection is the sharpest statement of this: confirming the direction is not confirming the scope, and at 400 stars the colour and brightness pipeline could still ship mustard, purple and hull-band boss skies.
- Sequencing: 1502 lands with or before the star retune, and the far galaxies move out of the bake before the downsize (finding 7).
- Values: treat 400 as a frame average scaled by the region stars column (Qwen: deep about 260, nebula about 360), hero stars in single digits to low teens, band core 0.10 to 0.18 with the skirt under 0.03, post-mood gas capped inside Band 2.
- One disagreement recorded, not adopted: Gemini would clamp gas strictly inside Band 1 across most of the frame; the chair holds that Band 2 exists precisely for landmarks, so the rule is majority Band 1 with Band 2 peaks, not Band 1 everywhere. Gemini's own objection (the empty-black-box risk) is answered by findings 5 and 6 together: the frame keeps its gas and its band, just subordinated and structured.
- The Dead Star seam fix stays as scoped in the 1766 build brief; no leg re-derived it, and the council's only Dead Star note is Qwen's: regrade the painted 8K panorama through the same value caps so that board matches the V2 skies around it.

## Top 5 upgrades, ranked (what you would see)

1. The star cut with star_floor 0.0 (findings 1 and 2). The battle frame goes from TV static to deep space: ships, beams and hex lines sit on near-black, dust lanes carve real black shapes through the band, and a few hero stars gleam by temperature colour. The single biggest visual change available in the game right now.
2. The band as the landmark (finding 6). A tilted Milky Way spine split by dark rifts gives every battle a horizon and a sense of place, different per sector, at no cost to foreground brightness.
3. Calm the regional sky gas and cap the moods (finding 5). The rose on-board nebula, cyan storms, comets and V2 planets stop fighting a second faction-coloured sky; nebula regions still feel gaseous, but distant, low and behind the board, and boss fights keep dark space behind the weapon fire.
4. Palette governance (findings 3 and 4). Eclipse skies stop being purple (ash-obsidian and ember-gold, which matches Eclipse's own ships), Chorus skies lose the mustard, and no generator path can quietly reintroduce either.
5. The 16-bit bake at half size, galaxies moved to the frame shader (finding 7). Dark gradients stop banding when the camera moves or a mood lifts them, the far galaxies stay crisp instead of smearing at 2048, and sector bakes get faster, not slower.

## References, distilled from the legs

- The Expanse: near-black space, sparse hard stars, one motivated key light; ships own the frame.
- Homeworld (1, 2, Cataclysm, Remastered): one painterly low-frequency gesture per sky, kept dim behind a tactical board; this game's seeded feature direction already does this, protect it.
- EVE Online: faction-coloured gas as regional identity with rifts that fully occlude stars; treat its brightest nebulae as the cautionary ceiling, not the target.
- Elite Dangerous: the galactic band with a dust rift you can orient by; take the band and the temperature colours, not the star count.
- Nebulous: Fleet Command: dark space tuned so sensors, grid and fire are the bright things.
- Battlestar Galactica (2004): almost empty black around the fleet makes every hull light read; confidence to leave the frame empty.
- 2001 and Interstellar (film): hard pinpoints, near-black void, and one HDR hero landmark against disciplined black, which is exactly the Dead Star board's model.
- Freespace 2 and the original Star Wars trilogy (DeepSeek): restrained density with a readable band; the special editions' star snow is the counterexample.
