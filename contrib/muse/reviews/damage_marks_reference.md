# Damage marks reference (brief muse_2026-10-26c, Job 3, synthesis)

Chair synthesis by Rey, Oct 26, 2026. Inputs, all filed on main: DeepSeek leg (contrib/deepseek/damage_marks_review_deepseek_raw_2026_10_26.md), Qwen leg (contrib/qwen/damage_marks_review_qwen_raw_2026_10_26.md, Qwen3.8-Max, both gates passed, no live web access in session, claims from-knowledge), Gemini Argon leg (contrib/gemini/reviews/damage_marks_review_gemini_2026_10_26.md, Argon High), Rey audit (contrib/muse/reviews/damage_marks_review_rey.md). Screening notes for each leg are with the chair. This file is the merged reference for the R43 ruling (every weapon leaves its own mark on the hull); the legs are the evidence, this file is what to build from.

## Verdict

Buildable as specified, with strong four-way convergence on the architecture and on most individual marks. The system the four legs describe is the same system: per-type marks on a shared damage layer, read at fleet distance by shape family and freshness light, budgeted per hull section, with repeats merging instead of stacking, battery fire kept deliberately weakest, and silhouette change reserved for ram and torpedo. The disagreements are small and named below. Two marks are judgement-led in every leg (acid pitting, flak pockmarks): no reference game gives either a sourced signature treatment, so those two entries are proposals to prototype, not references.

## Where all four legs agree

1. Two-tier read. At fleet distance (20 ships), a mark reads by shape family, brightness and silhouette, never by texture detail. Detail (cracks, punctures, pit texture, structure) is a close-zoom layer. War on the Sea's developer chose region-level damage texture over small decals for exactly this reason (DeepSeek leg); every leg lands on the same split.
2. Homeworld 3 is the anchor reference. Blackbird's dev blog specifies four damage types (Kinetic, High Explosive, Energy, Ion), each with its own visual treatment and per-type decals, then Splat Damage, which alters surface geometry at the impact point, with scars persisting so a ship's history reads at a glance. Three legs source this directly; the Qwen leg's Homeworld 3 account (generic darkening, no per-weapon marks) understates the documented system and is the outlier (see Screening).
3. Budget per hull section, merge repeats. Every leg caps signature marks per section and grows or deepens the existing mark on a repeat hit instead of placing a twin. Numbers converge on a starting point of 3 signature marks per section (Argon: 3 hero decals per section, 8 to 10 per ship; Qwen: 3 distinct plus 1 zone plus 1 overlay per section; Rey: 3 per section; DeepSeek is looser at 4 to 6 signature, 12 to 20 total). Start at 3 per section and tune on a real board.
4. Fresh glows, scars do not. Fresh: bright glow, sparks, particles. Cooling: glow dies, colour settles. Scarred: dark, unlit, albedo change only. FreeSpace 2's beam decals (glow, then blacken) are the classic pattern (DeepSeek). Argon adds the turn-based framing: fresh means this turn, so a bright mark also answers "what just got hit".
5. Battery fire is the weakest mark and bakes into a general scorch layer. All four legs, independently. Past a low count per section, stop placing individual battery marks and darken the section instead. Hegemony plasma-trait batteries are the one exception: they use the plasma splash at reduced size (Rey, Qwen; Argon covers plasma as its own entry).
6. Silhouette is the strongest channel. Ram crumple and the torpedo crater are the only marks all four legs allow to change the ship's outline, and they anchor the heavy end of the language. Children of a Dead Earth and Nexus support structural over surface damage (Qwen, Rey).
7. Smoke and fire are a severity channel, rationed, never the only channel. DeepSeek's negative finding on Sins 2 (player threads: hulls look new until they explode; particles attached, hull surface unchanged) is the failure mode. Rey: reserve persistent smoke for the worst ships and sections or 20 ships become a fog bank. Argon: a disabled section vents or smokes until the disable expires.
8. Ion is a branching figure in all four legs (lightning tree, Lichtenberg pattern, fern): bright blue-white fresh, etched dark branches scarred, one growing tree per section rather than a new pattern per hit. The most settled mark in the set.
9. Plasma is a splash: a crater bowl or cluster with droplets thrown out, merging into a worked crater field under sustained fire. All four legs.
10. A destroyed or disabled section replaces accumulation. Past the budget, the section goes to its heaviest state (breach, exposed structure, lights out) instead of collecting more marks. Argon's section lights shutoff on disable and Rey's heaviest-scar-plus-smoke rule are the same move; this also satisfies the pack requirement that critical-hit section disables read on the hull.

## The merged per-mark reference

Sizes are relative to the hull section unless stated. Fresh / cooling / scarred applies to every entry.

1. Lance, burn-through. Distance read: a lit slot, a narrow line segment, the only line-shaped mark. Fresh white-hot core with sparks; cooling to deep red, then a dark glassy seam with a melted rim. A pierce may show a smaller lit point on the far side (Rey, Argon exit bloom). Budget: cap 2 to 3 slots per section (Argon, Qwen); repeats lengthen or widen the slot, then peel it open (Qwen "lanced open").
2. Plasma, splash craters. Distance read: a soft bowl or crater cluster with a fan of droplet spots on one side; the droplet fan is the identifier (Rey). Fresh the brightest battery-class mark, hot core (colour family is contested, see Divergences); cooling, glow retreats to the bowl floor, droplets dark first; scarred, a matte dark bowl with a freckled skirt. Bowls merge into one crater field.
3. Gauss, punctures. The honest problem mark: the hole cannot read at fleet distance (all four legs agree). Distance read is a bright companion cue: a pale stress ring with short radial cracks (Rey) or a silver-white petaled collar (Argon); a row of two or three along the shot path is the signature. The clean black hole is the zoom read. Scarred: dark pinhole with a pale cracked halo, deliberately quiet. If the ring fails in playtest, grow the ring, not the hole (Rey); a bigger hole collides with the crater marks.
4. Torpedo, blast craters. Distance read: the biggest mark, a wide crater that dents the local outline, plates peeled outward. Fresh: fire and smoke, the one mark allowed to carry real fire into cooling (Armada 2's fires as a state with a lifetime, Rey and DeepSeek). Scarred: large dark crater, bent plate petals, exposed ribs at zoom. Budget: one crater per section; a second hit upgrades it a stage instead of placing a second (Rey; Argon allows 1 to 2, Qwen up to 3, chair takes Rey's stricter rule as the starting point because torpedoes are rare and heavy).
5. Flak, pockmarks. Distance read: one stippled matte patch per section, a grey rash with a hard cluster boundary that catches light differently from the hull. Fresh: a sparkle of tiny flashes settling in seconds. Scarred: matte, roughened, lower shine. The patch grows in area and density, never in depth; flak never exposes structure in the mark language (Rey). Judgement-led in all legs (Starsector's speckle sheet is the closest analogue, DeepSeek and Argon).
6. Bio, acid pitting. Distance read: an irregular eaten patch with lacy edges and a colour shift (sickly green-yellow to olive) plus a wet sheen that outlasts other marks' matte finish. Fresh: glossy, bubbling edge, faint vapour. Cooling breaks the usual pattern on purpose: the acid keeps working briefly, the patch spreads a little, then visibly stops (edges go matte inward) when the effect ends, the Armada 2 ongoing-state pattern (Rey). Scarred: pitted corrosion bloom with stained streaks along the hull's flow lines. On Chorus hulls read as blistered flesh, same shape language (Rey, per the game's art rules). Judgement-led in every leg; no sourced reference exists.
7. Ion, burns. Distance read: the branching tree (see agreement 8). Fresh arcs crawl the branches for a second or so, tying the mark to the shield-strip moment; scarred, a faint fern etching, quiet at fleet distance, which is fine because ion's tactical moment is fresh. Repeats re-energise and extend the existing tree.
8. Ram, crumple. Distance read: a dent that breaks the outline, folded plating, bare-metal scrape streaks leading in along the impact direction. Must read with no glow and no particles. Deformation does not cool; the dent is permanent unless the section is actually repaired, which makes repair visible too (Rey). The ramming ship takes a matching smaller crumple on its own contact area. A second ram on the same section widens the dent and may tear a plate free.
9. Boarding, breaches. Distance read: a torn gash or pried opening at a plausible boarding point (airlock, docking collar), never scattered randomly (Rey, Argon socket rule), with what comes out of it while the action is live: venting gas plume (Rey), interior light and intermittent sparks (DeepSeek), muzzle-flash strobes from inside (Argon). Cooling: venting stops when the boarding resolves; scarred, a dark gash partly covered by a mismatched patch plate, with the tendril attach ring (a rough arc of small holes) visible at zoom (Rey). Chair note: the strobe idea is strong but check it against the game's flash discipline (at most 3 counted flashes per second, accessibility job) before adopting.
10. Battery, generic. Distance read: almost nothing per hit, on purpose; a section gradually looking worked over. Small scorch dot with a brief glow kiss, softening to grey-brown, merging into the section's general scorch layer. Hegemony plasma batteries use entry 2 at reduced size. Voidsworn las guns may take a faint streak instead of a round dot, at the same low strength (Rey).

## Readability rules (merged)

1. The one-second test (Rey): at fleet distance a player glancing at any ship should be able to say how hurt it is, which section is worst, and roughly what has been hitting it (heavy explosive, energy, kinetic, bio, boarding). Exact weapon identity is a zoom read. Build and review marks on a full 20-ship board, not on a hero ship (all four legs).
2. Two channels carry the distance read: brightness (freshness and active states) and shape family (slot, bowl, ring, crater, patch, tree, dent, gash, dot). Colour supports and carries neither alone: hull paints, faction-coloured shields and cyan interface elements already spend the colour budget (Rey; Qwen's hot / cold / structural family split is the same discipline stated as palette, and is adopted as a sorting check, not as the primary carrier).
3. Glow ration: only the freshest marks per ship glow, starting point at most 2 glowing marks and 1 particle emitter per ship at fleet distance (Rey; Qwen caps emitters at the 3 worst sections per ship; tune by profiling).
4. Contrast floor: every mark must pass on the lightest, darkest and most saturated hull paints in the game; dark marks get a pale or hot rim, pale marks a dark core (Rey).
5. Size floor: no distance-tier element below a few screen pixels at default tactical zoom; subpixel marks shimmer and vanish, which is why gauss gets a ring and flak gets a patch (Rey).
6. Facing honesty: marks land where hits land; a ship hurt on its far side reads through the freshness channel over the silhouette (smoke, venting, edge glow) plus the target readout. Do not mirror marks to the camera side (Rey).
7. Repairs and regrowth visibly reverse marks: new plate or growth covers scars, patches appear over breaches, crumple stays only where the section was not truly restored. Hull language and game state must not disagree (Rey).
8. Model-space or triplanar mapping for the big marks so lance slots and torpedo craters do not clip at UV seams (Argon, from Nebulous: Fleet Command's documented approach), and design hulls with clean armour slabs where marks land; heavy greeble breaks projected decals (Argon).

## Divergences the chair is not smoothing over

1. Homeworld 3's system: three legs document per-type marks and Splat Damage from the dev blog; the Qwen leg describes generic darkening. The documented version wins on sourcing. Qwen's leg is still used for design reasoning, where it is strong.
2. Starsector's mechanics: DeepSeek and Argon document an armour cell grid plus decal and glow overlays; the Qwen leg's "pre-drawn sprite variants, not procedural decals" is a mischaracterization (see its screening note). Use the DeepSeek and Argon accounts.
3. Plasma colour: Rey specifies a hot white-blue core (with an explicit warning to avoid shield colours and let shape carry identity); Argon offers green-white or magenta-orange matching Hegemony plasma colour; DeepSeek and Qwen use orange-yellow thermal. Unresolved. Chair recommendation: decide from the game's actual Hegemony plasma projectile colour so mark and shot match, and keep the droplet fan as the carrier either way.
4. Continuous masks versus discrete states: Rey's channel-mix system and DeepSeek's Splat account accumulate continuously; Qwen and parts of Argon favour authored discrete states per section (BattleTech pattern). These combine cleanly: continuous channels underneath, with the section's visible state stepping through authored stages (intact, marked, heavily marked, breached or disabled) for the heavy marks. The chair reads this as an implementation choice for Opus, not a design conflict.
5. Budget numbers differ by leg (above). All are judgement placeholders; the merged starting point is 3 signature marks per section, 1 zone mark per section, battery in the scorch layer, 1 torpedo crater per section. Playtest on the real board before locking.

## Turn-based specifics (Argon and Qwen, adopted as flags)

Fresh means this turn in a turn-based game, so emissive decay should be keyed to turns, not seconds (Argon: decay fresh emission by roughly 80 percent after the turn of impact). A full turn's damage can land at once, so simultaneous fresh flashes may need staggering or queueing to keep each hit readable (Qwen, flagged UNSURE by its own leg). Both need playtesting, neither blocks the reference.

## Open questions for Opus

1. Performance: per-ship damage textures, mask channels and decal counts for 20 ships in Godot at the game's art scale. No leg could source a Godot figure; all mark budgets above are provisional until profiled. Fallback that keeps most of the value (Rey): baked scar textures plus live effects only for fresh hits.
2. Hull section count per ship: the budgets assume roughly 4 to 8 sections; the pack did not state the count (Qwen UNSURE). Budgets scale with the real count.
3. Shield-only hits: should damage absorbed entirely by shields or armour leave any hull mark? Qwen raises it as the empty-hull problem. Chair recommendation: no hull mark for shield-absorbed hits (shield visuals already carry that read); hull marks mean hull damage. This is a design call for Ryan if he disagrees.
4. Boarding strobes versus the flash discipline: reconcile Argon's muzzle-flash strobes with the counted-flash cap before building them.
