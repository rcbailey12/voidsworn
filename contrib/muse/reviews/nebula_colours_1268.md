# Outside review: nebula region colours (issue 1268 sheets, PR 1304)

Review of the issue 1268 nebula colour sheets on PR 1304 (branch
opus/issue-1268 at f438eaf7, docs/shots/issue-1268/): five regions
(nebula, frontier, boneyard, deep, world), five cloud options each
(A violet, today's colour; B smoky slate; C dust gold; D steel blue;
E rose), same battle, seed 1269, ships hidden, teal ion storm beside the
nebula in every tile.

Council: blind Rey leg first, then DeepSeek (DeepThink on, Search off)
and Qwen (Qwen3.8-Max, gate verified both ends). Grok was blocked by the
cloud bot wall before the brief reached it. The Gemini (Flash) seat did
not run: its Google sign-in was still waiting on the phone approval when
this review was chaired. Both seats are recorded empty; the review
stands on the three legs that landed. Caveat carried by every leg: the
sheets carry the Board's printed pick labels, so no leg was fully blind;
the divergences below show the labels did not decide the outcome.

## Chaired picks

- nebula: C dust gold (runner-up A). Two legs to one. A sinks into this
  region's own violet sky at c26, the one place today's colour fails;
  gold is the only option that reads as the field's central object and
  separates warm from the teal storm instantly. Qwen dissented for A on
  faction safety and classic signature; its point is answered by the
  hull test below, not by the backdrop.
- frontier: C dust gold (runner-up E). Unanimous. Dust over a near black
  frontier field is the strongest cloud against storm split in the set
  and the right mood: raw, agitated, unsettled.
- boneyard: E rose (runner-up C). Two legs to one. Rose reads as faded,
  dried red over wrecks and is the clearest cloud in the row; gold reads
  alive, wrong mood. Qwen dissented for B smoky slate on mood (ash over
  wrecks); the chair overrules on readability, the same ground on which
  B fails in every other region. The dissent is recorded because
  boneyard is the one region where B's mood case is real.
- deep: A violet. Unanimous. Deep's sky is black, not violet, so violet
  reads as depth and quiet instead of blending; C and E both shout over
  the quiet field.
- world: A violet (runner-up E). Two legs to one, and the weakest pick
  in the set. Keeping today's colour in the home region anchors the set,
  and rose in world reads candy bright against the dark field in the c26
  crops. Qwen dissented for E (a home signature of its own, and A makes
  a third violet); the chair accepts the violet repeat with deep as a
  deliberate cost, to be carried by the sky change below rather than by
  loading Chorus camouflage onto the home region.

The chaired set (C, C, E, A, A) matches the Board's printed picks on all
five regions. That agreement was reached from the pictures by two of
three legs independently; Qwen's three divergences are the live
minority report and are named per region above.

## Ruled out

- D steel blue: out everywhere. All three legs converge: at c170 the
  blue cloud sits directly beside the teal storm and the two read as one
  weather system, so cover and hazard stop being tellable apart; it is
  also the Hegemony blue grey share and fights Hegemony hulls by design.
- B smoky slate: ships nowhere. DeepSeek and the blind leg rule it out
  everywhere (too faint, a grey wash a player can miss as cover, no
  colour identity in any region). Qwen holds it as a boneyard-only tool;
  the chair records the split and ships it in no region.

## The cross set finding (all three legs)

The five regions do not read as five places from these backdrops. The
hex lattice, the teal storm band and the cloud texture are constant; the
rows differ in sky tint and darkness only. Nebula and world share a
violet sky, frontier and deep are both near black, and the per-region
props (planet limb, wreck silhouettes, star density) are small enough to
crop out at gameplay zoom. With the chaired picks the nebula colours
carry most of the region identity, on three families for five regions.

The single change that would most improve the set: grade the skies, not
the clouds. Give one region in each near identical pair a different sky
(lavender, amber black, blue black, pure black starfield, magenta dusk,
with the storm teal and the lattice held constant) and let the sky carry
which place this is, freeing cloud hue for cover legibility and faction
safety. If the skies cannot change, the fallback is DeepSeek's: move
frontier off gold to E rose so gold belongs to nebula alone, accepting
frontier as hostile rather than energetic. The chair does not adopt the
fallback now, because the frontier gold case was unanimous on mood and
readability.

## Before any pick locks: the hull in frame capture

No hulls are in frame in these sheets (ships hidden), so every faction
clash call above rests on the brief's named faction colours, not on
observed hulls. The council's merged test list:

1. All six factions' hulls parked inside each picked cloud, same seed,
   ships visible, at c26 and c170. The named failure risks: Sunward
   ember and Hullbreaker rust over gold (nebula, frontier), Chorus red
   over rose (boneyard), Hegemony blue grey at any blue-grey adjacency.
2. Hulls straddling the cloud and storm boundary, so cover against
   hazard grammar is confirmed with ships present, and cover-read with a
   hull inside the nebula edge.
3. Drain frames with bolts firing: bolts render magenta white, between
   A and E in hue, so bolt flash can momentarily read as cloud bloom at
   contact edges (Qwen's finding; it burdens the violet and rose picks
   most).
4. Deutan, protan and desaturated passes: gold against rust and rose
   against red are exactly the pairs that collapse under colour vision
   deficiency.
5. A motion and zoom pass; every judgement in this review is from
   static renders.

Until at least the Sunward and Hullbreaker over gold and Chorus over
rose captures exist, the picks are recommendations, not locks.
