## Verdict: GO WITH CONDITIONS (notes only, nothing blocking)

1. (NOTE) The "older hulls are unchanged" framing needs one correction, or the turret anchor needs gating on authored. The anchor mechanism is new in this PR (main's fx_death.gd has no "anchor" anywhere; the chair verified) and it is not gated on the hull being authored: in precompute, any surface whose node sits under a registered turret gets sd["tur"] and sd["anchor"], and _cut then sends the whole surface to the piece under its pivot. Any hull carrying PR 576 turret nodes (the recipe cruiser is one) will now keep its turret turning meshes whole at death instead of splitting them by triangle centre. The behaviour is in RULEBOOK 5.17's spirit and may well be wanted, but the PR body says older hulls take the old path, and the queued bench job expects every other moment unchanged; a turreted older hull's death is the one place that expectation can break. Gate it, or correct the claim and let the bench sheets show it knowingly. (DeepSeek raised; chair verified against main and adopted.)
2. (NOTE) State the inside zero at its standard on the record: zero at one split step. The check's tolerance is 0.012 of the length, which is exactly the cut's own refinement step (REFINE_EPS), and its enclosure test also lets a corner shift by that much along an axis before the ray test. At half the tolerance the PR body's own count is 10, all at the torn line. The standard is fair (it is the finest the cut itself can meet, and it is disclosed in the check header), but the merge record should carry "0 of 14,265 at one split step", not a bare zero, so a later reader does not take it as absolute.
3. (NOTE) The fixture's refresh rule is manual and nothing detects a stale fixture. If A16's sheet changes and the fixture is not regenerated, the listed CI check keeps passing against the old ship and nobody is told. A sheet version or hash recorded beside the fixture, asserted by the check, would close it cheaply.
4. (NOTE) authored_interiors_check's conservation assertion changed in this PR from an exact triangle count to at least 90 percent by area (with a 1.0001 upper bound). The change is disclosed in the PR body and is principled (the torn strip is blown away by design, so exact conservation no longer holds), but the 90 percent floor is now the only guard against inside loss in that check; the Board should keep it visible rather than let it drift down later.
5. (NOTE) The cost evidence is sandbox only, and the check prints times but asserts none of them. The worker cut (about 3.1 s on A16, two sandbox cores) and the death pieces' triangle growth (245,828 against 201,910, plus 22 percent, no budget asserted anywhere) get their real reading only from the PC work the PR itself queues (the installed A16 job and the 20-ship destruction_fleet_probe). Nothing here blocks on it; the numbers should not be quoted as PC figures until then.

## The Board's questions

1. ONE HOME: yes. export7.py writes voidsworn_destruct.machines from the build's own machine stamp, grouped by grp, and carries each seam's path; fx_hull.gd reads both into the hull analysis frame (the path transformed as [x, z] points and ordered from -z to +z, the boxes as eight transformed corners); fracture_plan copies the path into the break and builds plan["mach"] from those boxes, and _piece_at routes a point in a machine's box to that machine's piece. The chair grepped fx_death.gd at head for ship and faction names: no ship-specific numbers anywhere; every new constant is a fraction of the hull's own size (REFINE_EPS 0.012 and REFINE_EPS_SKIN 0.02 of the length, path "near" 0.06 of the length, boxes grown 0.002 of the length), and the only faction reference is the pre-existing Chorus warp. One discipline caveat (DeepSeek's inference, chair agrees): a future sheet that omits the machine stamp or the path falls back to the old behaviour silently; check_export is the guard, not the death code.
2. RAGGED, NOT SLICED: yes in mechanism. A path break's signed field is _path_f (crossing parity plus nearest segment distance) plus the same wave sum a plane break gets (_waves at L times 0.012, three waves), with the break's V term set to zero so the authored kink is not doubled; the waves vary with height only near the flanks, so a deck tears where the plating above it tears. The bite on an authored hull is a shaft into the hull from the hit side (drill depth 0.8 of its radius) with wavy walls, placed on the path at the hit side via _break_x, so plating, liner and decks go together where the old ellipsoid left the liner over the hole. Chip seeds and scorch anchors are likewise moved onto the path. Whether it reads ragged in the game is the queued PC pictures job's to show; no headless check asserts the look, and the PR says so.
3. THE CHECK: it proves the PR's numbers under its stated standard, like for like against main. Machines: parts-surface triangles count for a machine only when all three corners sit inside its box (grown 0.001 of the length), ties broken by normalised centre distance, and the same attribution finds 9 split on main, so the 9 to 0 comparison is honest; turret turning meshes are counted by their node and merged into the machine whose box holds the pivot. Insides: a corner already outside the whole ship's plating by more than 0.003 of the length is skipped as a build sheet matter (150 triangles on this branch, the same panels main counted as 68 before they were split), and any other corner must be enclosed by its own piece's skin plus the torn fragments within the 0.012 tolerance. The tolerance equals the cut's refinement step, so the claim the numbers support is "no inside corner stands more than one split step proud of its piece", which is the property the game needs. The one soft spot, beyond condition 2: a machine triangle straddling a box edge is invisible to both the cut and the check, so the check cannot catch a box the export itself drew too small; that is the stage 4 audit's ground, not this check's.
4. FRAME COST AND THE FALLBACK: the main-thread figures (piece build 60 to 90 ms headless, death 22 to 35 ms, break-up about 1 ms, before and after) and the worker growth (1.5 to 2.1 s to about 3.1 s on A16) are the worker's sandbox runs; the check prints the cut's worker time and the profile times and asserts none, so treat them as sandbox figures (condition 5). The fallback itself is sound and pre-existing (issue 461, verified in _lance_recut at head): the ship waits for the lance re-cut unless it is late, and only then breaks on cut_prev, the last finished cut. On an authored hull that earlier cut is built on the same authored seams, so what a late lance kill loses is the lance line's exact placement, not the seam structure; the PR body discloses that A16 lance kills will now usually take this path.
5. THE FIXTURE: it belongs. At 1,559,341 bytes it is the only way the listed headless check can run where the painted C19_51 is not installed (the check prefers the installed GLB and falls back to the fixture), and its refresh rule is stated in three places (the check header, the PR body, the method line): sheet_hull.py, then export7.py unbaked=1, then gzip -9 -n, when A16's sheet changes. Condition 3 covers the one real risk, staleness going unnoticed.

One more chair observation for the record: in _cut, authored skin and interior surfaces are refined against a copy of the plan with "mach" erased, so plating and insides can never be machine-assigned and always tear, while parts surfaces keep the machine routing. That split is exactly what RULEBOOK 5.17 as reworded requires, and it is worth the Board knowing the code enforces it structurally rather than by the boxes happening to miss.
