# First-run experience audit - synthesis (Oct 29 program)

Four prongs: Argon LOST IN THE VOID, DeepSeek LOST IN THE VOID, Rey (blind) LOST IN THE VOID, Qwen ROUGH EDGES.

## Verdict: ONE SYSTEMIC TEACHING FAILURE, GRADED HARSHLY BY THREE OF FOUR

The split is calibration, not fact. All four prongs agree on the structure: the battle layer is taught passably, the campaign and meta layers are not taught at all, and the run's designed centerpiece teaches nothing on the path it is designed to take. Qwen grades those facts ROUGH EDGES; the other three grade them as the first-run experience failing at its job.

## The blocker, stated once with its facets

The tip system structurally cannot teach the campaign layer. tutorialCheck returns whenever there is no battle object or the app shell is up, and the map screen nulls the battle object, so the map, routing, patrons, command points, and run structure receive zero contextual teaching, by construction rather than by weak writing. The facets the legs found are this one fact wearing different clothes:

- Battle 1 can stack up to five modal tip cards 400 ms apart, each carrying a permanent "Hide tips" kill switch, so the teaching that does exist can be ended accidentally in the first minute (Argon). Recovery exists in Settings, and toggling tips back on clears the seen flags so everything re-shows; the surviving defect is discoverability, not absence (chair, from code).
- The one strong micro-teacher is the action strip's hint line, which teaches where to click and never why (Qwen). Extending the hint-line pattern to the campaign layer is the cheap direction all four reads point toward.
- The map's boon badge strips a node to a glyph, a patron short name, and an unexplained star, never printing the word "Boon" or what the star means, so routing decisions (the campaign layer's core decision) are made blind, even though the pick screen itself explains everything (Argon, DeepSeek, Rey).
- The start tip is a purely tactical ship-selection tip. Nothing in the first minutes frames the game's actual contract: runs are generational, loss is the curriculum, and the first run is near-impossible by design (chair, from the TIPS array; Ryan's Oct 28 rulings).

## The Nemesis meeting teaches nothing on the loss path, and the ruling is not yet built

Ryan ruled on Oct 29 (battle Q5): a lost meeting still teaches a weakness on the debrief, and the re-roll of weaknesses per run is fine. At this pin, the win branch of nemAfterBattle reveals a weakness by name and the loss branch returns only the "drove you off" line. So the meeting a first-run player is guaranteed to lose (and, per the Oct 28 ruling, is supposed to lose) delivers neither the weakness nor the fiction that a lesson was carried home. The one mitigation, found by the blind Rey leg: a weakness the player triggers during the battle itself stays revealed even in defeat, so the fight can teach; the debrief cannot. Adaptation debriefing exists only on the boss-win path at sector transition.

## What softens the verdict (chair, from code)

A run 1 loss is not a dead end. Ending a run pays renown on any outcome and mastery XP, itemized on a "Carried forward" panel; the first mastery threshold is only 6 XP, and a sector 1 run that reaches the boss usually clears it, unlocking the alternate fleet for run 2. The failure is legibility, not generosity: the learning-between-generations frame Ryan ruled for on Oct 28 shows up as currencies on a panel, never as story, and nothing tells the new player that this is the intended shape of the game.

## Chair corrections applied

Argon's "forced sector 1 boss loss" overstates: the meeting is forced, the loss is near-certain by design, and those are different claims. Its four blockers are carried as the one systemic blocker above. DeepSeek's "no shown recovery path" for tips is pack-true and code-false; the Settings row exists.

## For Opus (observations, not a gate)

- The Q5 loss-debrief reveal is ruled and unbuilt; it is the single highest-leverage first-run fix on this list.
- Tip gating: let tutorialCheck serve the map layer, or give the campaign its own hint surface modeled on the battle hint line.
- Print "Boon" and explain the star on the map badge.

## Designer questions

None new. The framing work (a first-run moment that states the generational contract) implements Ryan's Oct 28 learning-frame ruling; its text is a design task, not an open decision.
