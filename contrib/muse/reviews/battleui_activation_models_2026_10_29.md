# Battle UI activation models - solutions memo (Oct 29 program follow-up)

Commissioned by Ryan, Oct 29, 2026, in answer to the battle UI synthesis's designer question: rather than picking from the audit legs' remedy patches (confirm, undo window, contextual Fire button), look at how comparable tactics games structure the activation itself, specifically move-then-fire turns and combined action-point pools, and come back with solutions. Chair memo; comparator descriptions are design knowledge, marked as such, not code evidence. Voidsworn facts are from the battle UI item at pin 264f441d.

## The problem to solve, restated

Voidsworn activates one ship at a time, alternating with the enemy. A ship has movement points, a firing set (every bearing weapon, or one armed weapon), special actions (ram, board, ability, wing orders), and Brace as the defensive alternative to firing. Three defects from the audit: (1) a board click on an enemy IS the fire order, so inspection and lethal commitment share one gesture; (2) clicking an out-of-range enemy silently attack-moves the ship to a planner-chosen hex, with the odds the player saw computed from the ship's current hex, not the arrival hex; (3) there is no commitment boundary anywhere in the activation, so nothing can be taken back once clicked, while the game's own information layer (per-facing odds, expected damage, boon attribution) is good enough that players will trust it and click fast.

## How the field structures an activation

Model A: phased activation, move then fire (BattleTech, 2018). One unit activates. The move decision comes first and is freely revisable while you are making it: you see the destination, the facing, and what the move costs you (walking, running, and jumping carry different to-hit penalties, and sprinting forfeits fire entirely). Only when you commit the position does the fire decision begin, and it is made from the arrived position with a per-weapon table of real hit odds. A separate Fire control executes. Brace (and its sprint cousin) is the explicit alternative to firing. Borrowable properties: two decisions, two commitment points, in the order the tactics actually flow; odds are always computed from the real firing position; a planner-chosen destination cannot exist, because the player placed the ship before anyone talked about shooting.

Model B: two-action economy (XCOM 2). Each unit gets two actions. A standard move costs one; firing costs one and ends the turn; dashing spends both on movement. Fire is ability-first: you select the shot, the game shows a confirmation panel with hit, crit, and damage, and a second deliberate input executes. Board clicks never fire anything. Borrowable properties: firing is a named action with its own confirm surface, and the move/fire tradeoff is priced in one visible currency (actions), so "move farther or shoot" is a decision the UI states, not an accident of click order.

Model C: one combined action-point pool (Jagged Alliance 2, Phoenix Point, Xenonauts). Movement drains the pool per hex; firing costs a fixed chunk; aiming tiers spend more points for better odds (Phoenix Point's snap versus aimed shots). Everything a unit does in its activation draws on the same pool, so every hex of movement is visibly bought with firepower. Borrowable properties: the tradeoff is total and legible; post-fire movement and partial moves are native rather than special rules. Cost: it is a rules redesign, not a UI change. Every number that assumes separate movement and fire budgets gets rebalanced: speed values, ram and board costs, wing orders, mid-move fire, and any effect phrased as move-or-fire.

Model D: move and attack with the commitment on the attack (Into the Breach). Each unit moves, then attacks; movement is undoable without limit until an attack is declared, and attack outcomes are shown exactly before commitment. Borrowable property: the lightest possible boundary. Nothing about budgets changes; the game simply declares that movement is rehearsal and attacking is the commitment, and lets you rehearse freely.

Model E: queued orders with a recall window (Battlestar Galactica Deadlock). Orders are issued per ship and execute together; a short window allows revision. Least applicable here: Voidsworn's alternating activations make execution immediate by design, and the audit legs already rejected full undo as genre-wrong. The recall-window idea survives only as a component (see the undo window the legs proposed), not as a model.

## The solutions, mapped onto Voidsworn

Solution 1: Phased activation (Model A with Model D's rehearsal rule). Split each ship's activation into two beats. Move beat: the player positions the ship within its movement points, with the ghost, arrival facing, and reach all live; repositioning is free until the player leaves the beat, and clicking any enemy during the move beat only ever inspects it. Fire beat: entered by ending movement (or automatically on arrival); from the real position, the hover shows odds computed from that hex, the player builds the salvo (armed weapon, a multi-weapon checklist, or all), and executes with the strip's Fire control or a confirming click on the target. Brace remains the alternative to entering the fire beat at all. Attack-move as a hidden behavior disappears: if the planner has a suggested position (the bestFacingReach helper already computes one), it is offered as a visible ghost the player can accept, adjust, or ignore. Mid-move fire, a veteran feature, survives as an explicit choice during the move beat: declare the firing hex on the path, see the odds from that hex, then commit. What it costs: one extra beat of UI state per activation and a strip redesign; no balance numbers change, because movement points and firing sets are untouched. What it fixes: all three defects, structurally rather than by patch.

Solution 2: Action-point pool (Model C). Convert each activation to a single pool: hull speed becomes pool size, each hex costs 1, firing the full set costs a fixed price (say half the pool, tunable), a single armed weapon costs less, ram and board are priced actions, Brace is free and ends the activation. Sequencing problems vanish (the pool is the sequence), move-after-firing effects like the r_slip boon become native instead of exceptional, and the move-versus-fire tradeoff is on the table every activation. What it costs: a genuine rules redesign and a rebalance pass over speeds, action prices, and every move-or-fire effect in the data file, with sims to re-measure admiral and faction balance afterward. This is a Godot-era decision, not a patch: the shared data file carries into the port, so doing it before the playable battle is tested would spend the balance baseline the sims just established.

Solution 3: Minimal commitment patch (the legs' remedies, for completeness). Keep the current single-beat activation; add a confirm on fire-all and attack-move, a destination preview with arrival-hex odds for attack-move, and an undo window until the first animation resolves. Cheapest by far, and it fixes the worst case (the surprise relocation) if the preview is honest. What it does not fix: the inspect/fire gesture conflation remains (a confirm makes the wrong fire deliberate, not impossible), and sequencing stays three manual steps.

## Chair recommendation

Solution 1 now, Solution 2 kept as a named Godot-era experiment. Phased activation is the structure both of Ryan's instincts point at (move-then-fire is BattleTech, the game he named as a feel benchmark, and the action economy he described is Solution 1's beat structure with the pool left implicit). It solves the audit's blocker complex by construction, preserves the information layer the legs graded best in class (odds are still shown pre-commitment, now from the true firing hex), and spends no balance capital before the Godot playtest. Solution 2 is the cleaner core in the abstract and the wrong move today: its cost lands exactly on the sims baseline and the shared data file at the moment the port needs stability. If the phased model still feels wrong in Ryan's hands on the Godot battle, the pool is the next experiment, with the sims re-run to price it.

## Decision for Ryan

Pick the structure: (1) phased activation, (2) action-point pool as a deliberate redesign, or (3) the minimal commitment patch. The attack-move destination preview ships under any of the three and is not a separate question.

DECIDED (Ryan, Oct 29, 2026, evening): phased activation. Solution 1 is the battle UI direction; the AP pool remains a named Godot-era experiment on file, and the minimal patch is superseded (its attack-move preview is absorbed into the phased model).
