# Battle UI and selection process audit - Rey leg (Oct 29 program, blind)

Chair note: this leg was run blind and independently by a separate Rey agent working only from the evidence pack and direct code reads at the pack pin (264f441d). It did not read the other legs, the screenings, or any synthesis. Filed as the fourth prong for the battle UI item under the four-prong pattern Ryan restated on Oct 29, 2026.

## 1. Proof of reading

Read: the EVIDENCE PACK title line; THE COMMISSIONER'S QUESTION; CHAIR CENSUS; WHAT TO JUDGE (items 1-6); and the code slices from ui3d.js: 105-140 (roster pick, weapon rows, panel assembly), 161 (keysHelp), 388-402 (selectUnit and cancelAction), 413-431 (firing set and click-to-fire flow), 466-500 (move damage preview and hover odds), 501-535 (onKey and ACT.arm/board/ability), 665-683 (actionStrip, camera keep/restore/show, stripHelp), and 642-652 (TIPS). Spot-verified against the repo at the pin: attackMovePlan (ui3d.js:412), the wingSel clearing sites (ui3d.js:425), and the onBoardClick branch order.

Overall verdict: FRICTION - the pre-commit information layer (hover odds by facing, expected damage, threat map, boon attribution) is genuinely strong, but the default attack gesture is a single irreversible click that can also move the ship to a hex the player never previewed, and the state model has sticky corners the cancel key does not reach.

## 2. Findings

1. [BLOCKER] Clicking an out-of-range enemy silently becomes a move-and-fire order to an unpreviewed hex. READ: onBoardClick (ui3d.js:428-429) fires the firing set if any weapon bears; if none does, it calls attackMovePlan and immediately executes moveUnit to the planner's chosen state, then fires on arrival. READ: attackMovePlan (ui3d.js:412) scores candidate states by weapon count, cost, and turns; the player never sees the destination ghost, the facing on arrival, or the hit odds from that hex before committing. READ: attackTip (ui3d.js:491-500) computes range, arc, and shield quadrant from the ship's CURRENT hex, so the hover card for an out-of-range target shows weapons as out of range or out of arc with no expected damage, and the only preview of the attack-move is a text line naming its movement cost and weapon count. INFERRED: this is the worst misclick shape in the UI, because the same click means "inspect" in the player's mental model (the panel's own empty state says "Click an enemy to see its weapons and firing arcs") and "move my ship across the board and fire" in the game's. A confirm step, or a ghost preview of the destination state with odds computed from it, is the minimum fix.

2. [BLOCKER] Fire-all is the default and is irreversible, with no confirm and no undo. READ: firingSet (ui3d.js:417) returns every bearing ready weapon when nothing is armed; the click executes them in sequence at 80 ms gaps; the census states "there is no undo" and "a ship that has fired cannot brace afterward." READ: the one irreversible-with-consequence action that does get protection is the admiral Command, which requires a second tap within 6 s (actionStrip, ui3d.js:665-671); fire, ram, and board get no equivalent. INFERRED: the protection is allocated backwards. Command is once per battle and telegraphed gold; a fire-all click happens dozens of times per battle, spends the ship's whole activation value, and forecloses Brace. Either a pre-commit confirm for fire-all and attack-move, an undo window that closes when the first animation resolves, or single-weapon as the default with fire-all as the explicit gesture would align the commitment cost with the gesture cost.

3. [FIX] The inspect-vs-fire split is modal and state-dependent. READ: the identical left-click on an enemy inspects it when nothing usable is selected (ui3d.js:431), fires on it when a usable friendly ship is selected (:428), attack-moves when nothing bears (:429), and shows a cannot-fire reason on first click with inspection only on a second click inside 3 s (the G.lastWhy branch). READ: a roster click on an enemy always inspects and clears the selection (ACT.rosterPick, ui3d.js:112), so the roster and the board disagree about what clicking an enemy means. INFERRED: inspecting enemies is a constant, low-risk information action in a 20-ship battle; binding it to the same gesture as irreversible fire guarantees misclicks in both directions (shots the player did not intend, and inspections that deselect the ship they were about to order). Left-click should inspect always; fire should require the armed state, a second click on an already-inspected target, or a dedicated control.

4. [FIX] Arming is single-slot, non-persistent, and its default is undocumented in the help text. READ: ACT.arm (ui3d.js:524) toggles one weapon index; selectUnit clears B.armed (:391); onBoardClick clears B.armed after firing (:428-429). READ: keysHelp (ui3d.js:161) says "1-6 pick weapon" and never states that no armed weapon means fire everything. READ: weapon sequencing matters mechanically, because the weapon table carries shield-stripping (ion), piercing, anti-squadron, and cooldown properties, and boonMods (ui3d.js:475-489) shows shot-dependent effects keyed to which weapons fire. INFERRED: firing an ion weapon first and a hull weapon second is a two-step manual re-arm under time pressure, and a player who forgets the reset silently dumps the whole rack, including long-cooldown weapons. A visible Fire All / Single mode indicator, persistence of the armed choice per ship until changed, and simple weapon groups would fix the discoverability and the sequencing cost without slowing the fast path.

5. [FIX] Wing selection is sticky state that the cancel paths do not clear. READ: clicking a friendly squadron sets B.wingSel (ui3d.js:426); the wingSel branch (:425) intercepts the next board click on any non-squadron ship and assigns a strike or guard order, clearing the selection only then (or when the wing dies or empty water is clicked). READ: cancelAction (:402) unwinds mode, then armed, then selection, and never touches B.wingSel; selectUnit (:391) and ACT.rosterPick (:112) do not clear it either, verified by grep at the pin (B.wingSel is assigned null only inside the :425 branch). INFERRED: a player who selects a wing, changes their mind, and presses Esc believes the state is cancelled; their next click on a ship gives the wing an order instead of selecting or firing. Esc's top layer should be wingSel, or selecting any ship should clear it.

6. [FIX] The hint line can declare a ship finished while it still has options. READ: actionStrip (ui3d.js:667-669) computes moved as u.mp < u.speed and canHit from the current target list; when a ship has moved any amount and nothing is currently hittable, striphint reads "Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship." regardless of remaining movement points, an unused Brace, an ability, or a wing stance change. INFERRED: the hint line is the game's most trusted teacher (it is contextual and always present), so a false "nothing left" trains players to end activations early and strands real options. The condition should test remaining movement and unspent orders, not merely that movement was spent.

7. [FIX] Help coverage lags the input surface. READ: keysHelp (ui3d.js:161) lists Tab, Q/E, 1-6, B, V, Esc, Space, and Enter. READ: onKey (ui3d.js:501-520) also binds R (threat map), X (ram), G (board), F (focus camera), T (top view), and Home (camera reset), none of which appear in keysHelp; the threat map's own TIP teaches R, but ram and board are taught only in a later TIP as "(X)" and "(G)" parentheticals. READ: stripHelp explains six buttons in one static block while the strip's contents change with ship type and state. INFERRED: the undocumented keys are not exotic; R and the ram/board keys are core combat inputs. Dynamic help keyed to the visible strip, plus a complete keysHelp, is a cheap fix. Separately, READ: the strip and hints use "Tap" wording throughout (striphint, stripHelp, the wing toast) in a game the census describes as desktop-only; the wording is a leftover worth a text pass.

8. [NOTE] The information layer is above the field standard, with three specific gaps. READ: attackTip shows per-weapon hit chance, the shield facing struck and its current strength, which hull section lies under it, expected shield and hull damage in aggregate, a likely-kill flag, terrain and motion modifiers, and boonMods lists the active boons modifying the shot by name. READ: moveDmgLine (ui3d.js:466) gives expected damage for a hovered move hex. Still hidden, INFERRED against the comparators: the destination and odds of an attack-move (finding 1); per-enemy threat attribution (the R map shades aggregate reach, while the inspect panel names which of your ships one enemy can reach, so attribution exists in one direction only); and moveDmgLine's expected damage is computed against the auto-facing helper's chosen gunTarget, which the line itself does not name, so a player can read an expected-damage number without being told which enemy it describes.

9. [NOTE] Auto-facing optimizes gun count only. READ: bestFacingReach (ui3d.js:393-399) scores each arrival state by ready non-flak gun count, then movement cost, then turns, and stores the best target per hex. INFERRED: defensive facing (which of your own shield facings you present) is not part of the score, and arming a single weapon does not recompute the facing choice around that weapon. At small fleet sizes this is a convenience; at 20 ships it quietly makes tactical decisions the player may not endorse. Low severity, worth a look when the arming model is revisited.

10. [NOTE] Camera discipline is a strength with one scale risk. READ: camKeep/camRestore (ui3d.js:678-679) save the player's view before the enemy turn and restore it after, unless the player moved the camera themselves; camShow (:680) pans most of the way toward an off-screen actor and never changes zoom; battles open wide, then push in. INFERRED: this respects player orientation better than the comparators' action cameras. The risk is readability, not orientation: pan-without-zoom at 20 ships plus wings can leave an off-screen activation small and edge-bound. A brief, optional soft zoom on enemy activations, or edge cues for off-screen actors, would cover it; note the designer has already ruled against edge indicators as the primary direction (Oct 28 ruling, in the census), so the soft zoom variant is the live option.

## 3. Explicit checks

1. SELECTION MODEL. One-ship selection is the right foundation for alternating single-ship activations, and Tab cycling plus the roster make it workable at 20 ships. Fire-on-click as the default is not right at that scale: it is irreversible, it hides the weapon-level decision the game's own mechanics make important, and in its attack-move form it commits movement the player never previewed. The inspect-vs-fire split fails as designed: one gesture, four outcomes (fire, attack-move, error toast, inspect) selected by invisible state. Keep one-ship selection; separate inspection from firing. INFERRED comparator basis: BattleTech and XCOM 2 both separate target inspection from a confirmed fire action; Into the Breach previews everything before commitment.

2. ARMING. Keys 1-6 and clickable weapon rows are fast, faster than menu-driven called shots, and the per-weapon panel data (arc, shots, damage, status) is excellent. The model fails on persistence and signposting, not speed: one slot only, cleared by selection and by firing, with the fire-all default nowhere stated in the help. INFERRED: the comparators' weapon-group checklists (BattleTech's per-weapon toggles in the fire confirmation) solve exactly this by making the salvo composition visible and sticky for the activation. Adopt a salvo checklist or per-ship persistent arming; refuse deep called-shot submenus, which this game's facing and section system already covers through positioning.

3. INFORMATION. Closest to BattleTech's odds displays, strengthened past them: BattleTech shows hit percentages per weapon but not expected damage aggregated by shield facing with named boon attribution. It is not Into the Breach's standard, because three things ItB would show are hidden here: the full consequence of the contemplated action (the attack-move's destination and odds), deterministic outcomes (this game is probabilistic, fairly, but its crit and damage spread tails are not shown), and per-enemy intent (aggregate threat shading instead of attributed threat). Against FTL's pause-and-inspect, this game wins on speed and loses on inspectability mid-decision, because inspecting an enemy costs the current selection (finding 3). The single most valuable information fix is previewing the attack-move.

4. ERROR RECOVERY. No-undo is defensible only when commitment gestures are deliberate; here the most frequent commitment is one ambiguous click, so the current pairing (hair-trigger gesture, zero recovery) is the worst combination. Esc layering is good practice and should be extended to wingSel and the Command arm state. The two-tap Command confirm proves the pattern exists in the codebase; extend that discipline to fire-all and attack-move rather than inventing a new one. ADOPT: pre-commit confirmation or undo-before-first-animation for fire and attack-move, Esc coverage of all modal state, and a complete keysHelp. REFUSE: full undo after animations resolve and damage is revealed, which would dissolve the roguelike commitment the rest of the design assumes, and refuse modal confirmation dialogs for routine single-weapon shots, which would tax the fast path.

5. CAMERA AND SCALE. Save/restore plus pan-without-zoom is the right philosophy and better-mannered than the comparators' cinematic cameras. INFERRED: Sins 2 and Deadlock solve 20-plus-unit readability with zoom range and screen-space unit readability rather than camera choreography; this game's equivalent lever is a modest zoom allowance during camShow or a soft action zoom, since its zoom is currently fully manual except the opening move. The camera serves selection well through roster and Tab focus pulls. It fights scale only at the moment of off-screen enemy action, finding 10.

6. COMPARATOR VERDICTS. All INFERRED.
- BattleTech (2018): steal the fire-confirmation panel with per-weapon toggles; avoid its slow end-of-turn camera ceremony.
- Into the Breach: steal total pre-commit preview, including the destination of any move-and-fire; avoid importing its perfect-information puzzle framing wholesale, since this game's uncertainty is a feature.
- FTL: steal pause-and-inspect clarity for enemy loadouts without losing your selection state; avoid its real-time pressure model entirely.
- Battlestar Galactica Deadlock: steal fleet-scale framing and ordnance readability at range; avoid any camera that takes control during resolution.
- Sins of a Solar Empire 2: steal zoomed-out fleet readability and cursor-anchored zoom; avoid RTS right-click-to-focus-fire semantics, which rely on reversibility this game does not have.
- Rogue Trader voidship battles: steal the weight and readability of facing-based broadsides, which this game already echoes; avoid its opaque stacking of modifiers, which boonMods already improves on.
- XCOM 2: steal the explicit shot confirmation and the end-of-turn unused-action warning; avoid its camera rubber-banding.
- Star Wars: Zero Company: no verdict; insufficient reliable basis in this pack to judge its shipped UI against this one.

Single change that would most improve the battle UI: decouple firing from the inspection click, and preview any attack-move (destination ghost, arrival facing, odds from the destination) before it can be committed. One thing it already does better than the field: the hover card's causal transparency, hit odds by named shield facing with expected shield and hull damage, the hull section underneath, and every active boon modifying the shot listed by name. No comparator in the set explains a shot that completely.

## 4. Not checked

Live rendering and legibility at 20 ships plus wings; animation timing and the stall watchdog in practice; the Classic whole-fleet mode's selection model (the slices cover the alternating flow); right-click behavior (mentioned in WHAT TO JUDGE, not present in the slices); the full boarding, ram, and ability flows beyond their click branches; sound, accessibility beyond the reduced-flash setting referenced in the drift material, and gamepad input; the Godot port's implementation of any of this; tutorial effectiveness beyond the TIPS text itself.

## 5. Questions for the designer

1. Is attackMovePlan a deliberate core mechanic (fire on the way in) or a convenience shortcut that accreted? Stakes: if it is core, it deserves a full preview like every other order; if it is a shortcut, removing it, or gating it behind a modifier key, deletes the UI's worst misclick at a stroke.
2. How often should a skilled player fire a subset of weapons rather than the full rack? Stakes: if subset fire is a regular expert move (ion before hull, holding a torpedo), arming deserves persistence and groups; if fire-all is the intended norm, the arming UI should be honest about being an exception path and fire-all deserves the confirm protection instead.
3. When an enemy acts off-screen at 20 ships, which matters more: never disturbing the player's framing, or guaranteeing the action is readable? Stakes: the answer decides between the current pan-only discipline and a soft action zoom, and it sets the camera rule the Godot build will inherit.
