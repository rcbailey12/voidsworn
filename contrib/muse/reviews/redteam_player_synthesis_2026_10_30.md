# Red team audit synthesis: tactics and roguelike player seat (Oct 30, 2026)

Chair synthesis of the player persona legs. Prongs in hand: blind Rey, DeepSeek, Argon (3 of 4). Qwen prong: PENDING FOLD-IN (daily cap at send; folds in as an addendum when it lands). Pin: main head b3a3b802.

## Seat verdict

The 80 percent loss rate is not the problem. The problem is that the game never teaches the contract it enforces, and its centerpiece learning loop runs in one direction. All three prongs converge: the Nemesis adapts for real and pays out almost nothing in return, the first run explains neither the generational frame nor the Nemesis debrief, and the run's actual wall is an anonymous sector 2 elite class while the bosses are endurance checks. A roguelike can survive a high loss rate. It cannot survive losses that teach nothing.

## Claims register: disposition

1. **"Nemesis snowball keeping pace, not snowballing; first adaptation is the lever": OVERTURNED as a general claim, 3 of 3 prongs.** It holds only at full strength. The powered rerun (filed with the wave 5 measurements addendum) decomposes the sector 2 step into the trait package (about 8.5pp) plus one adaptation (about 2 to 7pp), so there is no single adaptation lever to tune. Against battered arrivals, which the measurements themselves call the typical case, the growth cost widens from +14.6pp at full strength to +28.1pp at 65 percent hull and +38.1pp at 40 percent (DeepSeek finding 1, Argon finding 1). The Chorus fast cadence is the one genuinely snowball-shaped channel: +30.8pp at sector 3 and a 15.8 percent bot win rate. The base premium itself (about 1.3x, flat) is not in dispute.
2. **Weakness intel as the counterweight: REFUTED at measurement power, all three prongs.** Rey finding 1 prices both directions: adaptation is worth up to 27.7pp of win rate by sector 3; the weakness payback measured +3.8pp in both sectors, and the rerun withdrew significance ("at this power no sector shows a significant weakness value"). Argon finding 1: even once the Q5 loss-debrief reveal is built, the thing it pays out is worth about 4 points. The loop is a ratchet wearing a two-way costume.
3. **"Run win rate about 20 percent, cross-validated": OVERTURNED as validation (Argon finding 2).** The campaign simulator never spawns the campaign Nemesis. The wave 3 Monte Carlo (60 runs, 20 percent) and the Oct 28 pool (20.8 percent) agree with each other because both measure the same Nemesis-free game. The true campaign win rate, with a Nemesis who beats full-strength bots 32 to 48 percent of the time and battered fleets far more often, is unmeasured and certainly lower. The 80 percent loss ruling itself is Ryan's and stands; what falls is the claim that 20 percent was ever verified.
4. **Q5 ruling (a lost Nemesis meeting still teaches a weakness): ruled and UNBUILT, confirmed 3 of 3.** Rey finding 1 reads the code: the win branch of nemAfterBattle calls nemReveal(); the loss branch at campaign_run.js:309 returns only "drove you off. He will remember this." DeepSeek finding 2 and Argon finding 3 concur. This is implementation work, not a new question.
5. **Firstrun ONE SYSTEMIC TEACHING FAILURE: REFINED from one mechanism to three (Argon finding 5).** The tutorialCheck gate is real (Rey finding 2: ui3d.js:654 returns when there is no battle object, and the map layer nulls it, so campaign teaching is zero). But two more mechanisms are independent of the gate: Battle 1 can stack up to five modal tip cards 400 ms apart, each carrying a permanent Hide tips kill switch; and keysHelp omits R, X, G, F, T, and Home, several of them core combat inputs the tips were the only teacher for. Fixing the gate alone fixes one of three.
6. **Battle UI FRICTION, phased activation as the fix: diagnosis UPHELD, fix REFINED.** The shipped activation still has no commitment boundary (Rey finding 3, DeepSeek finding 3): clicking an out-of-range enemy attack-moves, arrival facing is never previewed, hover odds come from the current hex, and fire-all on that click is irreversible. Phased activation (ruled Oct 29) kills the accidental attack-move. Argon finding 4 shows what it does not fix: the planner's bestFacingReach never weighs which of your own shield facings you present (while TIPS instructs "keep your strongest shield facing the enemy"), the move hover's expected-damage line does not name its target, and the armed weapon resets after one shot and on every selection change, so a shield-strip salvo is three manual steps per ship. Those three belong in the phased activation build spec, or the fix ships with its friction moved, not removed.
7. **"THE LOOP LEAKS, at run 4": REFINED to runs 1 to 2 (Argon finding 3, DeepSeek finding 5, Rey finding 9).** The leak starts before run 4's novelty question matters: run 1's loss teaches nothing (items 4 and 5), the Nemesis rerolls weaknesses per run so run 1's tactical discovery does not carry, and run 2 silently promotes the AI to veteran (main3d.js:11, runs>0) at the exact moment the player's first meta unlock should make them feel stronger. The setting is discoverable in Settings; nothing announces it when it happens.
8. **AI COMPETENT BUT HOLLOW: CONFIRMED, with its campaign consequence priced (Argon finding 9).** At CP parity (0.97, sector 1 normal battles) the player bot wins 94 percent in 3.8 rounds losing 0.28 ships per fight; across the 216-battle matrix the bot wins 75 to 89 percent per faction. Difficulty is therefore manufactured by CP inflation (1.40 to 2.03 in later sectors), which is why normal battles feel like cleanup and all the danger concentrates in sector 2 elites and battered Nemesis meetings.
9. **Boon tax on specialization: UPHELD, 3 of 3.** Zero off-patron fillers while the patron fields 3 or more useful boons, then exactly 1 filler per offer at 4 held and 2 at 5; the favour staircase is about 15pp of Rare-or-better at tiers 1 and 2 and about 52pp at tier 3, worst for the most invested players (Rey finding 7, DeepSeek finding 6, Argon finding 6). Argon adds the routing half: the map's boon badge never prints the word Boon or explains the star, and boons are 62 percent of normal battle rewards and 100 percent of elite and boss rewards, so the campaign's core routing decision is made blind about its most common reward.
10. **Economy MIXED, repair accept-as-is: ruling NOT REOPENED; one new code defect found.** The player cost of the flat regroup stands as recorded (Rey finding 4, DeepSeek findings 7 and 8). Argon finding 7 finds a separate wiring defect: repairMult is live at four call sites, all scaling FREE healing amounts, while the shipyard's paid price never passes through it. The h_repair Heat condition at 0.5 therefore halves free healing, functioning as a penalty on the players who took it expecting a repair discount. That is a bug, not a balance position.
11. **Faction price principle at the capstone: VIOLATED in shipped data, 2 of 3 prongs with data.** The Chorus carrier at 370 is the most expensive carrier in the game (Rey finding 8, Argon finding 8) while Chorus small hulls carry the ruled discount; the discount vanishes at the purchase the whole run saves toward. Argon adds: class-track costs are gated by priceMult but not FAC_PRICE, and capture math ignores priceMult and hull condition, so add-then-decommission at a flat 35 percent can beat a late-decay ransom, an exploit that pays best on a crippled 1 HP capture.

## Ranked actions: Opus lane (technical)

1. Build the Q5 loss-debrief reveal (ruled Oct 29, unbuilt). Flagged chair decision rides with it: the fix spec caps reveals at one new reveal per lost meeting; Ryan has neither vetoed nor confirmed that cap.
2. Fix the repairMult wiring: either route it to the paid shipyard price it names, or stop it scaling free healing. Do not leave a Heat condition functioning as a hidden penalty.
3. Build the Nemesis into the campaign simulator and re-measure the true run win rate and the three-meeting trajectory. Every campaign balance claim in the register is provisional until this exists.
4. Carry the three planner and arming fixes into the phased activation build: shield-facing weighting in bestFacingReach, a named target on the move hover damage line, and weapon arming that persists across shots within an activation.
5. Fix the hint line's false completion state ("Nothing left to do" while movement or Brace remains), clear wingSel on Esc, selectUnit, and rosterPick, and complete keysHelp (R, X, G, F, T, Home). Break the Battle 1 modal stack: queue tips, and make Hide tips recoverable.
6. Print the boon badge's meaning on the map (the word Boon, what the star is) so routing is a decision again.
7. Price the Chorus carrier under the faction principle and route class-track costs through FAC_PRICE. Make decommission payout condition-sensitive (or priceMult-aware) so add-then-decommission stops beating ransom on wrecked captures.
8. Announce the run 2 AI step in the fiction at run start (one line converts a hidden tax into the generational frame), or surface it in the run summary. Technical half is trivial; the framing choice is Ryan's, below.

## Ranked actions: Ryan lane (human direction)

1. **Where should a run go to die?** Story: bosses run 6.2 to 7.9 rounds at 82 to 92 percent bot win rates (long, not lethal), while sector 2 elites cost 1.32 ships per fight at a 69.9 percent bot win rate, the hardest class in the game, and the plurality of runs end in sector 2 (135 of 400). Stakes: players learn to fear a map node and relax at the boss, which inverts the emotional shape every other tactics roguelike trains. Cost: retuning the elite step or the boss lethality is data work in the shared file, cheap to do, expensive to validate without the Nemesis-capable simulator in Opus item 3. Full treatment in the final synthesis.
2. **How much should a weakness be worth?** Story: you ruled that a lost meeting teaches a weakness, and the reveal is being built. Measured, that knowledge is worth about 4pp and is not statistically significant, against adaptation worth up to 27.7pp. Stakes: if intel stays near-worthless, the Nemesis loop is a ratchet no matter how good the debrief text is; if intel is buffed (stronger weakness effects, or weaknesses that also slow adaptation), the rivalry becomes a real two-way system. Cost: a data-side change to weakness effect sizes, plus re-measurement. This is a design intent call the sims cannot make for you.
3. **The run 2 AI step: announce, soften, or keep hidden?** Story: run 2 promotes the enemy AI to veteran with no announcement, at the same moment your first unlock lands. Stakes: announced, it reinforces the generational fiction ("they studied your generation too"); hidden, it reads as personal regression and teaches a false lesson in a game whose contract is that losses teach true ones. Cost: one line of framing text if announced; nothing if kept, except the fairness smell.
