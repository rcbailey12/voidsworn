# Red team audit synthesis: UI/UX seat (Oct 30, 2026)

Chair synthesis of the uiux persona legs. Prongs in hand: blind Rey, DeepSeek, Argon (3 of 4). Qwen prong: PENDING FOLD-IN (daily cap at send; folds in as an addendum when it lands). Pin: main head b3a3b802. Accessibility is carried as a named sub-lens throughout, per the charter.

## Seat verdict

FRICTION and NOT READY both stand, 3 of 3 prongs, and the seat sharpens both. The pre-commit information layer (per-weapon odds by shield facing, expected damage, likely-kill flag, ghost preview) is genuinely best in class. The commitment layer is where the game betrays intent: one click spends a ship's position without showing the destination, inspect and fire share a gesture whose meaning flips on selection state, the primary button cycles through four labels with four meanings, and the settings panel a player opens mid-battle contains developer debug buttons and phone copy on a PC-only build. The accessibility foundations are half built: the second channels and keyboard alternatives mostly exist, and are collapsed, hover-gated, or undiscoverable by default.

## Claims register: disposition

1. **Battle UI FRICTION: UPHELD, 3 of 3.** The Rey leg's firsthand reading sharpens the attack-move finding in one important way: the hover tooltip does disclose the move in words ("Click to burn ${cost} movement into position," ui3d.js:499), so the missing destination hex, arrival facing, and arrival-hex odds are a design choice, not an oversight. Hover odds are computed from the ship's current position (ui3d.js:412, 429); attackMovePlan picks the destination for the player. The empty panel teaches "Click an enemy to see its weapons and firing arcs" (ui3d.js:128); with a ship selected, that same click fires every bearing weapon. Protection is allocated backwards: the only two-tap confirm in the battle guards the admiral Command, the rarest click, while fire-all on the attack-move click is irreversible and leaves the ship unable to brace.
2. **Phased activation (ruled Oct 29): diagnosis fix UPHELD; two build requirements and one cost note added.** The ruling is not reopened by any prong. DeepSeek finding 2 records the cost the chair must carry honestly: phased activation was the heaviest of the three memo solutions, adding roughly 20 extra beats per round at 20 ships, against a synthesis that warned any remedy must preserve the information layer's speed. That is a cost note on a settled ruling, recorded here so the Godot build can budget for it (animation speed and beat pacing become load-bearing). The build requirements: (a) the move preview must gain what it currently lacks even for rehearsal (DeepSeek finding 9): threat overlay, turn count, and path; the threat map exists behind the R key but the preview does not fold it in, so free rehearsal of an under-informed preview rehearses the same mistake; (b) weapon arming must persist across shots within an activation and the shipped TIPS must be rewritten for the two-beat model (Argon finding 4): the current target tip teaches "click to fire every weapon that bears," a gesture that under phased activation only inspects during the move beat.
3. **HUD NOT READY: UPHELD, 3 of 3, with the camera ruling's scope CORRECTED.** In the nine captures the enemy is absent from the start state and the threat view, nameplates merge in the carrier cluster, the Engagement legend is collapsed and unlabeled in all nine, and the tutorial card teaches a cue the screen contradicts. Fairness note all prongs carry: the captures are Godot G2 builds, so they convict the Godot surface directly; neither build has pack evidence of implementing the ruled zoomed-out opening. Argon finding 5 corrects the HUD synthesis's claim that the Oct 28 ruling "settles the framing question in the wider form": the ruling governs battle start. It does not reach the round 2+ threat view (the threat tip itself is gated to round 2 or later, ui3d.js:655), where the follow camera pans without zooming out and can leave an enemy activation half off-screen at 20 ships, with edge indicators explicitly not chosen. Threat hexes without visible threat sources is the unresolved remainder, carried to the Ryan lane below as a scope question, not a relitigation.
4. **The Command gold cue: mechanism found, all three prongs converge.** The tutorial says the Command "glows gold on the strip" (TIPS resolve entry). In the browser build the gilt styling sits on #resolvelbl, a non-button span in the top bar, whose tooltip says Command grows "each round you hold it," omitting the ship-loss growth the tip itself states. The clickable button that actually glows gold is End turn (the primary button). A player following the tutorial cue clicks the gold button and ends their activation instead of firing their once-per-battle signature ability. The HUD synthesis graded this the highest-leverage single fix in its audit; this seat agrees.
5. **Firstrun teaching failure: UPHELD and extended from one mechanism to three (converges with the player seat).** The tutorialCheck gate (no battle object, no tips; the campaign layer untaught) is mechanism one. Mechanism two, Battle 1's modal stack (up to five cards 400 ms apart, each with a permanent Hide tips kill switch), is in this seat's evidence at ui3d.js:661-662. Mechanism three is the recovery asymmetry in item 6.
6. **The firstrun synthesis's tips-recovery correction: SCOPED, per the tracker screening.** The synthesis corrected DeepSeek's "no shown recovery path" by noting the Settings row exists. Argon finding 2 shows that correction is true of the campaign and settings layer and false where the failure happens: the in-battle #setpanel (18 buttons) contains no control that resets o.tipsOff. Chair verification: tipsOff appears exactly twice in ui3d.js, the gate and the setter; there is no reset path in the battle UI. One reflexive Hide tips click during the Battle 1 modal burst silences all 10 battle tips and, because the flag is global, all 16 campaign tips too. Both halves are now on record with their scopes; the synthesis's original correction stands for the settings layer and is superseded for the battle layer. A related trap: wiring the campaign tips through the same chained tutorialCheck will reproduce the 400 ms modal stack on the first sector map.
7. **The assembled campaign tutorial: DEFECTIVE in two named places (Argon finding 3, stands against the filed assembly).** c_nodes lists node kinds and omits boons and patrons entirely, so it never decodes the map's boon badge (glyph, patron short name, unexplained star), and c_boon explains the reward pick screen without mentioning the map glyph or star. And the set's only generational framing sits in c_loss, which fires after the fleet is lost: the contract arrives after the first defeat it was meant to frame. Both are copy-level fixes to two tips plus a framing move, and they are the chair's assembly defects, owned here.
8. **Dead-end progression UI and silent loadout deletion: RECLASSIFIED as blockers (Argon finding 1, converging with the narrative seat).** Locked admiral cards point to "Legacy" (renamed Progression) behind which buyAdmiral and buyLegacy have no UI at all: a functional dead end on the screen where the between-runs currency is spent, filed in Oct 27 as a copy finding. Four hull variant cards delete weapons silently; the shipwright event heals the wrong ship. This seat's position matches the narrative synthesis: broken commitment surfaces are blockers wearing a copy-editing label, and the final synthesis carries them at that weight.
9. **Drift ROT: CONFIRMED in the live HUD.** The settings panel ships developer controls to players (chair verified in the markup: "Copy your recent play data to paste to Claude" on the Copy report button, "Save a snapshot of the game state for debugging" on Report a problem, a concept-hull test toggle, Diagnostics) in one flat 18-button popover with the accessibility toggles (Reduce flashing, Reduce screen shake) buried at the same level. Phone copy ships on the PC-only build ("Medium: tuned for phones," the battle log's "(phones)" label; chair verified). refreshTop still branches the turn label on G.mobile ("Your move" vs "Your activation"), and the same refresh pass first writes "Your turn" into #turnlbl and then overwrites it with the activation count, while #endbtn cycles End activation, Pass, Continue, End turn (Argon finding 8, Rey finding 10). "Turn" in the TIPS means both one ship's activation and a full round, so players cannot tell whether "recharge each turn" resolves after the next ship or the next round.

## Chair-verified facts

1. ZONE_INFO (ui3d.js:101): exit is #7dff9b; texit (Transport exit) and guard (Station perimeter) are both exactly #e07a70, the identical color string. Two distinct zones are indistinguishable by color for every player, not only color-blind ones; the Godot pair was separately measured at about 1.46:1 and 1.23:1 composited. Second channels exist (hover prints name and effect at ui3d.js:455; the legend lists names and effects when expanded at ui3d.js:711) but the legend defaults to collapsed color swatches with names only in hover tooltips.
2. The #setpanel markup and the Retreat button surface are as item 9 and this item state (chair read of ui3d.js lines 62-64 and the disabled logic): Retreat sits in the same row as End turn, its 10 percent fleet-wide hull penalty exists only in its title attribute, and its disabled state checks busy and battle-over, not side. Whether the c3retreat handler itself asks for confirmation was not in any pack and was not checked; the surface defect (an unconfirmed, penalty-hidden destructive button beside the most-pressed button in the game) stands either way.
3. Text is small, fixed, and unscalable in both builds: browser CSS body sizes run 13 and 15 px with no text-scale setting anywhere at the pin (Rey leg search); the Godot build measured battle HUD body text at 14 to 15 px against an 18 px floor, a scaling floor that takes the help line to about 7 px, and the controls help line at 3.1:1 to 4.2:1 contrast against the 4.5:1 bar. The accessibility review prices part of the fix: the help line color to BONE2 (#b3aa95) computes to about 5.96:1 to 7.82:1.
4. keysHelp (ui3d.js:161, Rey verified) lists Tab, Q, E, 1-6, B, V, Esc, Space, Enter and omits R (threat), X (ram), G (board), F (focus), T (top-down), and Home. The camera hint bar covers a different subset again. Hotkeys and the retreat penalty also live only in title attributes (Argon finding 9), invisible to keyboard-only users. The Godot build has no input remapping at all: no InputMap use anywhere in the pack evidence (DeepSeek finding 6).
5. title.gd sets FOCUS_NONE and an empty focus stylebox on every title and settings button (accessibility review, Argon catch), blocking keyboard and controller menu navigation outright, including navigation to the accessibility options themselves. Priced in the review as a two-line change; the highest exclusion-to-effort ratio in the seat.
6. The hint line's false completion state is verbatim at ui3d.js:669 ("Nothing left to do: tap Done with ship." regardless of remaining movement or an unspent Brace), and wingSel survives Esc, selectUnit, and rosterPick (clears only on order assignment or empty-space click, ui3d.js:425, with a hint line that names the mode while it is live). Both also appear in the player synthesis; they are carried here as the UI surface defects and there as the player cost.

## Ranked actions: Opus lane (technical)

1. Fix the Command cue: put the gold state on the actual Command button, align the tooltip's growth rule with the tip (rounds waited and ships lost), and correct the tutorial card. Highest leverage single fix in the seat.
2. Give the battle settings panel a tips recovery control, break the tip modal chain (queue, do not stack at 400 ms), and strip the developer controls and phone copy out of the player-facing panel; group what remains so Reduce flashing and Reduce screen shake are findable.
3. Zones: change one of texit or guard off #e07a70, default the legend to its expanded named form, and grade zone fills by luminance as the threat view already does.
4. Retreat: print the 10 percent penalty on the button or in a confirm step, and gate the button on the player's side like End turn.
5. Text: add a user text-size control in both builds (the port is the cheap moment), raise HUD body text toward the 18 px floor, and apply the BONE2 help-line color fix.
6. Input: complete keysHelp (R, X, G, F, T, Home), print hotkeys on their buttons instead of only in title attributes, add InputMap-based remapping in the Godot build, and fix the title.gd FOCUS_NONE two-liner so menus are keyboard and controller navigable.
7. Carry into the phased activation build: move preview gains threat overlay, turn count, and path; weapon arming persists within an activation; the battle TIPS are rewritten for the two-beat model (with the player seat's planner items).
8. Fix the hint line's false completion state, clear wingSel on Esc and selection changes, settle the commit button on one label per state with one word for activation across the HUD (kill the turn/activation double meaning in TIPS), and remove the G.mobile branch from HUD strings.
9. Chair copy fixes to the tutorial assembly: rewrite c_nodes to decode the boon badge, tie c_boon to the map glyph and star, and move the generational framing ahead of the first loss instead of inside c_loss.

## Ranked actions: Ryan lane (human direction)

1. **Round 2+ threat view: inside your camera ruling, what finds the off-screen enemy?** Story: your Oct 28 ruling settled battle start (open zoomed out on the whole field, then zoom in; edge indicators not chosen). It did not address the threat view from round 2 on, where the camera has zoomed in, the follow pan can leave activations half off-screen, and a player pressing R sees shaded threat hexes projected by ships they cannot see. Stakes: the threat view is the game's best planning tool; if its sources are off-screen, players learn to distrust or ignore it. Cost: the in-ruling remedies are camera behavior only (zoom out on Threat toggle, or zoom the follow camera out for enemy activations), both cheap; the remedy you ruled out for battle start (edge indicators) would also work here and would need you to revisit that one word of the ruling for this narrower case. The chair's lean is the Threat-toggle zoom, which changes nothing you decided. Full context in the final synthesis.
