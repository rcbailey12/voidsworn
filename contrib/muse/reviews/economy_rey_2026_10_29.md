# In-run economy audit - Rey leg (Oct 29 program)

Blind leg note: written from the economy evidence pack plus the chair's own direct code reads at pin 264f441d (hull table evaluation in data.js, rewardFlow in campaign_run.js, rollNodeReward and shipValue in core.js, SECTOR_CONDS in data.js, the free-repair call sites). Where a number comes from a code read beyond the pack's slices, the finding says so.

## 1. Proof of reading

Read: EVIDENCE PACK title line; THE COMMISSIONER'S QUESTION; CHAIR CENSUS; WHAT TO JUDGE (all five items); code slices data.js 538-544 (FAC_PRICE), core.js 220-235 (priceMult, classTrackCost, mkCost, repairMult), campaign_run.js 155-200 (shipyard), campaign_run.js 431-460 (capture, strip and ransom), core.js 251-300 (genSector).

Overall verdict: MIXED - prices and the capture triangle mostly behave, but paid repair is priced so low, and free healing is so common, that the post-boss regroup the Oct 28 ruling asked for is not a real tradeoff at this pin.

## 2. Findings

1. [BLOCKER] Paid repair cannot carry the Q11 regroup ruling. READ (pack, shipyard slice): "Repairs cost 1 credit per 2 hull," charged as Math.ceil(miss/2*priceMult). Worked case in check 3: a 120 HP cruiser at half hull repairs for 30 credits against a 200 credit replacement. Worse, the regroup moment is pre-empted by free healing (own read): winning any battle patches every survivor 15% of max hull free (campaign3d.js, finishBattle), the post-boss transit patches 40% free (campaign_run.js:536, afterBattle), repair nodes restore 25% free, field nodes 20% free, and a drydock rest stop restores about 60% free. The paid decision the ruling imagined (repair, upgrade, or buy, with tradeoffs) is mostly answered before the player reaches a shipyard. INFERRED: at these numbers the regroup is a formality, not a tradeoff.

2. [FIX] The repair modifier never touches the paid repair price, and its only live effect is a penalty. READ (pack): repairMult combines h_repair at .5 and a sector repair field. Own read: the shipyard's paid price uses priceMult only; repairMult appears at exactly the free-healing sites (field repairs, the repair node reward, the post-boss transit patch, the drydock rest), where it scales hull RESTORED. So taking the h_repair Heat halves your free healing and changes nothing you pay. The modifier's name promises a repair discount; the code delivers a free-heal tax. Either the paid price should pass through repairMult, or the Heat's description should say what it actually does.

3. [FIX] Replacement is never the right answer on credits alone, at any damage level. READ (pack): decommission pays a flat 35% of hull cost regardless of remaining hull, and repair costs half a credit per missing point. For the 200 credit cruiser: repair from 1 HP costs 60; sell the wreck (70) and rebuy (200) nets 130, and also loses the captain, any Mark, and fitted modules' ship. INFERRED: the repair/replace leg of the triangle is decorative; only CP pressure or a dead captain's empty berth ever argues for replacement, and neither is a credit decision.

4. [NOTE] Price honesty: per-CP prices spread 2.2x and per-HP prices 3.3x across factions after FAC_PRICE, and the two measures crown different liars (own read, hull table evaluated with the data.js cost loop: base cost is the explicit cost or round((60*cp+10)/5)*5, then FAC_PRICE by class, rounded to 5). Deathless hulls carry the highest per-CP prices in the game (frigate 100 per CP, cruiser 98 per CP against a 63 to 70 baseline). Ithar hulls carry the highest per-HP prices (frigate 3.00 per HP, cruiser 3.22 per HP), partly honestly: Ithar value sits in shields and evasion, and counting shields too (cruiser 290 over 90 HP plus 26 shields) drops it to about 2.50, still the game's highest. Chorus frigates are the best raw value shipped (45 credits, 0.98 per HP). Full table in check 1.

5. [FIX] FAC_PRICE identity inverts at the top of the Chorus ladder. READ (pack): chorus:{Frigate:.65, Destroyer:.75, Cruiser:1, Carrier:1.2}. Own read of outcomes: the swarm discount is real for the hulls a swarm actually fields in numbers (frigate 45, destroyer 50 per CP), but the Chorus carrier lands at 370 credits, the most expensive carrier in the game in absolute terms, priced above the Voidsworn carrier (325) whose 1.25 multiplier the census singles out. If Chorus is the swarm faction, its top end charging a premium while its bottom end is discounted reads less as swarm economics and more as two unrelated policies sharing a table.

6. [NOTE] FAC_PRICE applies to hull purchases and to prize valuation, and nowhere else. Own read: shipValue returns HULLS cost after the FAC_PRICE loop has run, so ransom, strip scrap, decommission and full-fleet scrap all inherit faction pricing (an Eclipse cruiser prize is worth 390 in every formula). But classTrackCost and mkCost are computed from CP and priceMult only, so the faction's price identity stops at the hull counter: refits cost a Chorus player and an Eclipse player exactly the same per CP. Whether that boundary is intended is a designer question, not a defect on its own.

7. [NOTE] The price multiplier stack is narrower and more one-directional than the census implies. READ (pack): priceMult is wrights .85 times h_coffers 1.25 times the sector condition's price field. Own read of SECTOR_CONDS (data.js:602-607): exactly one of the five conditions carries a price field, scarce at 1.2. The shipped stack therefore ranges from 0.85 (wrights alone) to 1.275 (wrights plus coffers plus scarce), or 1.5 at the extreme without wrights, and no condition ever pushes prices down. Fine as tuning, but the economy has no deflationary state, and Heat plus Scarcity together are the only real price event a run can feel.

8. [FIX] The capture triangle's flip is real but small, and the game already knows it. READ (pack): ransom is 55% of ship value scaled .6 to 1.0 by remaining hull, decaying .85 per prior ransom with the factor floored at .4. Own read: prizePlan computes its default with exactly the comparison in check 4 (strip valued with its module at half shop price, against ransom), so the shipped default flips at roughly the same point a careful player would. The decision that remains genuinely open is Add, and Add is gated by CP and the 40% hull default, not by credits. The triangle is a two-way credit decision (strip versus ransom) bolted to a CP decision (add), which is a reasonable design, but it should not be credited as a three-way credit decision.

9. [NOTE] Credit income is steadier and less node-dependent than the map suggests (own read, rewardFlow at campaign_run.js:396-403 and rollNodeReward at core.js:191). Every battle win pays round((25 + 12*sector + 3*column) x kind multiplier) credits before any node reward: about 43 for a sector 1 battle in column 2, 69 for an elite, 129 for the boss, scaling to about 55 base in sector 2. Elite and boss nodes always pay a boon, never credits; normal battle nodes pay a boon 62% of the time, a module 16%, a credit bonus (35 + 10 per sector) 12%, and free repairs 10%. Events add occasional 20 to 80 credit outcomes, and declining a boon pays 30 + 10 per sector. The node's labelled reward is the smaller half of a fight's income; the invisible battle pay is the larger half.

10. [NOTE] Decommission ignores condition entirely. READ (pack): the yard's sell price is Math.round(HULLS cost * .35) with no hull term, and the same 35% (prizeScrap) applies to full-fleet prize scrap (own read, core.js:194). A ship at 1 HP sells for exactly what a pristine ship sells for. Combined with finding 3, this makes damage purely a repair bill and never an asset-value question, which flattens one of the few decisions damage could have created.

## 3. Explicit checks

1. PRICE HONESTY. Own read, final prices after FAC_PRICE (faction, hull, CP, HP, price, per CP, per HP): Voidsworn Warden frigate 1/45/70/70/1.56; Voidsworn Vigil cruiser 3/120/200/67/1.67; Voidsworn Hearth carrier 4/130/325/81/2.50; Chorus frigate 1/46/45/45/0.98; Chorus destroyer 2/82/100/50/1.22; Chorus carrier 5/200/370/74/1.85; Eclipse frigate 1/56/100/100/1.79; Eclipse cruiser 4/145/390/98/2.69; Glasswright frigate 1/30/90/90/3.00; Glasswright prism cruiser 4/90/290/73/3.22; Corsair frigate 1/46/55/55/1.20; Corsair hulk-carrier 4/170/290/73/1.71. Per CP, Deathless (Eclipse) prices lie the most, upward: a flat elite tax of roughly 45% over baseline at every class, rising slightly with class (1.45/1.5/1.55). Per HP, Ithar (Glasswrights) prices lie the most, upward, though about a third of that gap is shields and evasion the HP measure cannot see. Chorus prices lie downward at frigate and destroyer, which is the honest direction for a swarm.

2. FAC_PRICE AS IDENTITY. Chorus small hulls: yes, swarm economics, the cheapest seats in the game and the only faction whose frigate undercuts 50 credits. Chorus top end: no (finding 5). Voidsworn Carrier 1.25: reads as nothing. Voidsworn's table is otherwise empty, so the single entry is a 25% tax on the generalist faction's only hangar hull; in outcome terms it delays carrier play rather than expressing a character, and the Hearth carrier's 2.50 per HP is the worst carrier value in the game. Eclipse's rising 1.45 to 1.55 gradient is the cleanest identity in the table (elite, and more elite as hulls grow). Ithar's falling gradient (1.3 frigates down to 1.1 carriers) has a shape, artisan small craft, bulk discount on big hulls, but nothing in the game says that out loud. Corsair (cheap raiders, taxed heavies) coheres. Hegemony's empty table is baseline by absence, acceptable for the default navy.

3. REPAIR ECONOMICS. Concrete case (own read numbers): a Vigil-class cruiser, 120 HP, cost 200, ends a boss fight at half hull. First, the transit home patches 40% of max hull free, so it actually arrives at about 108 of 120; the regroup dilemma the ruling imagined is mostly dissolved before any choice appears. Take the harder version anyway, half hull at a yard with no free heal pending: missing 60 HP, repair costs ceil(60/2) = 30 credits at the baseline multiplier. Replace: decommission pays 70, a fresh cruiser costs 200, net 130, and the veteran captain and Mark are gone. Fight damaged: costs nothing now and the next win patches 18 HP free, but a cruiser at half hull in an elite fight is how fleets die. Repair is right, by a factor of four over replacement, and it is not close. Does the regroup survive as a tradeoff? No. Between the free transit patch, the 15% win patch, and a 30 credit full cure, the post-boss choice is repair-always with pocket change; the only real regroup decisions are the Mark upgrade and class refit price tags, which are progression purchases, not recovery tradeoffs.

4. THE CAPTURE TRIANGLE. Concrete prize: a Vigil-class cruiser, ship value 200 (own read: shipValue is the post-FAC_PRICE shop cost). At run start (no prior ransoms): at full hull, ransom pays 200 x .55 x 1.0 = 110; strip pays 30 credits plus a module (modules price at 40 to 90 in the yard, worth 20 to 45 at the half-price valuation the game's own default uses), so strip totals roughly 50 to 75 in value; add keeps a 200 credit asset if CP fits. Ransom wins the credit comparison clearly. After three ransoms: the decay factor is .85^3 = .614, so ransom pays 68 at full hull and about 51 at 40% hull, while strip is unchanged at 50 to 75 of value. The right answer flips: strip catches and then passes ransom somewhere around the second or third ransom, earlier for damaged prizes. Does the decay rule matter? Yes, but only in that band: the factor floor of .4 binds from the sixth ransom onward (.85^6 = .377), after which ransom is a flat 22% of value at full hull and strip wins permanently. Note the code comment's "never below 40%" describes the decay factor, not the payout; a full-hull ransom floors at 22% of ship value, which a player reading the comment would not expect.

5. FLOW. INFERRED trace, assumptions stated: a competent sector 1 path takes one node per column, roughly four battles and an elite before the boss, one shipyard visit, starting credits 120 (own read, core.js:324; up to +225 more if the chest LEGACY unlocks are owned). Income: battle pay about 43 x 4 plus 69 (elite) plus 129 (boss) is about 370; a credit node or a boon decline adds about 45; event outcomes and any ransoms are swing, call it 0 to 150. Total sector 1 resources: roughly 540 to 690. Spending pressure: repairs are trivial (finding 1); the real sinks are a new hull (45 to 200 in the classes sector 1 yards stock), a Mark II upgrade (70 for a frigate, 150 for a cruiser at mkCost (40*cp*mk + 30)), a module (40 to 90), a boon reroll (25, escalating). The pinch is real but brief: sector 1 before the boss, the player chooses between banking for a cruiser and patching plus upgrading what they have, and CP (cap 10) binds at least as hard as credits. By sector 2, battle pay rises about 28% while hull prices do not move, the cpCap rises by 3, and the only sinks that scale are Mark III (up to 430 for a battleship's second upgrade) and class refits (60/110/160 per class level). Verdict on flow: a competent player keeps making credit decisions in sector 1, stops facing scarcity (as opposed to shopping) around the sector 2 shipyard, and from then on credits gate only the optional power sinks. That is close to the right shape for a three-sector run, except the pinch lands on hull purchases, not on the recovery the design says it wants to tax.

## 4. Not checked

Module shop prices beyond the range visible in yard stock behaviour; event table credit outcomes in full (only the common branches were read); salvage and tow yields for wrecks; boon reroll and decline as economy levers beyond their price formulas; Heat options other than h_coffers and h_repair; the Godot port's economy code; whether enemy fleets' FAC_PRICE-baked costs distort prize income by faction faced (the direction is clear from shipValue, the magnitude across a run was not simulated).

## 5. Questions for the designer

1. Should the paid repair price pass through repairMult, or be repriced outright (for example 1 credit per hull point), so that the post-boss regroup costs enough to be a decision? Stakes: at the current half-credit rate plus free transit and win patches, Q11's regroup tradeoff does not exist in play, and h_repair currently punishes free healing instead of discounting paid repairs.
2. Is the Voidsworn carrier's 1.25 a deliberate delay on carrier play for the starter faction, or an orphan from an earlier balance pass? Stakes: it makes the generalist's carrier the worst value in its class while reading as no identity at all, and the Chorus carrier at 1.2 has the same problem from the other direction.
3. Should decommission and scrap value scale with remaining hull the way ransom does? Stakes: flat 35% makes damage free to ignore at sale time, while a hull-scaled value would create the one credit decision damage currently never creates.
