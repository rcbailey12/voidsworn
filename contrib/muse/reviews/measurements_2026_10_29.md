# Wave 5 phase 1: the two ruled-but-unrun measurements (Oct 29, 2026)

Chair-run evidence for Opus to verify, against the browser code at main 264f441d in ~/workspace/voidsworn-fresh. Tools: the harness loader (~/workspace/voidsworn-harness/game_loader.js) and the game's own simulator sim3d.js, unmodified. Every figure below comes from a run executed for this pack; run sizes are stated per table. Nothing here is a ruling; both verdicts are measurements Opus can re-run.

## Measurement 1: Nemesis snowball (battle program Q6, ruled MEASURE FIRST)

Question: does the Nemesis compound faster than the player across a run, at the ruled roughly 80 percent loss rate?

### How he grows, from code

- One meeting per sector, three per run at most (campaign_run.js nemNodeIs: a filled clock makes the next fight his, and the last fight before the rest stop is his if he has not been met that sector).
- His base fleet cannot snowball by construction. A meeting is generated as an elite fight (campaign_run.js:95) and genEnemyFleet scales its budget with the player's own fleetPower, so the base tracks the player. Spot check below confirms the ratio is flat.
- The compounding layer is adaptations plus persistent state. nemAdapt (campaign_run.js:287) is called once per sector advance (line 535) and its cap is max(0, sector-1): the sector 1 meeting is fought with 0 adaptations, sector 2 with 1, sector 3 with 2. The Chorus Nemesis (adaptFast) also adapts after every meeting and its cap grows with beaten plus scars, so it can run ahead of that schedule. The absolute cap is 6.
- Adaptation effects (data.js NEM_ADAPT, applied in battle.js applyNemesis): shields +20% (shielded hulls), armor +10 points of armor fraction (capped 0.6 total), evasion +8, aim +8, or point defense bolted onto two ships. Each is a real, permanent, stacking counter to how the player has been fighting (nemLearn and nemBeatTeach feed the choice).
- Two strength traits are present from birth and never grow (NEM_TRAITS: aim +6, hull +12%, shields +20%, crit +10, and six more, two drawn per rival).
- Scars: each time his flagship is destroyed in sectors 1 or 2 he escapes scarred, up to 2 scars before sector 3, and each scar is +10% damage on his flagship only (battle.js applyNemesis).
- Faction extras, all capped: Chorus fed bonus +4% hull per player ship lost anywhere, cap 8 stacks (+32%, campaign_run.js nemFeedLosses); Corsair riches, one extra ship per escape; Ithar honed, +10% shields and +3 aim per escape, cap 3.

### Power trajectory (harness spot check, 60 fleets per sector per side)

Player fleets built the way sim3d's nemMatrix builds them (the admiral's start fleet grown with roster hulls to CP targets 7, 12, 18), fleetPower from battle.js, and the Nemesis's base elite fleet generated by genEnemyFleet for the same node (column 4, elite):

| sector | player fleetPower | player CP | nemesis base fleet CP | nemesis hulls | base CP / player CP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7.94 | 7.3 | 9.9 | 5.5 | 1.36 |
| 2 | 13.33 | 12.3 | 16.5 | 7.8 | 1.34 |
| 3 | 20.01 | 18.4 | 23.8 | 11.8 | 1.29 |

The base is a roughly constant 1.3x elite premium over the player's CP and the premium slightly narrows as the player grows. All compounding is in the adaptation layer measured next.

### Battle measurement (sim3d nemmatrix, 20 battles per cell, 180 battles)

Three arms per sector, same sector-grown player fleets in every arm: plain (a plain elite fight of the same node), nem (the full Nemesis: two weaknesses live, sector-1 adaptations, sector-2 scars), nemNoWeak (the same Nemesis with his weaknesses removed, isolating what his growth alone costs). Player win rate, bot-piloted:

| sector | plain elite | nemesis (nem) | nemesis, no weaknesses (nemNoWeak) | growth cost (plain minus nemNoWeak) | weakness value (nem minus nemNoWeak) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 65% | 60% | 65% | 0 pp | -5 pp (noise) |
| 2 | 80% | 65% | 45% | 35 pp | +20 pp |
| 3 | 60% | 45% | 35% | 25 pp | +10 pp |

Nemesis fights also run long: 7.2 to 9.3 rounds in the nem and nemNoWeak arms against 6.4 to 7.8 for plain elites.

### Verdict: KEEPING PACE, after one hard step. Not snowballing.

His growth is real but it is a step, not a slope. At the first meeting the whole compounding layer (two traits, zero adaptations) costs the player nothing measurable against a plain elite. The first adaptation landing before the sector 2 meeting is the single hard step: the growth cost jumps to 35 pp of win rate in sector 2 and then does not widen in sector 3 (25 pp, inside this sample's noise), even though a second adaptation and the first scar arrive. Meanwhile the intel loop pays out on schedule: his weaknesses are worth 20 pp back in sector 2 and 10 pp in sector 3, so against the full Nemesis the bot still wins 60, 65 and 45 percent of meetings. The design caps agree with the measurement: the standard adaptation cap reaches only 2 by the final meeting, fed is capped at +32%, scars at +20% flagship damage, and the base fleet is indexed to the player's power, so there is no channel through which he can run away across a run. If tuning is wanted, the lever the data points at is the size of the first adaptation step (or the sector 2 adaptation specifically), not the cadence and not the caps.

## Measurement 2: boon offer distribution (progression Q7, the rarity tax)

Question: how often do off-patron picks appear in rollBoonOffer offers, and what rarity do they roll versus on-patron picks? The Oct 28 ruling: off-patron spillover stays, but off-patron picks must retain real value; the hidden rarity tax is the defect. From code (core.js rollBoonOffer): on-patron picks roll rollRarity(minR, sector, patron); off-patron backfill picks roll rollRarity(minR, sector) with no patron argument, so they get neither the patron's favour boost in rollRarity nor the tier 3 favour floor (r at least Rare). Backfill fires only when the offering patron's useful, unheld pool plus any duo fills fewer than 3 slots, and it fills before the patron's own not-useful (junk) boons.

Method: the shipped rollBoonOffer run headlessly on the harness loader, 184,800 offers in total (105,600 in part 1, 79,200 in part 2), every patron (6) in turn, across sectors 1 to 3, boons held 0 to 5, minimum rarity 0 and 1, with two fleets: a broad fleet covering every boon kit (warden, harrow, penitent, reliquary) and a narrow gun-only fleet (three corsair raiders). Cross-run favour (META.favour, which is what favourTier reads) set uniformly to 0, 4 (tier 1, Partial sync) or 25 (tier 3, Full sync). Rarity effect multipliers: Common 1, Rare 1.5, Epic 2, Legendary 1 (data.js RARITY), so mean m is the expected effect size of a pick.

### How often off-patron picks appear

| run state | offers | offers containing an off-patron pick | off picks per offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| broad fleet, 0 to 3 of the patron's 6 useful boons held, favour 0 | 66,000 | 0.0% | 0 |
| broad fleet, 4 held (pool down to 2) | 39,600 across favour 0, 4 and 25 | 100.0% | exactly 1 |
| broad fleet, 5 held (pool down to 1) | 13,200 | 100.0% | exactly 2 |
| narrow fleet, 0 held, sector 2, favour 0 | 13,200 | 16.7% | 0.33 |

Backfill is a cliff, not a gradient: it is exactly zero while the patron can still field three useful boons, and then it fills every remaining slot in every offer. The narrow fleet shows the second trigger: kit coverage. For a gun-only fleet the Hunger Below has only 1 useful boon of 6, so its offers carry 2 backfill picks from the very first node (the pooled 0.33 per offer is exactly one patron of six contributing 2). At sector 3 with 1 boon held and favour 4, the narrow fleet's pooled rate rises to 33.3% of offers, 0.67 off picks per offer.

One masking interaction: at elite and boss rewards (minR 1) with 2 or more of the patron's boons held, the patron's legendary is eligible and takes a slot in 100.0% of offers (13,200 of 13,200 in both legendary-eligible configs), which can hide the backfill slot entirely.

### The tax, quantified (sector 3, backfill states, 13,200 offers per favour level)

| favour | class | Common | Rare | Epic | Rare or better | mean m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | on-patron | 52.4% | 35.7% | 12.0% | 47.7% | 1.298 |
| 0 | off-patron | 52.4% | 35.8% | 11.8% | 47.6% | 1.297 |
| 4 (tier 1) | on-patron | 37.1% | 48.0% | 14.9% | 62.9% | 1.389 |
| 4 (tier 1) | off-patron | 51.9% | 36.2% | 11.8% | 48.0% | 1.300 |
| 25 (tier 3) | on-patron | 0.0% | 85.0% | 15.0% | 100.0% | 1.575 |
| 25 (tier 3) | off-patron | 51.8% | 36.0% | 12.2% | 48.2% | 1.302 |

- Favour 0 is the negative control: with no favour anywhere, on and off distributions are identical to within noise. The tax is entirely a favour artefact.
- At favour tier 1 the tax is 14.9 percentage points of Rare-or-better (62.9% on-patron vs 48.0% off), 3.1 pp of Epic, and 0.089 of mean m, about 6% less expected effect per filler pick.
- At favour tier 3 (Full sync, whose whole promise is that the patron's protocols are never Common) the tax is total: on-patron picks are 100% Rare or better while filler picks are still 51.8% Common, a 51.8 pp gap in Rare-or-better and 0.273 of mean m, about 17% less expected effect per filler pick. The filler also silently breaks the tier 3 promise, since the player is shown a Common boon inside a synced patron's offer.
- The narrow-fleet run at favour 4 reproduces the same tax independently (on 62.8% Rare or better vs off 48.0%), so the gap is not an artefact of the broad fleet.

Adjacent observation, same shape: duo boons enter offers at a hardcoded rarity of Common (rollBoonOffer builds the duo pick as r:0 and never rolls it). In the duo-context config (13,200 offers), all 4,936 duo picks were Common, mean m 1.00 against 1.24 for on-patron picks in the same offers.

### Verdict: THE TAX IS REAL, CONDITIONAL, AND WORST FOR THE MOST INVESTED PLAYERS.

Three facts bound it. First, it does not exist without cross-run favour: a first-run player loses nothing on filler picks. Second, it grows with favour, reaching a 51.8 pp Rare-or-better gap and a 17% effect-size gap at full sync. Third, backfill itself only appears once a patron is nearly exhausted or a fleet's kit leaves a patron almost nothing useful, which is precisely the situation of a loyal, invested player deep in a patron. So the shipped code taxes exactly the players the favour system is meant to reward, in exactly the offers where they have the least choice left. The Q7 fix spec already written (merged pool with explicit weights, favour applied to every pick) addresses the mechanism directly; these numbers are the before picture for verifying it.

## Limitations

- Nemesis: 20 battles per cell means one battle is 5 pp; the sector 2 to 3 narrowing of the growth cost (35 to 25 pp) is inside that noise, and the verdict rests on the gap not widening, not on its exact size. The pilot is the game's own bot, not a player. nemmatrix applies the standard sector-1 adaptation schedule to every faction, so the Chorus Nemesis's faster cadence (adapt after every meeting, cap growing with beaten and scars) and its fed stacking in a real run are understated here; the caps cited are the code-traced bound on that understatement. The sim also seats the player at each meeting with a sector-grown fleet at full strength; real runs at the 80% loss rate often arrive battered, and fleetPower's hull term means a battered arrival faces a relatively stronger Nemesis than these numbers show.
- Boons: offers were rolled with the two stated fleets; a fleet with partial kit coverage lands between the broad and narrow backfill rates. Favour was set uniformly across patrons to isolate the roll difference; a real profile with uneven favour sees the tier 1 tax shape from the first patron it raises. The measurement is offer mathematics only (what is offered and at what rarity), not battle outcomes, and it takes rarity's effect multiplier at face value, which is exact for fx boons that scale with m and approximate for boons whose text changes by tier.
- Both: the pin is the browser build. The Godot port inherits these rules through parity, so the numbers are the baseline the port should reproduce, not a description of the Godot build's current state.
