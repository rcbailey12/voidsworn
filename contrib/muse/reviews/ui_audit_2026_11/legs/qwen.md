VERDICT
THE STACK DOES NOT HOLD.
The UI stack of Embers of the Hollow Sun cannot be defended as a set of design gates. Of 64 inventoried items, zero are MEASURED, zero are DERIVED, five are ANCHORED, and 59 are ASSUMED. No capture in the project record has ever judged any of these values at the resolutions the owner targets (1440p floor, 4K goal). The two builds disagree silently on constants that govern the most-seen elements in the game. Structural absences in accessibility bind every player without any recorded decision. The stack does not hold because it was never assembled as a stack: it is two independent sets of implementer choices, one frozen and one in progress, that have never been compared, captured, or judged.
The three items this verdict most depends on:
HD1 (the HUD scaling law). Every size in the Godot battle HUD derives from s(v) = round(v * k), k = clamp(height/1080, 0.6, 2.2). This single formula denominates the entire battle interface. It was designed at 1080p and has never been judged at 1440p or 4K. If the design point or either clamp is wrong, every screen is wrong by the same factor. No capture in the record tests it. The stack cannot hold when its denominating assumption is unjudged at the resolutions where the game will actually be played.
HD7 (nameplate geometry). The JS nameplate bar is 54 px; the Godot port's is s(90). At the shared 1080 design height, that is a 67 percent difference in the size of the element that sits on every ship in every battle. The nameplate is the core battle readability ratio (pack section 3, X3). Two builds showing plates at sizes nobody chose means the game does not agree with itself about its most fundamental combat read. The stack cannot hold when its two implementations teach different visual languages for the same information.
AC1 (no colour-vision support). No colourblind mode, no palette variant, no redundant encoding rule exists in either build. The search covered every file read for the pack. The game's IFF (HD10), danger bands (MP8), and hull-low state (HD9) all speak colour first. Roughly 1 in 12 male players reads red-green distinctions poorly. This is not a missing feature; it is an unchosen absence that binds every session for a substantial share of players. The stack cannot hold when a structural exclusion of that size has no provenance, no ruling, and no record of being considered.

OWNER QUESTIONS
Should the Godot port's UI be treated as the design authority going forward, or should the frozen JS build's values be adopted where the two disagree? My pick: the port, but only after a capture round at 1440p and 4K proves its values at your resolutions. Stakes: this decides whether the 64 items are resolved by one scripted diff (H1) or by 64 small rulings. If the port is the authority, every JS value becomes a baseline to judge against; if the JS build is the authority, the port owes a reversion or a recorded reason for every change.
Is a UI scale setting in scope for the shipped game? My pick: yes. The height-only scaling law gives the player no reading-comfort lever; the window size is the only control, and it changes the game's rendering, not just the text. Stakes: without it, the 1440p floor and 4K goal are rendering targets only, and a player who finds 15 px too small at 1080 has no recourse. With it, the consolidated scale function (TY4) gains one multiplier and the accessibility column gains its first structural addition.
Should the reduce-flashing default be on rather than off? My pick: yes. The toggle exists to protect players from flash and strobe; defaulting it off means the players it protects meet the unmitigated game first, in the biggest battles, before they find the setting. Stakes: the cap itself is fenced (F2) and is the best-derived number in the fx stack, but the cap only helps if the player reaches it. A default-on costs nothing for players who do not need it (they turn it off once) and serves the players who do need it from the first session.
