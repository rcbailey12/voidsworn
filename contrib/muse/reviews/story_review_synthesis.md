## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS. All four inputs rate the writing itself strong: the faction voices are distinct enough to identify blind, the win and loss lines are consistently good, and one beat (Hegemony Beat 2) is called the best dilemma in the set by all four. The conditions are all in the choice architecture and the rival, not the prose, and every one of them is fixable inside the existing format: no new mechanics, screens, or scope. The outlines should not ship as drafted, but they do not need a redesign either.

## Where the four inputs agree

1. The rival does not remember you in words. His hail, last words and gloat are fixed per faction; nothing he says changes with what you did in beats 1 and 2. All four inputs rank a flag-keyed rival line at or near the top (Qwen fix 1, Rey fix 3, Argon fix 4, DeepSeek fixes 2 and 10). The machinery already exists: the Beat 2 bodies carry flag-conditional sentences about the player's fleet. The same pattern, pointed at the rival, is the cheapest large gain in the set.
2. Beat 2 blue options stack. Each blue combines the standard options' rewards while removing or reversing their costs (weakness plus experience, weakness at a discount with no ambush, weakness plus the clock going back). All four inputs found this independently. A blue should be a different kind of good, or carry a cost the standard options avoid (the legs' examples: the raised hand cracks, the auction house is angered, a brood is spent).
3. Beat 3 is the weakest beat in every arc. DeepSeek calls beat 3 the weakest in all six; Argon, Qwen and Rey land in the same place from different angles (resource trade with no dilemma, generic choice labels, a promise the coda does not honour). The finale currently gets the least argued choice in each arc.
4. The 40k proximity is concentrated, not general. All four inputs clear Hegemony and Corsairs, rate Ithar low, and put the risk in the same two places: Deathless against the Necrons (tomb, crown, warden, long sleep, contempt) and the Crusade against the Imperium (heresy, confessors, litanists, shrine-moon density). All four also agree on the remedy's direction: lean on each faction's own obsession (census and law for the Deathless, oath and beacon for the Crusade) and thin the borrowed furniture. Argon and Qwen converge on near-identical swaps (archive or ledger worlds, registrars or custodians).
5. Faction voices are the set's strength. All four inputs identify factions from vocabulary alone. The disagreements are about execution in specific beats, not about the voices themselves.
6. The arcs will go stale on same-faction repeats. Fixed text, one solved path per arc, and a rival who changes name but not behaviour. The legs' defences differ (Chronicle records, rival-faction flavour lines, dead-flag payoffs), and they stack rather than compete.

## Chair-verified facts (checked against outlines.md)

- Each of the 12 Beat 1 flags appears exactly once in the document, at its Beat 1 option. Six are picked up narratively in Beat 2 (vesper_hand, hg_offbook, cs_rigged, ch_silence, it_song, dd_patient). Six are never used again (vesper_sold, hg_orders, cs_party, ch_ate, it_mercy, dd_vengeance). Argon and the Rey review mapped this independently; DeepSeek's fixes imply it; Qwen missed it. This is the single most mechanical finding in the review and it is not a matter of taste.
- Beat 3 reward shapes, counted exactly: three of the six finales are the identical pair, repair 20 percent against captain experience 20 (Voidsworn, Hegemony with the order reversed, Deathless). Chorus offers regrowth against a weakness reveal, Ithar offers experience against a weakness reveal, and Corsairs offers experience against credits. Two legs phrase this more loosely; the count here is the document's.
- The Chorus arc header states its voice as "hunger, many-as-one, song, short lines" (outlines line 109), while Chorus body prose includes long descriptive sentences (the beat 1 opener Qwen quotes is verbatim). Qwen's premise is confirmed; see the divergence note below for how the chair weighs it.
- The Deathless crest is "a crowned eye over a closed tomb door" (line 169), and the sample rival in Hegemony Beat 1 is "Dynast Setekh the Patient" (line 53).
- Two bracketed sample rivals are women (Petra Lindqvist, Ilyra Glasswing) while rival text across the beats hardcodes male pronouns; "learns his scent" and "He offers to return it" are both in the document as Argon states.
- The rival's scripted deeds assume a humanoid actor in at least three scenes (sitting in the auction's front row smiling, the stolen crown worn openly, a message cut into altar stone), which cannot be true of a Chorus swarm rival; the outlines' own Chorus text says the swarm does not answer in words. Argon found this; the Rey review found two of the three cases.
- The draft names flags only for Beat 1 choices. No Beat 2 choice in any arc has a named flag in the document, so any Beat 3 callback to a Beat 2 choice (Qwen's and Rey's Chorus teaching point included) cannot be verified from the draft as written.

## Divergences worth naming

- Strongest-beat picks split by taste, with one exception. Hegemony Beat 2, "Acceptable Limits", is the consensus best beat in the set (all four). Chorus Beat 1 and Deathless Beat 2 are each picked strongest by three of the four. Voidsworn, Corsair and Ithar strongest picks scatter across beats 1 to 3; no action follows from the scatter.
- The Chorus voice. Qwen holds the Chorus body prose to the arc's own stated "short lines" spec and finds a mismatch. The Rey review reads the same prose as in-character and praises it. Chair's weighing: both are true at different levels. The vocabulary is unmistakably Chorus (all four agree), and the sentence length does drift from the faction's own stated spec. Treat Qwen's pass as a spec-conformance fix, not a quality complaint, and scope it to the longest descriptive sentences rather than a full rewrite.
- Corsair Beat 3 is the weakest beat by four different reasons: the shares choice is lopsided (DeepSeek), the credit reward may land after the run's last shop (Argon, UNSURE), the promise is not honoured by the coda (Rey), and the choices ignore the rival's chained-fleet gambit in the setup (Qwen). Four independent reasons for the same beat make it the clearest single-beat rewrite in the set.
- Qwen's Hegemony Beat 2 summary compresses the option rewards loosely. Directionally right, not exact; the filed leg stands verbatim and the point is recorded in its screening note.

## Merged ranked fixes (chair's ranking across all four inputs)

1. Give the rival a memory in words: one flag-keyed line per arc in the Beat 3 hail (thwarted and unthwarted variants, per Argon), plus the Beat 2 callback line where a leg names one. All four inputs.
2. Wire the six dead Beat 1 flags to a Beat 3 payoff, so Beat 1 becomes a choice between a Beat 2 advantage and a Beat 3 advantage. Argon fix 1, Rey fix 1, chair-verified wiring above.
3. Rename the sample rival Setekh. One word. It is a near namesake of the Necron dynasty Sautekh and is the single most citable proximity item in the document (Rey review, section 3e). Do this regardless of ranking.
4. De-stack the Beat 2 blues: each blue gains a cost or becomes a different kind of good. All four inputs.
5. Rebuild Beat 3 choices as story decisions tied to each arc's own objects (the hand, the names, the split, the taught spawn, the archive, the law), with choice labels written in faction voice. DeepSeek fix 1, Argon fix 3, Rey fix 4, Qwen fixes 5, 8 and 10.
6. Take the 40k distance pass: Deathless first (census, ledger and law vocabulary forward; tomb, crown and warden density down), then the Crusade (oath vocabulary forward; heresy and confessor density roughly halved). Argon fixes 7 and 8, Qwen fixes 2 and 3, Rey fixes 5 and 6, DeepSeek fix 8. Includes Argon's smaller items: Ithar "elders" and the two "Warlord" sample names.
7. Make the rival's scripted deeds faction-safe (fleet-scale framing or one faction-specific action line per scene), and replace the hardcoded male pronouns with variables or neutral phrasing. Argon fixes 2 and 5, Rey section 3c in part.
8. Chorus voice pass on the longest body sentences, toward the arc's own short-lines spec. Qwen fix 6, scoped per the divergence note above.
9. Specify the Chronicle: an entry records the run's flags (hand carried or sold, law kept or broken, column saved or left), not only completion. Rey fix 7. This is the set's main structural defence against same-faction staleness.
10. Name the Beat 2 flags in the draft for every arc, so Beat 3 callbacks can be checked on paper before they are built. Chair item, from the verified facts above.

## Open questions the legs could not settle (for Opus or the build, not for more review)

- Argon, UNSURE: none of the six sample rival names belongs to the Chorus, so whether a Chorus admiral can actually roll as the Nemesis in stream B code is unverified. If it cannot, the rival-faction mismatch in fix 7 narrows; if it can, fix 7 is required.
- Argon, UNSURE: whether anything (a shop, repairs, further fights) follows Beat 3 in a run. If nothing does, the Corsair Beat 3 credit reward is dead weight and should become an immediate repair or weakness, as Argon and DeepSeek both suggest.
- All legs, UNSURE: whether the built game adds text variation, randomness, or rival-faction deed variants beyond these outlines. Several recommendations assume only what the draft shows.

## What this means for the approval decision

The B5 exit gate asks Ryan to read and approve the arc text per faction before it ships. The four inputs support approving the arcs' direction, voices and structure, with the ten fixes above as the conditions list. Nothing in any leg argues for new story scope: Option B's shape (three beats per faction, Nemesis met in beat 2 and settled in beat 3, Chronicle close) survives all four reviews intact. The cheapest sequencing is fixes 3, 10 and 2 first (one word, one specification pass, one wiring pass), then 1, 4 and 5 in the same editing round, since they touch the same cards.
