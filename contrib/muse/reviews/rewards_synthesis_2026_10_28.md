Rewards synthesis, Oct 28 program item 2 (the in-run offer layer: node rewards, protocol offers, prizes, salvage, modules, relics). Chair synthesis of the DeepSeek, Qwen, and Argon (Gemini) blind legs, all pinned to main 264f441d, plus chair verification against the code at that pin. Findings are observations for Opus to confirm, not orders.

## Verdict: REWARDS ARE MIXED (unanimous)

All three legs returned the same verdict, and the chair concurs. The disagreement is only about severity, and it is genuine: DeepSeek explicitly says this layer does not have the events audit's solved-choice disease, while Qwen files two BLOCKERs and Argon eight FIXes. The chair's position after verification: the offer at the center (pick 1 of 3 kit-filtered protocols) is a real choice, and every leg agrees on that. The solved choices live in the defaults and edges around it, and two of them are not matters of taste but of arithmetic and code structure, verified below.

## Verified findings (chair-checked at the pin)

1. The prize screen pre-computes the optimal default. prizePlan defaults each prize to Add when it fits and is at least 40% hull, otherwise to whichever of strip or ransom is worth more, counting a stripped module at half its shop cost. A player who clicks Confirm without reading follows the code's own valuation exactly. (Qwen BLOCKER 1; DeepSeek NOTE 4 as default bias; Argon agrees.) Whether a convenience default is acceptable is a design call; that it is the optimum by construction is a fact.
2. The rarity promise is broken on 8 of 36 core protocols. Six rule-changing protocols (i_wall, s_chain, h_brood, a_mark, r_slip, e_spread) use effect functions that ignore the rarity multiplier entirely; two more (h_hatch, e_reload) cap at the Rare tier, so Epic equals Rare. The game's own text says rarity sets strength. This compounds with core sync: at favour tier 3 every primary-patron roll is guaranteed Rare or better, so the waste grows as the player's long-term progression succeeds. (Argon, verified in data.js; no other leg caught it.)
3. L_storm is not gated by shields. It is absent from BOON_NEED while its shield siblings are gated, and the legendary check treats an ungated legendary as useful for any fleet. A shieldless fleet that takes two Vyre protocols gets a shield legendary it cannot use parked in slot 3 at every elite and boss. (Argon, verified.)
4. Legendaries are deterministic and overwrite duos. Once a player holds two protocols from a patron, that patron's legendary always takes slot 3 at elites and bosses (min reward tier 1), is inserted after the duo step so it deletes a rolled duo, and re-inserts on reroll so it cannot be rerolled away. (All three legs; verified in rollBoonOffer and legendaryFor.)
5. Cred nodes are dominated by declining. Declining a protocol pays 30 + 10 per sector (40/50/60); a dedicated cred node pays 35 + 10 per sector (45/55/65). A boon node is therefore a free look at three fleet-wide protocols for a 5-credit discount, and map routing at the node-type level is solved arithmetic. (Argon, constants verified.)
6. The reroll trap is real. The reroll check never looks at remaining pool depth; when a patron has three or fewer eligible protocols left, rerolling charges 25 + 15 per prior reroll and returns the same three protocols with re-rolled rarities. (Argon, verified.) On reroll pricing the legs genuinely split by baseline: DeepSeek calls the first reroll possibly underpriced against module costs (55 to 90); Qwen calls it a luxury against sector 1 battle income (about 37); Argon calls it a trap against the decline payout (a 65-credit net swing). All three baselines are true at once; the synthesis does not pick one.
7. Salvage is deterministic in two ways. A wreck's module is always the first match on a fixed 9-module list (four modules, prow, drive, gyro, and regen, can never drop from a wreck), and stripping a prize always yields a module, while ransom decays by 0.85 per prior ransom in fixed battle order (the player cannot choose which prize gets the better rate). Scrap pays 19/28/37 credits for intact 1 to 3 CP hulls against a 55 to 90 credit module. (All three legs in parts; verified in salvageYield, prizeOffer, prizeRansom.)
8. Offer fill order, settled. One pipeline explains three separate leg findings: same-patron kit-useful protocols first (with the patron's favour applied), then a duo, then other patrons' protocols rolled without the patron's favour bonus and displayed under the wrong patron's colour, then same-patron junk, then the legendary. The spillover step (DeepSeek FIX 1, Argon rewards FIX 8) is real and costs the player their sync-earned rarity boost on the spilled slots. Separately, modulePool falls back to offering all modules when fewer than three are useful to the fleet (DeepSeek FIX 3, verified verbatim), so refit offers can include modules the fleet cannot use.
9. Pool depth. Modules (13) and relics (12) saturate before protocols do; per-patron protocol pools are 6 deep and kit filtering cuts them further, so exhaustion fallbacks ("nothing left to offer" credits) are reachable by sector 3 on a focused build. (All three legs.)

## Run 1 / Run 5 / Run 10

RUN 1: The layer feels rich. Aspects set the flagship's identity, kit filtering prevents dead offers, and discovering duos and the two-protocol legendary rule is genuinely rewarding. None of the verified defects are visible yet except the prize default quietly making decisions.

RUN 5: Optimization replaces discovery. The player has seen all aspects, relics, and modules, knows the prize default is safe to accept, knows decline beats cred nodes, and plans routes around forcing legendaries. The rarity break becomes noticeable exactly here, because sync growth guarantees higher rarities on protocols that cannot use them.

RUN 10: Routing and reward screens are solved except for duo and legendary chasing. Under the designer's learning frame (ruled during this program), within-run rewards are what the current crusade learns; the defects above are lessons that teach nothing: a rarity that changes nothing, a slot occupied by an unusable legendary, a reroll that returns the same three cards. That is the frame in which run 10's reward layer goes flat.

## Questions for the designer (collected, per program rules)

1. Should rule-changing protocols gain numeric rarity scaling, or be moved to a fixed tier like duos so high-rarity rolls are never spent on them? (Argon)
2. Should cred nodes pay meaningfully more (Argon suggested 75 to 100+) so they compete with a boon node's decline floor, or is boon dominance the intent?
3. Is the deterministic legendary intended (a guaranteed payoff for patron loyalty), or should it be a weighted roll, or occupy a slot that does not delete duos? (All three legs asked a version of this.)
4. Is the off-patron spillover intentional (offers stay full at the cost of patron identity and the sync bonus), or should offers shrink to what the patron can actually give? (DeepSeek)

## Addendum: designer rulings (Oct 28, 2026)

(1) Rule-changing protocols: numeric rarity scaling is RULED. The eight rule-shaped protocols should gain value across rarity tiers; the designer explicitly likes rarity RNG carrying real value, as in other roguelikes. The fixed-tier alternative is rejected. (2) Credit nodes: RULED, rebalance them so they make sense in the economy; the dominance gap is a tuning defect, not an intent. Exact figures are a tuning task. (3) The legendary: RULED as the hybrid. It stays engineerable (a player can build toward the conditions), but the contested slot resolves by weighted roll between the duo and the legendary rather than by deterministic overwrite. Question 4 of this synthesis (off-patron spillover) was not understood by the designer and has been re-explained in plain terms; it remains open, and no ruling is recorded for it here.
