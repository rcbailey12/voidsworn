## Seat verdict

NOT READY FOR CUTOVER, 3 of 3 prongs, and the seat's sharpest work is against the project's own QA artifacts: the saves audit's headline recovery guarantee is false for two of its failure paths, the findings ledger's completeness claim omits the saves audit itself, and the regression suite is red by design with no green baseline and no wiring into any build or release step. The cutover BLOCKER stands with fixtures and no importer. None of this is an argument the testing program is bad; the findings-to-tests ledger is the project's best defect asset. It is an argument that the program currently produces knowledge, not gates.

## Claims register: disposition

1. **Saves BLOCKER at cutover: UPHELD, 3 of 3, with the transport gap named.** The Godot campaign loader reads only user://voidsworn_campaign.json; no GDScript references the browser key voidsworn.v1; campaign.gd records a no-carryover decision in comments (R33, carried fully in the engineer synthesis). Argon finding 2 adds the mechanism the fixtures never touch: a native desktop build cannot read browser localStorage, the frozen browser build offers no export path (Copy report emits a diagnostic summary, not an importable envelope), and cloud storage is browser-side too. The 26 fixtures verify a converter's transformation logic against in-memory objects. The transport (export, bridge, or cloud path) does not exist in either build. Fixtures are test assets, not an importer.
2. **Saves audit Finding 1 ("failed to load is kept raw, never silently lost"): OVERTURNED for two paths (Argon finding 1, chair-confirmed with refinement).** loadLocal writes the .repaired copy only on the _repairs path or when migrate throws. On a JSON parse failure (o=null) or an insane half with no good .bak, the .repaired write never happens, so the guarantee the audit's B1 comment asserts is false exactly where a player would need it. The refinement: a good .bak does recover the player (recovered=true); total loss requires the .bak to be bad or clobbered, and the clobber path is real (saveLocal rotates the previous save into .bak on a bare JSON.parse check with no saneSave validation, so a malformed-but-parseable save overwrites the last good backup on the next write). Persist.connect compounds it: on any saneSave failure it pushes the local save over the whole cloud envelope with no half-salvage, where loadLocal at least attempts half-salvage. The write-once guard on .repaired (core.js:117, Rey finding 8, direct read) means a second, different corruption later in the profile's life keeps no raw copy at all.
3. **The unlockAll grant: CONFIRMED as a QA blocker, 3 of 3.** The boot grant (main3d.js:11) persists into saved META, so (a) the designed progression configuration has never been exercised by real play in the shipped build, (b) every save the importer would receive arrives fully unlocked, and (c) the importer loses its clean decision point (DeepSeek finding 2). Whatever R33's answer is, the grant question rides with it: strip unbought unlocks on import, or ratify them.
4. **Findings-to-tests ledger: completeness claim REFINED (Argon finding 3).** The ledger's own numbers stand (125 findings: 44 TESTED, 41 TESTABLE-NOT-WRITTEN, 40 NOT-TESTABLE; 48 tests at 8 PASS / 40 FAIL, every failure a correct regression test). But the README's scope list omits the Oct 29 saves and persistence audit (1 BLOCKER, 4 FIX findings) from the program it claims to map completely, and zero tests run against the Godot build, the tree where fixes actually land under the freeze. The 41 unwritten tests mostly need the campaign-file loader extension game_loader.js lacks (it loads data.js, core.js, battle.js only, game_loader.js:41), which is why the campaign layer, where the worst defects live, has the thinnest coverage. The ledger is a defect backlog with good reproduction, not verification; the engineer synthesis reaches the same conclusion from the cutover side.
5. **The suite as a gate: RED BY DESIGN (Rey finding 4, DeepSeek finding 3).** findings_tests.js exits 1 while any pinned defect is unfixed; at the pin that is 40 of 48. As a ledger that is the point. As a gate it produces no signal: a 41st failure, a new regression in a fixed area, is indistinguishable from the 40 expected ones without reading every message, and nothing in the evidence wires the suite into a build, push, or release step. The fix is mechanical (an expected-failures manifest, so the gate fails only on new or changed failures) and belongs on the cutover checklist.
6. **EVT-5 and the free-exit guarantee: the tracker reconciliation GOVERNS over Argon finding 4's framing.** Chair reconciliation: findings_tests.js EVT-5 fails on exactly one event, haven. The eval failed 7. EVT-5 is scoped to fight-start events; of the 7 eval failures only haven is one. The other six are pay-or-suffer events (verified example: storm's only exit costs 40 credits, disabled by eventOptOk at campaign_run.js:232 when the player is broke, so a broke player must take 10 percent hull damage). Under Ryan's Oct 28 ruling as written, which guarantees a free exit at fight-start events, haven is the sole failure and EVT-5 is its correct regression test. Argon finding 4's false-green warning is therefore valid only against a broader guarantee that was never ruled. The Rey leg's two-definitions concern (finding 6) resolves the same way: the eval applied a broader definition to all 33 events as an audit probe; the ruling's scope is fight-start; the oracles do not conflict once scope is stated. What remains genuinely open is a decision, not a defect: whether the guarantee should extend to pay-or-suffer events, where a broke player currently has no free option at all. That decision is in the Ryan lane below, stated in full.
7. **The elite guarantee fix: REFINED by Argon finding 9, carried into the patch pack as an addendum item.** The wave 5 patch's fix at core.js:268 (convert a rest node when no battle or event node exists in the column) still runs before the budgets loop and before the lone-node field conversion at core.js:277, whose target column can be 4. A fix at :268 alone can be undone downstream by :277 converting the guaranteed elite. The patch must also guard :277 (or re-run the guarantee after conversions). The underlying defect is confirmed 3 of 3 (84 of 4,500 maps, 1.87 percent; the code comment asserts the opposite of the code).
8. **Silent id repair: CONFIRMED, with a forward consequence (Argon finding 5, DeepSeek finding 4, Rey finding 7).** repairRunIds drops unknown ids across relics, inventory, fleet ships, ship mods, captain perks, and the pending reward; a deleted ship takes its captain, perks, and refits with it; the player sees one toast with counts; the _repairs fields are then deleted and the run re-saved; the only per-ship record is a raw JSON no UI reads. The forward consequence deserves emphasis: remediating the 40 failing tests requires touching the broken hull, weapon, and module definitions (ROS-2, ROS-3, MOD-2, WPN-5), and any id-level change there triggers silent deletions in active runs loaded across it. The remediation pass needs a drop report and an id-stability check before it lands, not after. SAVE_VERSION is 2 with a single v1-to-v2 step and generic default filling (core.js:49); there is no per-version transform chain for a future rename to hook into (DeepSeek finding 10).
9. **Mid-battle reload: RECLASSIFIED from defect to design decision (Argon finding 7, Rey finding 3, tracker adjudication).** The mechanics are agreed by all prongs: battle state is never saved, hull damage commits only in finishBattle, the per-node seed rebuilds the identical battle, so closing the tab mid-battle is a free, unlimited retry at full strength. The saves audit graded this benign (Finding 7, "replay based, not resume based"). This seat's position, per the tracker: the behavior is inherent to replay-based persistence, so it is a design decision about the port's persistence model, not a code defect in the browser build. In a game whose contract is permanent generational loss, the decision deserves to be made on purpose. The telemetry half of Argon finding 7 is a plain defect, carried separately: pagehide logs battle_abandon via saveLocal only, unstamped, so abandon events are lost on cloud reconciliation.
10. **Wave 3's reassurance: SCOPE-LIMITED (Argon finding 10).** The wave 3 sweeps check map structure and battle setup legality; the 60-run Monte Carlo samples the campaign thinly (12 sector 3 elite fights, 13 sector 3 boss fights). They structurally cannot see the reward layer, where the ledger's tests prove duo boons survive 0 of 300 offers (RWD-4), tier-3 favour backfill rolls Common on 378 of 600 picks (RWD-8), three ram boons collide in one key pool (BN-1), and epic outscales legendary (BN-2). "No softlocks" is true inside its scope and was at risk of being read as broader. (The player synthesis separately overturns the wave 3 win-rate cross-validation; the two findings are distinct.)
11. **Boot residue and settings sync: CONFIRMED (Argon finding 6, Rey finding 10, DeepSeek finding 6).** The G.mobile boot branch fires on coarse-pointer desktops in small windows, forcing reduced-flash off and quality to medium before the player touches a setting, with no test covering boot flags. saveOpts() writes local-only, so settings never stamp or sync until an unrelated save pushes, and a second device resolves without them. Both are small, both are live, and the first is an accessibility hazard (cross-ref vfx and uiux seats).

## Ranked actions: Ryan lane (human direction)

1. **Should the free-exit guarantee extend to pay-or-suffer events?** Story: your Oct 28 ruling guarantees a free exit at every fight-start event; the eval found 7 of 33 events failing a broader reading, and the chair reconciliation shows only haven actually violates the ruling as written. The other six (storm, reactor, squall, mutiny, scout, saboteur) are pay-or-suffer events: their only exit costs credits, and when the player cannot pay, the exit disables and the player must take the damage or consequence (storm: 10 percent hull damage when broke). Stakes: as written, a broke fleet can be forced into hull damage by weather, which sits oddly beside the ruling's intent that no event traps the player; extended, six events gain a genuine walk-away and lose some of their teeth as resource pressure. Cost: extending is a small data change per event (a zero-cost leave option with no forbidden effects) plus one eval re-run; not extending costs nothing and keeps the pressure, at the price of the broke-player trap. The chair's lean: extend it. A guarantee that fails exactly when the player is poorest is the haven bug wearing a different coat. Full context in the final synthesis.
2. **Mid-battle persistence in the Godot port: is replay-from-seed the intended model?** Story: the browser build never saves battle state, so closing mid-battle restarts the fight at full strength, free and unlimited. That is inherent to the replay design, not a bug, but the port is the moment the model gets chosen rather than inherited. Stakes: in a game where about 80 percent of runs are lost by design and losses are the generational engine, a zero-cost mulligan undercuts the contract for any player who discovers it (and players discover it by accident, by closing a laptop). Cost: keeping replay costs nothing and matches the browser build; saving battle state (resume where you left off, damage committed) is real port work in the battle layer and changes how abandonment and the Nemesis meetings behave. The chair's lean: keep replay for the port's first playable, and make the call with play data on how often battles are abandoned mid-fight, which the telemetry spec can count once the pagehide path is fixed. Full context in the final synthesis.
