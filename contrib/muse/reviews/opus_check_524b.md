## The comfort question

The Board asked whether gun hits and muzzle flashes drawn soft (flash_at's yield_last path) are truly outside the WCAG 2.3.1 general flash definition, at the rise time the code uses and at the close cameras with many ships. The answer is no, in three parts.

**(a) One soft flash is a flash.** The definition the counter implements (fx.gd, the issue 504 comment) counts a pair of opposing relative-luminance changes of 10 percent or more, the darker image below 0.80, over a combined area past 0.006 steradians in any 10-degree field, more than three times in any rolling second. Rise time is not a term in it. The soft draw (fx_shaders.gd lines 131 and 159) freezes the baked sequence at cell 0 for the first half of the flash's life and multiplies ALBEDO by sin(age * pi): the envelope rises from 0 to 1.0 at mid-life and falls back to 0. Two things soften the peak in absolute terms, and the chair states them because both legs overshot in opposite directions: _burst (fx_weapons.gd lines 447 to 457) cuts the material power to 1.25 (from the caller's 4.2 for most gun bursts) whenever flash_at returns false, and the soft duration is floored at 0.4 s, so the soft rise is about 0.2 s against the bright lives of 0.08 to 0.30 s. Even so: a baked flash cell at 30 percent intensity over a near-black space background is a relative-luminance change far past 10 percent. The soft flash is one opposing pair, which is to say, one flash.

**(b) The aggregate is unbounded.** Yielding triggers when the combined lit area in a 10-degree box already exceeds flash_limit_px2 (fx.gd lines 566 to 579): the soft population appears exactly where the field is busiest. The yield branch appends the time to _refused (which only the event light reads, for its soft rise) and returns false without remembering the flash: it spends no FLASH_CAP slot, and it does not enter the area accounting later flashes see. Torpedoes and deaths never yield, which protects them, but nothing counts the soft flashes themselves: not per field, not per second, not at all. If a salvo puts six gun hits into one field inside one second, six full flash pairs draw there, and the counter that exists to prevent exactly that pattern cannot see them. hit_recipe_check's yield test proves only that gun hits yield and counted flashes stay at three; it measures no soft luminance, area or rate (both legs, chair-agreed).

**(c) Close cameras make it worse, and nothing measures it there.** A flash's screen area grows as the camera closes (the counter's own _flash_disc works from the live camera), so at cameras 26 and 60 more hits yield, into smaller fields holding more ships. The worker's gate evidence (2 to 12 yielded per 12-second session, zero refused at the head) is real but answers a different question: it runs at the battle camera, and its logging does not bucket yielded flashes by field or by second (scene_gate.gd reports a session count), so no per-second-per-field rate exists at any camera. The comfort claim currently rests on the average, and the average is not what the threshold limits.

**What clears it (either one).** (1) Bound the aggregate in code: remember yielded flashes for rate purposes, and when bright plus soft pairs in a field would pass three in a rolling second, draw the later gun hits with the no-peak soft lift the reduce-flashing path already uses, instead of the baked soft flash. Deaths and torpedoes keep their budget untouched; only the fourth and later gun flashes in a busy field lose their peak. (2) Or measure it: extend the gate to log yielded pairs per second per 10-degree field, run it at cameras 26 and 60 in the storm, and show the rate under three with the combined area under the limit. The chair's read is that (1) is the smaller and more durable fix, because (2) leaves the bound resting on volley patterns staying polite.
