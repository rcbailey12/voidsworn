# Research findings: how other games make hull damage readable

## Homeworld 3 (Blackbird Interactive, Gearbox). The strongest single source for this commission.

The Homeworld 3 design team specified four distinct damage types (Kinetic, High Explosive, Energy, Ion), each with its own visual treatment and its own effects on units, and the art team built per-type impact decals: in the dev blog's words, each weapon leaves a unique impact decal, and the decal gives you a basic idea of who shot your ship and with what type of weapon. The blog then describes going past flat decals to a system they call Splat Damage, where impact points directly alter the surface geometry and properties of the hull in the exact area hit, so a salvo visibly rips through armour instead of just marking it. Two stated goals matter for Voidsworn: the wounds persist from mission to mission, and they give at-a-glance information about whether a specific ship has been through combat before. Each encounter leaves scars that tell that ship's story. Source: Homeworld 3 dev blog, "Weapons and Unit Damage in Homeworld 3" (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html), coverage also at https://bleedingcool.com/games/homeworld-3-releases-new-blog-featuring-battle-details/

What the blog's described screenshots show, in words: a hull plate used as a test target carries several separate impact craters side by side, one per damage type, each a darkened pit with a different rim character and a different spread of debris staining around it. The point of the image is comparison: at a glance the craters are not interchangeable, because shape and rim do the identifying, not colour alone. (Judgement: Voidsworn needs exactly this test plate. Build one hull, apply all ten marks, and check them side by side at fleet distance before tuning any of them in combat.)

## Sins of a Solar Empire (Ironclad, Stardock). Dynamic effects at the hit point, not a canned damaged skin.

A Stardock developer answer on the official forums, asked whether players would see hull damage, promised "non-canned damage effects including flames, billowing fire, smoke, steam, electrical damage and more", and forum discussion at the time understood this correctly: the game does not swap in a pre-made damaged skin at a health threshold, it introduces damage effects dynamically where the ship is hit. Source: https://forums.sinsofasolarempire.com/143068/get;1256204

The lesson is about channels, not marks. Sins reads at fleet scale mostly through motion and light: fire, smoke and electrical discharge placed on the hull, layered over clean ship models, with health carried by UI. A screenshot of a hurt Sins capital ship, described in words: the hull itself stays largely clean, but two or three points along the flank carry orange flame and rising dark smoke, and occasional blue-white electrical arcs snap across the superstructure. You read "that ship is hurt, roughly there" from across the gravity well, and nothing more specific than that. Voidsworn's ruling asks for more than Sins gives (per-weapon identity), so Sins is the floor for the freshness channel, not the model for mark identity. Whether Sins 2 added per-weapon marks on top of this, I could not verify from developer material in this pass: UNSURE. Community posts describe a crippled state for badly hurt ships in Sins 2 (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1575940/discussions/1/798967628136872433), which supports a distinct near-death read, but that is community description, not a dev source.

## Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2 (Tindalos). Damage as ongoing visible states, and location damage with consequences.

Critical hits in Armada 2 disable subsystems (generators, engines, deck, weapons), can cause hull breaches, and can start fires; fires are a damage-over-time state, up to four can stack on one ship, and using the repair ability puts them all out. Sources: https://www.magicgameworld.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-ii-advanced-tips-tricks/ and the community Armada 101 mechanics guide (https://steamcommunity.com/app/363680/discussions/0/1457328927834606989/?l=greek&ctp=1)

Described in words, a burning Armada 2 ship at battle distance is the easiest damage read in this whole survey: flame and smoke pour from one or more points on a cathedral hull that is otherwise intact, and because the fire keeps burning until repaired, the mark is a state with a lifetime, not a scar. Two transfers for Voidsworn. First, any mark that represents an ongoing effect (fire, venting, acid still working) should live in the active channel and visibly end when the effect ends. Second, Armada ties the worst visible damage to specific subsystems, which is the same job Voidsworn's disabled sections need: the section that is down should carry the heaviest visible wound.

## Starsector (Fractal Softworks). Armour comes off where you are hit, and the ship carries its damage history.

Starsector models armour per location around the hull: weapons strip armour where they strike, and once a facing's armour is gone, hits there reach the hull. Community primers describe the armour display as a silhouette of the ship whose individual bits burn off as armour is lost on that side. Sources: https://www.idlethumbs.net/forums/topic/9732-starsector/?tab=comments#comment-324898 and https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/wip-starsector-space-battle-sandbox-adventure.781877/

Starsector also separates battle damage from permanent wear: ships can carry damage mods (D-mods) with a visible overlay whose strength runs in steps from pristine to heavily worn, per the game's dev-mode documentation (https://starsector.fandom.com/wiki/Dev_mode). That two-layer idea, temporary battle marks over a permanent wear layer, maps directly onto Voidsworn's fresh/cooling/scarred evolution. I could not find primary Fractal Softworks writing on the in-combat damage rendering itself in this pass, so the specifics above rest on community primers: UNSURE on rendering detail, solid on the location-armour behaviour.

## BattleTech (Harebrained Schemes). Location is the information.

A BattleMech has eight separate locations, each with its own armour and then internal structure; the paper doll shows each location's state at a glance, armour breach exposes structure and the components inside that location, and destroyed locations pass damage inward toward the centre torso. Sources: comprehensive game guide (https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1376736707) and the classic system overview (https://www.belloflostsouls.net/2011/06/overview-classic-battletech-gameplay-in-a-nutshell.html)

On the 3D model, the same story is told with smoke, fire, exposed structure and lost limbs at the damaged location, and the MechWarrior series generally makes weapon type visible on armour: lasers melt it, missiles mangle it, flamers scorch it, autocannons crater it (series summary at https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage). Voidsworn is turn-based with hull sections, so BattleTech is its structural benchmark: the mark's job is not decoration, it is the section's state drawn on the section. A player should be able to look at a hull and say which section is hurt and roughly by what, the way a BattleTech player reads a paper doll, without opening a panel.

## Children of a Dead Earth (Q Switched Productions). Damage is physical and local, and it shows because systems die where they are hit.

COADE simulates every module on a ship individually (radiators, reactors, engines, weapons), each with its own parameters, and modules can be targeted and destroyed: kill the radiators on a power plant and the ship loses power and is a mission kill. Kinetic penetration is physical: a round that punches through armour destroys modules in a cone behind the penetration point (spalling). Sources: developer-answered Steam discussion (https://steamcommunity.com/app/476530/discussions/0/3200369647701833486/) and the subsystem damage summary (https://TVTropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Videogame/ChildrenOfADeadEarth)

COADE's visuals are austere, so the transfer is a principle, not a look: a through-hole with internal damage behind it is a different statement from a surface scorch, and players read the difference instantly. Voidsworn's gauss puncture and lance burn-through both need that "this went through" read, which a flat dark decal cannot give on its own.

## Star Citizen (Cloud Imperium). The best documented damage model in the survey, and it is a channel model.

Cloud Imperium's damage design notes describe recording four quantities per hull area instead of blending in a damaged texture: temperature (current heat), burn (maximum heat reached), thickness (how much outer hull remains; paint comes off first, then the underlying metal), and deformation (how much physical force has bent the surface). Those four channels drive hull glow, paint bubbling and peeling, exposed metal, dents, and holes cut through to modelled internals. The notes state the payoff explicitly: the damage pattern and strength are unique to each weapon or impact type, so lasers make the hull light up for seconds and leave burnt paint and exposed metal, while a powerful ballistic tears a hole straight through. Source: https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568

This is the technical shape I recommend Voidsworn copy at its own scale (judgement): every mark is a small bundle of channel values (heat now, heat scar, material loss, deformation), and the ten marks differ because their channel mixes differ, not because ten unrelated textures were painted. That is what makes evolution cheap: cooling is the heat channel falling, scarring is the burn and thickness channels staying.

## Supporting examples

EVE Online's visual damage system puts the damage on the hull where the impact happened, with different visuals for armour damage and structure damage; period reporting of the system describes scorch on armour and, once armour is breached, flaming holes in the hull (https://evenews24.com/2015/11/20/sisi-new-shield-and-armour-visual-effects-released-on-test-server/ and https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage). The transfer: layer change should change the mark's character. Damage that only reaches armour looks like surface injury; damage into structure looks like holes and fire.

FreeSpace 2 scales a whole-ship signal with damage: heavily damaged ships arc electricity across the hull, and more damage means more arcing, until a nearly dead ship is almost constantly wracked with arcs (https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage). The transfer: one cheap, ship-wide severity cue (arcing, smoke volume, list or flicker) beats ten more small marks when the question is just "how close to death is that ship".

Nexus: The Jupiter Incident lets weapons damage the hull leave visible scarring, and its subsystems (engines, power plants, drives, shields, weapons) can be targeted individually (https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SubsystemDamage; period review at https://boards.straightdope.com/t/nexus-the-jupiter-incident-kicks-ass/295413). Weaker sourcing than the games above; included because Nexus is a fleet-scale game where hull state, not UI, carries a lot of the read. UNSURE on detail.

## Technique note: decals versus baked damage textures

Classic impact decals are textured polygons floated over the surface; they are cheap per mark but fight the surface (z-fighting, visible float on curves), and deferred approaches instead write into the surface buffers so the mark takes the hull's lighting. Reference: polycount wiki, Decal (http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/Decal). Homeworld 3's move from decals to Splat Damage, and Star Citizen's channel model, are both moves in the same direction: marks written into the hull's own surface data. For a game with persistent scars and up to 20 damaged ships, baking accumulated marks into a per-ship damage texture (with a small number of live decals or effects only for the freshest hits) is the approach the evidence points to (judgement, grounded in the two sources above). Exact cost in Godot for Voidsworn's ship sizes: UNSURE, not benchmarked here, and it is Opus's call as a technical decision.


# The ten marks: reference entries

Common frame for all ten (judgement, built from the sources above). At tactical distance a ship is small, so each mark is specified in two tiers. The distance tier is what must read with the whole fleet on screen: shape family, size relative to the hull, brightness or silhouette change. The zoom tier is detail that only has to work up close. Evolution uses three stages everywhere. Fresh: the hit and the seconds after, glow and particles allowed. Cooling: mid-battle, glow gone or nearly gone, colour settling. Scarred: late battle, a permanent albedo and surface change with no light of its own. A mark that cannot state its distance tier in one sentence is too complicated.

## 1. Lance: burn-through (lance array, pulsar lance, doom arc)

Distance tier: a short bright slot. A narrow elongated cut, noticeably longer than it is wide, with a glowing core while fresh. On a hull at fleet distance it reads as a lit slash, the only mark shaped like a line segment rather than a spot or a patch.
Evolution. Fresh: white-orange core with a thin halo, a brief wisp of smoke from the slot, and (zoom tier) a matching smaller lit point on the far side when the shot pierced, the through-hole read COADE argues for. Cooling: core dims to deep red, then to a dark glassy seam with raised blistered edges. Scarred: a black slot with a slightly melted, glossy rim, no glow.
Accumulation: repeated lance hits on one section lengthen the slot or add a second parallel slot, capped at two per section; past that, deepen the existing slot to structure (dark interior visible at zoom).
Strongest source: Homeworld 3's Energy damage treatment and per-type decals (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html), with Star Citizen's laser behaviour, hull lights up for seconds and leaves burnt paint and exposed metal (https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568), and the MechWarrior series pattern that lasers melt armour (https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage).

## 2. Plasma: splash craters (Hegemony plasma batteries and turbolasers, by faction trait)

Distance tier: a round crater with a splash. A soft-edged bowl, wider than the lance slot and shallower, with a fan of small droplet spots thrown out on one side (the splash direction, away from the incoming shot). The droplet fan is the identifier: no other mark throws spots.
Evolution. Fresh: the bowl floor glows hot white-blue and the droplets glow with it, the brightest battery-class mark in the game while fresh. Cooling: glow retreats to the bowl floor, droplets go dark first, rim takes a heat-discoloured ring (straw to blue metal tints, judgement). Scarred: a matte dark bowl with a freckled splash skirt, still identifiable by shape alone.
Accumulation: bowls merge when they overlap; a section under sustained plasma fire becomes one large worked crater field rather than many small bowls, which is correct, because at that point the read is "this section is plasma-burned", not a hit count.
Faction note (judgement): plasma is Hegemony's battery trait, so this mark does double duty as their battery mark at reduced size (see entry 10). Keep its colour family hot white-blue at the core but never use shield colours; the shape, not the colour, carries identity.
Strongest source: Homeworld 3's High Explosive and Energy crater comparison, where rim character and debris spread separate the types (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html), plus Star Citizen's burn plus deformation channel mix for a hot, dented, paint-stripped result (https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568).

## 3. Gauss: punctures (gauss flayer; gauss sentries are flak kind and use entry 5 at small size)

Distance tier: a ring, not a hole. The hole itself is far too small to read at fleet distance, so the mark is a pale stress ring, a bright metal circle with short radial crack lines, like a tiny starred bullseye. The ring is the distance read; the hole is the zoom read. A line of two or three rings along a shot path is unmistakable and is this mark's signature at range.
Evolution. Fresh: ring is bright fresh metal, one spark jet and a small flash at impact, brief thin smoke. Cooling: ring tarnishes from bright to grey, cracks darken. Scarred: dark pinhole with a pale cracked halo, low contrast but still patterned, deliberately the quietest signature mark once old.
Accumulation: punctures stay individual longer than other marks (a dotted row is information: this section was machined by gauss fire), but cap the row per section and fade the oldest rings into the general scar layer.
Risk stated plainly: this is the mark most likely to fail at fleet distance (judgement). If playtesting shows the ring vanishing, grow the ring, not the hole; a bigger hole just becomes a small crater and collides with entries 2 and 4.
Strongest source: COADE's physical penetration model, where a punched hole and the damage cone behind it are a distinct event from surface damage (https://steamcommunity.com/app/476530/discussions/0/3200369647701833486/), and Star Citizen's ballistic result, a hole torn straight through rather than a burn (https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568).

## 4. Torpedo: blast craters (torpedo salvo, heavy torpedoes, star torpedo; Voidsworn hellfire torpedoes)

Distance tier: the biggest mark in the game. A wide crater that dents the hull's local outline, with armour plates peeled outward at the rim. It is the only surface mark besides ram crumple that is allowed to change the silhouette, and at fleet distance that silhouette dent plus size is the read.
Evolution. Fresh: fire and smoke from the crater, rim edges burning, a debris puff at impact; this is the one mark that may carry a real fire into the cooling stage, in line with Armada 2's fires as a burning state that lasts until repaired (https://www.magicgameworld.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-ii-advanced-tips-tricks/). Cooling: fire dies to a few glowing pockets in the crater floor, smoke thins to a steady trail while the section is badly hurt. Scarred: a large dark crater with bent plate petals and, at zoom, exposed structure ribs inside.
Accumulation: one torpedo crater per section is usually enough; a second hit in the same section upgrades the crater a stage (wider, deeper, structure exposed) instead of placing a second crater beside it. Torpedoes are the heavy end of the scale, so their mark budget is small by design.
Strongest source: Homeworld 3's High Explosive craters (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html), Armada 2 hull breaches and fires (above), and EVE's structure-stage flaming holes once armour is breached (https://evenews24.com/2015/11/20/sisi-new-shield-and-armour-visual-effects-released-on-test-server/).

## 5. Flak: pockmarks (flak battery, spore mines, gauss sentries; point defence kind)

Distance tier: a rough patch, not individual holes. Flak and point-defence fire lands as a tight cluster of tiny pits, and at fleet distance the cluster reads as one stippled, matte patch that catches light differently from the surrounding hull. The identifier is texture: a grey rash with a hard cluster boundary, roughly circular, sized by how much fire the section took.
Evolution. Fresh: the patch appears with a sparkle of small flashes across it (many tiny hits landing together), then settles within seconds. Cooling: pits dull to mid-grey, patch edges stay defined. Scarred: a matte, slightly roughened patch with lower shine than the base hull; it reads by light response when the ship turns, which is a feature, not a bug (judgement): flak damage is widespread and shallow, and its scar should be the subtlest of the nine.
Accumulation: the patch grows in area and density, never in depth. Flak cannot produce structure exposure on its own in the mark language, whatever the numbers say, or the language stops meaning anything (judgement).
Strongest source: Homeworld 3's Kinetic damage type, the many-small-hits end of its four-type system (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html), with the MechWarrior pattern of autocannon cratering scaled down to pit size (https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage). No source gives flak its own signature treatment that I found; the patch treatment is judgement, flagged again in the UNSURE list.

## 6. Bio: acid pitting (bio-plasma, boarding tendrils at range 1, spore weapons where bio kind)

Distance tier: an eaten patch with a wet look. An irregular blob with lacy, eaten edges, a sickly green-brown colour shift against any hull paint, and a glossy centre that stays glossy after other marks have gone matte. The wet sheen plus the colour shift is the identifier; acid is the only mark that changes the hull's colour family rather than just darkening it.
Evolution, and this mark breaks the pattern on purpose. Fresh: glossy wet patch, small bubbles or drips at the edge, a faint vapour wisp. Cooling: acid keeps working briefly after the hit, so the patch spreads a little and its rim crusts and darkens while the centre stays wet; this is a damage-over-time read like Armada 2's fires, and it must visibly stop (the whole patch goes matte at the edges inward) when the effect ends (https://www.magicgameworld.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-ii-advanced-tips-tricks/ for the state-with-a-lifetime pattern). Scarred: a pitted matte patch, pits clustered like corrosion bloom, with stained streaks running away from the patch along the hull's flow lines.
Accumulation: patches grow by spreading at the edges, not by stacking pits; two nearby patches join into one larger eaten area. On Chorus hulls (grown flesh, per the game's art rules) acid should read as blistered, discoloured flesh rather than corroded metal, same shape language, different material response (judgement).
Strongest source: no reference game in the survey gives acid its own hull treatment that I could source; the entry is built by analogy from Armada 2's ongoing fire state and Star Citizen's burn channel (a surface chemistry record that persists after the heat is gone, https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568). This is the most judgement-led entry in the audit: UNSURE, flagged again below.

## 7. Ion: burns (ion cannon, kind lance with the ion shield-strip property)

Distance tier: a branching scorch. A lightning-tree or fern pattern radiating from a small bright centre, branches reaching two to three times the centre's width. Shape is the identifier: a tree, where the lance is a slot and plasma is a bowl. Colour supports it (blue-white while fresh) but must not be the only carrier, since shields already own faction colours in this game.
Evolution. Fresh: the branches are bright and, for a second or so, small arcs crawl along them; the centre flashes with each ion hit, tying the mark to the shield-strip moment. Cooling: arcs stop, branches fade through pale blue to etched grey lines, centre goes dark. Scarred: a faint fern etching, the most delicate scar in the set, visible at mid zoom, quiet at fleet distance, which is fine because ion's tactical moment is the strip, and that happens fresh.
Accumulation: a second ion hit on the same section re-energises the existing tree (branches brighten, tree extends a branch or two) rather than planting a second tree; ion damage reads as one growing scorch fern per section.
Strongest source: Homeworld 3 specifies Ion as one of its four damage types with its own visual treatment (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html); FreeSpace 2's hull arcing, which scales with damage, supports the crawling-arc fresh stage (https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage); Sins' electrical discharge effects support arcs as a damage channel at fleet scale (https://forums.sinsofasolarempire.com/143068/get;1256204).

## 8. Ram: crumple (movement action; both hulls take it)

Distance tier: a dent you can see from across the board. A contact patch where plating is folded and buckled, breaking the hull's outline, with scrape lines (bare metal streaks) leading into the dent along the direction of impact. This mark must read with no glow, no colour and no particles, by silhouette and shading alone, because it often happens on the bow where a player is watching the other ship, not this one.
Evolution. Fresh: a dust and debris puff, a few sparks, one or two plates visibly sprung or missing at the patch edge, thin smoke for a few seconds. Cooling: smoke stops, scrape lines tarnish from bright metal to dull grey, the dent is unchanged (deformation does not cool, and that permanence is the point). Scarred: the dent plus a fan of scrape scars, the only scar that survives repairs visually unless the section is actually fixed; if repairs restore the section, plates are replaced and the dent goes, which makes repair readable too (judgement).
Accumulation: a second ram on the same section widens the dent and may tear a plate free (a small notch in the outline). Ram crumple never appears anywhere except the contact area, and the ramming ship takes a matching, usually smaller, crumple on its own bow: both hulls tell the story, per the action's rule.
Strongest source: Star Citizen's deformation channel, physical force recorded as bent and distorted surface, separate from heat and material loss (https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568). COADE's collision and module-loss physics support real geometric consequences over decals (https://steamcommunity.com/app/476530/discussions/0/3200369647701833486/).

## 9. Boarding: breaches (boarding action at close range; boarding tendrils are its visual cousin)

Distance tier: a torn opening with a ring of small punctures around it. Boarding leaves two things: the breach itself, a short jagged gash or pried hatch opening, and the tendril attach pattern, a rough ring or arc of small holes where the tendrils held on. At fleet distance the read is the gash plus, while fresh, what comes out of it.
Evolution. Fresh: venting. A white gas plume jets from the breach and keeps jetting while the boarding fight is live; sparks and a spill of interior light at the gash; this is the most alive mark in the set while it lasts. Cooling: venting thins and stops when the boarding resolves; the gash rim darkens; if the ship keeps the section, a pale sealant or patch plate appears over part of the gash (repair made visible, judgement). Scarred: a dark gash partly covered by a mismatched patch plate, with the tendril puncture ring still visible around it at zoom. On a captured ship this scar is its history: it should be the last scar type to fade (judgement, from Homeworld 3's scars-tell-the-story goal, https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html).
Accumulation: one breach per boarding action, placed at a plausible boarding point (airlock, docking collar, habitat section), never scattered randomly; a second action on the same ship makes a second breach at a different point, and two breaches plus venting is a whole-ship emergency read on its own.
Strongest source: Armada 2, where assault (boarding) actions cause critical damage including hull breaches and fires, tying the breach visual to the boarding event specifically (https://www.magicgameworld.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-ii-advanced-tips-tricks/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/363680/discussions/0/1457328927834606989/?l=greek&ctp=1). The venting plume and tendril ring are judgement; no surveyed game gives boarding a dedicated hull mark I could source: UNSURE on those specifics.

## 10. Battery fire: the generic mark (autocannon, macro batteries, turrets, laser cannon, turbolasers, scatter cannons, shuriken cannon)

Distance tier: a small scorch dot with a short streak, deliberately the weakest mark in the language. Batteries are the most common weapons in the game, so their mark must be cheap, quiet and quick to fade, or it will drown the nine signature marks. Individual battery hits should barely register at fleet distance; what registers is accumulation, a section gradually looking worked over.
Evolution. Fresh: a small dark dot with a brief orange kiss of glow, gone in seconds, no smoke except on heavy batteries. Cooling: dot softens to a grey-brown scorch. Scarred: dots merge into a general scorch shading over the section (a dirtied, darkened area rather than countable spots), which doubles as the generic wear layer under the signature scars.
Faction variants (from the pack's weapon traits). Hegemony batteries are plasma weapons in name and trait, so Hegemony battery hits use entry 2's splash crater at reduced size and brightness, its droplet fan smaller and quicker to cool. Voidsworn guns read as las weapons, so their battery mark may borrow a faint slot tendency (short streak rather than round dot) at the same low strength (judgement). Keep both variants clearly weaker than the true lance and full plasma marks.
Accumulation rule specific to batteries: never let battery dots stay countable. Past a low threshold per section, stop placing dots and darken the section's general scorch layer instead (the bake-into-texture behaviour from the technique note).
Strongest source: Homeworld 3's premise itself, that standard decals did a good job of showing damage and identifying the weapon class at a glance, is what the battery mark is: the plain decal tier, kept plain on purpose (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html). EVE's armour-stage scorch, surface injury before holes, supports the dot-to-scorch-shading progression (https://evenews24.com/2015/11/20/sisi-new-shield-and-armour-visual-effects-released-on-test-server/).

# Readability rules: 20 damaged ships at fleet distance

The test every rule below serves (judgement): with 20 ships on screen, a player glancing at any one ship for one second should be able to say three things: how hurt it is, which section is worst, and roughly what has been hitting it (heavy explosive, energy, kinetic, bio, or boarding). Exact weapon identification is a zoom read, not a fleet read. Any mark or rule that does not help one of those three answers at fleet distance is decoration, and decoration is what gets cut first.

1. Two channels only at distance: brightness and shape. Freshness is carried by light (glow, fire, venting, smoke). Identity is carried by shape family (slot, bowl, ring, crater, patch, tree, dent, gash, dot). Colour supports both and carries neither alone, because hull paints and shield colours already spend the colour budget. This mirrors the survey: Sins reads by light and motion at range (https://forums.sinsofasolarempire.com/143068/get;1256204), Homeworld 3 by crater shape (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html).

2. Mark budget per section, not per ship. Cap full-strength signature marks per hull section (judgement starting point: three), because sections are the unit the player reasons about (BattleTech's locations, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1376736707). Battery marks do not count against the signature budget; they live in the scorch layer. When the budget is full, a new hit upgrades or replaces the weakest existing mark in that section instead of adding another.

3. Merge repeats, deepen stages. A second hit of the same kind in the same place grows the existing mark (longer slot, wider crater, bigger patch, extended fern) or advances it a stage (surface, through, structure). Never render the same mark five times side by side and call it accumulation; repeated identical marks are the fastest route to noise.

4. Glow is rationed. Only the freshest few marks per ship may glow, and only active states (fire, venting, working acid, live ion arcing) may keep glowing. If everything glows, nothing does. As a starting point for tuning (judgement): at most two glowing marks and one particle emitter (smoke, vent, sparks) per ship at fleet distance, more allowed at close zoom. FreeSpace 2's damage arcing works because it is one ship-wide signal, not twenty small ones (https://TVtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShowsDamage).

5. Smoke belongs to the worst ships. A smoke trail is the strongest "badly hurt" cue at range, so reserve persistent smoke for ships or sections past a damage threshold, and for torpedo craters and breaches while active. Lightly damaged ships get marks, not weather systems. Twenty smoking ships is a fog bank, not a battle read.

6. Contrast floor. Every mark must pass against the lightest and the darkest hull paint in the game: dark marks get a pale or hot rim, pale marks (gauss ring, acid crust) get a dark core or shadow. Test on white, black, and the most saturated faction hull. A mark that only reads on mid-grey test armour has failed (judgement, standard decal practice per http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/Decal).

7. Size floor at fleet zoom. No distance-tier element smaller than a few screen pixels at default tactical zoom; subpixel marks shimmer, alias and vanish, which is why the gauss entry specifies a ring and the flak entry specifies a patch (judgement). If a mark cannot meet the floor honestly, its information moves to the zoom tier and its distance tier is borrowed from a bigger companion cue (halo, ring, patch).

8. Disabled sections must shout. A section knocked out by a critical hit gets the full treatment regardless of budget: heaviest scar stage on that section, plus smoke or the section's active-state effect, plus (zoom tier) exposed structure. The pack requires crit state readable on the hull, and Armada 2 and BattleTech both tie their most legible damage to dead subsystems and locations (https://www.magicgameworld.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-ii-advanced-tips-tricks/, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1376736707).

9. Scars accumulate, fresh marks rotate. The scar layer per ship only grows during a battle (baked into the ship's damage texture, per the technique note); the live layer (glows, decals not yet baked, particles) stays small and recycles. This is Starsector's two-layer split, battle damage over permanent wear (https://starsector.fandom.com/wiki/Dev_mode), and Homeworld 3's persistent scars goal, applied inside one battle (https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html).

10. Repairs and regrowth must visibly reverse marks. When a section is repaired or a Chorus ship regrows hull, step its marks back: glow returns briefly as new plate or new growth covers scars, patch plates appear over breaches, crumple stays only if the section was not truly restored. If healing shows only in a UI bar while the hull still looks wrecked, the hull language and the game state disagree, and players will trust the hull (judgement).

11. Zoom contract. Close zoom may add: exit holes, crack detail, acid gloss and pit texture, ion fern detail, structure inside craters, tendril rings. Fleet distance may never depend on any of it. Build and review the marks at fleet distance first, zoom second, which is the reverse of how they will naturally get art-directed (judgement).

12. Facing honesty. Marks land where hits land; a ship whose damage is all on the far side will look clean to the camera. Mitigation is not fake marks: the freshness channel (smoke, venting, glow bleed at the hull edge) shows over the silhouette from any angle, and the section state is also in the target readout. Do not solve facing by mirroring marks to the camera side; players who rotate the camera will catch it and stop trusting every mark (judgement).

## Failure modes to avoid

- Decal soup: dozens of small dark spots per hull until every ship reads as generic grey noise. Caused by no budget and no merging (rules 2 and 3). This is the single most likely failure for this feature.
- Glow inflation: every mark emissive, all the time. The freshness channel dies and night-side hulls look decorated rather than damaged.
- One scorch fits all: if lance, plasma and battery all land as round black blobs, the who-shot-what benefit that justifies the whole system (Homeworld 3's stated reason for per-type decals) is gone, and only the cost remains.
- Holed cheese: too many through-holes and breaches at full size makes hulls look fragile and comedic, and spends the torpedo and ram marks' silhouette power. Through-holes are rationed by entries 1, 3, 4 and 9 on purpose.
- Colour collisions: ion blue against cyan interface elements and faction-coloured shields, acid green against Chorus hull colours, plasma tint against Hegemony shield colour. Shape must carry identity precisely because colour is already spent (rule 1).
- Static scar hiding fresh damage: late in a battle, a new hit on a scarred section must still flash and glow over the scar layer, or players stop seeing new information exactly when the battle is most dangerous.
- Marks that fight the model: floating, clipped or z-fighting decals on curved hulls (http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/Decal). One visibly floating scorch mark teaches players the whole system is stickers.
- Particle weather: smoke and venting on most ships at once (rule 5). Particles are the loudest channel and the easiest to overuse.

# Ranked list: the recommendations that matter most

1. Build the ten marks as channel mixes on one shared damage system (heat now, heat scar, material loss, deformation), not as ten unrelated textures. It makes evolution, accumulation and repair reversal all cheap, and it is what both documented high-end systems actually do (Star Citizen, https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/14568; Homeworld 3 Splat Damage, https://www.eprison.de/spiele/homeworld-3/steam-news/5059267908810906377/7006/12772.html).
2. Enforce the two-tier read: shape family plus freshness light at fleet distance, detail reserved for zoom. Review every mark on a full 20-ship board before approving it in close-up.
3. Cap and merge: signature mark budget per section, repeats deepen the existing mark, battery fire bakes into a general scorch layer and never stays countable.
4. Ration glow and particles hard: freshest hits and active states only; smoke reserved for the worst ships and sections.
5. Give ram crumple and the heavy torpedo crater real silhouette presence; they are the only marks that read at any distance in any light, and they anchor the heavy end of the language.
6. Solve gauss honestly with the ring and crack star as the distance mark and the hole as the zoom mark; do not upscale the hole into a crater.
7. Tie the heaviest visible state to disabled sections (heaviest scar stage plus smoke), so critical hits are readable on the hull as the pack requires.
8. Make the over-time marks (torpedo fire, working acid, boarding venting) true states with visible starts and ends, on the Armada 2 fire pattern, and keep every other mark inert between hits.
9. Keep battery fire deliberately weakest, with Hegemony plasma splash as its one stronger variant at reduced strength, so the signature marks keep their meaning.
10. Run the contrast and collision tests early: every mark against lightest, darkest and Chorus hulls, and every mark colour checked against shield and interface colours.
