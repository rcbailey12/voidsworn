Review of the issue 1291 run C build (PR 1308, branch opus/issue-1291 at

close frames B20_11_b, _d, _q, _s, _t, _u, and the two battle frames

## Verdict: partly the creature

Unanimous across the three legs. The silhouette, massing and parts list
are the concept's: tapered bow ending in the spiked mouth ring, the row
of sac bulges along the flank, the banded shell over the back, the paired
stern veil. The close three-quarter (B20_11_q) is recognisably the C13,
and the red clay problem of earlier runs is fixed. What is not the
concept is the surface: run C shades grown tissue like assembled
hard-surface parts, so at the battle camera the creature reads as a
banded shell with balls under it, and the first wrong pixels are the even
grey comb of the bands and the hard chartreuse flash at the veil roots,
the brightest pixel in both battle frames. The concept leads the eye to
the bow mouth and the backlit veil; run C leads it to a lime root and a
grey comb.

## What reads wrong, by element

- Dark red veins: nearly absent, unanimous. What exists is faint
  reddish mottling on some sac domes (B20_11_b, _s, _u), flat albedo
  patches that read as dirt or bruising. The network is absent exactly
  where the concept runs it hardest: between the swellings, in the
  fissures, on the bow hide, and everywhere at battle distance. The side
  and underside also lose the olive brown and go almost black grey
  (B20_11_b, _s, _u). This is the largest single gap from the concept.
- Armour bands (B20_11_d, _q, _s, _t): too even and too grey tan.
  Uniform width, spacing and ribbing with clean seams, sitting on top of
  the body like strapped plate or corrugated sheet, almost vinyl in
  B20_11_d. The dorsal comb runs at constant height over the lumps
  instead of rising and dissolving into them; at the bow the bands wrap
  as full concentric rings, a segmented-worm read, where the concept
  shell is dorsal and dies into flank flesh. Blown white rim hotspots on
  the crests add a plastic, studio-lit read.
- Stern veil fins (B20_11_b, _s, _u): flat paper. Opaque, even tan
  sheets with hard cut edges, held out stiffly, where the concept veils
  are torn, translucent membranes backlit amber. The yellow green at the
  roots is a hard edged star or splash with no falloff, an unshaded
  emissive decal, not light coming out of bile slits. The council parts
  company with the comparison sheet here, which credits the root glow
  as closer: on the frames it is a new mismatch, and at battle distance
  the loudest single defect in the set.
- Sac pores and bile slits (B20_11_b, _s, _u): the pores read as rivets,
  small black dots in tidy clusters, several carrying a specular dot
  that reads as a stud head; each sac also meets the hide with a clean
  circular seam rim, so the sacs read as bolted domes instead of
  swellings grown out of the hide. The concept has no glowing slits at
  all; the thin chartreuse dashes on a few sacs are invented emissives
  and read as scratches.
- Mouth spikes and ring (B20_11_q, _d, _t): the mouth ring itself reads
  grown and is the best element. The spikes are too neat: uniform white
  pegs of equal length in perfect radial spacing, where the concept has
  irregular bone spines of varied length, gauge and curve, several
  broken. The ring closes over a flat disc like a capped hatch where
  the concept has a wrinkled, sunken throat, and the dorsal pore rings
  repeat the identical ring-plus-pins module, a copy-paste tell.
- Hide (B20_11_d, close): the one place the flesh works, wet sheen,
  folding and reddish depth under and between the bands. That register
  does not extend over the sacs and flanks, which are what the battle
  camera actually sees.

## The one change

One organic material and lighting pass over the whole creature, with the
vein and fissure network as its first component. All three legs land
here from different directions: the blind leg named restoring the dark
red vein and fissure contrast, with deep fold shading, on the exposed
sacs and flanks so it still reads at battle distance; DeepSeek and Qwen
named the wider pass (wet subsurface flesh, fold occlusion, per-tissue
light response, graded emissives) because every defect above shares one
cause. The chair adopts the pass and orders it: veins and fold shading
first (the largest area and the largest gap), then the emissive fixes,
then the local geometry (band irregularity, spine variation, pore
jitter), which reads as growth regularity once the edges blend. If only
one atomic edit is possible, it is the veil roots: convert the flat
chartreuse emissive to graded amber backlight through a translucent
membrane. That is the only defect visible at every distance in the set.
