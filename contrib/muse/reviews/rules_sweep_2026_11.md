# Rules sweep 2026-11: the outside leg (issue 495)

Chaired by Rey (Muse). Legs: rules_sweep_2026_11_leg_rey.md (blind, written from AGENTS.md, the eight playbooks and the newest thirty FINDINGS rows only, before the other legs), rules_sweep_2026_11_leg_deepseek.md (writer, FINDINGS.md in full), rules_sweep_2026_11_leg_qwen.md (writer, AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, HANDOFF.md, REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md, the ship pipeline README, BLOB_SWEEP.md and the go-forward plan), rules_sweep_2026_11_leg_flash.md (ideation, the eight playbooks), rules_sweep_2026_11_leg_grok.md (ideation, the thirty briefs in contrib/opus/briefs/). Argon seat empty, blocked since Nov 5. This leg was written independently of the Board's own sweep legs (issues 493 and 494); those PRs were not read.

Headline: the project's rules do not live in one place, so they rot in four distinct ways. Live documents state as fact things a later ruling already changed (the breach check, the Board's own seat). Dedicated documents contradict the general rules file on the same question (the blob sweep's verdicts against R121). Corrections are filed in a new FINDINGS row and never carried back into the document they correct (the nose-gun grammar, the Kimi sentence). And dead material (phone pipeline steps, a frozen renderer's format, retired numeric gates, briefs superseded within a day) sits unmarked beside live rules, so a newcomer obeying the documents to the letter will build the wrong thing in at least six places. All five legs found this independently, from different reading sets.

Screening notes. DeepSeek's item 15 cites an "R233 capture round"; FINDINGS runs to R190 and no R233 exists, so that reference is invented and nothing here rests on it. Qwen's B8 counts this job's own instruction pack as a third copy of the no-em-dashes rule; discounted, the two project copies stand. Flash's frigate-ribbon contradiction is weak (the 0.72 s it cites is the death window, not a flame life) and is not carried. Flash's challenge to the 0.70 s flame cap is not carried either: the cap was re-ruled by Ryan with its reasoning on Nov 10 (issue 352, question 1) and examined again in issue 486, where the open question is flame versus matter inside the cap, already with Ryan. Grok's briefs claims were spot-checked against the briefs set; its debrief_spec catch (Part 3) is the most valuable single find in the briefs.

## Part 4: rules that should exist and do not

1. **A precedence order.** When two documents disagree: the rulebook (once it exists) wins; then the playbook for that area; then AGENTS.md; then briefs; HANDOFF.md and LOOP.md win for current status only. Between two of Ryan's rulings, the later date wins, and the Board records the resolution in FINDINGS. (Qwen D1, extended.)
2. **Promotion.** A ruling is not in force until it is carried into the rulebook or the owning playbook. FINDINGS is the inbox, not the law. Every FINDINGS row that changes a rule names the document and section it edits, and the edit lands in the same Board pass as the row.
3. **Sunset conditions.** Any rule resting on a technical fact (a model version, a tool limit, a platform constraint) must name the condition that ends it and who re-checks it. The Board reviews open conditions when a phase closes. (Qwen D2.)
4. **Playbooks supersede older documents on method.** Where a playbook and an older document disagree on method, the playbook wins, and the older section is marked "superseded by (playbook)" at the next edit. (Qwen D3.)
5. **"Frozen" defined.** The browser game is frozen: permitted changes are bug fixes that affect rules correctness and fixes that keep the headless sim running; rendering, UI polish and features are out; the Board rules edge cases. (Qwen D4.)
6. **Missing-reference handling.** If a document references a file or tool an agent cannot reach, the agent notes the reference, states what it could not verify, and continues with what it has; it never invents the missing content. (Qwen D5.)
7. **One canonical home per rule.** Each rule lives in exactly one place; other documents point, they do not restate. When the canonical copy changes, the Board updates or removes the pointers in the same pass. (Qwen D6.)
8. **A number must bring its measurement.** Any numeric gate (a cap, a fraction, a count, a ratio) must record what measurement or observation set it, or be marked ASSUMED with the test that would settle it. R121 already states this principle for performance gates; nothing extends it to design numbers like the breach table, the stern fraction or the take count.
9. **Exceptions live with the rule.** An exception stated in a different document or a later row does not count as stated. If R162 qualifies R132, the qualification is written inside R132's rulebook entry.

## Part 5: the shape of the one rulebook

Precedents worth copying: ADR collections (one decision, one entry, status and supersession links, immutable history); the Rust RFC book (numbered entries, one home, searchable); game design bibles fail where this project fails, by restating rules per department until the copies drift. The shape below is built for docs/RULEBOOK.md as issue 492 specifies.

**Areas, mirroring the playbooks so promotion is mechanical:** Play and campaign; Ships and concepts; Battle presentation (effects, deaths, sound); Screens and interface; The pipeline (build, formats, tools); How the agents work (seats, checks, deliveries); The owner's standing calls. Seven areas, no more.

**One entry per rule, capped at about 120 words, in a fixed form:**
- The rule, stated as what to do (a prohibition is allowed only with its reason attached).
- Why: one sentence, the harm prevented or the effect wanted, in plain words.
- Set by: Ryan, the Board, or an agent, with the date and the FINDINGS row. Ryan's entries carry his words, quoted, beside the rule, so the Board's later batches to him are diffs against his own text, not paraphrases.
- Check: the automatic check or capture that enforces it, named, or "judged by eye".
- Ends when: the sunset condition, for any rule resting on a fact that can change.

**How a ruling gets in:** the Board's loop gains one step. A FINDINGS row is filed as today; before the loop closes the item, the row's rulebook or playbook edit lands in the same pass, and the row records the section it edited. No edit named, no closure. That single discipline kills patterns 1, 2, 4 and 9 in Part 6.

**How it stops rotting:** (a) rules are edited in place; corrections never live only in a later row, the row points at the edited entry. (b) Status is part of the entry: live, provisional (with its settling job named), retired (with its replacement named). (c) A standing sweep job, on the model of this one but smaller, re-reads the rulebook against FINDINGS once a month and reports only drift; issue 496's check-hunt feeds it the code side. (d) Live state (seats, counts, prices, balances) never enters the rulebook; it lives in LOOP.md and the rulebook points there.

## Part 6: where the Board itself is the problem

Be direct, as the brief asks. None of these are about intent; they are habits of writing, and each one produced a named failure in Parts 1 and 2.

1. **Rulings land in FINDINGS and never move.** FINDINGS is an append-only log being used as the rulebook. The documents agents are told to build from (playbooks, grammars, pipeline README) are updated late or never. Exhibits: the nose-gun grammars (Part 1, item 6), the breach table (item 3), the factions redo table (Part 3, item 9).
2. **Corrections are written as new rows instead of edits.** "That wording is WITHDRAWN" (R183) leaves R176 reading as the rule. A reader's landing spot is random, so a correction that does not edit its target corrects nothing for half its readers. (DeepSeek D4.)
3. **Supersession is applied to part of a compound row.** R35 supersedes one clause of R33; the rest reads as standing. Rows bundle unrelated decisions (R5 holds an engine test, an app option and a ship gate in one cell), so later reversals can only point at fragments. One decision per row would fix most of this. (DeepSeek D5, D8.)
4. **Exceptions and qualifications hide in parentheticals and other documents.** The Kimi rule inside a seat-table parenthetical; R121's no-bar ruling inside an AGENTS.md sentence while BLOB_SWEEP states the opposite; R162's exception invisible from R132. The stricter, simpler text always wins with a literal reader, and most readers are literal. (Qwen E2, A12.)
5. **Conditional rules are left standing after the condition resolves.** "Until the engine call" survived the call by weeks. "Until ChatGPT is back" survived ChatGPT. A condition with no owner and no review date is a permanent rule wearing a temporary costume. (Qwen E1; DeepSeek C5.)
6. **Temporary arrangements are written in the same form as permanent law.** Seat maps, helper caps and PC workarounds (R152, R164, R167, R160) are recorded in the same owner-ruling voice as settled design law, so nothing about them signals "this expires". (DeepSeek C5 to C8.)
7. **Provisional values are written with exact numbers.** R175's eight protocol values, labelled PROVISIONAL in the sentence and exact in the table; R131's ASSUMED slew rates inside a "build reference". Numbers detach from their labels the first time they are copied, and they are always copied. (DeepSeek D6; R179 then had to re-settle three of R175's values.)
8. **Facts that change monthly are frozen into rules text.** Model names and versions in the seat map, a price ratio in a parenthetical, a cloud model's quality as a standing ban. The rules file changes rarely and is read as authoritative, so its stalest sentences carry the most weight. (Qwen E4.)
9. **Status tables are not updated when the ruling lands.** factions.md's redo table survived R185 unchanged, still listing dropped routes as open and a dropped ship as the current redraw. A status table that is not edited in the same pass as the ruling becomes a second, wrong source of truth.
10. **The Board grades its own documents.** The rules were written by the Board, restated by the Board, and until this sweep, audited by the Board. Every pattern above is invisible from inside because the writer remembers what the text was meant to say. That is the structural reason issue 495 exists as an outside leg, and it argues for keeping a standing outside sweep (Part 5) rather than treating this as a one-time clean-up.

What the Board does well, for the record: gate line 1 of concepts_round2.md quotes its own withdrawn wording in place, which is exactly the discipline the rest of the corpus needs; ships.md and screens.md are dated, single-purpose and honest about their traps; and the FINDINGS habit of recording Ryan's words verbatim is what made this sweep possible at all. The problem is not the record. The problem is that the record is where the rules stop.
