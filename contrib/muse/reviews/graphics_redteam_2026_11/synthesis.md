Owner's commission (Ryan, Nov 6, 2026): red team the tri and other graphical work as a game dev, a graphics designer, and an agent lead, with the other LLMs involved, hunting artificial constraints while clearly documenting findings, possible limitations, and approaches.

Evidence: PACK.md (65 inventoried numeric constraints, C1 to C65, each with provenance) plus four legs: blind Rey (adopted in full by Argon, one trivial flag), DeepSeek (adopted with six minor flags), Qwen (verdict headline only; the leg is heavily fabricated beneath it, see section 5), Argon review (chair-verified, see the screening header on legs/argon_review.md).

## 1. Verdict

THE STACK HOLDS ONLY WITH REPLACEMENTS. Unanimous across all three writer legs, and the screening does not disturb it: the architecture is sound (per-faction references, the blur test, the nine-cell sweep, banded judgements), but of the 65 numbers that govern the work, only 4 are MEASURED, 11 are DERIVED, 6 are ANCHORED, and 44 are ASSUMED. The stack's rules are mostly conventions wearing the clothing of measurements. Nothing in the red team says the conventions are wrong; it says they are unproven, and it prices the proof for each one.

## 6. Owner questions (chair picks)

Q2. Should the two remaining triangle-denominated rules (the C54 detail share and the C60 station ceiling's tri half) be re-denominated under the same logic that retired the ship triangle caps?
Chair pick: YES, re-denominate both, after the C59 station count and the 207 benchmark give the replacement units something to stand on. Stakes: a retired unit still binds builders and review rounds until it is replaced.

Q3. Should the two bars over the nine-cell grid be reconciled in one sentence: 7 of 9 signs paint, 9 of 9 ships, with the missing cells listed on the sign-off sheet?
Chair pick: YES. Stakes: builders optimise for the last bar quoted, and the gap between the bars burns review rounds.

Q4. Is the hero cap (C11, at most 2 heroes per battle) a design rule or a reporting convention?
Chair pick: A DESIGN RULE (encounter composition, yours as game design), struck from the performance stack, where it never belonged: under the scene gate, performance needs no hero count. Stakes: leaving it unclassified lets a gameplay choice keep masquerading as a performance constraint, which is how the fleet cap happened.

## 8. Owner rulings (Ryan, Nov 6, 2026)

Ryan ruled after the full-context delivery (Nov 6, 2026, about 08:36 CST), in his words: "Agreed. We should use the measurements and test to see where we can scale up for improved graphical fidelity vs scale down for performance. I'm good removing that random share cap. Agreed on fixing that noise cap restraint. Remove the hero cap too. Let's just run all of the tests with uncapped tris to improve the graphics, as well as anything for effects."

Recorded rulings:

R1 (scene gate, C3). Adopted, with Ryan's framing as the standing philosophy: measurements and tests exist to find where fidelity can scale UP and where performance forces scale DOWN. The gate share remains an owner decision taken after the 207 measurement, not the measurement by default.

R2 (detail share, C54). REMOVED outright, stronger than the chair pick (which was re-denomination). Ryan: "I'm good removing that random share cap." D18's 25/40 percent caps bind nothing from this ruling. What detail costs is reported (triangles, draw calls, surfaces, scene-gate ms), not rationed by a share.

R3 (station ceiling tri half, C60). Removed under the same uncapped-tris direction. The station ms figures (C59 target 0.35 ms, C60 ceiling 1.0 ms) stand as assumed reporting figures until the C59 count and a timing run settle them.

R4 (noise gap, C45). The restraint is to be fixed by measurement: the spacing ladder runs (gaps of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6) and the gap is set from what Ryan judges, not kept at 4 by default. Until the ladder runs, the 4-unit figure is provisional.

R5 (hero cap, C11). REMOVED outright, stronger than the chair pick (which was reclassification as a design rule). There is no hero count cap in any stack. Heroes per battle is encounter composition, decided per encounter, with no standing number.

R6 (test direction, new). All tests run with UNCAPPED triangles, oriented to improving graphics: the question every test answers is where fidelity can scale up versus where performance binds. This extends to effects: the same measurement approach applies to the effects work (deaths and weapons, nebulas and storms, planets, max-tier volumetrics), including naming the effects share of frame cost.

Q3 (the two bars, C34/C35) was not separately addressed in Ryan's reply. It is recorded as adopted under his general "Agreed" (7 of 9 signs paint, 9 of 9 ships, missing cells listed on the sign-off sheet) and was flagged to him in the delivery message as the one item resting on the general agreement, correctable on his word.

Filing: R1 and R6 go into issue 207 as an addendum (benchmark framing). R2 to R5, the settlement program, and Q3 go to Opus as one opus-labeled outcome issue for canonization into the #188/#200 standard texts and FINDINGS.
