# #495 blind leg: Rey, written before reading any other leg

Reading set for this leg only: AGENTS.md, the eight playbooks in docs/playbooks/ (PROGRAM.md, ships.md, screens.md, effects.md, deaths_and_hits.md, sound.md, factions.md, concepts_round2.md), and the newest thirty rows of contrib/opus/FINDINGS.md (R161 to R190, plus the older tables the extract carries). Nothing else. Where I name a contradiction, both sources are inside this set.

## The headline

The rulebooks do not agree with each other, and the front door disagrees with itself. The single most load-bearing fact in the project, who runs the Board, is stated three different ways in the documents a newcomer reads first, and all three are stale against the newest FINDINGS rows. Underneath that, the same pattern repeats at ship level: a playbook states the fixed, future state of a rule as if it were already true, while the FINDINGS row that commissioned the fix says the old behaviour is still what the code does. A newcomer who obeys the playbooks to the letter will draw banned ships, assume checks that do not exist, and wait for approvals the Board has already stopped waiting for.

## Rules that read as arbitrary, or carry no reason

1. **Breach anchor base counts (factions.md).** Escort 2, destroyer 3, cruiser 4, capital 6, flagship 8, with break lines 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 or 4. A precise table with no reason recorded anywhere in the set: not play feel, not frame cost, not wreck readability. It reads as a number that was easy to check, which is the exact pattern the sweep exists to catch. Who set it: the Board, in the playbook. Ryan's R164 ruling covers only that the count must vary and follow the faction, not these numbers.
2. **The engine fraction (concepts_round2.md, gate line 3).** The engine group fills "between a quarter and a half of the stern face". A quarter to a half is a wide band stated to a fraction, with no reason. It survives because it is measurable, not because anyone argued a fuller or emptier stern looks wrong.
3. **Camera 170 (factions.md, deaths_and_hits.md, ships.md).** Every readability rule is keyed to "the battle camera (camera 170)" and no document in the set says where 170 came from, what it is measured against, or what would change if it moved. It is the project's most quoted constant and its least explained.
4. **Faction proportions stated as grammar (concepts_round2.md).** Sunward 5:1:0.7, Hegemony 4.5:1:0.5, Hullbreakers 3.5:1:0.9, Chorus 3.5:1:0.9. Two of these are already overridden inside the same reading set (Hegemony is "about 3:1" in R183), and none carries a reason beyond the shape words next to it.
5. **Up to three tries each (concepts_round2.md, "How round 2 is drawn").** A process cap with no reason. It quietly decides how much exploration a faction gets, and it is the kind of rule that turns into "we ran out of tries" instead of "the picture is still wrong".
6. **FLASH_CAP := 3 (FINDINGS R187).** The one hard number in the set with a real reason (WCAG 2.3.1), and it appears in no playbook. The effects playbook, where a builder would look for it, does not state it. A rule that binds every effect lives only in a FINDINGS row and in code.
7. **"Argon is blocked since Nov 5" (AGENTS.md, title line).** Stated with no reason and no pointer. A newcomer cannot tell whether Argon is a person, a model or a tool, or what would unblock it.

## Contradictions, with both sources

1. **Who runs the Board.** AGENTS.md's title block and seat map: "Board seat moved to Fable 5.2 on Nov 9, R152", and the table says "Fable 5.2 for the Nov 9 and 10 design window; Opus for the build-out, R169". PROGRAM.md seats Fable as the Board throughout. R180 (newest rows): Ryan used the final Fable reset, no Fable helpers at all, Opus worker threads only, and the Board thread itself moves to Opus once key items close. Three statements, three answers, and the two documents a newcomer is told to read first (AGENTS.md: "Start with HANDOFF.md section 0 and FINDINGS.md", then PROGRAM.md) are the stale ones.
2. **How many Fable helpers may run.** PROGRAM.md allows at most two. R166 said one at a time. R167 replaced that with two, sixteen minutes later. R180 says none. The rule changed three times in five days and only the FINDINGS rows track it; the playbook still carries the middle version.
3. **Who drives the PC when Codex is parked.** AGENTS.md's seat map: "until then Opus PC threads". PROGRAM.md: until then a Fable helper drives the PC queue. Two different stand-ins for the same seat, in the two front-door documents. (Both are moot since R170 un-parked Codex, which neither sentence reflects: AGENTS.md's job board section does record the un-parking, so AGENTS.md contradicts its own seat map twenty lines apart.)
4. **Breach anchors: fixed in the playbook, unfixed in the record.** factions.md states the scaling as present fact and says check_export.py "reads the expected count from the hull's class and faction (issue 363), never from a constant". R164 says the opposite about the present: check_export.py "enforces six on every hull", and scaling "goes into the hard point contract job #363". The playbook describes the destination as the current state. Anyone trusting it believes a protection exists that does not.
5. **The Hegemony bow, inside one file.** concepts_round2.md's amended gate line 1: the ion weapon "is a recessed emitter trench across a blunt, armoured bow face. No prongs, no barrels." The same file's Hegemony grammar, further down: "The ion cannon is the pair of prongs at the bow", and variation H1 still carries "the ion prongs". The amendment was pasted into the gate and never pushed down into the grammar it governs. A brief written from the grammar section produces the banned ship.
6. **The Sunward lance, inside one file.** Amended gate line 1: the lance is "an ember-lit emitter recessed between the two halves of a split prow; nothing sticks out past the prow tips". The Sunward grammar in the same file: "The lance is one long weapon on the centre line at the bow". Same fault as the Hegemony bow, same file.
7. **The Eclipse ring.** concepts_round2.md variation E1: "The ring faces forward and never lies flat." R183: the redo "lays the ring flat: the ring IS the ship", because an upright ring is a bar from above. factions.md's redo table agrees with R183. E1 was never corrected.
8. **Chains on Hullbreakers.** factions.md's Hullbreaker sheet lists "Chains between hull sections" as shape language and its death rule has chains holding the pieces for a moment. R185 puts into gate line 11 that nothing hangs off any ship, "no chains, no cages", naming A11's hanging cages as a tell to redraw. A chain strung between sections is defensible either way, which is exactly the problem: the two documents leave the reader to guess which chains survive.
9. **The Glasswright redo table vs the newest rows.** factions.md's concept redo table says the three fresh routes are The Unfinished, the Mirror Deck and the Racing Hull, all open. R185 drops The Unfinished ("a satellite") and drops the third route (shown to Ryan as the Racing Hull), and settles the direction as the Mirror Deck made prismatic. The table also lists Hullbreakers as "being redrawn (round 5, A10)"; R185 drops A10 and makes A11 the base. The playbook's own status table is two rounds behind the FINDINGS rows bound into the same reading set.
10. **Merging without Ryan vs waiting for Ryan.** R167: "no merge waits on Ryan; he still gets the options sheet before a new look is built". R165, two hours earlier, on the campaign map: PR #349 "merges only after this build, Muse's three conditions and his yes on the real captures". R167 relaxes that consequence in general terms; neither document says plainly which rule a given merge follows. PROGRAM.md's "Options first, then build" adds a third phrasing. A newcomer cannot tell when a merge needs Ryan's yes.
11. **The outside check.** AGENTS.md's seat map gives Rey the independent checker seat: "nobody grades their own work". R162 rules that a tools-only pull request "may merge on the Board's own diff review while Muse is idle; the outside check stays requested". The exception is reasonable and invisible from the document that states the rule. Exceptions that live only in FINDINGS rows are how rules quietly stop existing.
12. **The Kimi sentence.** AGENTS.md's seat map still says Kimi "was slower and 50x the price per job on the API". R164 corrects the record at length: the subscription tier was never tried, and the 50x figure describes one pay-as-you-go tier only. The correction never reached the file that repeats the claim. This is the clearest case in the set of a corrected fact staying wrong in the document people actually read.

## What a newcomer gets wrong by obeying the documents to the letter

- Draws a Hegemony flagship with bow prongs and a Sunward flagship with a long bow lance, from concepts_round2.md's own grammar sections, and fails gate line 1 of the same file.
- Stands the Eclipse ring cruiser's ring upright (E1), producing the bar-from-above that R183 already diagnosed and banned.
- Puts chains and hanging cages on a Hullbreaker from factions.md's shape language, and fails the R185 naval pass.
- Assumes breach anchors already scale by class and faction, because factions.md says the check reads them that way, and never learns the live check still demands six on every hull.
- Staffs the Board as Fable, allows two Fable helpers, and holds GPU work for a Fable stand-in, following AGENTS.md and PROGRAM.md, a full day after R180 ended all three.
- Builds the R175 protocol values as settled numbers. R175 marks them provisional in its own text, but they sit in FINDINGS as a neat table, and R179's changes (three of eight) are in a different row a newcomer may never reach.
- Waits for Ryan's yes before merging a look, or merges without it, depending on which of R165, R167 and PROGRAM.md they happened to read last.
- Treats the Oct 25 and 26 tables bound into FINDINGS (the F, S, B, D, K and X rows, all about the retired Babylon and JS build: battle.js, ships3d.js, hulls_hi) as live rules of the Godot project. Nothing in the file marks them as a closed era. They sit in the same tables as R190.

## What is healthy, for contrast

ships.md and screens.md show the format working: dated, single purpose, checks an agent can run, traps each tied to a FINDINGS row, and a worked example. concepts_round2.md's gate line 1 does one thing exactly right: it quotes the withdrawn wording ("the forward gun is the largest object on the bow"), says who withdrew it and why, in place. If every amended rule carried its own withdrawal note in the document that holds the rule, half the contradictions above could not have happened. factions.md's redo table is the right idea (one status table, the reference picture, Ryan's words, what is still to fix) let down only by not being updated when R185 landed.

## My shape instinct, before the other legs

One rulebook will fail the same way these documents did if a ruling can land in FINDINGS and never move. The fix is procedural, not editorial: a rule is not in force until the rulebook carries it, FINDINGS rows are the inbox rather than the law, and every ruling that changes a rule names the rulebook section it edits in the same breath. I will test that against the other legs when they land.
