## The Board's six questions

1. EXPORT: verified. check_export reads every point through the node tree and refuses each named case: a kit-marked turret with no turret_<n> node of its own ("the export merged it into the ship mesh"), a muzzle of a turret hanging on the ship node, a pivot off the mesh (measured against the ship's triangles with the export tolerance), a missing extra, a rest aim aft and a rest outside its arc, and, through the contract's turret_problems, a turret with no turning mesh or no muzzle. export7 itself also dies on a turret with no turning mesh under it, so that case cannot reach the check silently. The pre-560 export of the fixture is refused for exactly the four claimed problems (turA and turB merged, both muzzles on the ship node), which exercises the first two refusals end to end; the no-mesh refusal is exercised by the contract selftest's refused copies, and the pivot and rest-aft refusals rest on the check code the chair read. One more rule worth naming: only hp_w muzzles may hang under a turret; anything else there is refused.
2. THE TURN: verified. The traverse is a cubic segment that starts at the turret's current speed and ends at rest; _traverse_time sizes each segment so the average rate is at most TURN_RATE (150 degrees a second) and the acceleration at most TURN_ACC (900), with a floor and a cap on duration. The reported peak of 225 degrees a second is not a violation: a rest-to-rest cubic peaks at exactly 1.5 times its average, and the code comment says so. The reach is computed once per hull by walking both ways from rest in 2 degree steps with the edges bisected to about a tenth of a degree, testing each barrel's line (offset by its radius on four sides) against the hull and every part that does not turn; turret_yaw additionally clamps a non-full turret's pose into its reach, so even a mid-segment reversal holds at the edge rather than swinging through. turret_check's output (read directly by the chair) shows the behaviour: 20 shots at 10 bearings, every shot starting 0.0000 from the turned muzzle node, out-of-reach targets held at the exact edge (turA at its +-120 arc ends, turB at +72.0 and -257.7 behind its mast), no frame past the reach, step changes of at most 0.24 degrees. The shot leaves the turned muzzle because fx_weapons fires each turret shot at T0 plus its lead with the muzzle point muzzle_at computed at the arrival yaw, and turret_release holds the aim through the last shot plus TURN_HOLD (0.9 s) before training home; the pose is a pure function of the effect clock, so hit pause and slow motion move the turrets and their shots together.
3. FIXED GUNS: verified. A mount gets an aim, a lead and a release only when its point carries a turret index; points are tagged with their turret only when they hang under a turret_<n> node, and casemates and broadside batteries stay in the ship mesh with no such node. Fixed guns' mounts therefore take the same path they took before this PR, with lead 0. turret_check states fixed guns never move, and the code leaves them no way to.
4. RULES: unchanged, and the comparison is enough, for a stronger reason than the counts. No rules file is in the PR (the chair verified the 576-only file list against PR 570's head: the deltas are the export, the fx look, the two checks and the docs). The volley consumes the rules event's shots, hit flags and targets unmodified; the only changed outputs are presentation timings: impacts, fires and done are computed from the lead-delayed fire times, so a turret volley's presentation runs longer by its traverse (bounded by TURN_MAX, 2.4 s), and a muzzles field is added for the checks. Nothing that decides a rules outcome reads those timings back; when a hull has no mounts the battle already keeps its own timing, which is the same separation RULEBOOK 7.4 states. So the hit and shot count comparison is a fair summary here, because the code shows there is nothing else the counts could be hiding. The one real effect to keep in view is pacing, not rules: battles against turreted ships will take longer on screen per volley.
5. COST: verified as claimed. turret_check's own output line gives turret step 6.8 microseconds a frame in the sandbox (the chair read it in the file, not only in the report); the report's PC figure is 7.4, and two extra draw calls per turreted ship with no new triangles. turret_step itself is a transform write per turret per frame plus finished-segment pruning, which matches that scale. The missing scene-gate rerun is note 1 above.
6. ATTACH CHECK: a fair fix. attach_check's invariant is that an authored point lies on the ship the triangles describe, and its triangles are the ship at rest. A turned muzzle measured directly would fail spuriously: a barrel trained outboard has its tip past the hull's edge in empty space, which is where a barrel tip is supposed to be. The change turns the point back through the turret's own transform pair (turret_xf at the recorded yaw, inverted, into the rest frame) and measures that, under its own row label so the output shows what was done. What it gives up is stated in note 3: the turned position itself. That position is covered, by turret_check's 0.0000 start proof and by the clearance walk that keeps the barrel line off the ship at every yaw; between the two checks nothing the Board asked about is unmeasured. The chair also checked the failure this could have hidden and did not find it: the turned-back point uses the same yaw the volley recorded for that muzzle, not a recomputed one, so a wrong yaw in the service would show up as a wrong rest point here or a wrong start point in turret_check.
