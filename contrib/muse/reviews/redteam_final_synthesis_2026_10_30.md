# Red team audit: final cross-persona synthesis (Oct 30, 2026)

Chair synthesis across all nine seats (gamedev, player, producer, engineer, narrative, audio, uiux, vfx, qa). Prongs in hand per seat: blind Rey, DeepSeek, Argon (3 of 4). Qwen prong: PENDING FOLD-IN for all nine seats (daily cap at send on Oct 30; each persona synthesis will be addended when the Qwen legs land, and this final synthesis addended after). Pin: main head b3a3b802. Chair adjudications in the red team tracker govern where legs conflicted with them.

## Overall verdict

The game underneath the program is better than the program around it. Every seat that read code found well-made parts: a battle engine with a genuinely best-in-class information layer, a Godot port whose engineering (atomic saves, synchronizer, parity method) is ahead of its documentation, an effects craft with real transient skill, a sound engine with proper bus craft. Every seat also found the same failure at the joints: the composition layer is missing. Systems fold additively with no caps, specifications contradict each other and the code, ruled decisions sit unbuilt while content piles onto the structures they were meant to fix, and the audit apparatus (816 contrib files, 64 MB, against 8,947 lines of browser game code) has outrun the integration lane it feeds. The single sentence version: freeze the audits, sequence the backlog, ship the playable run, and let play data, not another audit round, arbitrate the open tuning questions.

Three structural facts drive most of the detail. First, the cutover's hardest problems are undecided, not unbuilt: the port's code says browser saves are not carried over (R33) while the program's fixtures and blocker register assume an importer, and no transport between browser storage and the desktop build exists in either direction. Second, the ruled-but-unbuilt list is now the most expensive list in the project: the Q5 loss-debrief weakness reveal, the Q7 offer fix's duo naming, the Oct 27 text remediation, and the telemetry instrumentation behind three "revisit with play data" rulings are all decisions already made waiting on implementation. Third, several prior verdicts this program relied on did not survive contact with the pin, including two of the chair's own (the telemetry spec's clash join and the wave 5 patch pack's first section). The overturned table below is the audit's receipt.

## Ryan-lane decisions (story, stakes, and cost up front)

1. **Freeze the audit apparatus until the Godot run is playable end to end.** Story: the audit tree is 816 files and 64 MB against an 8,947-line game; wave 4 drafted variant text for events that fail your free-exit ruling; the tree assemblies wait on systems the gamedev seat says are not composed; and the scarcest resource in the program is your own time, as playtest source, sign-off gate, and ruling authority. Stakes: every further audit round produces findings faster than the single integration lane can land them, and two programs in a row have had their headline verdicts overturned by the next round, which means the marginal audit is now finding errors in audits. Cost: freezing costs the discovery of whatever the next round would have found; the findings suite and the playtest kit remain live, so defects found by playing still get filed. The chair's recommendation is the freeze, with the already-approved loader and test workstream as the exception since it builds gates rather than findings.
2. **R33: does browser progression carry over at cutover, or does everyone start over?** Story: the port's code states twice that browser saves are not carried over; the wave 5 fixtures and the saves BLOCKER register assume an importer; no ruling document records the decision, and no transport exists between browser storage and the desktop build. Stakes: this is the difference between building an importer (plus an export path in a frozen build, plus the unlockAll strip-or-ratify rule) and writing one honest sentence in the release notes. Cost: deciding no-migration today costs almost nothing, because the only browser profiles in existence are your own test profiles; deciding after any external player touches the browser build costs their progression. The undecided state is the expensive one, because the port is being built around an assumption its own code contradicts.
3. **Chorus song: enforce r8 or amend it?** Story: the bible bans song for the Chorus three times ("no faction sings; the Chorus speaks as we"; "Never sings"; "No faction sings"), and your approved Chorus learning fiction is the flesh idiom. Song persists in a shipped admiral name (Mending Choir, in data.js, predating r8) and in Oct 29 drafting drift (firstrun verse lines, tier 3 texts). Stakes: enforcing means renaming a shipped admiral and re-voicing the drafted Chorus text; amending means the bible's most-repeated Chorus rule was never load-bearing and every future drafter needs to know which. Cost: enforcement is a bounded rename and rewrite pass; amendment is one bible edit plus accepting the drift as canon. The chair's lean is enforce: three bans is not an accident, and the flesh idiom you approved is the stronger identity.
4. **The Crusade arc's borrowed register: rewrite the arc or amend the bible?** Story: the Crusade arc's centerpiece is a saint's hand relic that the player later burns; the bible's prohibition list bans saints, relics, beacons, lanterns, cathedrals, and singing for the Crusade; the incorporation review kept the motif deliberately as "the one borrowed register" on a reviewer's stated preference, without amending the bible. Stakes: the arc is the flagship faction's story spine; shipping it as-is makes the bible advisory, and every later arc dispute reopens. Cost: a rewrite of one arc's Beat 1 and Beat 3 in the approved chalk-and-Roll, debt idiom; amendment costs one bible edit and a precedent. The chair's lean is enforce the bible and rewrite: a prohibition list with a flagship-sized exception is not a list.
5. **Chorus: build the feed/grow system, or admit the exception?** Story: the Chorus is the swarm faction with a ruled CP discount meant to express as more hulls, and it has a stat block but no system: nothing in battle or campaign feeds, grows, or converts for it, while every other faction's definer is implemented. Stakes: the faction shape verdict (SHAPES PARTIAL) cannot close for the Chorus without either the system or a documented exception; the tier 3 Chorus texts already write as if feeding exists. Cost: the system is real design and code work in the battle layer; the exception costs one paragraph and a pass over the Chorus texts to stop promising it.
6. **Where should a run die?** Story: measured attrition says sector 2 elites are the wall (1.32 ships lost per fight, 69.9 percent bot win rate, the plurality of sampled runs ending in sector 2), while bosses run long rather than lethal (6.2 to 7.9 rounds at 82 to 92 percent battle win rates) despite having their own objective table by your ruling. Stakes: the roguelike contract you ruled (about 80 percent losses, run 1 near impossible) is currently delivered by mid-run elites, not by the set pieces; whether that is the intended shape decides where tuning effort goes after the campaign Monte Carlo lands. Cost: no code yet; this is a direction call that the tuning grids workstream will price. The chair's lean: keep the elite wall, make sector 3 bosses shorter and more lethal, so the run's story is survive the grind, fear the flagship.
7. **How much should a Nemesis weakness be worth?** Story: measured at the pin, knowing a weakness pays back about 4 points of win rate (not statistically significant) against an adaptation worth up to 27.7 points by sector 3; the intel economy you ruled (win reveals, loss reveals per Q5, intel events) trades in a currency worth roughly a seventh of the opposing one. Stakes: if weakness value stays this low, the reveal system is flavor; raising it (stronger weakness effects, or reveal quality) makes intel a real build decision. Cost: a data-side tuning change to weakness effect sizes, measurable with the existing harness; the Q5 build (master list item 3) should land first so the loss path exists to be valued.
8. **The run 2 veteran AI step: announce it, soften it, or keep it hidden?** Story: from your second run onward the enemy AI permanently upgrades (target revalidation, flak pre-emption, ability use, focus fire) with no fiction, patch note, or debrief line; the step keys on runs played, so it lands exactly when a returning player is forming their model of the game. Stakes: hidden, it reads as the game cheating or as the player's own skill regressing; announced in the generational fiction (the enemy learns too, which your learning-frame ruling already implies), it becomes the frame's best evidence. Cost: announcing is one debrief or framing line; softening means re-keying the step to depth instead of run count, a small code change with balance follow-on. The chair's lean: announce it in fiction.
9. **Telemetry: full spec during the port, or minimal logger first?** Story: the filed spec is good and uninstrumented; three of your rulings end with "revisit with play data," and the current event set has no nemesis_meeting, weakness_revealed, regroup, or tree_pick events at all. Stakes: without any instrumentation, your first playtest produces impressions where the rulings ask for data; with the full spec up front, port time goes to event plumbing before the run is playable. Cost: a minimal logger (runs, battles, meetings, regroup choices, written as standalone JSONL outside the save ring) is days; the full spec is a port-phase project. The chair's lean: minimal logger before the first playable, full spec after, with the clash-join amendment (lineage, not single run) applied either way.
10. **Extend the free-exit guarantee to pay-or-suffer events?** Story: your ruling covers fight-start events and haven is its sole violation. Six other events (storm, reactor, squall, mutiny, scout, saboteur) offer only a paid exit, and when the player cannot pay, the exit disables and the consequence is mandatory (storm: 10 percent hull damage when broke). Stakes: extending keeps the guarantee's spirit (no event traps the player) at the price of some resource pressure; not extending keeps the pressure and a broke-player trap. Cost: one zero-cost leave option per event plus an eval re-run. The chair's lean: extend.
11. **Browser build music: leave it silent until cutover?** Story: the browser build has no music system at all (one synth ambient bed and a roomtone loop); the Godot build has the full adaptive system integrated. Your play sessions happen in the silent build. Stakes: porting music into a frozen build breaks the freeze for the layer you will hear most; leaving it means your playtest impressions of battle feel are formed without the game's strongest mood channel. Cost: the Godot cues and crossfade logic do not exist in Babylon form, so a browser port is new code, not a copy. The chair's lean: leave it silent, and discount browser playtest feel accordingly.
12. **Mid-battle persistence in the port: replay, or save the battle?** Story: the browser build never saves battle state, so closing mid-battle restarts the fight at full strength, free and unlimited; inherent to replay persistence, and the port is where the model gets chosen rather than inherited. Stakes: a zero-cost mulligan sits against the permanent-loss contract for any player who finds it, and players find it by closing a laptop. Cost: replay costs nothing; saving battle state is real battle-layer work and changes abandonment and Nemesis meeting behavior. The chair's lean: keep replay for the first playable, decide with abandon-rate data once the telemetry path counts it.
13. **Plan the playtest session like it is on the critical path, because it is.** Story: your play-through is the sign-off gate for the playable battle, the visual verdicts in three seats are explicitly reserved to it, and the wave 5 sign-off kit exists to make one session answer the most questions. Stakes: the Godot program's next milestone cannot close without it, and an unplanned session answers whatever it happens to touch. Cost: one scheduled session with the kit's checklist, against weeks of sequencing that assume it happened. This is a scheduling item, not a nag: the chair's ask is that when the playable battle lands, the session gets a date in the same conversation.
