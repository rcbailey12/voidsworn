## 3. Amendments to the blind set (chair rulings on leg disagreements)

A1. Fall_final timing (blind 121-12 Block 2 and Qwen Gap 4 corrected; Argon catch, chair-verified). The adopted table text settles this: the fall_final lines are spoken by the commander as he rises ("Then I rise once more. One final fight, as archived."; "I rise once more, flagship first, for the final fight."), and the fall lines describe early destruction ("The flagship falls early. The archive notes it and sends the next copy forward."). Corrected check: a destruction before the last sector speaks a fall line; the last-sector destruction that triggers the revive speaks a fall_final line; the second, permanent last-sector death does not speak a fall_final line (he does not rise again). Both writer legs had mapped fall_final to the permanent death; that mapping is cut.

A2. Adaptation cap formula (blind 121-6 and 121-8, labelling). The pack names the cap's variables (RUN.sector, and for fast adapters beaten + scars) but not the algebra; the blind leg's max(0, RUN.sector - 1) form is a reconstruction and is marked INFERRED wherever the numeric example (cap 3 at sector 2 with beaten 1 and scars 1) is used. The governing content of the checks is the invariant: the cap is computed from the active commander's own beaten and scars, so a cross-commander mix-up changes the result. Whoever runs the check confirms the formula against campaign_run.js:294 first; if the shipped formula differs from the reconstruction, the invariant still governs and the example is corrected, not the check.

A3. Clash check cases (Qwen 121-4 corrected). Qwen's cases use trait-side names (swift, boarders) as adaptations. Adaptations are keyed shields, pd, armor, evasion, aim, special (NEM_ADAPT, data.js:437). The cases stand only when re-expressed with real adaptation ids and the counter pairs the shipped clash tests exercise. The blind leg's 121-7, which works through "the adaptation c that counters w" as pinned by the tests on file, governs.

A4. Who adapts on the sector advance (Qwen 121-5 inverted, cut). On the advance, the commander who was active in the sector just ended adapts, from his own seen data; the incoming commander has no seen data yet and does not adapt on entry. The blind leg's 121-6 governs.

A5. Scars shape in constructed saves (Qwen 120-7 and 121-6 corrected). Qwen constructs an old save with scars = ['fled'] and expects the same shape after a flee. The shipped shape (NEM_CARRY, and the panel's "scarred 1x" rendering) treats scars as the code's own record, increased by exactly 1 per scarring event with the scar recorded through nemScar; a branch name in an array is not the shipped shape. Constructed saves and expected states use the shipped representation, as the blind leg's 120-7 (scars: 1) and 121-9 (increases by exactly 1, epithet gained) do.

A6. Table structure (Qwen 121-8(d) wording). fall and fall_final are sibling keys on each Eclipse persona, not sub-keys of fall. The blind leg's 121-11(c) governs.

A7. Compounding counter (Qwen 122-4 cut as self-contradictory). The check asserts in one step that the Chorus counter does not change under another active commander and in the next that it increments on any player loss. The blind leg's 122-3 governs: the fed counter grows only from losses in that commander's own meetings, starts at 0 when he is created whatever happened in sector 1, and caps at 8. The synthesis phrase "grows from the player's losses anywhere" is read in its shipped sense (the Nemesis feeding on the player's losses to him, as the on-file Chorus scenario and the panel's "Fed on your losses" line use it); section 4 proposes a clarifying edit so the next reader does not have to re-derive this.

A8. Before and after captures (Qwen 122-6 insufficient alone). The Done-when phrase means pre-build and post-build captures of the panel, as the blind leg's 122-7 requires (files in the PR, labelled by state). Within-run panel states are useful evidence and are already pinned functionally by 122-4 and 122-5, but they do not by themselves satisfy the clause.

A9. Enumeration tolerances (DeepSeek 120-2 rejected). DeepSeek's plus or minus 108 per cell takes the chair simulation's largest observed deviation and uses it as the pass bound. An observed maximum from one sample is not a statistical bound; at 1.91 sigma per cell a correct draw fails the matrix with near certainty over enough verification runs. The blind leg's sigma-derived tolerances govern. DeepSeek's pooled return tolerance (plus or minus 600, 4 sigma) is consistent with, and looser than, the blind leg's per-faction bands; no conflict remains once the per-faction bands govern.

A10. Sectors beyond 3 (DeepSeek Gap 1 and its Q1 killed). The premise fails on the record: a run has three sectors and sector 3 is the last (the R49 comment at campaign_run.js:311, "before the last sector"; the synthesis's sector 3 deciding fight), and the run 5 finale is that run's SP3 meeting under R85 Q4, not a sector 4 or 5 event. There is no sectors 4 and 5 mapping to specify, and DeepSeek's quotation of R85 Q4 as leaving "SP assignments and sector alignment" open misquotes the ruling. The real ambiguity underneath (story point index versus sector index) is closed by edit G5 in section 4 and tested by 120-6.

A11. NEM_VOICE keying (DeepSeek 121-7 statement cut). The table is keyed by all six factions, the player's own included, because which faction is the player's varies per run; the blind leg's 121-10 (an entry for every faction key in NEM_FAC) governs. DeepSeek's statement that the top-level keys are the five non-player factions is wrong.

A12. Chorus banned-word list (DeepSeek Q3 cut). The adopted rule is the table's own header rule: no song or choir words. DeepSeek's extended INFERRED deny list (sing, melody, harmony, tune, hymn) is not adopted: the table was adopted as-is under R101, and widening the ban after adoption would fail adopted lines with no ruling behind it. The blind leg's 121-11(b) governs (word scan for song and choir, plus the pronoun scan).

A13. Escape counter question (DeepSeek Gap 2 answered from the record, not an owner question). The synthesis text and the blind leg's 122-2 agree: the counter counts commanders who escaped (the fled branch, or a flagship destroyed before sector 3), at most two per run, one per SP1 and SP2 slot. A returning commander who flees again at SP3 through the fled branch does not increment it (his escape was already counted, and SP3 outcomes are terminal); a sector 3 death or revive never increments it. DeepSeek's Run 2 step scripting a fresh sector 3 escape is cut; the blind runs govern.

A14. Enumeration harness (Qwen 120-1 not the acceptance test). Qwen's isolated-draw script tests a draw entry point in isolation, which may not be the shipped run-start path; the acceptance enumeration runs full run starts through the title flow (blind 120-1), matching how the chair enumeration in the assignment synthesis was produced. Qwen's determinism check (its 120-2, same seed twice gives the same assignment) is retained and folds into the blind leg's drawn-once check 120-4. Qwen's stream-shift protocol (its 120-3) is consistent with the blind parity process and adds nothing beyond it.

A15. Clock reset between sectors (note on Qwen 121-2). Its sector 2 pacing block moves from an Eclipse sector 1 to a Crusade sector 2 without stating that the sector 1 meeting reset the clock. Under edit G4 the meeting reset is explicit; the blind leg's fresh-run blocks in 121-3 avoid the ambiguity and govern.

## 4. Gaps in the issue texts, with exact proposed edits (for Opus to apply)

G1 (issue 120, migration clause). The clause does not say how the remaining story point entries of a migrated run are produced. Proposed edit: replace "old saves migrate (an existing RUN.nem becomes the SP-current entry)" with "old saves migrate (an existing RUN.nem becomes the SP-current entry, and the remaining story point entries are drawn at load from the run RNG under the same no-consecutive-repeat rule, with the migrated hunter fixed in the current story point's slot)". Matches owner question Q2's pick.

G2 (issue 121, lessons storage). Lessons have no existing home in the shipped code, and issue 122's panel clause depends on the storage existing. Proposed edit: replace "debrief lessons attach to the commander" with "debrief lessons are recorded on the commander met, as a per-commander list of the lessons taught by lost meetings against that commander, holding the NEM_VOICE lesson text for the weakness taught".

G3 (issue 122, compounding scope). Proposed edit: replace "a per-commander compounding counter" with "a per-commander compounding counter where the behaviour compounds (the Chorus fed counter, capped at 8); factions without a compounding behaviour carry no such counter, and the existing per-commander riches and honed fields are not re-counted". Matches owner question Q3's pick.

G4 (issue 121, clock storage). The shipped code stores the clock on the commander object; the clause says run-level without saying where it lives or who resets it, and a per-commander storage that resets identically would pass every behavioural check while violating the wording. Proposed edit: replace "one run-level clock paced by the active commander's beh.clock" with "one run-level clock, stored on the run and not on any commander, paced by the active commander's beh.clock; a meeting resets it to 0 and clears any pending strike, whoever the active commander is".

G5 (issues 121 and 122, index pin). Proposed edit: in issue 121's Done-when, replace the first occurrence of "the active commander" with "the active commander (the registry entry for RUN.nemAssign[RUN.sector - 1])", and in issue 122's Done-when, replace "the current sector's assigned hunter" with "the current sector's assigned hunter (RUN.nemAssign[RUN.sector - 1])".

G6 (assignment synthesis wording, for the record). In synthesis section 4, "Chorus grows from the player's losses anywhere" should read "Chorus grows from the player's losses in meetings with the Chorus commander" when Opus next touches that file, so the compounding scope in A7 does not have to be re-derived. This is a wording clarification of shipped behaviour, not a behaviour change.

No other conflicts found: the three Done-when blocks are consistent with R85, R101 and R106 once G1 to G5 land.

## 5. Coverage notes and traps

Traps: all seven pack traps are caught by named checks (stream coupling by the invariant-first parity rule, lazy creation order by 120-5 and 120-6, the SP3 draw by 120-3 and by 120-5's return seed, indexing by 120-6 and G5, old saves by 120-7 to 120-9, parity drift by the re-copy and file-diff steps, text by 121-10 to 121-12). Four additional traps from the blind leg are adopted: the battle snapshot's derived met must not be wired as the met-this-sector flag (negative control in 121-2); a registry keyed by faction holds exactly 2 commanders on a return, not 3, and any code assuming one object per story point breaks the return fiction (120-5, 122-5); half-step clock arithmetic false-fails integer-assuming ports (121-3's exact sequences); the 40-entry signature cap makes telemetry history lossy by design, and checks must expect the loss (122-1).

## 6. Owner questions (three, with chair picks)

Q1. Should the full enumeration (120-1: 120,000 run starts) gate the PR for issue 120, or run once as a recorded verification with the PR gated on the exact constraints (120-2, which hold at any run count) plus the scenario suite? Chair pick: run it once as a recorded verification for issue 120, and gate the PR on the exact constraints plus the scenario suite. Reason: a statistical tolerance inside a merge gate invites flaky blocks, while the structural constraints are exact at any sample size. Cost of the other option: 120,000 title-flow run starts on every push (the dominant runtime of the check suite, per-run minutes on every PR iteration, INFERRED), plus a standing flake risk from the INFERRED tolerances.

Q2. For a migrated legacy save, should the non-current story point entries be drawn at load (edit G1) or left undrawn until each sector begins? Chair pick: draw at load. Reason: one invariant ("RUN.nemAssign is complete from the moment a run exists, new or migrated") that every reader of the assignment can rely on, instead of a second fill path with its own timing. Cost of the other option: one extra fill path in the build, extra scenario blocks at each sector boundary of a migrated run (a one-time test build on top of 120-7 and 120-8), and every reader of the assignment tolerating holes until the last sector starts.

Q3. Should the per-commander compounding counter be scoped to the Chorus fed counter only (edit G3), or should riches and honed also be re-expressed as compounding counters for uniformity? Chair pick: Chorus fed only, per the assignment synthesis, which names Chorus growth as the commander-specific compounding behaviour; riches and honed already persist per commander through NEM_CARRY and are shown by the panel. Cost of the other option: a wider telemetry schema and extra counter cases in 122-3 and 122-9 (a one-time test build plus longer scenario runs per iteration), spent re-counting state that already exists, with no ruling behind the wider scope.

The other legs' remaining questions were answered from the record and are not owner questions: creation timing (the issue text says lazy creation, and the checks assert an empty registry at run start), the escape counter's counting unit (A13), the Chorus word list (A12), and the panel check's scope (content presence is checked; layout is owner-judged from the captures, per A8 and the coverage notes).
