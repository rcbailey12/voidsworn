Validation synthesis, Oct 28 program addendum (cross-model validation of the learning-frame approaches and the six faction learning cycles, commissioned after the progression synthesis). Chair synthesis of the DeepSeek, Qwen, and Argon (Gemini) blind legs, which judged a design pack stating the designer's premises as constraints (about 80% losses by design; run 1 near impossible; first completion a generational achievement around runs 4 to 6), the systems as built, the approach set (learning frame, horizontal-first shape, failure-as-tuition debrief, Heat reframing), all six faction cycles, and seven load-bearing lore facts (L1 to L7). Findings are observations for Opus and the designer, not orders.

## Verdict: SOUND WITH CONDITIONS, with one cycle NOT READY

Scoreboard: DeepSeek NOT READY; Qwen SOUND WITH CONDITIONS; Argon NOT READY (harshest; five of six cycles rated NOT READY). The chair's verdict sides closest to Qwen on the set as a whole, with DeepSeek and Argon on the single worst item: the frame, the power shape, and five of six cycles stand with conditions. The Hegemony cycle (F2) does not stand at all and must be rewritten. The premises themselves survived intact: no leg refuted the loss rate or run 1's near-impossibility as design; every serious attack landed on implementation language, milestones, or fiction mechanics, which is what validation is for.

## Unanimous findings (3 of 3 legs)

1. F2 violates L7. The Registry ingesting telemetry from destroyed task forces and pushing doctrine to all fleets "overnight" requires faster-than-light communication the lore forbids (exceptions: a fleet's own battle network, and the Eclipse Archive). All three legs quoted the same lines. The cycle as written is dead; the rewrite direction all three legs converge on is courier-speed doctrine: telemetry travels home physically or at light speed, doctrine applies where and when it arrives.
2. F2 and F6 are one mechanism in two coats until F2 is fixed. Both are "central records in, updated fleet out," instant, total, lossless. Eclipse is the lore-legal version (the Archive exemption is written into L7); Hegemony is the illegal duplicate. The L7 rewrite is also the differentiation: once Hegemony learning moves at courier speed and lands unevenly, it stops resembling versioning.
3. The Glasswright debrief problem. The procession (settled with the designer Oct 28) survives every leg on story tempo, but all three legs converge on the debrief: A3 promises the next attempt inherits what this attempt taught, and the lagged queue means the next Glasswright fleet may inherit nothing from the fleet just played. Argon adds that the Nemesis also adapts mechanically from run N to N+1, so two systems contradict a literal "not the fate of the fleet just played" reading. Chair adjudication: the progression mechanics (renown, mastery, sync) are immediate for every faction; only the fiction carrier lags. The fix is a per-faction debrief honesty rule (below), not a change to the procession.
4. Debrief honesty has a second, general edge: when a loss crosses no threshold and buys nothing, claiming a "named lesson" is false. All three legs found this independently. Qwen supplied the remedy the chair adopts as a proposal: let the debrief sometimes say the honest thing, in the Crusade's debt idiom: the attempt taught nothing new, and the debt stands. Rarity would make the real lessons land harder.

## Majority findings (2 of 3)

5. The Memory track resists the frame (DeepSeek, Argon; Qwen dissenting). Core sync as written is a patron recognizing and trusting the player's line: favor, not faction learning. Qwen held that a patron-petitioner relationship maps fine onto all six factions. Chair adjudication: both are right about different things. As a relationship it maps; as LEARNING it does not, because the subject of the knowledge is the core, not the faction. The frame's other two tracks are knowledge the faction owns. The synthesis carries this as a designer question (recast sync fiction so the faction learns the core's protocols by heart, with the core's recognition as the visible sign, or accept a two-learning-one-relationship frame and say so in text).
6. A4's milestone language overpromises the economy (DeepSeek, Argon; Qwen validating). DeepSeek traced run 5 cumulative renown at roughly 200 to 300, Argon at 140 to 180, Qwen at 300 to 500; the chair's program arithmetic sits in the DeepSeek band. All three agree the sinks total 1,815 and full completion is a 30-plus run project. The disagreement is only about whether "first doctrine tier complete, one or two lineages deep" describes run 5: on the two lower traces it does not. Chair adjudication: A4's language should be rewritten to match the traced economy (run 5: first doctrine purchases made, one lineage underway, a favored core at tier 1 to 2; full sync and deep lineages are run 10-plus), rather than retuning the economy up to the language. The premises do not require run 5 to be rich; they require it to feel different from run 1, which the traces support.
7. The Crusade cycle under-uses the debt idiom (Qwen, Argon). "Studied by the next fleet" is scholarship; L5's culture is debt-keeping. Unlocks should read as debts being paid down. Cheap copy fix, high flavor yield.
8. Lineage needs the admiral to be an office, not a person (DeepSeek, Argon). Per-admiral XP across runs contradicts captains dying with their hulls, and for unaugmented humans contradicts generational spacing outright. Both legs converge on the same fix: mastery belongs to the house, seat, or line; UI copy must say so.

## Single-leg findings the chair carries forward

9. The recovery gap (Argon, its strongest contribution). Generalizing L7 past F2: every serial cycle assumes the lesson gets home, and a total wipe three sectors out has no carrier. Crusade records, Hullbreaker trophies, Chorus adaptation data, and Doctrine's salvage endowment all need a return path in the fiction. Chair note: partially blunted, because renown as a mechanic already presumes value returns, and not every lost run is a total wipe; but no cycle currently names its carrier, and the fiction work is owed. A shared mechanism (automated black-box relays crawling home at light speed, scout screens that outlive flagship losses, drifting beacons) named once would patch all four cycles at once.
10. The Astra terminology collision (Argon). The pack's L3 calls Astra "the last core" while the sync system runs on six patron cores. Chair adjudication: this is a wording collision, not a systems contradiction (Astra is the last GREAT core; the patrons are lesser cores), but the chair owns the pack wording, and any player-facing text will trip on the same literal reading Argon did. Split the term before lore text ships.
11. Hegemony mastery tension (Qwen). If doctrine is uniform and Registry-issued, why does one admiral's lineage hold options the Registry has not pushed to everyone? Needs one sentence of fiction (lineages are the Registry's sanctioned experiment tracks, or mastery is what an admiral's staff learns faster than doctrine can be revised). Genuine, small, previously unnoticed.
12. Eclipse captain-death tension (Argon). If a destroyed captain's mind is already in the Archive, in what sense did the captain die? Chair proposal, not a ruling: the iteration compiled from records is a new individual, and whether the recorded mind's continuity survives compilation is the Eclipse's own open theological question, which fits their iteration language and costs nothing mechanically.
13. Chorus direction problem (Qwen). Evolution is selection, not choice, but the player picks upgrades from a menu. The Chorus needs a thin fiction for directed adaptation (residual shaping instincts, tropism steering) or its upgrade screens are a human UI wearing flesh.

## Where the chair overrules the legs

Argon's further collapse claims (Crusade/Hullbreaker as one mechanism; Chorus/Glasswright as another) over-aggregate. Judged on mechanism, tempo, and fidelity (Qwen's table: serial study vs oral inheritance; lossy mutation vs perfect delayed replay), the cycles are distinct where it matters to a player. At Argon's altitude every learning system in any game is "the dead teach the living." Not adopted. Argon's "35 to 45 runs to complete the economy" also measures against full completion of all sinks, which no approach claimed for run 10; its traces are used, its benchmark is not.

## Run 1 / Run 5 / Run 10

RUN 1: Validated. Bare toolbox, no Heat, power from skill alone; near impossible as ruled. The legs' one addition: run 1's debrief will usually name no lesson, and the frame must survive that honestly from the very first screen.

RUN 5: The milestone language is corrected per finding 6. What run 5 actually holds on the traced economy: a few admirals or doctrine purchases, an alternate fleet, a favored core climbing, and the first completion in reach. That is a real generational step from run 1, provided the debrief presents it as inheritance rather than totals (progression synthesis, verification 4: the current summary omits sync entirely).

RUN 10: The long tail is depth, not breadth: lineage depth on a few admirals, one or two cores fully synced, Heat climbing. No leg found run 10 empty; Argon's point that full completion of everything is a 35-plus run horizon is a pacing fact to state plainly in any player-facing roadmap, not a defect.

## Questions for the designer (collected for the program report)

1. Hegemony rewrite: courier-speed doctrine with uneven arrival (chair recommendation; no L7 exception), or does the Registry get an exception to L7? An exception would cheapen the Eclipse's defining exemption.
2. The return path: adopt one shared recovery mechanism for the serial factions (black-box relays, surviving screens), or leave the carrier implicit and accept the gap Argon found?
3. "Core": split the term (patrons renamed, Astra stays the last great core), or qualify Astra in lore text wherever the six patrons appear?
4. Debrief honesty rule: adopt per-faction debrief language, including the honest empty lesson ("the debt stands") when a run crosses no threshold, and lagged-inheritance wording for the Glasswrights?
5. Memory track: recast sync's fiction so the faction is the learner, or formally accept a frame of two learning tracks plus one relationship track?

## Addendum: designer rulings (Oct 28, 2026)

(1) Hegemony rewrite: RULED, courier-speed doctrine; no exception to the travel-lag rule. Finding 1 is closed on those terms. (2) The return path: RULED in favor; the lore should speak to how lost fleets' lessons get home, in some fashion. A named shared mechanism (the chair's relay/screen proposal or the designer's own) is the remaining work, not the principle. (3) "Core": RULED differently than either option posed. All cores are equal: the great-core versus patron-core hierarchy goes away, and Astra is a peer core, the one the Glasswrights caged. The terminology collision dissolves by flattening, not by renaming. Lore text should be checked against this wherever Astra's status is described. (4) Debrief: RULED in substance; the debrief is to be tied to progression (including core sync, per the progression synthesis) and to Nemesis adaptation (visible on debrief per the loop synthesis addendum), with the no-fake-lesson honesty clause riding along. (5) The Memory track question was not understood by the designer and has been re-explained in plain terms; it remains open. Finding 6's milestone dispute is settled by the designer's separate framing ruling: run counts are rough milestones, not tuning targets, so milestone language gets rewritten descriptively and the economy is not retuned to chase it.
