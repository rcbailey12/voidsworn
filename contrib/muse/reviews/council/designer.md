# Council audit: designer lens (effects, graphics, art direction)

Reviewer: Muse (Rey), designer seat. Basis: commit `4fa29bd` ("Add files via upload"), repo main at review time. Scope: the art direction brief, the ship pipeline docs (GRAPHICS_REVIEW.md, AUDIT_SYNTHESIS.md, SHIP_QUEUE.md), gfx3d.js read in full (479 lines, first read of this file by any reviewer on this council), scene3d.js, fx3d.js, head.html, the FACTIONS and PATRONS tables in data.js, a structural skim of ships3d.js, and BACKLOG.md sections 1 to 3. Prior reviews skimmed to extend rather than repeat: effects_world.md (performance only), plain_parts.md, the ship pipeline audit in contrib/rey/reviews/, and contrib/gemini/reviews/texture_pass.md. Every claim below is marked OBSERVED (seen in a file, with the citation) or INFERRED (my judgement built on observed facts, with the reason stated).

## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS, and the conditions are not polish items. Voidsworn has strong visual ingredients and no art direction that governs them. What exists is an accumulation of individually clever systems (a faction colour table, a shader detail layer, a cinematic post stack, a procedural planet, a gothic UI) that were each tuned in isolation, plus one ship that received the full treatment while 35 others wait. At screenshot distance, which is how the owner judges this game and how a store page would sell it, the game reads as "dark space tactics with orange and blue glow" rather than as six civilizations at war. The bones are genuinely good. The coherence is not there yet, and the gap between the one finished ship and the rest of the fleet is currently the single most visible art fact in the game.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. There is no filled art direction brief. The only one in the repo is a blank template.

OBSERVED. `docs/skills/create-game-assets/assets/art-direction-brief.md` lists the right headings (shape language, silhouette priorities, palette roles and exact swatches, materials, lighting direction, detail density, explicit exclusions, approval owner and date) and every single field is empty. No other file in the repo fills that role: a search for a palette, shape language, or silhouette doctrine finds only the ship pipeline README's approval step (`docs/ship_pipeline/README.md:29`: "Ryan approves the side view (length and silhouette) before anything else runs") and per-incident fixes in GRAPHICS_REVIEW.md. INFERRED: this is the root cause under most findings below. Decisions that belong in one signed-off document instead live as comments inside code, as owner reactions to individual screenshots in docs/feedback/, and as open questions in BACKLOG.md (music style, Pyre-Barge flanks calm or busy). Each new system then invents its own answers, and nothing checks the answers against each other. A game judged by screenshots, with no screenshot-level target document, will keep drifting.

### 2. The fleet is one finished ship and 35 unfinished ones, and that gap is the art direction a player actually sees.

OBSERVED. SHIP_QUEUE.md lists 36 hulls; 35 are marked "not started" in every pipeline column, and the one worked example, C19_51 Pyre-Barge, is still "awaiting owner verdict" after v14. `ships3d.js:764` confirms the full-detail path covers exactly one hull: `const AI_VSM=new Set(['C19_51']);`. BACKLOG.md section 1 still lists dressing the Pyre-Barge's big plain parts as open work, and plain_parts.md proved the plainness is structural: the detail pass is invoked with the named parts (sponson housings, side tanks, front armour slabs, engine block) as keep-clear obstacles, so it can never touch them. INFERRED: any first screenshot taken today is a screenshot of the gap. It shows one weathered, baked, hand-dressed capital ship next to hulls running an older finish regime (see finding 7), and a competitor screenshot (Void War, Crying Suns, any shipped tactics game) shows a fleet that all went through the same pipeline. The gap also poisons judgement internally: every tuning decision (brightness, detail density, atlas packing) is being calibrated against the one ship that is least representative of the other 35.

### 3. Six factions do not yet read as six civilizations at screenshot distance. Two pairs collapse into each other, and the shared systems keep erasing what difference exists.

OBSERVED. The palette table is real and, on paper, well separated for four factions (ships3d.js:3 to 8, FAC3D): Hegemony light grey and blue, Ithar pearl and jade, Deathless black and terminal green, Chorus purple flesh and bile yellow-green. But Voidsworn (hull `'#4a4440'`, trim `'#c8501f'` in data.js, glow `'#ff7a2a'`) and Corsair (hull `'#6a2c22'`, glow `'#e0402c'`) are both dark hulls lit by red-orange energy, and they are the two factions paired in the sector 1 pool (data.js SECTOR_POOL). Voidsworn and Deathless are both near-black hulls; gfx3d.js:221 admits the compensation in code: `const dk=(fac==='voidsworn'||fac==='deathless')?1.7:1; // the black fleets need a stronger rim to read against space`. The shader energy layer, which is the main per-faction surface signature, assigns the same style to two factions: gfx3d.js:10, `const HULLFX_STYLE={deathless:1,chorus:2,ithar:3,voidsworn:4,hegemony:4,corsair:5};` (Voidsworn and Hegemony share style 4). The sky does not rescue identity either: BIOMES (scene3d.js:240 to 241) gives Chorus and Deathless both green-dominated nebulae, so the two green-glow factions also share a green backdrop family. INFERRED: at battle zoom, where a hull is a few centimetres of dark shape, faction identity reduces to glow colour and rim light. On that test Hegemony, Ithar and Deathless pass, Chorus half passes (its glow is distinct, its hull value is not), and Voidsworn versus Corsair fails outright. The player faction failing the identity test against the first enemy faction is the worst possible pairing to fail on.

### 4. Faction colour has two sources of truth, and they disagree.

OBSERVED. data.js FACTIONS (lines 110 to 121) carries hull, trim and glow per faction. ships3d.js FAC3D (lines 3 to 8) carries hull, dark, trim, win, eng, bolt, beam, shield and tip per faction. The values differ: Voidsworn trim is `'#c8501f'` in data.js and `'#9c3a1c'` in FAC3D; Deathless hull is `'#1d2220'` in data.js and `'#2c3431'` in FAC3D. The UI and campaign read the data.js values (patron and faction colours in campaign screens), the 3D scene reads FAC3D. INFERRED: even where a faction does have a distinct identity, the game shows two slightly different versions of it depending on whether you are looking at a menu or a battle. This is exactly the kind of drift a filled brief (finding 1) exists to prevent, and it will get worse with every tuning pass that edits one table and not the other.

### 5. The effects language contradicts itself on its most important signal: shields.

OBSERVED. fx3d.js:2 states the doctrine in one line: "one visual language: cyan is always shields, amber is always hull; green/red only marks friend or foe". fx3d.js:3, the very next line, breaks it: `function shCol(v){ ... } // v45: shields wear their faction's colour`. So the 3D shield ring (makeShieldRing, fx3d.js:30 to 43) draws arcs in the faction shield colour, which for Voidsworn is orange, the colour the doctrine reserves for hull. Meanwhile the DOM nameplate still renders shield pips in the old cyan: fx3d.js updateWorld sets pip backgrounds from `SHIELD_HEX='#62ccff'` (fx3d.js:4), and head.html defines `--shield:#62ccff` for the panel shield bars. A Voidsworn ship therefore shows orange shields in 3D and cyan shields in its own UI plate at the same moment. INFERRED: the hierarchy problem is wider than shields. Gold simultaneously means UI chrome (head.html `--gilt:#d0a64b`), selection and reach outlines (ui3d.js reachOutline uses a gold line material), the flagship ring (fx3d.js flagRing, emissive gold torus), a charged Command button, and one of the six patrons. Additive glow is the default rendering mode for nearly everything that moves: plumes, wakes, rifts, beams, shield domes, shatter shards, lens flare, nebula banks and dust all add light. When everything glows, priority is decided by brightness accidents, not by importance. BACKLOG.md section 3 already records one symptom ("Gold selection ring too bright at close zoom on the Pixel (re-check)"). The JUICE tiers in wfx3d.js are the honourable exception and are credited below; the colour language needs the same discipline the timing language already has.

### 6. The UI wears one faction's costume for all six factions, and it is not the costume the 3D scene is wearing.

OBSERVED. head.html defines one global look: blackletter display type (`--gothic:"UnifrakturMaguntia","Old English Text MT",Georgia,serif`, head.html:17, loaded at head.html:9), bone text, gilt accents, dark iron panels, used for the title, the sector map, every modal, every campaign screen and the battle HUD, regardless of which faction the player picked. "Gothic" is, in the game's own data, one faction's style: data.js gives Voidsworn `style:'gothic'` while Hegemony is `'wedge'`, Ithar `'sail'`, Chorus `'bio'`, Deathless `'tomb'`. The 3D scene, meanwhile, is graded as a film: scene3d.js:29 to 30 sets ACES tone mapping, exposure 1.12, contrast 1.42, and colour curves commented "cinematic grade: teal shadows, warm highlights". INFERRED: playing the Ithar means piloting pearl-and-jade sail ships through a blackletter-and-gold crusader UI. That is not a neutral default; it is the Voidsworn's identity silently promoted to the game's identity. The pairing inside the UI itself (a medieval blackletter against Barlow Semi Condensed, a modern grotesque) works as Warhammer-flavoured contrast for exactly one faction and as a mismatch for five. A player who picks a different faction should feel the frame change at least in accent colour and sigil treatment. Right now nothing in the frame changes except the ships.

### 7. Patron glyphs are single Latin letters. That is a placeholder, and it shipped.

OBSERVED. data.js PATRONS (lines 387 to 393) defines each god's entire visual identity as a colour plus one character: `glyph:'K'` for Kharon, `'V'` for Vyre, `'H'` for the Hunger Below, `'A'` for Astra, `'R'` for Rook, `'E'` for the Ember Choir. Every rendering site draws the same thing: a filled circle with the letter in the UI font. The sector map node (campaign_run.js:20) is an SVG circle plus a `<text>` element containing P.glyph; the boon modal header (campaign_run.js:284) is a `.sigil` span (head.html:276, a 42px circle) containing the letter; the progression screen (campaign_meta.js:121) repeats the pattern at 30px. OBSERVED, by contrast: the game already knows how to do better, because ships3d.js emblemMat (lines 16 to 25) procedurally draws six genuinely distinct faction emblems (Voidsworn wings around an eye, Hegemony hexagon and chevron, Corsair skull and crossbones, Chorus spiral, Ithar crescent and star, Deathless ankh-like mark) and puts them on hulls. INFERRED: the patrons are the game's gods, the source of its build-defining boons, and the thing the sector map asks the player to route toward. Their mark is the initial-letter avatar a chat app generates when someone has no profile picture. A player cannot learn to recognise "the Hunger" at a glance, cannot spot its sigil on a map node at phone size (a 12.5px letter inside a 20px circle), and gets no sense that six different powers are watching. This is the cheapest high-visibility fix in the whole report: the emblemMat pattern, applied six more times.

### 8. The finish problem (clay, melted, one even sheen) is fixed for one ship and merely managed for the other 35.

OBSERVED. GRAPHICS_REVIEW.md is a good document and an honest one, and its open items are the tell. Item 7: baked AI hulls had "one even sheen" and a "uniform plastic look"; status is "Improved", via shader tricks on the curvature map, not via per-hull finish. Item 8, brightness retune after the winding fix ("pale hulls (Dominion) can blow out to white in direct sun"), is still open. Item 9: "All screenshots so far come from SwiftShader (software rendering)"; the real-GPU and Pixel check is still open. In gfx3d.js, the painted-hull finish block derives steel, rust and grime from the albedo's saturation and luminance (the v55 block in CUSTOM_FRAGMENT_UPDATE_ALBEDO), which means any desaturated pale texel is treated as worn steel and any saturated orange-brown texel as rust, whatever faction painted it. The close-up plating layer in HullFx is a procedural panel grid in ship units, the same generative pattern on every hull it touches; GRAPHICS_REVIEW item 5 records the owner's version of this complaint ("the linework is all the same") and notes it is only switched off for ships with baked finish maps, which today means the Pyre-Barge. INFERRED, concurring with the Gemini texture review's central point: a shader that guesses material from colour, plus one generic panel pattern, produces a fleet that reads as one kit in six paints at close zoom, which is precisely the owner's "Lego / melted / clay" complaint history in docs/feedback/ (pixel_hull_melted.png, the three v14 plain-part screenshots). The system is clever. Clever is not the same as directed, and the owner has now rejected the output of this approach on at least three separate occasions.

### 9. The backdrop is a dice roll that fights the board, and the game already knows it.

OBSERVED. composeBackdrop (gfx3d.js) picks a planet look at random from a per-faction shortlist and a position at random from PLANET_SPOTS. The eight PLANET_LOOKS are shared across factions with heavy overlap (look 5 appears in the voidsworn, hegemony and deathless lists in FAC_PLANETS, gfx3d.js:296), so the same forge world can host three different civilizations. applyBiome (scene3d.js:242) reseeds the sky shader with `Math.random()` per battle. buildBattleEnv (scene3d.js:264 to 266) lays a smoked-glass plate under the board at alpha .6, with a comment explaining it exists so the grid and ships "stay readable over bright planets, rings and nebulae": the backdrop is bright enough that the game has to put a dark sheet under the playfield to compete with it. BACKLOG.md section 3 lists the backdrop's own defect list as open: "rocks clipping into moons, two identical moons, stray 'distant traffic' ships, rim rock wall reads as a slab". INFERRED: individually the planet shader is the best visual in the game (see credits). As a system, the backdrop has no sense of place. A sector does not look like somewhere; it looks like a screensaver re-rolled per fight, and its brightest elements compete with the tactical layer the player is actually reading. Thirty-four additive nebula banks on desktop (scene3d.js makeSunAndSky), a galaxy plane, a lens flare system and a glowing sun halo are a lot of artillery aimed at the background of a tactics game.

### 10. Signature silhouettes are unfinished where they matter most: Ithar sails exist on exactly one hull, as a test.

OBSERVED. ships3d.js:720: `const ITHAR_SAIL_TEST={on:true,hull:'I14_23',keep:.075,root:-.15,R:.6,lift:.3};`. The thin, glassy, iridescent sail fans (aiSailMat, ships3d.js, alpha .34 with full iridescence) replace the concept mesh's moulded fan volumes on that one frigate only. Every other Ithar hull, including the carrier and the Ark, keeps whatever the image-to-3D pass produced. BACKLOG.md section 2 still lists "Ithar sails in engine" and the Chorus approach as undecided ("Chorus and Ithar approaches (organic and porcelain hulls don't fit the Corsair kit)"). SHIP_QUEUE.md adds that for Chorus and Ithar the owner must be asked before a hand-built style is invented. INFERRED: the Ithar are the one faction whose identity is a silhouette rather than a colour. A sail is visible in pure black. Leaving that silhouette as a single-hull experiment while the mechanical factions get the kit pipeline means the faction with the strongest possible screenshot identity currently has the weakest one in practice. Chorus has the mirror problem: its identity is organic form, and its pipeline track is the least defined of the six.

### 11. The post stack applies one film to every civilization, and its heaviest settings are aimed at the wrong target.

OBSERVED. init3D (scene3d.js:21 to 31) builds one lighting rig for the whole game: a warm key sun (intensity 3.2), a cool blue fill, ACES tone mapping, contrast 1.42, vignette weight 2.0, plus a glow layer, and on higher tiers SSAO2, cascaded shadows and (Ultra) volumetric light scattering. applyBiome tints the sun only 35 percent toward the biome colour (scene3d.js:242), and gradeFor (scene3d.js:680 to 684) varies only highlight hue and density per faction; global saturation actually drops from the initial 18 to 8 there. INFERRED: the result is a competent, moody, teal-and-amber space look that is identical in character whether the battle is a Hegemony parade ground, a Chorus feeding ground or a Deathless tomb. Lighting is the cheapest place to give a civilization a mood (Ithar battles could be high-key and blown out, Deathless battles nearly monochrome until the green lights move), and the current rig spends its budget on uniform cinematic polish instead. Combined with the open brightness retune (finding 8), pale factions are simultaneously the most at risk of blowing out and the least served by the one grade they all share.

## What genuinely works visually

Credit where it is earned, because several systems here are better than the coherence around them.

OBSERVED. FAC3D itself (ships3d.js:2 to 8) is the right architecture: one table feeding hull, windows, engines, bolts, beams and shields, so a faction's light language is consistent across every effect that touches it. Most games never build this. The failure is in the values and the duplicate table (findings 3 and 4), not the idea.

OBSERVED. Movement is art-directed per faction, and this is rare and valuable. MOVE_FEEL (ships3d.js:12 to 14) gives Hegemony linear retro-burn movement, Corsair jitter and cough, Chorus sway and squash, Ithar a bobbing arc, Deathless a phase blink, Voidsworn a heavy overburn pitch. animateFactionMats (fx3d.js) pulses Chorus windows like a heartbeat, strobes Hegemony tips, and lets Corsair lights flicker and drop. A fleet in motion identifies its factions better than a fleet at rest, which is the correct priority for a game watched in motion.

OBSERVED. The HullFx energy styles that do exist are the right idea: Deathless circuit traces with travelling pulses, Chorus veins with a heartbeat, Ithar light sweeping the hull (gfx3d.js header, lines 5 to 9). When a player can tell a faction from its surface animation alone, that is real identity work.

OBSERVED. Damage presentation is layered and mostly hierarchical: per-ship cloned materials let scorch and glowing cracks spread across one hull only (shipDamageFx, gfx3d.js), the shield dome ripples from the actual impact point with a hex pattern (shieldDome, fx3d.js), a broken facing shatters into shards in the shield's colour (shieldShatter, gfx3d.js), and the hull gauge arc sits inside the ship's own hex (hullArcs, fx3d.js). The v53 nameplate restraint (plates only for the ship in hand) is a genuinely good readability decision.

OBSERVED. The weapon families in wfx3d.js have distinct speeds, layer stacks, colours and impact behaviours per family (plasma, las, tracer, needle, spine, glob, rail, lance, ion, torp), and JUICE gives events importance tiers for camera and clock. The stated owner goal in NEXT.md ("plasma must feel nothing like lasers") is not finished, but the table structure to finish it exists.

OBSERVED. The procedural planet shader (gfx3d.js PLANET_GLSL) is excellent in isolation: domain-warped gas giant bands with a storm, rocky worlds with relief shading, oceans, ice caps, two drifting cloud decks, night-side city lights and limb scattering. So is emblemMat's set of six drawn faction emblems. The game can clearly produce identity art when someone sits down and makes a set. The problem is how rarely a set gets finished.

## Top 3 recommendations

1. **Sign off a one-page art direction, then judge every future screenshot against it.** Fill the brief that already exists as a template: per faction, one silhouette rule, three palette swatches with roles, one material rule, one lighting mood, one glow behaviour, plus the global rules (what gold means, what shields look like, how bright the backdrop may get). Attach three approved reference screenshots at battle zoom on the Pixel: one mechanical faction, Chorus, Ithar. This is a document and a decision, not a code project, and it unblocks findings 1, 3, 4, 6 and 11 simultaneously. No ship 2 work should start before it exists, because ship 2 is where the current drift becomes 36 hulls of drift.

2. **Restore one colour language and one colour table.** Merge FACTIONS and FAC3D into a single source, then decide the shield question on purpose: either shields go back to one consistent read everywhere (3D ring and DOM pips the same), with faction identity carried by bolts, engines and rim, or faction-coloured shields stay and the fx3d.js:2 doctrine, the cyan pips and the panel bars are all updated to match. While that is open, give Voidsworn and Corsair separation they currently lack (value first: one of them gets lighter or darker hulls; hue second), and let the player's chosen faction tint the UI accent in place of some of the universal gilt.

3. **Finish identity sets instead of new systems: patron sigils and Ithar sails.** Draw six patron marks with the same canvas technique as emblemMat and replace the letter glyphs at all three rendering sites (map node, boon modal, progression row). Promote the Ithar sail from a one-hull test to the faction standard, or make the explicit call to cut it; a signature silhouette in test limbo on one frigate is the worst of both. Both are small, bounded, screenshot-visible wins that a store page would actually show.
