# Voidsworn council audit: the gamer lens

Basis: commit `4fa29bd` ("Add files via upload"), read in full on 2026-10-23. Files read completely: campaign_meta.js, campaign_run.js, campaign3d.js, core.js, data.js, BACKLOG.md, NEXT.md. Battle numbers taken from battle.js and data.js directly; player flow from ui3d.js. I also ran the game's own harness: `node sim3d.js fuzz 20` (0 exceptions, average 7.8 rounds per battle) and `node sim3d.js campaign 10` (bot pilot on both sides, veteran AI, the fixed campaign fleets). Those sim numbers are quoted as sim numbers, not as human results. Prior reviews skimmed so this extends rather than repeats them; where a finding overlaps one (the dead Legacy economy in independent_audit.md, LOS-blind scoring in enemy_ai.md) I say so and move on.

## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS as a game, and the conditions are about shape, not content. The moment-to-moment battle is genuinely good: firing on the move, shield facings, rams with a visible charge lane, a boss who telegraphs his big move a round ahead. The problem is everything around those moments. A new player is asked to learn roughly 34 systems and track 10 resources before the game has earned that trust, the difficulty curve is a speed bump followed by a wall, about a third of the clock in a long battle is animation the player did not choose to watch, and several systems on screen (Resolve, three of the four Orders, flak as a listed gun) are dead or unreachable and teach the player wrong lessons. The build layer looks deep (37 boons, 13 modules, 14 perks, 12 relics) but the maths underneath mostly stacks percentages on the same few axes, so experienced play converges instead of diversifying. None of this needs new content. It needs subtraction, a difficulty dial the player can actually reach, and agency in the reward chain.

## The learning bill: what a first-time player must absorb

Counted from the files, a new player meets all of this, most of it in the first run (OBSERVED):

1. Hex movement with facing (6 facings, turn allowance per hull, big ships get 1 turn)
2. Thruster hops: sideslip, reverse, climb over terrain, dive under ships (battle.js MOVE_BASE), each with a cost
3. Six faction movement bends (MOVE_RULES, data.js), one per faction
4. Firing arcs: prow 120 degrees, two broadside cones, turrets, rear (core.js inArc)
5. Four shield facings that soak by direction and regenerate per turn
6. Sections: prow, midships, engines, each with its own hull pool and a destroyed effect (battle.js damageSection)
7. Crew, a hidden per-ship resource that gates boarding and degrades aim at 50% and 25%
8. Hit chance: accuracy, range falloff past optimal, evasion capped at 45, terrain cover, motion bonuses and penalties, crew state (battle.js hitChance)
9. Weapon kinds: battery, lance, torpedo, flak, bio, gauss, squadron, plus pierce, ion shield stripping, and cooldowns
10. Ten weapon traits (WTRAITS, data.js) that silently rewrite a gun's stats by faction
11. Abilities: 13 of them, one per hull class, with cooldowns (ABILITIES, data.js)
12. Orders: brace is the only one the player can actually give (see finding 4)
13. Admiral's Command: once per battle, three power tiers, charging on round count and on losing a ship
14. Rams: run-up geometry, a 40% hull cap, recoil, section targeting
15. Boarding: crew maths, capture odds, sabotage on failure, prize ships
16. Squadrons: autonomous wings, fighter versus bomber roles, an 8-hex leash, strike and guard orders, flak interception, spare wings
17. Eight encounter types (rout, ambush, defend, hunt, intercept, break, hold, flagship), each with its own win and partial-win rules
18. Twelve board shapes and three arena geometries with different deployment angles
19. Terrain: seven types plus a dead-star gravity well with slingshot, drag and tidal damage
20. Mid-battle events: reinforcements and solar flares, announced a round ahead
21. The sector map: 7 columns, forward-only routing, node types, archetypes, node modifiers, sector conditions
22. Patrons: 6 exist, 4 watch each run, sigils on nodes decide which patron pays out
23. Boons: 37, in three rarities, plus duo boons gated on holding two patrons
24. The flagship aspect: 1 of 3 (or 4) at run start, transfers when the flagship dies
25. Relics: 12, elite and boss rewards, run-long
26. Modules (refits): 13, fitted in slots, movable between ships
27. Captain XP, 5 ranks, a perk choice at each promotion, 14 perks
28. Marks: Mk I to III ship upgrades, +20% hull and shields and +15% damage per step (campaign_run.js shipyard text)
29. The shipyard economy: hulls, repairs at 1 credit per 2 hull, decommission at 35%
30. Salvage: after each win, pick one wreck to strip, scrap or (Corsair) tow
31. Events: 11 text events with costs and gambles
32. Retreat rules, permadeath, lifepod captains at 60%, the reserve captain pool
33. Meta progression: renown, hull variants, admiral mastery (4 levels), patron favour (4 tiers), Heat (7 toggles)
34. Two turn modes, alternating and classic, which change what the End turn button does

Resources in play at once: credits, command points, hull, shields per facing, movement points, turn allowance, captain XP, renown, mastery, patron favour. Plus per-battle: Command charge, ability cooldowns, weapon cooldowns, spare wings, crew. That is the bill. The game pays it back eventually, but it presents almost all of it up front: before the first battle a first-time player picks a faction from 6, an admiral from 4 (24 total, campaign_meta.js admiralScreen), reads run options, then picks a flagship aspect, then reads a sector condition and a map legend. The only teaching layer in battle is 8 tips (ui3d.js:622, TIPS), all battle-local. Nothing teaches patrons, CP, salvage or the economy at the moment they first appear except the help screen, which is a wall of roughly 1,500 words (campaign_meta.js helpScreen).

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. The difficulty curve is a speed bump, then a wall, and the player has no dial (OBSERVED)

The owner's own notes admit the start: BACKLOG.md section 4 lists "Sector 1 balance (owner won 4/4 with no losses on v53.2)" and NEXT.md closes with "Sector 1 too easy: owner won 4/4 in 3 rounds each with 0 losses on v53.2". My campaign sim run agrees and shows what follows (bot pilot, veteran AI, n=10 per cell): sector 1 battles 100% wins in 3.1 rounds with 81% hull left; sector 1 boss 70% in 10.7 rounds; sector 2 elite 40% wins with 4.0 ships lost per battle; sector 3 elite 60%. So the first hour teaches the player the game is easy, then sector 2 elites and the boss fights (10 to 12 rounds, two to four ships lost) punish habits that sector 1 rewarded. The budget maths explains the shape: enemy budget is `[0,4.2,9.5,14][s]+col*...` (battle.js:133), so sector 1 columns run 4.2 to about 9.6 CP against a starting fleet of 6 CP (Vex: penitent 3, harrow 2, warden 1, data.js ADMIRALS), with enemy hulls unscaled in sector 1 (sc = 1+.12*(s-1) in startBattle) while the boss gets BOSS_SCALE .5. Worse, there is no player-facing difficulty choice anywhere. AI_LEVEL defaults to 'veteran' (campaign3d.js:2) and the Standard toggle lives inside the Settings panel. Heat, the only other dial, is locked until the first full victory: campaign_meta.js:86 gates the whole Heat card behind `(META.stats.wins||0)>0`, with a locked card that says "Win a run to unlock Heat". A player who is losing cannot make the game easier except by finding a settings toggle they were never told about; a player who is winning cannot make it harder until they have already won. Both directions of the dial are missing at the moment they matter.

### 2. A large share of play time is watching animations the player did not pick (OBSERVED)

The sim harness prices every animation (sim3d.js CLOCK model: animMove, animWeapon, animExplode with per-weapon timings). In my campaign run the "new" animation clock per battle was 34 seconds for the shortest sector 1 fight, 77 seconds for the sector 1 boss, 99 to 135 seconds for sector 2 and 3 elites and bosses. That is animation wait alone, before thinking time. Fuzz average was 7.8 rounds. Alternating activations (the default, ui3d.js:13, SETUP.alt=true) genuinely help by interleaving enemy moves, but the tail of each round re-concentrates the wait: in ui3d.js aiStep, once the player has no pending ships the enemy batch is every remaining enemy unit at once (`batch=pp.length?[aiPickActivation(pe)]:pe`), each with its own aiUnit wait(60) plus volley waits. In classic mode the whole enemy fleet goes in one block under aiTurn. On a phone, a 2 minute animation tax per boss fight, repeated across roughly 13 to 16 fights per run (7 columns per sector, core.js:207 genSector COLS=7, three sectors), is the single biggest fun leak in the game. There is an animation speed setting (SPEED, Fast halves waits) but nothing surfaces it when a player first sits through a long enemy phase, and no skip or fast-forward exists mid-phase.

### 3. The first run front-loads its hardest decisions before the player knows anything (OBSERVED, impact INFERRED)

Inferred from the flow in campaign_meta.js: faction pick, admiral pick (doctrine, starting fleet and Command on each card), mastery-gated run options, then the aspect pick (aspectScreen: "it lasts the whole run"), then a sector condition and a patron roster the player cannot evaluate because they have never seen a boon. These are the decisions veterans find delicious and new players find arbitrary, asked in that order, and a wrong admiral or aspect costs a whole run: neither can be changed mid-run, and the first boon offer arrives only after the first sigil battle. A first run should be a guided default (Vex, a preselected aspect, mastery and Heat hidden) with the picks unlocked as the player learns what they do.

### 4. Dead and hidden systems teach wrong lessons (OBSERVED)

Three exhibits. (a) Resolve: B starts every battle with `resolve:{player:1,enemy:1}` (battle.js:305), gainResolve (battle.js:348) adds to it on kills, section breaks and captures, capped at 5, and the fuzz harness even asserts it stays in range. Nothing anywhere spends it. It is a vestigial currency that still accrues and is still named in the top bar element id (`resolvelbl`, ui3d.js:61, which actually displays the Command pips). A player who notices the name expects a resource; there is none. (b) Orders: canOrder (battle.js:790) implements four orders, full, lock, helm and brace, and giveOrder applies real effects (+50% movement, +15 aim, +1 turn). The ORDERS table in data.js:146 defines only brace, and the player action strip (ui3d.js actionStrip) only ever renders a Brace button. Three designed systems are unreachable by the player and half-wired in data. (c) Flak: weapon lines in the ship panel list flak with shots, damage and range like any gun, but canFire hard-blocks it against ships: battle.js:456, `if(u.dead||t.dead||u.side===t.side||u.braced||w.k==='flak') return false`. Flak is point defense and interception only. Every hull from the warden up carries one, so every new player tries to shoot with it, gets a generic cannot-fire state, and learns to distrust the panel. Dead UI is worse than no UI: it spends the player's learning budget on things that are not real.

### 5. One dominant line of play: focus fire into the rout threshold, alpha torpedoes early (OBSERVED numbers, dominance INFERRED)

The rout rule ends most fights early: checkEndRules (battle.js:786), "if(bossDown||sideCP('enemy')<=B.cp0.enemy*.35)" the enemy breaks and flees. You never need to kill the fleet, you need to remove 65% of its command points, so damage spread across hulls is strictly wasted, doubly so because every shield facing regenerates each turn (regen 5 to 15 per facing by hull, data.js HULLS) and a facing you do not finish recharges. The expected-damage preview and the AI's own scoring (evalOffense, kill bonus `if(e.hull>=t.hp) v+=15+t.maxHp*.1`) both push the same way. Within that, weapon maths from data.js W (raw expected damage per volley at listed accuracy, before range and evasion): heavy torpedoes 40.8, doom arc 31.2, torpedo 26.9, heavy battery 25.2, battery 17.3, lance 16.4, ion 9.6. Torpedoes carry cd 2, which sounds like the balancer until you do the cycle maths: fired in round 1, cdLeft decrements at each of your turn starts, so the tube is ready again in round 3's start of turn processing only after two decrements, effectively firing rounds 1, 4, 7 in alternating play. In a sector 1 fight the sim says ends in 3.1 rounds, the cooldown never lands: the torpedo is pure alpha. Malek (torpCd 1, data.js ADMIRALS) plus a magazine module (torpCd 1, magazine) collapses the cycle further. So early game dominant play is torpedo alpha into one hull, focus to the rout line, done. Batteries out-damage torpedoes over a 10 round boss fight, which is a real tradeoff late, but the player learns the torpedo habit in the part of the game that teaches. Separately, aim stacking beats crit stacking almost everywhere: auspex (+10 aim, 80 credits) versus cogitator (+10% crit, 75 credits). Ten points of hit chance applies to every shot before the crit roll and also defeats evasion; +10 crit at x1.5 is worth about +5% expected damage on hits only. The same pattern repeats in perks (gunnery +10 aim versus steady, ignore 2 hexes of range penalty). One of each pair is a trap pick at a near-identical price.

### 6. The reward chain is five forced modals with no decline anywhere (OBSERVED)

After a win the player walks a fixed corridor: victory recap (rewardFlow), salvage pick (salvageStep), boon or refit pick (rewardNext, boonStep), relic pick on elites and bosses (relicStep), then one promotion modal per pending captain (promotionsStep loops). None of these offers a skip. Salvage forces a choice among the wrecks' strip, scrap or tow options. The relic choice (2 options on elites, 3 on bosses) cannot be declined even when both are useless to the fleet. The boon pick is 1 of 3 with no reroll and no pass; BACKLOG.md section 4 itself lists "boon decline and reroll" as unbuilt v54 work, so the owner already knows. Perk promotions are a forced 1 of 2. Forced picks feel good when the options are good and resentful when they are not, and with rarity as a flat multiplier (RARITY m 1, 1.5, 2 in data.js) a Common roll of a patron you are not building around is a real outcome. The chain also breaks the post-victory high: the best moment in a roguelite is the recap, and here the recap is the first of up to five interruptions before the map returns.

### 7. Map nodes and events contain real decision deserts (OBSERVED)

Two node types resolve themselves: field repairs (campaign3d.js fieldRepairs, every ship +20%, one tap, no choice) and drydock (campaign_run.js drydockScreen applies 60% of missing hull, then shows a modal whose only buttons are Continue and Manage fleet). The pre-boss column is always a rest stop (genSector forces shipyard, drydock or field at COLS-2), so the most dramatic routing decision, how beaten up to arrive at the flagship, is partly made for you. Events are the other desert. There are 11 (EVENTS, data.js); most carry a "leave, nothing happens" option, and the interesting options are dice, not decisions: board the derelict is `rnd()<.6` for a module and 40 credits, else 25% hull damage on a random ship (campaign_run.js applyOutcome); the shrine gamble is `rnd()<.5` repair 30% or nothing. Because outcomes are hidden coin flips with fixed stakes, the correct play is arithmetic, not judgment, and the events repeat inside a run since the seen pool resets when exhausted. Routing itself is forward-only along edges (availableNodes), with 2 to 4 nodes per column that often converge on the same next column; the patron sigil is usually the only real differentiator, which is a good differentiator, but on columns where both fights carry the same patron and reward type the "choice" is cosmetic.

### 8. The economy starves the interesting purchases and pays in the wrong places (OBSERVED)

Start: 120 credits (core.js newRun). A sector 1 battle pays (25+12*1+3*col), so 40 to 55 credits (campaign_run.js rewardFlow); a boss pays x3. Prices: warden 70, harrow 130, penitent 200, cathedral 340 (data.js HULLS cost); modules 55 to 90; a Mk II upgrade costs `40*cp*mk+30` (core.js:184), so 150 for a cruiser, 230 for a battleship, and repairs at 1 credit per 2 hull compete with all of it after every fight. A realistic sector 1 shipyard visit buys one module, a warden, or repairs. That would be fine tension except the shipyard stock hulls are hardwired to the three smallest Voidsworn hulls mapped through equivHull (campaign_run.js shipyardStock: 'warden','harrow','penitent'), with carrier, battleship and dreadnought gated behind LEGACY unlocks. And per independent_audit.md, which I verified in main3d.js, boot grants every LEGACY unlock and every admiral unless unlockAll===false, so the gate is open anyway and the renown prices on LEGACY and admiral cards are fiction. The net player experience: credits feel scarce, the shop feels small, and the meta shop feels broken, three different frustrations from one economy.

### 9. Renaming everything per faction multiplies confusion without adding decisions (OBSERVED)

The same weapon wears up to six names: FAC_WNAMES (data.js) renames the voidsworn battery to "Las-battery", hegemony guns to plasma names, and so on. Modules and relics get five rename tables each (MOD_NAMES, RELIC_NAMES), and the numbers also change silently because FAC_WTRAIT applies one of 10 WTRAITS (las holds aim at range, scrap adds a shot and drops accuracy 12, rockets triple the shots at a third damage) inside nameWeapons. A "torpedo" is not a torpedo across factions, so weapon identity cannot be learned by name or by stats. The panel shows the stats, which saves it from unfairness, but 25 base weapons times trait variants is the real count a returning player must hold. Related mismatch: the help screen teaches classic turns ("Every ship can move, turn and fire each of its weapons once per turn", Enter ends the turn) while the campaign runs alternating activations by default (SETUP.alt=true), where Enter ends one ship's activation. The first battle does not match the only rules text offered.

### 10. Build diversity is narrower than the content count suggests (OBSERVED mechanics, convergence INFERRED)

derive (core.js) builds every ship by summing flat fx from relics, the admiral, boons, modules, perks and the aspect into one sumFx pool, then applying percentage stacks with no diminishing returns (armor alone caps at .6). Rarity scales a boon linearly (Common x1, Rare x1.5, Epic x2), so an Epic is the same decision, bigger. Offers are pre-filtered for usefulness by fleetKit and BOON_NEED (core.js rollBoonOffer quietly swaps out boons your fleet cannot use), which protects players from dead picks but also removes the interesting tension of building toward a boon. The result is that most levers push the same three axes (damage %, shields, torpedo or squadron %) and the optimal build in any run is "stack the axis your admiral already buffs". The systems that do change rules (chain explosions, riposte on brace, boarding sabotage, fires) are the best boons in the file and they are the minority. NEXT.md's v54 list (rule-changing boons, legendaries per patron, doctrines) is aimed at exactly this; the gamer lens agrees with the owner's own queue and would move it ahead of new hulls.

### 11. Squadrons and boarding are high-complexity, low-agency subsystems (OBSERVED, agency judgment INFERRED)

Wings fly themselves: sqPlan scores targets, respects SQ_LEASH 8 (battle.js:872), and the player's total input is an optional strike or guard order per wing (sqOrder). Carriers start with wings airborne and launch only replaces losses (ABILITIES launch text). So the carrier player manages two wing kinds with opposite target logic (sqWants: bombers hit ships, fighters hunt craft first), interception rolls and leash geometry, and makes about one decision per wing per battle. Boarding is similar: the capture maths is exact and honestly displayed (captureChance, and the panel shows the percentage), but the AI's own tactic only boards at 70% or better (battle.js:685, tacticBoard `o.chance>=70?100+...:0`), which tells you the designer's own bot treats sub-70 boarding as a mistake, while the player is invited to try it with no such guidance. Both systems are flavourful and both are where a first-time player's attention goes to die.

## What is genuinely fun (specific, not polite)

- Fire on the move. The attack-move plan (ui3d.js: tap an out-of-range enemy and the ship burns to the best firing spot and shoots as it arrives, battle.js moveUnit onLate) removes the deadest turns in hex tactics. Combined with the expected-damage line and the ghost ship preview, moving feels like attacking, not like commuting.
- Shield facing geometry. Four facings, rear hits halving armor and critting more (applyHit, shieldQuad), plus the rake bonus for broadsides after moving 2+ hexes, makes positioning a real puzzle every activation. This is the game's best system.
- The ram lane. When a ram is lined up the game draws a red lane and an arrowhead on the target and prints the estimate on the button (ui3d.js ramLane, actionStrip "Ram (~dmg)"). It is the most legible big-play telegraph in the game and rams feel earned.
- Bosses telegraph. Enemy flagships announce their Command a round ahead (bossCmdTick), with a banner, so the player gets a genuine brace-or-scatter decision. More games should steal this.
- Salvage from your actual kills. The wreck list is the ships you destroyed, with cause of death ("cut in two by a lance") and yield tied to what the wreck carried (campaign_run.js salvageYield). It makes the kill you just made matter twice.
- Faction movement bends. Chorus free first pivot, Corsair cheap sideslips, Hegemony cheap reverse, Deathless passing through rocks at no extra cost (MOVE_RULES): six factions that move differently is real variety, cheaply bought.
- The boon recap line. "Boons in action: Chain Reaction x3" on the victory modal (rewardFlow) closes the loop on build choices. When a boon never triggers, the game even says so and points at the trigger. That honesty is rare and good.
- Mid-battle events with a round of warning (rollBattleEvent, warnR then the event at round 3 to 4) change a fight's shape without feeling like theft.

## Top 3 recommendations

1. **Fix the curve and hand the player the dial.** Flatten sector 1 up (start budgets closer to the player's 6 CP, or scale sector 1 hulls less generously to the player) and pull the sector 2 elite spike down; the sim gate already exists, so add a campaign-shape check to the publish gate in NEXT.md using the bot at veteran on both sides, with target bands per cell. Move the AI level choice onto the admiral screen as a plain difficulty pick (Story, Standard, Veteran), and stop gating Heat behind a win: let players add Heat from run one, since voluntary difficulty is the whole point of it.
2. **Cut the wait and delete the dead systems.** Add a real fast-forward for enemy phases (hold to speed, or an animation speed that also compresses the aiStep tail batch), and surface the existing Fast setting the first time an enemy phase runs long. Delete Resolve outright, or spend it on something visible. Either ship full, lock and helm orders with buttons and ORDERS entries, or remove them from canOrder so the code stops promising them. Label flak in the weapon line as point defense that cannot fire on ships.
3. **Give the reward chain agency and collapse it into one screen.** One post-battle recap screen with sections (salvage, boon, relic, promotions) instead of up to five stacked modals; a decline on boons, relics and perks; one reroll per boon offer, priced in credits so the economy gains a sink. This is already the owner's v54 direction in BACKLOG.md; the gamer case for pulling it forward is that it touches every single battle, while a new hull touches one shipyard.

## Not checked

- No human playtesting: every pacing and difficulty number here is from the game's own sim harness with its bot pilot, plus the owner's recorded results in BACKLOG.md and NEXT.md. A human is slower and worse than the bot in some places, better in others.
- Skirmish mode balance and the FLEET_LISTS mirror matches (sim3d.js matrix) were not run; findings are campaign-shaped.
- Sound, music (there is none, per NEXT.md), visual readability on the Pixel, and touch targeting accuracy are other lenses' territory; I only flag where they change decisions.
- The 3-band space proposal in contrib/muse/reviews/space_3d.md was not evaluated for fun impact here; if it ships, findings 3 and 11 get worse before they get better and it needs its own gamer pass.
- Save and load behaviour, cloud sync, and performance are covered by the engineer and QA lenses; I did not audit them.
- Exact boon-by-boon win rates (sim3d.js boonsim exists) were not run for all 37 boons; finding 10 is argued from the fx maths in data.js and core.js, not from measured pick rates.
