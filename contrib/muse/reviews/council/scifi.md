# Voidsworn Council Audit: Sci-Fi Nerd Lens (Universe and Theming)

Basis: commit `4fa29bd` ("Add files via upload"), repo `rcbailey12/voidsworn`, read on 2026-10-23. Files read fully: `data.js` (448 lines), `BACKLOG.md`, `NEXT.md`, `HANDOFF.md` (skim), `campaign_meta.js`, `campaign3d.js`, `campaign_run.js`, `battle.js` (encounters, boarding, travel text), `core.js` (sector and patron helpers), `docs/skills/create-game-assets/assets/art-direction-brief.md`. Prior reviews skimmed to avoid repetition: `contrib/muse/reviews/*.md`, `contrib/gemini/reviews/texture_pass.md`, `AUDIT_v53H.md`. Claims are marked OBSERVED (seen in the text quoted) or INFERRED (judgement built on observed text). Quotes are short and exact, with file and line.

## Verdict

OBSERVED basis, INFERRED judgement: Voidsworn is not yet a universe, it is a very likeable collage whose strongest original voice (bio-horror Chorus, tomb-dynasty Deathless, junkyard Corsairs) is constantly drowned out by unexamined borrowings from Warhammer 40,000, Star Wars, and Necron and Eldar vocabulary used verbatim. The six factions play differently and, at the level of blurbs, names, and weapon traits, increasingly sound differently, but underneath they share the same hull classes, the same admiral Command rotation, the same sector structure, and the same unexplained patron gods, so the fiction reads as six paint jobs on one Imperial fleet template with excellent name generation bolted on. Fix the patrons, the travel physics, and the borrowed lexicon, and the collage could become a setting, because the best writing here is already distinctive when it stops quoting other settings.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. The patrons, the core progression fiction, are never explained at all

Severity: critical for theming. OBSERVED: The game asks the player to build a run around six beings and never once says what a patron is. The only definition offered is mechanical. `campaign_meta.js:160`: "Each run, four of six patrons watch your run." `campaign_meta.js:99`: "Patrons watching this crusade" (rendered with the run word substituted). `data.js:384`: "Each run four of six patrons watch the crusade." The help text at `campaign_meta.js:144` says only that "patrons grow fond of you as you take their boons." No codex entry, no intro line, no event, no admiral line explains whether these are gods, warp entities, alien intelligences, AIs, ancestral ghosts, or a gamified abstraction the fiction does not endorse.

That vacuum matters because the names imply mutually incompatible answers. OBSERVED: `data.js:388`: "Kharon, the Iron Saint" suggests a deified human. `data.js:389`: "Vyre, the Storm Queen" suggests a person or goddess. `data.js:390`: "The Hunger Below" suggests a cosmic horror. `data.js:391`: "Astra, the Silent Star" suggests a celestial object that is somehow a person. `data.js:392`: "Rook, the Breaker" uses a plain personal name with no title of divinity. `data.js:393`: "The Ember Choir" is a collective. A saint, a queen, a hunger, a star, a breaker, and a choir cannot all be the same kind of thing unless the game says so, and it never does. INFERRED: A player who cares about the universe will invent a headcanon in the first run and have it contradicted by tone in the second, because Kharon's boons are disciplinary and military while the Hunger's are biological and the Ember Choir's are pyromaniac religious. The favour ladder makes it worse. OBSERVED: `data.js:356` (FAVOUR_TIERS): tiers named "Watching", "Favoured", "Devoted", "Chosen", with the top reward that "Their boons are never common." That is cult language applied identically to a saint and to a star, and to every faction including the machine Deathless and the hive Chorus, a huge claim the game never makes.

This extends, it does not repeat, the career and story review in `contrib/muse/reviews/career_story.md`, which proposed admiral voices and faction arcs. Voices cannot fix this layer. An admiral can react to Kharon only if the fiction has decided what Kharon is.

### 2. Direct borrowings are not allusions, several are verbatim lifts from 40k, Star Wars, and their alien factions

Severity: critical for originality and, for a shipped game, for distinctiveness. OBSERVED, Warhammer 40,000 cluster in the supposedly flagship faction and shared systems: `data.js:281`: "Your fleet astropath wakes screaming." Astropath is a 40k proper noun for a sanctioned warp psyker navigator caste, not a generic word. `data.js:287`: "Warp storm" and `data.js:281`: "sailing through a warp-rift" import the Warp itself. `data.js:153`: "Auspex array" and the constant use of auspex for sensors (for example `campaign_meta.js:105`: "Long-range auspex confirms") is 40k sensor vocabulary. `data.js:164`: "Targeting cogitator" and `data.js:188`: "Archeotech rangefinder" import cogitator and archeotech, both 40k coinages. `data.js:203`: "Inquisitor-Captain Malek Draven" imports the Inquisition wholesale. `data.js:193`: "Wraithbone compass" and `data.js:261`: "Wraithbone keel" import wraithbone, the Eldar psychoplastic material, by name. `data.js:35`: Voidsworn guns renamed "Penance lance", "Hellfire torpedo", "Censer flak", plus hulls named Penitent, Reliquary, and Cathedral, complete a 40k Ecclesiarchy checklist rather than evoking it.

OBSERVED, Star Wars cluster, mostly loaded onto the Hegemony, who are also the 40k Imperial Navy analogue, so the two sources collide in one faction: `data.js:11`: "Turbolaser" and `data.js:12`: "Heavy turbolaser" are Star Wars weapon names, not generic laser terms. `data.js:258` (Hegemony reskins): "Proton magazine", "Hyperdrive motivator", "Durasteel weave" (in RELIC_NAMES, same line group), "Deflector capacitor", "Ion drive upgrade". Proton torpedoes, hyperdrive motivators, durasteel, and deflector shields are a Star Wars parts catalogue. `data.js:20` in the shared weapon table: "Ion cannon" sits in both universes, which is exactly the problem, the game never picks a lane.

OBSERVED, Necron and Eldar cluster loaded onto the other factions: `data.js:21`: "Gauss flayer" is the Necron infantry weapon name verbatim, and `data.js:40` repeats it as the Deathless rename, so the Deathless do not even get a synonym. `data.js:24`: "Shuriken cannon" is the Eldar weapon name verbatim, and the Ithar, the game's elder race, also get `data.js:193` wraithbone and a hull called `data.js:105` region: "Ithar wraithcarrier". INFERRED: The Ithar are Eldar with the serial numbers filed halfway off (dying elder race, `data.js:123` blurb: "An ancient, dying elder race", wraithbone, shuriken, seers, songships), and the Deathless are Necrons the same way (tomb worlds become tomb-arks, gauss, reanimation, dynasties, Egyptian names). A sci-fi literate player does not experience these as homages. Homage changes the angle. These keep the noun.


### 3. Faster than light travel has four incompatible names and no rules, and battles contradict the map

Severity: high. OBSERVED: The same act, moving the fleet, is called jumping on the map, warping in battle, and hyperspace travel in an event variant, with rifts as a fourth hazard. `campaign_run.js:55`: "Choose a pulsing node to jump there." `campaign_run.js:316`: "the fleet jumps deeper." `battle.js:23`: "enemy reinforcements will drop out of warp" and "drop out of warp" again in `battle.js:24` for friendly reinforcements. `data.js:281` Hegemony variant: "deep inside a hyperspace rift." `data.js:287`: a "Warp storm" rolls "across your jump route", mixing warp and jump in one sentence. INFERRED: Warp, hyperspace, jump, and rift imply four different physics (a hell dimension, a parallel layer, an instantaneous point transfer, a tear). The game uses whichever word sounded best in the sentence being written. That is collage writing at the level of physics.

The fiction also fails to support the mechanics it most needs. OBSERVED: Sectors are presented as geography ("contested space", `campaign_meta.js:105`: "activity along every jump route") but generated as a forward-only node graph. `campaign_run.js:55`: "You can only move forward along the lines." No fiction explains why a starfleet cannot go back, wait, or go around except through the graph, and no named place anchors any sector. Regions exist in code (`battle.js:39`: "Asteroid frontier", "Ion nebula", "Contested world", "Old battlefield", "Deep void") and do give sectors a shared biome, which is good, but the map screen leads with the enemy faction and a condition, not with a place the player can remember or a destination the run is trying to reach. Three sectors, three flagships, no named objective world, no home port, no reason given in fiction for why killing three flagships ends anything. OBSERVED: `campaign_meta.js:201` victory text: "Three sectors broken, three flagships burning behind you." Broken toward what? INFERRED: The run structure is Slay the Spire in space dress, and the writing never does the one paragraph of work that would disguise that: a destination, a blockaded world, a relic vault, a evacuation, anything at the far end.

Boarding and lifepods show the same gap. OBSERVED: Boarding resolves from crew numbers in code (`battle.js:667` boardUnit) with no text explaining how boarders cross, why a Chorus tendril and a Hegemony marine party resolve identically, or why, per `battle.js:675`, "your ships are never captured: boarders cripple, they don't steal", while the player can capture outright. Lifepods are pure dice: `campaign3d.js:75` gives a destroyed captain a flat random chance to enter `RUN.reserve`, and `campaign3d.js:84` says they "escaped in a lifepod, wounded". No faction exception exists (Chorus brood-minds and Deathless machine intelligences apparently also use lifepods), and the title promise at `campaign_meta.js:31`, "so does its captain" stays lost, is softened by a mechanic the fiction never mentions.

### 4. Six factions are distinct in play and in surface naming, but share one skeleton underneath

Severity: high. Credit first, OBSERVED: The mechanical identities are real. `data.js:111` to `data.js:135` blurbs are differentiated in one line each, move rules differ (`data.js:100` MOVE_RULES: Voidsworn broadside doctrine, Hegemony retro thrusters, Corsair sideslips, Chorus free pivot as "Living hull", Ithar fan sails, Deathless phase drive), weapon traits differ (`data.js:44` WTRAITS: las holds aim, hellfire ignites, plasma pierces, scrap is wild, rockets can be shot down, spines shred armour, acid eats hull), and the Chorus (no shields, regeneration on every hull) and Deathless (no shields, reanimate) genuinely break the shared template.

Underneath, the skeleton shows. OBSERVED: Every faction uses the same class ladder, Frigate, Destroyer, Cruiser, Carrier or Battleship, Dreadnought or boss equivalent, including the bio-swarm, whose `data.js:95`: "Chorus drone-spawn" is still `cls:'Frigate'`, and the Deathless, whose frigate is a scythe in name and a frigate in every system string. Every faction gets exactly four admirals on the same cost steps (0, 30, 45, 80) with the same four Command slots rotating through the same small pool of Command ids (barrage, hunt, scramble, iron, surge, restore). Compare `data.js:210` Voidsworn commands with `data.js:214` Hegemony and `data.js:218` Corsair: the titles are renamed ("Fire for effect" becomes "Synchronized broadside", "Scramble all wings" becomes "Launch the strike group") while the descriptions repeat near verbatim, for example "Every ship fires a free volley at the best target its guns can reach, right now." INFERRED: Renaming a shared ability is reskin work, and the game does a lot of it. The reskin tables prove both the effort and the limit. OBSERVED: `data.js:255` MOD_NAMES and `data.js:263` RELIC_NAMES give each faction its own words, and the best entries sing (Corsair "Gunner's lucky charm", Chorus "Predator instinct", Deathless "Repair scarabs"). But several entries are the base name with one word swapped or not swapped at all: Hegemony `data.js:258`: "Targeting computer" for a base item called "Targeting cogitator" is a downgrade in flavour, not a translation, and RELIC_NAMES repeats "Smuggler's ledger", "Salvage rig", and "Captured war banner" identically across factions that supposedly have nothing in common. FAC_WNAMES at `data.js:35` leaves Corsair `autocannon:'Autocannon'`, the base name unchanged. INFERRED: A civilisation is not a find and replace table. What is missing is not more synonyms, it is one thing per faction that only that faction has in fiction: a Hegemony law, a Chorus hunger cycle, an Ithar taboo, a Deathless succession rule, expressed in an event, a shipyard rule, or a boon refusal. Currently events gate by exclusion lists only (`data.js:276` beacon is `not:['hegemony']`, shrine is `not:['chorus','deathless']`), which is subtraction, not character.

Two naming collisions actively confuse the taxonomy. OBSERVED: The faction "The Chorus" (`data.js:119`), the patron "The Ember Choir" (`data.js:393`), a Chorus admiral "The Mending Choir" (`data.js:224` region), Chorus hulls and captains built on throats, songs, and hymns, and Ithar "songships" and "Lance choir" doctrines all compete for the same musical-religious register. A new player cannot tell, from names alone, whether the Choir is the Chorus, whether the Ithar sing to the same thing, or why the Ember Choir favours torpedoes. Naming is the cheapest worldbuilding there is, and here it spends its budget twice on the same metaphor.

### 5. The tech and magic line is incoherent because every faction borrows a different setting's metaphysics

Severity: high. OBSERVED: Within one shared universe the game simultaneously asserts a 40k style warp with astropaths and saints who answer prayers (`campaign_run.js:218`: "The saint answers. Every ship repairs 30% of its hull."), Star Wars style hyperspace with a "ghost signal" decoded by a sensor officer (Hegemony variant, `data.js:281`), Ithar seers who dream true (`data.js:281` Ithar variant: "Your seer dreams of a lost treasure"), Chorus brood-minds who taste the void, Deathless dynastic beacons, machine reanimation beams (`data.js:146`: "Reanimation beam"), phase jumps and dimensional shifts (`data.js:142`, `data.js:145`), and a patron who is literally a star granting aim bonuses. INFERRED: Any one of these is a fine foundation. Stacked without a rule for how they relate, they cancel out. Is the astropath's warp the same place the Ithar seer dreams in? Can a Hegemony ship, whose variant text carefully secularises the event into a sensor ghost signal, also benefit from a saint answering at a shrine, which its variant text at `data.js:299` explains away as crew superstition ("Spacers are a superstitious lot")? The event variants are actually the best worldbuilding tool in the game, because they show each faction interpreting the same phenomenon through its own lens. But they expose the missing centre: the game never states what is actually true, even ambiguously, so the variants read as the game hedging, not as cultures disagreeing about a real thing.

The shrine event is the sharpest case. OBSERVED: `data.js:299`: the shrine is excluded for Chorus and Deathless, offered to everyone else, and its gamble outcome is written in one voice for all takers: the saint answers, or "Silence. The void keeps its own counsel." A Hegemony crew whose own variant calls this superstition still gets a miraculous fleetwide repair with no comment. A Corsair crew gets the same saint. INFERRED: Either saints are real and faction-agnostic, which should terrify the Hegemony and interest the Voidsworn enormously, or the repair is morale and coincidence, in which case the text should wink. It does neither.

### 6. The owner's space, not naval, rule is honoured in ship modelling and violated all over the text

Severity: medium high, because it is an explicit owner rule. OBSERVED: `AGENTS.md` states the rule plainly: "Space ships, not naval ships: no keels, jackstaffs, bollards, crow's nests, yardarms or mast ladders", and "guns never face backward". The data text breaks the letter of it. `data.js:261`: the Ithar prow refit is named "Wraithbone keel". A keel is the first item on the banned list, attached to the faction least likely to have one. OBSERVED: The core combat vocabulary is thoroughly naval and is presented as the game's own terms, not as in-fiction slang: `data.js:29`: "Prow", "Port broadside", "Starboard broadside"; `data.js:152`: "Reinforced prow"; `data.js:422`: "Battering Prow"; `data.js:398`: "Anchored Volley", where anchored has no meaning for a ship under thrust in open space; Ithar movement is "Fan sails" (`data.js:104`) with a "Solar sail extension" refit (`data.js:261`), and admirals are Dawnsail and Starwarden of sails. INFERRED and fair: Broadside, prow, port, and starboard are defensible as inherited naval vocabulary that real space fiction also uses, and the prior space 3D review already reframed altitude in non-naval terms at the owner's direction. Sails are also defensible for the Ithar if they are literal solar sails, which the art direction treats as a signature. Keel and anchored are not defensible on those grounds. They are unexamined defaults, and they sit next to genuinely space-native inventions (phase drive passing through rocks, brood-minds, living hull pivots) that prove the game can do better when it tries.

### 7. The framing text still assumes you are always the Voidsworn, so five sixths of the game is narrated by the wrong voice

Severity: medium high. OBSERVED: The title screen never changes. `campaign_meta.js:31`: "Command a crusade of gothic capital ships through three hostile sectors." A player launching a Corsair raid, a Chorus hunt, or a Deathless reclamation (the game's own run words, `data.js:270`: crusade, campaign, raid, hunt, voyage, reclamation) is introduced to it as a gothic crusade. The code comment at `data.js:384` does the same: patrons "watch the crusade", whoever you are. The derelict event at `data.js:273` is written in Voidsworn Gothic for everyone: "an ancient gothic warship drifting without power, its hull etched with prayers", with no faction variant, unlike the astropath and shrine events which do vary. A Deathless fleet boarding a gothic prayer-ship should have a point of view about it. It gets the Voidsworn's. OBSERVED: The end screen partly fixes this, and deserves credit: `campaign_meta.js:171` WIN_TITLE varies well ("The crusade endures", "The loot is yours", "The Chorus swells", "The Dynasty rises"), and `campaign_meta.js:201` personalises the victory line by admiral and faction. INFERRED: The pattern is clear, the newer and more run-specific the text is, the more faction-aware it is, and the older framing text (title, derelict, patron comment, help headings styled "gothic" throughout `campaign_meta.js`) still speaks Voidsworn as the default human. Since the Voidsworn were clearly the first faction built (their hulls alone have hand-set costs, slots, and a unique dreadnought, per the comment at `data.js:325`), the universe reads as a Voidsworn game that five other factions were later allowed to play in, rather than a setting six civilisations share.

### 8. The event deck is too small and too generic to carry a universe, and its best idea is used twice

Severity: medium. OBSERVED: There are 11 events in `data.js:273` to `data.js:310`. Only two (astropath, shrine) have per-faction variants. The rest are single-voice and mostly setting-neutral: a mercenary for hire, a rogue shipwright, deserters with a bounty, a smuggler cache with "primitive" locks. Seizing the beacon cargo at `data.js:279` warns "Their escort might remember your colours", which sets only an ambush flag, no reputation or named pursuer. INFERRED: In a roguelite the event deck is where the universe lives, because battles repeat. Eleven events with coin flip outcomes in `campaign_run.js` applyOutcome teach the player the setting is wallpaper over dice, and events are the only place factions could want different things. Mostly they all want credits, experience, a module, or a relic, in the same words.

### 9. There is no art direction brief, so the visual universe has the same collage problem as the text

Severity: medium. OBSERVED: `docs/skills/create-game-assets/assets/art-direction-brief.md` is an unfilled template, every field blank including "Player fantasy", "Shape language", "Palette roles", and "Explicit exclusions". INFERRED: The factions do have a concise visual taxonomy in code (`data.js:111` to `data.js:135`: hull, trim, glow, style: gothic, wedge, junk, bio, sail, tomb), arguably the cleanest statement of the universe in the repo. But it lives as data, not direction, and the brief records none of the owner's rules (space not naval, guns never backward) where an artist would look. A look defined in code constants and chat memory will drift toward collage.

### 10. Captain and admiral writing is strong on names and thin on people, which wastes the permadeath promise

Severity: medium. OBSERVED: The name systems are excellent (see What genuinely works). But admirals, each faction's named face, get only a name, a doctrine label, and one mechanical sentence, with no dialogue, goal, or history in the files read. `data.js:203` Malek is an "Inquisitor-Captain" and nothing inquisitorial ever happens. The Matriarch of Nine Throats has nothing throat-related beyond the name. OBSERVED: `BACKLOG.md` section 4b already wants this fixed ("voice/personality lines, and their own goals; recurring rival admirals"). INFERRED: Until it lands, the permadeath promise is carried by a name in the Hall of the Fallen (`campaign_meta.js:124`: name, rank, ship, sector, nothing they did or said), and names alone fade.

## What genuinely works

Credit with the same specificity, all OBSERVED unless marked.

1. The patron quotes are tiny and perfect. `data.js:388` to `data.js:393`: Kharon, "Stand. Fire. Stand again." Vyre, "Every shield is a promise. Break theirs." The Hunger, "More mouths. More wings. More." Astra, "One clean line of light is enough." Rook, "Faster. Closer. Through them." The Ember Choir, "Sing, and let the void burn with you." Six voices, six playstyles, six personalities in under ten words each. This is the standard the rest of the lore should be held to.

2. The faction variant events, where they exist, are real worldbuilding. The astropath event at `data.js:281` retold as a Hegemony ghost signal, a Corsair navigator's hunch ("She is usually right. Usually."), a Chorus scent, an Ithar seer's dream, and a Deathless dynastic beacon shows five civilisations interpreting one phenomenon five ways. That single structure, extended, would fix half this report.

3. Weapon traits as fiction. `data.js:44` WTRAITS: Corsair scrap is "an extra shot, wild aim, lucky crits", Chorus spines "each hit strips 3% armor". Mechanics and character in one line.

4. Chorus and Deathless naming and systems cohere. No shields and regrowth for a swarm that knits flesh, no shields and reanimation for a dynasty that stands back up, spore swarms instead of fighter wings, brood-mothers instead of carriers in everything except the class string. When the game commits to a faction's biology or metaphysics in systems, the fiction lands without needing exposition.

5. Run words and win titles. `data.js:270` RUN_WORD (crusade, campaign, raid, hunt, voyage, reclamation) makes every run announcement faction correct, and `campaign_meta.js:171` WIN_TITLE follows through. Cheap, systemic, effective.

6. Sector conditions and salvage. `data.js:365` SECTOR_CONDS ("Ion season", "Wreck belt", "Scarcity") change every fight in a sector, and `battle.js:39` REGIONS tie terrain to place: the skeleton of a real geography. Salvage in `campaign_run.js` reads the wreck you made (causes from `battle.js:690`: "cut in two by a lance", "flayed by gauss fire") and, for Corsairs only, offers towing it home. Faction, cause, and reward in one beat.

## Top 3 recommendations

1. Write the patron paragraph and enforce it everywhere. Decide, in one short in-game codex entry plus one line on the aspect screen, what the six patrons are, why they watch every faction including the two that should theologically object, what a boon physically is, and what Devoted and Chosen commit the player to. Then audit every patron-adjacent string against that decision (favour tiers, gift boon text, duo boons, the Ember Choir versus Chorus naming collision, which should be resolved by renaming one of them). This is the highest leverage fiction work in the game because patrons touch every run, every map node, and the entire meta progression, and right now they are a mechanic wearing six masks.

2. Do a lexicon pass and replace verbatim lifts with Voidsworn's own coinages. Keep the flavour, change the nouns: astropath, warp, auspex, cogitator, archeotech, wraithbone, gauss flayer, shuriken cannon, turbolaser, proton, hyperdrive motivator, durasteel, and Inquisitor should each get a setting-native replacement, using the game's own best inventions as the model (auspex could stay only if deliberately claimed and defined, otherwise the Hegemony variant already shows the way with "sensor officer" and "ghost signal"). In the same pass, settle travel on one physics with one name and use it in map, battle, and event text, remove "Wraithbone keel" and "Anchored Volley" under the owner's space not naval rule, and give the run a named destination so three sectors point at something.

3. Extend the two systems that already work instead of adding new lore volume: faction variant text and faction specific consequences. Give every event at least a Chorus, Deathless, and player-faction variant line, give the derelict and the title screen the RUN_WORD treatment so non-Voidsworn runs stop being narrated as gothic crusades, and add one fiction-bearing rule per faction that only that faction triggers (a Hegemony legal seizure, a Chorus feeding choice, an Ithar taboo refusal, a Deathless succession dispute) in events or shipyards. Pair this with the already planned admiral voice lines from BACKLOG 4b, pointed at patrons and rivals, so the people carrying the permadeath promise finally say something. Fill in the art direction brief from the code's six style words and the owner's placement rules while doing it, so the visual universe stops depending on chat memory.
