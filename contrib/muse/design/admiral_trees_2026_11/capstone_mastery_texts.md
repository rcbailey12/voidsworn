# Capstone mastery texts (issue #311)

The 144 capstone mastery texts: for each of the 48 capstones (two per admiral, 24 admirals), Mastery I, II and III lines in the admiral's voice, tied to that capstone's final effect after this pass. Per PR #306 the in-game capstone mastery perks are three generic placeholders shared by every capstone; these texts are the per-capstone replacements. No new numbers: the build step that replaces the placeholders owns the numbers, the same text-versus-TREE_FX split as the tree itself. Pattern: I is the capstone learned, II is it deepened (a sharper condition, a surer hand), III is it perfected (the fleet flies it without thinking). Chaired by Rey from the blind Rey draft, with DeepSeek and Qwen mastery samples read against it (Qwen's pattern statement converged; its Vex Cap2 lines assumed a rewrite that was not adopted and were not used).

## Glasswrights

### Tamsa, Flight Cast Perfectly
- I: The cast is learned: every wing on one target, and its shields answer only once.
- II: The wings arrive as one thought now; the first hit opens the way for all the rest.
- III: A perfect cast. The target's most hurt place is found, opened, and finished by the flock.

### Tamsa, The Forge Casts Ahead
- I: One wing per carrier remembers its target and strikes it again when the next round opens.
- II: The recorded strike no longer drifts; the forge holds every path it has cast.
- III: The forge casts ahead of the battle itself: what was struck yesterday is struck again, harder.

### Saro, Sun Through One Lens
- I: The whole volley learns one target, and distance stops taxing the light.
- II: The lens steadies: no shot in the concentration is spent on range again.
- III: Sun through one lens, perfected: the fleet fires as a single instrument and the shields answer once.

### Saro, The Flaw, Corrected Forever
- I: The volley's most-hit target is recorded, and lances keep their edge against it.
- II: The correction is checked against every later shot; the flaw stays corrected.
- III: Forever means forever: the flaw, once seen, is never unlearned by any gun in the fleet.

### Vell, The Fleet Is Elsewhere
- I: The first enemy shot teaches the fleet to be elsewhere: movement, and a free turn.
- II: The scatter comes sooner in the body now; the fleet is gone before the second thought.
- III: Elsewhere is where the fleet lives. The enemy fires at where the glass was, always.

### Vell, A Hundred False Hulls
- I: Two enemy attacks in the scatter round are spent on hulls that are not there.
- II: The false hulls multiply in the enemy's sights; real ships pass uncounted.
- III: A hundred is not a number any more. The enemy runs out of belief before the fleet runs out of images.

### Brin, Ward of Clear Glass
- I: An enemy that fires at the ward shoots worse for it; the glass answers every shot once.
- II: The penalty sits deeper in the attacker's eye; firing at clear glass costs more nerve.
- III: The ward is clear and total: to shoot at it is to be unmade as a gunner for that shot's echo.

### Brin, Every Facet Lit
- I: Every warded ship carries hard light as hull, a fifth of its shields made solid.
- II: The facets hold their light longer in the hand; the borrowed hull is spent last and reluctantly.
- III: Every facet lit at once: the ward wears a second skin of light and calls it its own.

## Sunward Crusade

### Vex, The Grand Ledger
- I: The ledger opens on one name: every gun is entered against it, and its shields answer only the first hit.
- II: The accounting tightens; no volley is split and no shield is counted twice.
- III: The Grand Ledger closes when the named ship does. The debt was never in doubt, only in arithmetic.

### Vex, The Last Account
- I: The account does not close with the first killing: a ship that destroys its target fires once more, at a new name in the ledger.
- II: The second shot is entered without pause now; a settled debt opens the next one immediately.
- III: The Last Account is never the last shot. Every name struck from the ledger buys the fleet another, until the page is empty.

### Malek, Salvo Without End
- I: The salvo learns a second firing: one torpedo per ship speaks twice in the round it is sworn.
- II: The second firing comes without fumbling; the hunters reload by rhythm, not by order.
- III: Without end is the standard now. A Malek salvo is measured by the target's silence, not the tubes.

### Malek, The Named Prey Falls
- I: The prey is named, every travelling torpedo turns to it, and the whole fleet's fire weighs more on it.
- II: The naming is surer; nothing in flight is wasted on another hull.
- III: The named prey falls. It is a description of the salvo, not a hope attached to it.

### Ashgrove, The Roll of Wings
- I: Every strike from the roll burns, and the struck ship's flak answers one wing fewer from then on.
- II: The roll is kept in better order; the burning and the blinding arrive together, wing after wing.
- III: The Roll of Wings is read out in fire. By the last name, the enemy's sky is already closed.

### Ashgrove, Oath of Unbroken Wings
- I: The wall forms: screens renewed by the oath, and the first torpedo meant for each sheltered ship is taken by a wing.
- II: The wall holds its spacing under fire; unbroken is a drill now, not a prayer.
- III: No wing on the wall breaks, and no ship behind it goes unguarded. The oath is simply kept.

### Thale, Iron Testament
- I: The wall stands into a second round, and its shields drink again at half strength when that round opens.
- II: The second round is garrisoned like the first; the testament is read twice and believed both times.
- III: Iron testament, sealed: the wall outlasts the battle's temper, and the fleet stops checking whether it stands.

### Thale, The Wall That Moves
- I: The wall learns to walk: while it stands, every ship under it moves the faster.
- II: The moving wall keeps its dressing; speed no longer thins the line.
- III: It is not a wall that moves any more. It is a fleet that cannot be made to stop.

## Hegemony Navy

### Kassar, Living Relay
- I: The flagship's voice reaches twice as far, and the line fires as one body inside it.
- II: The relay is drilled into the fleet's hands; distance stops being an excuse for disorder.
- III: Living relay, perfected: wherever the flagship stands, the whole fleet is already in formation around it.

### Kassar, One Voice
- I: Once a battle, the synced line speaks with one voice at one target, and its shields answer only the first shot.
- II: The unison is rehearsed to the breath; no ship fires early and none fires late.
- III: One voice is all the enemy hears. The broadside is a sentence, and it is final.

### Vorn, Interdiction, Permanent File
- I: What the strike touches is filed permanently: the same bonus waits for it again next round.
- II: The file is cross-referenced by every gun in the fleet; nothing filed is ever mislaid.
- III: Permanent means permanent. A target leaves the file when it leaves the battle, and not before.

### Vorn, One Order, Every Gun
- I: One order, and every gun fires twice: the second shot at half weight, by the book.
- II: The second firing is part of the drill now; no crew waits to be told twice.
- III: Every gun, every order, twice. The enemy's copy of the book has the same pages missing every time.

### Reyes, One Target, Every Wing Filed
- I: One enemy is filed, every extra wing strikes it, and wing hits on it carry the filed bonus in full.
- II: The filing is done before the launch now; the wings leave already knowing the whole order.
- III: Every wing filed, every wing arrived. The target's report is a list of strikes with no gaps.

### Reyes, Standing Strike Group
- I: A patrol wing per launch ship keeps the watch, and strikes the first enemy that strays into its reach.
- II: The patrols overlap their watches; the group's sky has no unwatched hour.
- III: The strike group stands permanently. Entering its range is the mistake; the strike is only the paperwork.

### Maren, The Standard Covers All
- I: While the standard is up, every ship under the wall takes a further fifth less harm.
- II: The cover is measured to each ship alike; no one stands at the edge of the standard's mercy.
- III: Covers all means all. The enemy does the arithmetic and puts the guns down for that round.

### Maren, The Flagship Holds the Standard
- I: Ships in a line with the flagship wear shields above their maximum, borrowed from the standard it holds.
- II: The borrowed strength is issued faster; the flagship's presence is a regulation, not an inspiration.
- III: The standard has never been lowered. Ships form on it the way iron forms on a magnet.

## Ashfall Hullbreakers

### Grax, Burn Until the Hulls Meet
- I: The burn refuses to end on schedule: its speed and its protection walk into the next round with the pack.
- II: The pack stops bracing for the burn to end; the second round is hunted like the first.
- III: Until the hulls meet is the only order. Everything before the meeting is just the burn, still burning.

### Grax, Run of the Warlord
- I: The flagship moves, boards, moves again, and boards again: the warlord's run is two fights in one.
- II: The crews learn the run's rhythm; the second boarding party is over the rail before the first reports.
- III: Run of the Warlord is a title the enemy gives it. Grax just calls it arriving.

### Sable, Dark Full of Knives
- I: The hunt's edge carries past the round: the first shot from a rear arc tomorrow is already sharpened today.
- II: The knives stay out between rounds; the dark does not put them away any more.
- III: Full of knives is not a count. It is what the enemy's rear arc sees when it looks back, every round.

### Sable, The Cut That Feeds the Pack
- I: At the word, every ship slides two hexes toward a rear arc, and the pack eats what it reaches.
- II: The slide is read off the prey's facing without a pause; the pack arrives already cutting.
- III: The pack is fed by geometry now. Wherever a rear arc exists, the cut is on its way to it.

### Mott, Swarm Over the Scrap Field
- I: The swarm goes up twice: once at the word, and once more when the fleet's last ship has had its say.
- II: The second launch stops surprising Mott's own crews; the yard schedules around it.
- III: Over the scrap field, the swarm is weather. It comes back because coming back is what it does.

### Mott, Old Mott Private Wing
- I: One wing is Mott's own: patched stronger than the rest, and it does not come home until the battle does.
- II: The private wing learns its carrier's habits; its strikes land where the scrap is richest.
- III: Every fleet in the Reach knows that wing. It has been in every fight and it is in this one too.

### Vesh, Welded Whole
- I: One broken section per ship comes back whole: not patched, whole, as if the hit was a rumour.
- II: The welding is cleaner; whole sections come back with their fittings and their tempers.
- III: Welded whole is the Collector's signature. The enemy breaks it, Vesh returns it, the battle deducts nothing.

### Vesh, Nothing Stays Broken
- I: Once a battle, the break that would land simply does not: the section holds, restored to full on the spot.
- II: The welders watch the strain points now; the save arrives with less theatre and more certainty.
- III: Nothing stays broken is not a boast in Vesh's fleet. It is the maintenance record, read aloud.

## The Chorus

### Matriarch, Nine Throats, One Hunger
- I: One enemy is chosen, every extra swarm strikes it, and swarm hits on it bite a quarter deeper.
- II: The nine throats sing the choice without discussion; the hunger arrives already aimed.
- III: One hunger is the whole fleet's appetite wearing one name. The chosen ship is eaten in order.

### Matriarch, The Brood Does Not Stop
- I: A swarm that finishes its target keeps biting: another enemy, at half strength, in the same round.
- II: The second bite is taken without regrouping; stopping was the only habit the brood had to lose.
- III: The brood does not stop is a tide table, not a threat. Targets end; the biting finds the next one.

### Razormaw, The Throat Turns Red
- I: Ships that end the frenzy touching an enemy carry the red into tomorrow: the first shot weighs a quarter more.
- II: The red is held overnight in the throat; no cooldown, no forgetting.
- III: The throat turns red and stays red. Adjacency is a promise the next round collects.

### Razormaw, One Prey, Every Mouth
- I: The prey is named and every mouth moves on it at once, turn limits be forgotten for one surge.
- II: The surge is aimed better now; mouths arrive in biting order, not in a heap.
- III: Every mouth is not a count the prey can use. By the time it has counted two, the rest are feeding.

### Mending Throng, Tide Without End
- I: The tide comes twice: once at the word, and once more at half strength before the round is out.
- II: The second tide is expected by every wound in the fleet; crews hold their hurt open for it.
- III: Without end is approximate only in the enemy's reports. In the fleet's, the tide is simply the weather.

### Mending Throng, What Was Torn Grows Back
- I: Torn sections grow back whole, and a section the tide restores cannot be torn again this battle.
- II: The regrown flesh knits denser; restored sections stop being the fleet's weak reading.
- III: What was torn grows back is the whole doctrine. The enemy is invited to check the same section twice.

### Voice of the Deep, The Volley That Grows
- I: A ship whose first spit lands spits again at half: the volley grows by its own accuracy.
- II: The second spit comes from more throats now; a hit is treated as an instruction.
- III: The volley that grows is grown, past counting. The first spit is only the seed of the weather.

### Voice of the Deep, Mouth of the Deep
- I: One ship opens fully: every weapon it carries, twice, at one target the deep has chosen.
- II: The mouth coordinates its own teeth; the second volley of the chosen ship wastes nothing.
- III: Mouth of the Deep is a place enemy ships are taken to. The tour is short and entirely one way.

## Eclipse

### Nadir, Tenth Frame
- I: One ship is restored threefold, and a broken section returns whole: the tenth frame is the generous one.
- II: The triage of frames is practised; the chosen ship's restoration stops being the battle's interruption.
- III: Tenth Frame is the archive's best copy of mercy. It is issued once, and it is enough.

### Nadir, The Earlier Copy Returns
- I: One damaged ship is repaired on every section, the earlier copy of it walking back into the fight.
- II: The returned copy is checked against the original line by line; nothing is restored out of order.
- III: The earlier copy returns is how the fleet says a ship was never really gone. The record agrees.

### Umbra, Noon That Does Not Pass
- I: The noon volley fires, and fires again when the next round opens, at half strength, from every ship.
- II: The second noon is aimed by the first one's memory; fewer shots are spent learning ranges.
- III: Noon does not pass in Umbra's sector. It is always, briefly, the brightest moment of someone's last round.

### Umbra, One Sun, Copied
- I: Every volley is copied at half strength onto the enemy with the least hull, as if one sun were two.
- II: The copy is truer; the weakest enemy stops being able to hide behind the fleet's attention elsewhere.
- III: One sun, copied, is the whole sky. There is no ship too small to be under it.

### Ostrel, Out of Every Shadow
- I: The hunt's bonus holds a second round for ships that end their rounds behind an enemy or untargeted.
- II: The shadow discipline deepens; ships stop drifting out of their cover between rounds.
- III: Out of every shadow is where the fleet files its reports from. The enemy has never read one and lived briefed.

### Ostrel, The Long Copy Strikes
- I: At the word, every ship moves two hexes toward a rear arc: the long copy arrives before it is missed.
- II: The approach is copied from better and better originals; the strike starts closer every time it is taught.
- III: The long copy strikes is the last line of several archives. The copies keep writing it because it keeps being true.

### Seveth, Wall of Ten Thousand Copies
- I: While the wall stands, no ship in it can lose more than a quarter of its hull in a round.
- II: The copies share the load more evenly; the cap is met less often because the wall plans for it.
- III: Ten thousand is not the count of copies. It is the count of hits the wall has answered with the same refusal.

### Seveth, The Restored Fleet
- I: A ship repaired while the wall stands keeps the wall's protection into the next round, after the wall ends.
- II: The restoration and the protection are filed together now; one no longer lapses before the other is spent.
- III: The restored fleet does not lower its guard to heal. Healing is one of the things the guard is for.
