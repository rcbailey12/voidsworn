# Career screen, mockup round (issue 794, stage 3, second screen)

Screen: Career (rank 3 on the audit list in ../audit.md). Evidence: the Records tab capture career_screen_after.jpg, read in full. Ryan settled FL5 for the Fleet screen on Nov 15, 2026, which unlocked this round, and directed that ChatGPT run on it as a standing council leg.

## The job this screen holds

No decision and nothing to buy, equip or confirm. Its job is recognition: the player comes to see what the career has amounted to and which admirals have earned something. The current build shows it as filled panels full of sentences, in a style the rest of the game has retired: seven lifetime figures in filled boxes (with the loss figure in red, which breaks the house rule that red is the Nemesis only), five tabs as pills, and twelve admiral cards in two columns, scrolling, three of them carrying a service record and nine carrying a doctrine phrase and "No service yet".

## The three mockups

All three carry the same real content from the capture, follow the orders sheet system in docs/playbooks/screens.md, and keep Close as the one action box, top right. Common to all three: the seven lifetime figures in the open with no boxes and the loss figure in the same neutral tone as the rest; the five tabs as words with their keys after them, the active tab full strength with a neutral underline, never ember (ember marks the next action, and this screen has none); the subtitle kept whole in second strength; all twelve admirals on one screen with no scroll and no cut off rows. They differ on one real choice: how the roster is laid out, and how much of the record sits on the surface.

### CR1, The Service Roll (chair's pick)

The Fleet settlement applied to a screen that looks back instead of preparing. One line per admiral, stacked under their faction headings, in roster order. A served admiral's line is the name and earned title, then the record as labelled figure groups (runs, won, best sector, battles, kills, lost), so the celebration ChatGPT's leg warned about losing stays on the surface, not behind a click. An unserved line is the name, the doctrine phrase, and NO SERVICE YET in third strength, right aligned. Selecting an admiral opens the sheet at the right at about a fifth of the width: portrait where one exists (Vex is shown; he is the only admiral with a painting in evidence), the full record in the sentences the game writes it in, titles with their counts, and flagships. The sheet is the record; the line is its index.

Why this one: it is the same furniture the player just learned on Fleet (manifest plus sheet), bent to recognition. The three served lines carry figures dense enough to reward the visit, the nine quiet lines are honest about a young career, and as service accrues the screen fills without a redesign.

### CR2, The Three Columns

ChatGPT's shape, built as proposed. The three factions stand side by side as columns, four entries each, so the factions read in parallel and the whole roster takes less vertical room. Served entries keep name, earned title, runs, won and best sector on the surface; the inspector at the right (Grax Ironmaw shown) carries ranks, fuller name elements, the full record, titles and flagships. Strongest as a wall of honour; weakest where a faction column's tall served entry pushes its unserved entries down unevenly against the other columns, and the eye has to learn three starting points instead of one list. It stays in the folder as the round's record.

### CR3, The Honours Board

The served three get the top of the screen as heroes, each with rank, title, the full record sentence, titles and flagships on the surface, Vex with his portrait strip; the unserved nine sit below as a compact roster under their faction names. No sheet at all: everything the career has earned is on the surface, which answers the homework risk most directly. It is also the design that ages worst: it hard codes today's three and nine split into the layout, so the tenth admiral to earn service has to break the board's shape to join the top, and the unserved roster reads as a footnote. Not the pick; it stays as the record of the strongest form of the served first argument.

## Where every removed sentence now lives (CR1; CR2 and CR3 follow the same homes)

- The subtitle sentence: kept whole, second strength, under the title.
- The five pill tabs: words in a row, keys 1 to 5 after them; the active tab full strength with a neutral underline.
- The seven figures in filled boxes: the headline band, in the open; the loss figure neutral, red stays the Nemesis colour.
- The served admiral's record sentence ("1 run, 1 won. Best: sector III, 9 battles, 45 kills, 1 ship lost."): compressed on the line as labelled figure groups; the sentence itself verbatim in the sheet.
- "Titles: the Surgeon (1)" and its peers: the title itself sits after the name on the line; the full line with its count sits in the sheet.
- "Flagships: Wrath of Vesper" and its peers: the sheet. (In CR3 they are on the surface, in the hero block.)
- The doctrine phrases ("Hunter-killer", "Carrier doctrine" and the rest): on the unserved line; also in the sheet for that admiral.
- "No service yet": on the unserved line, in third strength, as NO SERVICE YET. It is stated, not implied by absence.
- Kassar's second name element, "Holder of the Anchorage": the sheet, with her full name. In CR3 it is on the surface.
- The ranks (Admiral, Oath-Captain, Commodore and the rest): in the sheet, with the full name. The line carries name and title only, so the figure groups have room to read.

Nothing is deleted. Every line of the current Records tab is on the surface or one click away in the sheet.

## The other four tabs, placed

Same skeleton in all three designs: title, subtitle, tab row, figure band, Close. Medals and Titles follow Records exactly (a manifest of earned entries first, unearned below in third strength, selection opens the same sheet). History is the one structural exception the chair backs: the runs as a timeline, one node per run, because the game already draws a run timeline on the settled debrief design. Chronicle is the one tab allowed to be prose: a single reading column at text size, since a chronicle is read, not scanned. No contents for any of the four are in evidence and none are invented here; the Board fills them from game data.

The chair's CR3 objection stands against both CR3 and CR4 and is recorded, not solved: the layout is shaped around today's three served and nine unserved, and a fourth served admiral will need a second hero row while the unserved roster shrinks.

Chair's updated pick: CR4, subject to Ryan's settle between CR3 and CR4.
