# Blind leg: Rey (chair), Career screen

Written before any council leg was seen.

## The screen

The Records tab as a service manifest, sibling to the settled Fleet design. The seven lifetime figures stay as the headline band, in the open, no boxes: figure in the mono face at headline size, label under it in second strength. The loss figure loses its red; red is the Nemesis colour and a career total is not a warning. The five tabs become words in a row under the title, the current one full strength, the rest second, each with its key (1 to 5) after it. Close is the one box, top right, with its key.

The admirals are one line each, grouped under their faction crest and name, served admirals first inside each faction. A line is: portrait or crest, name and earned title in the display face, rank in second strength, then the record as figures, not a sentence: runs, won, best sector, battles, kills, ships lost, each a small labelled figure group in the mono face. Vex's line reads 1 RUN, 0 WON, BEST I, 3 BATTLES, 15 KILLS, 0 LOST. An admiral with no service shows the doctrine phrase and NO SERVICE YET in third strength, and nothing else; nine quiet lines are honest, nine fake cards are not. Titles earned sit on the line as the title itself after the name (the Surgeon, the Battery Master, the Render), with the count in the sheet.

Selecting an admiral opens the sheet at a fifth of the width: portrait or crest large, the full record as sentences exactly as the game writes them, titles with their counts, flagships, and the doctrine phrase. Everything on the line is a compression of the sheet, so nothing is only in the sheet except the sentence forms.

The other tabs keep the same skeleton. Medals and Titles: a manifest of earned entries first, unearned below in third strength, selection opens the same sheet. History: the runs as a timeline, one node per run, because the game already draws run timelines on the debrief screen. Chronicle: the one tab allowed to be prose, set as a single readable column at text size, since a chronicle is read, not scanned.

## Risks

1. Twelve lines, nine of them near empty, could read as a screen with nothing on it. Answer: that emptiness is the truth of a young career, and the three served lines carry figures dense enough to reward the visit; as service accrues the screen fills without a redesign.
2. Compressing the record sentence into figures could lose the game's voice ("Best: sector III, 9 battles, 45 kills"). Answer: the sheet keeps the sentences verbatim; the line is an index, the sheet is the record.

Verdict: Seven figures, three served admirals
