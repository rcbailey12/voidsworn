# UI upgrade audit: every screen ranked by text load (issue 794, R260)

Owner ruling behind this job: R260 (Ryan, Nov 15 2026). "it's a WALL of text. Nobody will actually read that. Can we look at that screen and any other areas where it's all text and do an upgraded design?" His picks: audit all screens, fix the worst first starting with the after-run screen; visuals are portraits and ship pictures, icons and meters, a run timeline, big headline numbers; a headline plus three to five key items per screen, everything else one click or hover away, nothing deleted from the game.

Method: docs/playbooks/screens.md step 1. Name each screen's decision, then rank by how much the screen leans on sentences over visuals and how much a player must read to make that decision. Evidence: the orders sheet (screens.md section 2), the debrief captures on PR 789 (docs/shots/debrief_92/), the issue 644 capture set (docs/shots/text_floor_644/), the current debrief design (contrib/muse/design/debrief_screen_2026_11/synthesis.md), and the godot/ui, godot/campaign and godot/portraits trees. Four blind legs (Rey, DeepSeek, Qwen, Flash; Grok's seat was empty, bot wall) agreed on the top three and on the bottom two; disagreements are noted where they exist. The Board's fresh capture set (docs/shots/ui_audit_1115/) had not landed when this was written; ranks 4 to 8 rest on the captures and code named in the legs and should be confirmed against that set before build work is scheduled below rank 3.

## The ranked list, worst first

1. **After-run debrief.** Decision: name or confirm the heir, then optionally spend renown, then choose the next run. Two full viewports of scrolling prose in the win state; nine sections, nearly all sentences at one size; the only big numbers sit halfway down the scroll; the heir choice, the one permanent decision, is three text rows near the bottom. Becomes visual: the recap figures as the headline band, the run as a timeline, the heir pick as crest cards with meters, renown as a vault meter, the standout ship as a picture with figures. Mockups DB1 to DB3 are in the debrief folder. (R260 fixes this screen first in any case; it is also worst on the merits, three legs of four agreed, and the fourth, Flash, put it second. The chair keeps it first: no other screen buries a permanent decision under this much prose.)
2. **Fleet and refits (campaign).** Decision: which ships to repair, refit and ready before the next battle. Twelve ship cards at about 15 lines each: weapons spelled as sentences, captain progress as a sentence, refit slots as text, and the ships themselves never appear. Becomes visual: a ship picture per card, weapons as icon rows with figure chips, hull and shields as bars, captain progress as a meter. Unanimous second across all four legs.
3. **Career screen.** Decision: none in the moment; it is the archive (records, medals, titles, history, chronicle). Card bodies are sentences and the screen still wears the filled panels the orders sheet retired; its big lifetime figures are the one habit worth keeping. Becomes visual: admiral portraits or crests on the cards, medals and titles as icon rows, the chronicle as a run timeline. Unanimous third.
4. **Campaign event and result screens.** Decision: pick a choice, or absorb an outcome and continue. Story prose is partly the point, but outcomes (credits, damage, a refit won) are written inside the story sentences where a player skims past them. Becomes visual: an outcome strip of icons and figures under the story, choice cards with their cost figures showing.
5. **Nemesis dossier (campaign side, including the dossier page).** Decision: how to fight this enemy. Adaptations, flee state and weakness are stated as sentences in a panel; the battle side already demoted the same content to a hover, which is the correct shape. Becomes visual: the Nemesis portrait with scar icons for adaptations and a weakness icon row; the sentences move to hover.
6. **Battle log overlay.** Decision: reconstruct what just happened. Plain text lines by design, and already demoted behind the L key with a three line ticker on the HUD. Becomes visual: event type icons (hit, kill, loss) and an icon filter row; no layout change.
7. **Settings.** Decision: set options. Label rows and toggles; text is the interface and a wrong read costs little. Becomes visual: one win only, a live preview line at the chosen UI scale.
8. **Title screen and credits.** Decision: continue, new run, or quit. The menu is already spare. The credits dialog is a scrolling attribution list by nature and by licence; leave it. Becomes visual: nothing needed; the continue entry could carry the current run's sector and battle count as one figure line.
9. **Campaign map and briefing rail.** Already orders sheet (R176) and the playbook's worked example: the map is the visual, the rail holds the decision, intel sits behind hovers. No work in this job; it is one of the two references the screens above are judged against.
10. **Battle HUD.** The exemplar (E1 LEDGER, R210): one sheet in decision order, the odds as the big number, everything else behind hovers, with a published moved-items table. No work.

Not ranked: the load screen and the rename dialog. Neither holds a real decision and no capture of either was in evidence; the ui_audit_1115 set should confirm both before anyone schedules work there.

## What happens next

Ryan picks a mockup for the after-run screen (debrief folder). Then the next two screens on this list, Fleet and refits and the Career screen, get the same treatment: mockups first, his pick, then a build brief. Nothing below rank 3 is scheduled by this audit.
