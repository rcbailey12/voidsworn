## The audit (ranked worst first)

Ranking rule, from the brief: how much the screen leans on sentences over visuals, and how much a player must read to make the decision the screen holds (screens.md step 1: name the decision first).

1. **After-run debrief.** Decision: name or confirm the heir; then spend renown or move on; then choose the next run. Evidence: the win state runs two full viewports of scrolling prose (top and lower captures). Nine sections (headline, what this run bought, lineage, core sync, Nemesis lesson, the record, heir to the line, recap stats, next run) are nearly all sentences at one size. The screen's only big numbers (6 battles won, 1 flagship destroyed, 24 enemy ships destroyed, 2 ships lost, 47m played) sit buried halfway down the scroll. The heir decision, the one real choice, is three sentences in a list with text-only keep and confirm links below it, and the exits are at the very bottom. A player must read roughly 45 lines to act. What becomes visual: the recap numbers become the headline band; the run becomes a timeline (three sectors, six battles, the flagship kill, the two losses); the heir pick becomes three portrait cards with one meter each; renown becomes a vault meter with the next unlock marked; the standout ship gets its picture and its two figures.
2. **Fleet and refits (campaign).** Decision: which ships to repair, refit and ready before the next battle. Evidence: text_floor_644 fleet capture, 12 ship cards, each about 15 lines of small text: hull and shield figures in words, weapon entries spelled as sentences ("Sear battery Port broadside 4x6, range 5"), captain experience as a sentence ("0/30 experience to Commander"), refits as text slots. Nothing is a picture; the ships themselves never appear. What becomes visual: a ship picture per card (the game has the renders), hull and shields as bars (partly there), weapons as icon rows with figure chips (damage, range) instead of sentences, captain progress as a meter.
3. **Career screen.** Decision: none in the moment; it is the archive the player browses (records per admiral, medals, titles, history, chronicle). Evidence: text_floor_644 career capture: five tabs, card grids whose bodies are sentences ("1 run, 0 won. Best: sector I. 3 battles, 15 kills, 0 ships lost."), and it still wears the old panelled look (filled boxes everywhere) that the orders sheet retired. It does have the big lifetime numbers (4 runs, 2 victories, 27 battles, 135 destroyed), the one visual habit worth keeping. What becomes visual: admiral portraits on the cards (the game has painted portraits, godot/portraits/painted/), medals and titles as icon rows, the chronicle as a timeline of runs instead of paragraphs.
4. **Campaign event and result screens.** Decision: pick a choice, or absorb an outcome and continue. Evidence: godot/ui/campaign (choice_button, campaign_ui, map_intel) and the ui_redesign briefing set (04_dossier, 11_event_result in the rollout folders). Story text is the content here, so prose is partly the point; the failure is that outcomes (what changed: credits, damage, a gained refit) are written inside the story sentences instead of shown as figure chips beside them. What becomes visual: an outcome strip (icons and figures) under the story, choices as cards with their cost figures visible, not only in the sentence.
5. **Nemesis dossier (campaign panel and battle hover).** Decision: how to fight this enemy (weakness, adaptations). Evidence: nem_panel_shots in godot/campaign/test, the battle HUD's hover-only Nemesis block (screens.md's moved-items table). In battle it is already demoted to a hover, which is right; the campaign panel states adaptations and history as sentences. What becomes visual: the Nemesis portrait (the game has a full nemesis portrait pool) with scar icons for adaptations and a weakness icon row; the sentences move to hover.
6. **Battle log (full log overlay).** Decision: reconstruct what just happened. Pure text lines by design; the three-line ticker on the HUD is already the visual summary. Ranked low because reading is the job here and the HUD demotes it correctly. What becomes visual: kill and loss lines get ship icons and the log gains a filter row (kills, damage, orders) as icons; no layout change otherwise.
7. **Settings.** Decision: set options. Rows of labels and toggles; text is the interface and the cost of a wrong read is low. What becomes visual: almost nothing; group headings and the UI scale preview (a sample line at the chosen size) are the only wins.
8. **Title screen and credits.** Decision: continue, new run, or quit. The menu itself is already spare (title capture set). The credits dialog is a scrolling text list by nature and by licence; leave it. What becomes visual: nothing needed; the continue line could carry the current run's headline figures (sector, battles) as one line, which is consolidation, not decoration.
9. **Campaign map and briefing rail.** Already orders sheet (R176, the worked example in screens.md): the map is the visual, the rail holds the decision, intel sits behind hovers. Not text-walled. What becomes visual: nothing in this job; it is the reference the other screens are judged against, with the battle HUD.
10. **Battle HUD.** The exemplar (E1 LEDGER, R210): one sheet, decision order, odds as the big number, everything else behind hovers (the moved-items table in screens.md is the model the debrief fix should copy). Ranked last; no work.

Not ranked for lack of a capture in evidence: the load screen (no decision, near no text) and the rename dialog (one field). The fresh ui_audit_1115 capture set should confirm both before the Board schedules anything below rank 3.

## The after-run screen: three directions

Shared content (the win capture's real data, used in all three): headline THE CRUSADE ENDURES; one verdict line; renown 42 earned, 76 in the vault, next unlock Command authority I at 35 (affordable) with 11 more within reach; Admiral Roderic Vex +21 XP, level 3, 31 of 50 XP, 19 to level 4; heir candidates Malek Draven (shadow 18.5 XP, double rate), Sera Ashgrove (10.25), Oryn Thale (7.25); core sync: K-1 Kharon crossed into Synced at 12 protocols, 12 of 25 to Full; V-2 Vyre crossed at 14, 14 of 25 to Full; Nemesis lesson: broke Seventh Maw, Elder Throat; weakness found: poor point defense; no weakness taught; recap: 6 battles won, 1 flagship destroyed, 24 enemy ships destroyed, 2 ships lost, 47 minutes; standout ship Judgement Bell, Vigil-class cruiser, Captain Aldo Serrano, 7 kills, 612 damage; captains lost: Ilse Marrow; victories so far 2; won with Sunward Crusade; next run suggestion: Ashfall Hullbreakers.

Every direction obeys the orders sheet: one ember accent on the action only, three text strengths, three faces, no panels (words on the scene's darkening), one box (the action), nothing under 17 px at 1080, headline plus three to five key items, and every sentence the current screen carries is either on the mockup or named in the notes with where it now lives. Nothing is deleted.

**DB1, The Ledger Page (consolidation first).** One screen, no scroll, laid out like the battle sheet grown up. Headline top left with the verdict line under it. Under that, the headline band: five big numbers in a row (battles won, flagships, destroyed, lost, time). The run timeline runs full width under the band: three sector nodes joined by a line, six battle ticks, the flagship kill as an ember-ringed burst (the one ember outside the action is a risk; the timeline marks it in bone white and reserves ember for the action box, per the system), the two losses as broken ticks. Left column under the timeline: renown as a vault meter (42 earned arrow into 76 total, the Command authority I threshold marked on the bar) and the admiral's XP meter. Right column: the heir decision as three portrait cards in a row, each with name, rank, and the shadow XP meter; the chosen card takes the hairline; below them the action box: CONFIRM HEIR. Bottom strip: the fleet that finished, ship pictures with kills and damage figures, the standout first and marked. Core sync, the Nemesis lesson and the record line sit behind three labelled keys at the foot (Sync S, Lesson L, Record R), each opening its overlay in the dialog style. Next run: a single line at the foot right with the suggested faction's crest and name; the action box becomes PLAY AS ASHFALL HULLBREAKERS once the heir is confirmed; Pick a faction and Title screen stay as words beside it.

**DB2, The Portrait Hall (people first).** The screen is about the line, so the people are the layout. Left third: Admiral Vex's painted portrait large, his name in the name face, level 3, the XP meter under it, and the renown vault meter under that. Centre: HEIR TO THE LINE as the head, three portrait cards stacked (portrait, name and rank, shadow meter, the double-rate note as an icon and figure), the current heir marked; the action box CONFIRM HEIR under them. Right third: the run in figures: the five big numbers stacked, then the timeline compressed to a vertical voyage strip (three sectors top to bottom, battle dots, flagship burst at the kill), then the standout ship's picture with its figures. Foot: three keys (Sync, Lesson, Record) left; the next run card right (Hullbreakers crest, name, one line, action box after confirm). The flavour sentences (the arc line, the record) appear once, under the headline, in the second strength; everything else is hover or key.

**DB3, The Voyage Chart (the run first).** The timeline is the hero: a full width chart under the headline, sectors as three long spans, every battle a node sized by enemy ships destroyed, the flagship kill as the largest node, losses as downward breaks, the Nemesis lesson pinned at the node where the weakness was found (Seventh Maw at its battle). Hovering a node shows its battle line (the sentences the debrief used to print). Under the chart, three columns: Numbers (the five figures, big), The Line (admiral meter, then the three heirs as compact rows with portraits at 48 px and meters; confirm box), The Spoils (renown vault meter with the unlock threshold, core sync as two icon rows with progress bars to Full sync, standout ship picture and figures). Foot: next run suggestion with crest and the two exit words. DB3 carries the most on one screen; its risk is density, held in check by the 17 px floor and the three strengths.

Chair's lean before the legs: DB1. It is the closest to the system the game already proved on the battle screen, it puts the decision (heir) and the action in one eyeline, and it is the cheapest to build honestly. DB2 is the emotional pick if Ryan wants the inheritance to feel like the point. DB3's chart is the best single visual of the three and could be lifted into DB1 later.
