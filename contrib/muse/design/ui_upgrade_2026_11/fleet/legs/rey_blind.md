# Blind Rey leg, issue 794 stage 3: Fleet and refits

Written before any council leg ran, from the issue 644 capture and the orders sheet.

The decision: which ships sail ready, which get repaired or refitted first, and which doctrine the fleet carries into the next battle. The comparison is the job: twelve ships judged against each other at a glance. Any design that hides eleven ships to detail one has traded the screen's real work for tidiness.

## Direction FL1, The Muster Grid (my lean)
Keep the grid, rebuild the card. Twelve cards, four across by three rows, no scroll at 1080. Card, top to bottom: a ship picture strip (the class's own art, wide and short); name in Big Shoulders with mark and class in TEXT2; the hull bar with figures (the bar is the repair state; missing segments read instantly, and a damaged card carries its repair cost as a figure, not a sentence); the five stats as one icon and figure row (shield glacier, the rest bone); the ability as an icon and name only, its rule on hover; weapons as compact rows, mount icon, name, damage figure, range figure, the full sentence on hover; the captain as a name over an XP meter (0/30 reads as an empty meter with the figure at its end); refits as two square slots, empty ones hairline, a filled one carrying its refit's icon, drag or click to move between ships as today. The doctrine banner becomes a strip over the grid: its icon, its name, one line, the cost figure, and Change as words with a key. The one box is DONE, as today, because the screen's actions are per ship and per slot, not one commit. Damaged ships sort to the front of the grid (Vigilant Ash and Cold Fury lead), because readiness order is the screen's story; the sort is stated in TEXT3 so it never reads as a ranking of worth.
Risk: the card is still dense. It is held by cutting sentences, not size: nothing on the card is a sentence except the doctrine line.

## Direction FL2, The Drydock Sheet
A left sheet at a fifth of the screen lists the twelve ships as rows (name, class, hull bar, CP, a status mark), and the rest of the screen details the selected ship: big picture, full weapon rows with their sentences, captain and perks, refit slots with the fleet's spare refits beside them, and the repair box as the one action when the ship is damaged. Best screen for doing one refit carefully. Worst for the screen's actual decision, which is comparative; it also duplicates the battle sheet's shape on a screen whose job is different.

## Direction FL3, The Ready Line
Readiness bands across the screen: READY, DAMAGED, UNDER REFIT, each band headed by its count as a big number. Cards inside a band compress to picture, name, hull bar, refit slots. Everything else (stats, weapons, captain) opens on the ship's own sheet behind a click. The strongest story and the clearest first read. Its cost: it invents a triage the rest of the game does not use, the band of a ship changes as you work, so the screen moves under the player's hands, and the full comparison now takes twelve clicks.

Chair's lean before the legs: FL1. It keeps the one thing the current screen gets right (the whole fleet visible at once) and fixes the actual complaint, which is that every fact on the card is written as a sentence and the ships have no faces.
