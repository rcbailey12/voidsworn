# #407 blind leg: Rey (written before the other legs and before web research)

Status of this leg: from the chair's own knowledge of these games. Every factual claim here is a lead for the sourced research, not evidence; the synthesis keeps only claims with a source link and marks the rest unsourced.

The eight games: Sins of a Solar Empire 2, BattleTech, Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader (space scenes), EVE Online, Star Citizen, Battlefleet Gothic: Armada, Star Wars: Squadrons, Elite Dangerous. The played camera for Voidsworn is the wide battle camera (camera 170 on the look bench); ships read 60 to 200 px. What only reads in a hangar or a trailer does not count.

## (a) Shields: at rest, on a hit, with a side down

- EVE Online: shields are invisible at rest. Activating a shield hardener or taking shield damage shows a translucent bubble skin tight-ish around the hull with a shimmering, slightly hexagonal ripple that spreads from the impact point. Faction/race identity comes from ship and effect colour more than shield shape. A depleted shield layer just stops appearing; the read is "the bubble stopped flashing", armour and hull effects take over. At fleet-fight zoom the bubble flash is one of the few reads that survives.
- Star Citizen: shields are faceted bubbles (front/back or more faces on larger ships). At rest invisible; on a hit the struck face lights as a glowing panel with a hex-like pattern and a ripple. A face that is down or recharging reads darker/redder on the HUD more than in the world; in the world you mostly see the absence of the flash on that side. Reads well because ships are big on screen; at Voidsworn distances the faceting would be lost and only the face-flash would survive.
- Elite Dangerous: a blue-white ellipsoid bubble, invisible at rest, that flares and ripples outward from the hit point. When shields drop there is a distinct collapse (the bubble shatters/fades with a flash and an audio cue), and while they recharge they stay down entirely, then snap back at partial strength. The recharge-down period is the clearest "side down" read of the eight, but it is whole-shield, not per-side.
- Sins of a Solar Empire 2: capital ships and starbases have shields shown as a brief bubble flash around the hull when hit; at rest nothing. Mitigation-era Sins has shield effects as a localised shimmer rather than a full bubble on smaller units. At Sins' normal zoom (a whole gravity well), even the bubble flash is near the floor of readability; the game's real shield read is UI (shield bars).
- Battlefleet Gothic: Armada: void shields are a flickering energy skin that absorbs hits with a bright localised flare and sparkle; when the shield is overloaded it drops and the ship takes hull hits with fires and debris until the shield regenerates. The flare reads because ships are few and large; the down state reads as "hits now spark on the hull, not above it".
- Star Wars: Squadrons: deflector shields flare as a translucent shell segment where the shot lands (front or rear hemisphere on shielded fighters). A down shield is communicated by HUD and by hits sparking on the hull. At fighter scale and speed this works; the shell segment flash is quick and small.
- Rogue Trader (space scenes): void shields read as a shimmering veil that flashes on weapon impact, Gothic-styled; when void shields are down the macro-cannon hits land on armour with explosions on the hull. Few ships, large on screen, slow pace: the flash can afford to be brief.
- BattleTech: no ship shields; ground 'Mechs. Its battles are not a shield reference. (Its relevance is section (b), weapon beams, and map skies.)

Reads at small size: the localised flash at the hit point, and hits changing character when the shield is down (spark on a bubble vs explosion on a hull). Turns to noise: full-bubble constant glows, hex faceting, recharge percentages.

## (b) Trails, beams and engine flares

- EVE Online: long ribbon engine trails, colour-coded by race (Minmatar orange, Caldari blue, etc.), are THE fleet-read: at full zoom-out a fleet is a set of moving coloured ribbons, and direction of travel reads instantly. Beams (lasers) are sustained coloured lines with a bright source flare; projectiles are near-invisible, missiles leave smoke trails.
- Elite Dangerous: engine trails are short vapour ribbons whose colour follows the drive and engineering (weapon/engine colour customisation is a store item, which tells you players read them as identity). Beam lasers are thick sustained beams with heat shimmer at the muzzle; the beam's endpoint blooms.
- Star Citizen: main thruster flares are big and bloom-heavy, manoeuvring thrusters puff visibly; in atmosphere, wingtip contrails. Quantum travel stretches the ship with a light tunnel. At normal combat distance the flare size relative to the hull is large, a deliberate readability choice.
- Sins 2: engine glow points, minimal trails; beams (TEC laser/beam weapons) are thin sustained lines. Sins is the counterexample: at strategy zoom, trails would smear, so it spends its readability budget on icons and UI instead.
- Squadrons: engine glow coloured by faction (Rebel orange, Imperial blue-green for TIEs in film canon reads), blaster bolts are short bright dashes with glow heads; proton torpedoes leave a glowing trail. Everything is sized for a cockpit at speed.
- Battlefleet Gothic: Armada: engine flares are modest; its signature is weapon effects: lance beams as thick sustained lances with a charging glow, macro-cannons as shell streams. Plasma drives leave a short wake on "All Ahead Full".
- Rogue Trader: lance strikes are heavy beams with a build-up; macro batteries are volleys of shells with tracer-like reads; engine flares are gothic-torched. Few ships, big effects.
- BattleTech: PPCs are lightning-like blue bolts, lasers are sustained thin beams coloured by size, autocannons tracer streams, LRMs arcing smoke trails in swarms. The LRM swarm trail is a readability standout: you see the whole arc and know what is coming.

Reads at small size: ribbon trails for movement and facing; beam thickness plus endpoint bloom for lances; swarm arcs for torpedoes/missiles. Noise: muzzle-flash detail, smoke particle detail, thruster puffs on small ships.

## (c) The sky behind a fleet battle

- EVE Online: the reference standard for identity skies: each region has its own nebula skybox, huge colour fields (blue Caldari space, red-brown Minmatar, green Gallente), so a screenshot's region is recognisable from the sky alone. Stars are sparse; the nebula IS the sky. At battle zoom the sky is calm and dark enough that ships and trails win.
- Elite Dangerous: the sky is computed from the ship's real galactic position: the Milky Way band, real nebulae placed astronomically, star density varying by location. Near a nebula it fills the sky; in deep space it is near-black with the galactic band. The calmest and darkest of the eight; effects always win.
- Sins 2: skyboxes are painted nebula/starfield backdrops per map, moderately colourful, dimmed so the well and its icons read. Functional, not a feature.
- Star Citizen: mixed: real starfield plus volumetric nebula clouds in places (e.g. gas clouds), and planets dominating some skies. The volumetric clouds are close-up material; from a battle camera they read as colour wash.
- Battlefleet Gothic: Armada: moody, dark, painterly skies: deep greens, purples and warp-tinted clouds, often with a planet or wreck field. The gothic mood carrier; darker than EVE.
- Rogue Trader: the Koronus Expanse void: dark skies with warp-tinted nebula colour and star fields; space scenes lean on gothic UI gold against dark void.
- Squadrons: skies are film sets: a Star Destroyer, a nebula, a planet, always a composed backdrop at cockpit distance. Least applicable to a wide strategy camera.
- BattleTech: skies are over ground battlefields (day, dusk, storms); not a space reference, except its weather and sky colour discipline for mood.

Reads at small size: one or two big calm colour fields (EVE) or near-black plus a band (Elite). Noise: detailed nebula texture, dense starfields, bright sky detail behind small ships (exactly Voidsworn's current speckle problem, audit pass 2 findings 10 to 12).

## Blind draft directions (to be tested against the legs and the sources)

Shields: (1) "Impact skin" (EVE + Elite): invisible at rest, a hull-hugging ripple flash at the hit point in faction colour, down side a dark gap where the flash no longer answers. (2) "Face panels" (Star Citizen): four face flashes, brighter, more gamey. (3) "Veil and breach" (BFG + Rogue Trader): a brief full veil flash on the first hit, then localised flares, down side marked by hull sparks and a lingering dark patch.
Trails/beams: (1) "Ribbons of allegiance" (EVE): faction-coloured engine ribbons sized by class, beams thick with endpoint bloom. (2) "Flare and dash" (Squadrons + Star Citizen): big engine flares, short bolt dashes, minimal ribbons. (3) "Gothic weight" (BFG + BattleTech): lance beams with charge-up glow, torpedo swarm arcs.
Sky: (1) "Regional colour" (EVE): one calm two-colour nebula identity per battle board. (2) "True dark" (Elite): near-black, galactic band, nebulae rare and earned. (3) "Warp mood" (BFG + Rogue Trader): dark painterly tint, one planet, mood over information.
