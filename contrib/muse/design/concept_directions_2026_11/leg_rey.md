# #417 blind leg: Rey (written before any picture, before the other legs)

The chair's read of all eighteen directions from the faction sheets alone: which looks strongest per faction, and the failure each direction is most likely to die of in the image model. The model failure modes from the #398 trial are the prior: counts inflate, aspect does not transfer, and anything the words leave vague gets invented in the model's favourite genre shape.

## Sunward Crusade
- A. Oath Blade. STRONGEST. The identity is already proven (mass and spire cluster from the current concepts); the job is subtractive, blade hull instead of boat hull. Failure mode: cathedral drift. The model hears spires plus black iron plus names of the dead and draws a 40K cathedral ship, which the sheet's Never list explicitly bans. Guard: spires described as flat radiator panels shedding heat, no arches, no windows like stained glass, no prow ornament.
- B. Forge. Strong second. Failure mode: flank radiator fins reading as wings or gills, and the slab prow reading as a barge. Guard: fins flat against the hull, stacked like heat sinks, never angled out.
- C. Reach Militia. The best story, the weakest silhouette risk: a converted hauler can read as a generic cargo ship with guns glued on and lose the Crusade entirely. Guard: the black iron and ember spires must dominate the cargo bones, not decorate them.

## Hegemony Navy
- A. Clean Wedge. Failure mode is documented in the sheet itself: yacht and submarine drift (tall sail tower, stepped cabin tiers). The wedge survives only if the fin stays low and the deck stays one plane.
- B. Module Line. STRONGEST. The sameness is the faction's whole character, it photographs well (repetition reads at the battle camera), and it is the direction the build pipeline will thank us for. Failure mode: modules reading as shipping containers or a train. Guard: modules as hull sections with seams and rings, varied in length, never box cars.
- C. Drone Mother. Failure mode: the drone halo turning into flies, sparkles or a starfield at concept size, and the mother hull reading as an airport terminal. Guard: drones few (a dozen), large enough to read as machines, in a flat ring formation.

## Ashfall Hullbreakers
- A. Scrap Brick. STRONGEST on reliability: the current look minus the sea-ship parts. Failure mode: the model helpfully re-adding a mast, a crane or a keel (it did exactly that on every C15s picture), or cleaning the ship up into symmetry. Guard: name the replacements (dishes, scoops, claws) and ban the originals by name.
- B. Pack Hunter. The character pick. Failure mode: literal animal anatomy, ears and jaws reading as a cartoon wolf head. Guard: keep it a ship that leans and listens, anatomy as posture only, jaws as boarding claws that open sideways, never teeth.
- C. Den Ship. Failure mode: an asteroid with lights glued on, no ship read at all, and engines chained to stone reading as decoration. Guard: the rock must be clearly hollowed and worked, gun pits and decks cut into it, engines visibly mounted in a frame.

## The Glasswrights
- A. Collector. Failure mode: satellite dish. A forward mirror dish on a slim hull is one step from a NASA probe or a radio telescope, and the dish can also read as a sail, which the sheet bans in spirit. Guard: hexagonal facets, rigid ring mount, the hull clearly a warship behind it, lance at the focus.
- B. Prism. STRONGEST. One long three-sided crystal shard is the plainest weapon of the three and the least likely to read as a prop or an instrument someone left floating. Failure mode: jewel or perfume-bottle read, all beauty and no mass or menace. Guard: scale cues (the escort), surface damage kept at zero but mass emphasised, small mirror fins as gun mounts.
- C. Beam Line. Failure mode: space station or laboratory bench, fragile and stationary, no ship read. Guard: the truss must read as a hull spine under tension, engines as the stern optic bloom, rings thick and armoured at their rims.

## Eclipse
- A. Crescent. STRONGEST. Head-on it is the eclipse itself, the most instant icon of the six factions' eighteen directions. Failure mode: logo read, a crescent moon with a ship behind it, or a sickle. Guard: the crescent is layered plates with thickness and gaps, the body tucked inside the curve, rim light a thin line only.
- B. Fan. Failure mode: parasol or peacock fan, decorative and soft. Guard: plates overlapping like armour scales swept back, sharp trailing edges, nothing feathery.
- C. Annulus. Failure mode: portal or stargate, the ring as a doorway with a ship behind it. Guard: ring of separate plates with visible gaps and drones working the gaps, core offset from the ring's centre axis by the spar's angle, never a clean circle.

## The Chorus
- A. Leviathan. STRONGEST. Scale and menace are the brief, and a vast smooth hide with light inside it is drawable without segments. Failure mode: whale, literal and almost noble, or a slide back into grub segments. Guard: no tail fluke, no eye, no mouth line; ridges as chitin armour, scars and regrowth breaking the smoothness.
- B. Bloom. Failure mode: prettiness. A sea lily or jellyfish in space reads as a screensaver, and open membranes can blur into the Glasswright sail read the owner just killed. Guard: membranes thick and fleshy, crowned around a dark throat, never translucent-thin or symmetrical like petals.
- C. Overgrowth. The scariest on paper and the hardest rule to keep: the swallowed warship's outline must read while no metal shows anywhere. Failure mode: the model pokes bare metal through the hide (a rule break) or buries the outline entirely (a lump). Guard: outline carried by shape only, hide unbroken over every surface, bone and chitin following the dead ship's lines.

## Chair's prior, for the record
If the pictures matched the words exactly, my picks would be Crusade A, Hegemony B, Hullbreakers A (B as the risk worth taking), Glasswrights B, Eclipse A, Chorus A. The pictures will not match the words exactly, which is the point of drawing them.
