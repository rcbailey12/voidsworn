# #445 blind leg: Rey (written before any picture, before the other legs)

Job: concept round 3. Three armed variations per faction, each built ON the picked base (round 2 sheet as the reference picture), so the family holds and only the variation changes. The round's new law is gate line 1 rewritten: it must READ as armed in the top view at thumbnail size. My task in this leg: for each faction, how to make the weapons read from above without scattering guns, and which variation is most likely to break the gate and how to stop it.

Cross-cutting chair priors, from round 2's evidence:

1. The base sheet goes in as the reference picture and the prompt's first job is "same ship, same plan, same proportions"; the variation's change is stated as a short list of deltas. Every round 2 failure came from the model redesigning; the base-as-reference is the fix the brief itself orders.
2. "Armed" reads through three things only: barrels that break the hull outline, one oversized forward weapon, and symmetry you can see at thumbnail size. Recessed, flush, port-style weapons failed Ryan's eye in round 2 even when they were exactly right by the table, so this round every mount gets visible barrels or lenses, stated as objects with size words ("short thick barrels standing out of the gallery, breaking the outline"), never as openings or ports.
3. Flak is the scattering risk. The brief now allows two to four small matching turrets in symmetric pairs as ONE flak system. Chair rule for the prompts: flak turrets are always placed as named symmetric pairs at named stations ("one pair on the shoulders, one pair flanking the command block"), identical, and the prompt says "these matching turrets are one flak system, the only small turrets on the ship".
4. Counts still inflate. State the closed set twice (once in the ship description, once as a closing line), and count turret OBJECTS, not barrels: a twin-barrel turret is one mount.
5. Escorts: round 2's universal fault. This round the escort is specified by what it LACKS (no radiator bank, no flak pairs, no crown, no ring) as much as by what it has, and the beauty prompt names the escort's one or two mounts and stops.

Per faction:

Sunward Crusade (base: Codex's S1, chunky dagger, organ-pipe radiators, two bells). Most at risk: S6 Flak Throughout. Four extra turrets is exactly Ryan's round 1 complaint ("way too many random guns") if they land scattered. Guard: the four flak turrets are two identical pairs at two named stations (shoulders of the hull, symmetric about the centre line), same size, same shape, and the sheet prompt says "ordered in pairs, nothing else small on the hull". The galleries read armed by giving each flank ONE row of six to eight short thick barrels standing proud of the gallery and breaking the outline, stated as a row, not individual guns. The lance is "the largest object on the bow, on a braced mount". S4 is the control; S5's cross-guard must stay a section of the same hull (round 2's S2 try 2 split into two blades when the guard was emphasised; guard phrase: "a wider section of the same continuous hull, not a separate part").

Hegemony Navy (base: my H1 Kit Wedge). Most at risk: H5 Wedge and Twin Rail, because round 2's H3 multiplied turrets on the rails (four objects for two mounts, marked FAIL). Guard: "exactly two turbolaser turrets in the whole picture, both on the wedge section ahead of the split, none on the rails"; the rails carry only the hangar between them and the engine rows. For H4: the turrets get "long twin barrels" and size ("each turret as wide as a module"), the ion prongs "heavier, the largest objects on the bow". No loose drones beside the ship this round (brief), which also removes round 2's drone-grid scale cue, so panel and hatch size words carry line 10 alone.

Ashfall Hullbreakers (base: my A1 Scrap Brick). Most at risk: A6 Chained Brick, because "add-ons hung unevenly down one side" invites the model to unbalance the guns and to scatter fittings that read as guns. Guard: "the uneven cargo cages and chains hang on the port flank BELOW the casemate line; the scatter racks, torpedo tubes and flak stay symmetric and balanced; nothing hanging looks like a barrel". The scatter battery reads as "one visible rack of many short barrels" per flank: a rack is one mount drawn as a comb, which reads armed without adding objects. Colour correction from R178 applies to all three: patches are rust, bare steel and old paint, never camouflage yellow or olive; and exactly two hangar mouths (round 2 drew four pits; guard: "two hangar mouths, one low on each flank, and no other dark rectangular pits").

The Glasswrights (no base; new direction: the ship itself is the optic, no separate crystal object). Most at risk: G6 Aperture, because "lens rings set into the hull seen through open bays" is one step from the telescope reading that killed a round 1 direction. Guard: the prompt builds the warship first (faceted mirror-silver hull, command block, engines, bow point) and the rings are "armour-ringed lens mounts flush in the hull's back, like turret rings, not a tube through the ship"; if try 1 reads as an instrument tube, deliver G6 as nearest with the failure marked rather than burning all three tries. G4 is G3's hull with the weapons made large: "two LARGE bow lenses" with size words, lens turrets as armoured iris mounts (a ring with a visible aperture, reads as a gun at thumbnail size where a dome did not). G5's mirror tiles must lie flush like armour scales; guard against them standing up as solar panels or wings: "flat hexagonal plates flush on the hull surface, no gaps, no raised edges, angled only in their mirror faces". No rivets (Ryan's word in R178 via the brief), no crystal object anywhere, rainbow fringe stays off this round's grammar: light is stored white in seams, lenses and bow point.

Eclipse (base: my E2 Crescent). Most at risk: E6 Closed Ring, because closing the horns into a full ring walks back toward round 2 E1's failure (ring as flat halo, ship behind a ring). Guard: the ring is "a thick armoured ring in the plane of the hull, joined to the crescent body at two points, the doom cannon on the centre line firing through its centre, the hull's volume visible inside and behind it"; in plan it must read as the bow of the ship, not a halo around empty space. E4 fixes round 2 E2's two faults by brief: real engines (a tight cluster of dark drive cones with a green rim, replacing the detached hoop that never attached) and a thickened spine with volume. The gauss turrets go "large and plain on the upper centre line" (the brief moves them off the horns onto the centre line behind the crescent; follow the brief, not round 2's placement). Green stays rim line and core only; E2's beauty fired the core as a thick beam, so the sheet prompt repeats "the core is a small bright point at the focus, not a beam".

The Chorus (base: my C1 Bloom Mouth; leaner, whale-scale, not plump). Most at risk: C6 Bone-Backed, because pale bone plates and ribs along the back can slide into the Eclipse bone Never territory and into a skeleton or fossil reading, and "ribs" is a banned word picture for Eclipse, not Chorus, but the visual risk is real. Guard: "pale bone plates grown over the hide like armour scutes, smooth and overlapping, no bare ribcage, no skull shapes, the body still smooth and grown underneath". The leanness guard for all three: length to beam stated as numbers with "long and lean, whale-scale" and the round 1 grub failure named ("not plump, not segmented, not a grub"). Weapons as organs, plain to see: the two throats visible inside the open bloom (petal-jaws held open, not folded shut as in C1's sheet, so the throats show), the tendril crown as one cluster on the upper centre line, the spore vents as a visible field of raised pores; C5 swaps the spore organ for C2's dorsal crown, so its tendrils must move or shrink to keep the count of organs at the table's four.

Chair's order of work: sheets first from the base references, judged at thumbnail size for the armed read before anything else; beauties from accepted sheets; the escort rule enforced by naming absences. Marked failures expected somewhere in: S6 (scatter risk), H5 (turret multiplication), G6 (telescope risk), E6 (halo risk), C6 (fossil risk). A marked failure costs nothing; an unmarked one costs the round.
