# Eclipse class family: the mote and the flare (issue 627)

Owner: muse. Accepted by Ryan, 2026-11-12. Family anchors: E11 The Unsetting (tomb-ark, crescent) and E12 zenith (cruiser, ring), both accepted earlier and building under issue 618. Codes continue the E sequence: E13 mote, E14 flare.

Family language (from the Eclipse faction sheet, data.js and the accepted anchors): dark violet-black layered plate, the green-white corona line on shade rims, a small bright trapped-light core, a forward shade the body hides behind, guns firing through gaps in the shade, the eclipse-ring drive of pale light with no flame, and layered breaks with anchors on the shade bands. Never a spinning top, never a spire, never bone, never a pooled green glow. Each class is its own concept inside that language (Ryan's standing rule, 2026-11-12: family language without shape cloning).

## E13, mote (data id d_scythe), frigate, game length 6.5, size 0.5

The Fan. A short dart body almost hidden under five long overlapping shade plates. The plates root together at the bow and fan out and sweep back over the body like the blades of a hand fan, each plate a separate layered slab with its own green-white rim line, their tips reaching past the body's sides toward the stern. The core is a small bright point of trapped light glowing in a gap between the plates near their root, partly covered, never a bare bulb. One gun only (data: gauss turret): a low housing on the body's spine at the fan's root, firing forward over the plates' root; the beauty underplays it and the sheet draws it clearly. The drive is a small thin eclipse ring of pale light at the stern, no flame.

Why it is its own class: the flagship is a crescent, the cruiser and the destroyer are rings, the frigate is the fan. At battle size it reads as layered swept blades over a bright heart, the fastest-looking shape in the family, which fits the data (speed 4, evasion 14, the family's skirmisher). Image-to-image seed: the round 1 Fan direction (deathless_B), which Ryan rated in R172 ("Fan is definitely good").

Superseded mote directions, all ruled by Ryan on 2026-11-12: the scythe (DeepSeek's leg, from the data id) rejected as comical and not Eclipse themed; Annulus takes (an open arc, then a compact arc) rejected because the mote felt the same as the flare; crescent and blade takes rejected with the earlier rounds.

## E14, flare (data id d_jackal), destroyer, game length 9.5, size 0.62

The Annulus. A full broken ring of large curved shade plates with clear gaps between them, riding around a layered spar body, taken directly from the round 1 Annulus direction (deathless_C) that Ryan named for this class. At the ring's heart the spar carries the gun heart: the core held in a broken cage of small shade plates, green-white (the accepted beauty drew it teal; colour is fixed in the build per R204), and beside it the fixed forward gun (data: gauss fixed) as a dark housing with a recessed aperture and a thin green focus ring, the muzzle set back inside the housing, never a protruding tube. The second gun (data: gauss turret) is a low housing on the spar's dorsal line aft of the ring. Repair drones with green lights work the ring's gaps. The drive is the eclipse ring of pale light at the stern, larger than the frigate's, no flame.

Why it is its own class: the cruiser E12 is also a ring ship, and the difference is the heart. E12's ring carries its core and guns distributed on the ring; the flare's ring is a pure shade and everything that matters, core and main gun together, sits caged at the centre of the ring on the spar. At battle size the flare reads as a broken halo with a gun at its heart; the cruiser reads as a ring with a body. Ryan, 2026-11-12: "The flare is what I was thinking of for the annulus."

Superseded flare directions, ruled by Ryan on 2026-11-12: the split shade with flanking parenthesis blades (four generator rounds) rejected, the final one as not fitting the theme at all; the chaired split-shade text design (DeepSeek's chevron-banded horns) was never pictured to acceptance and is superseded by the Annulus ruling.
