## 1. Headline

The shipped battle tips are good text in a weak order: at battle open, "start", "turns", and "select" can queue back to back before the player has made a single decision, because the current checker (battle.gd, _tutorial) walks the TIPS list in order and "turns" is true from the first second. This design keeps nearly all the shipped text, re-times it so each card is earned by an action or an event, adds one new card (the reinforcement warning), and gives the pre-written campaign cards a way to appear on the screens they describe. The most important sequencing choice is that no two cards ever wait at once: after the opening card, every prompt fires only when the player has just done, or just seen, the thing it explains.

## 2. Prompt schedule

In play order. Texts in quotes are exact.

1. Moment: run start, the sector map opens for the first time. Trigger: map screen shown, c_map unseen. Where: campaign card on the map screen. Text: "Choose a connected location on the map to move forward. You cannot visit every location."
2. Moment: same map visit, behind the first card. Trigger: c_map dismissed, c_nodes unseen. Where: campaign card. Text: "The map marks ordinary fights, tougher elite fleets, the sector boss, events, rest stops, and shipyards. Choose what your fleet needs."
3. Moment: first time the player hovers or selects an elite location. Trigger: an elite location is highlighted, c_elite unseen. Where: campaign card on the map screen. Text: "Elite fleets are tougher enemies with better rewards. Each sector offers an elite fight on a route you can choose."
4. Moment: battle 1 opens. Trigger: existing condition start. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "start", unchanged): "The game hands you one ship at a time: the one marked UP, with the gold ring. Blue hexes show where it can move, and the fans show its firing arcs. The card at the bottom says what it can still do."

5. Moment: battle 1, just after the first enemy ship finishes its first turn. Trigger: new condition enemy_acted, replacing the current always-true "turns" condition, which is why the card currently piles up at the open. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "turns", unchanged): "You and the enemy take turns one ship at a time: activate one of yours, then the enemy activates one of theirs, until every ship has acted. Then a new round starts."
6. Moment: battle 1, just after the player's first confirmed move. Trigger: new condition first_move, the existing moved check latched at the moment a move is carried out. Where: battle tip card. Text (new, replacing the shipped "select" body; its ghost and confirm steps are dropped because the bottom card and the ghost already teach them): "Where a ship stops matters. If it ends its turn in the hex it started in, enemy shots against it get 10 extra hit chance, and turning in place with Q and E does not count as moving. Moving 3 or more hexes cuts enemy hit chance against it by 5. Bracing for impact cancels the penalty for staying in place."
7. Moment: battle 1, first time an enemy can be hit. Trigger: existing condition target. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "target", unchanged): "Enemies you can hit have a red ring. Click one to target it: the odds and expected damage show. Click it again (or Enter) to fire every weapon that bears. Clicking an enemy never fires by itself. Keys 1 to 6 pick a single weapon."
8. Moment: battle 1, just after the first weapon fires. Trigger: existing condition fired. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "fired", unchanged; the direct answer to the owner's "what do I do after firing"): "Each ship acts once per round. When this ship is done, press End ship (Space) and the enemy moves one of theirs. A ship with nothing left to do ends by itself."
9. Moment: first time any player ship's shields drop below full. Trigger: new condition shield_hit, replacing the current moved trigger on the shields card, so the card arrives with the first hit it explains. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped shields text, unchanged): "The cyan bars on the ship panel are its shields: four facings that soak hits and recharge each turn. Amber is hull: damage that stays. Keep your strongest shield facing the enemy." Expected in battle 1; slips to battle 2 if no shield is hit.
10. Moment: round 2 of the first battle that reaches it. Trigger: existing condition threat (round 2 or later). Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "threat", unchanged): "Before you end a move, check where the enemy can reach. Press R, or Threat in the top bar, to shade every hex their guns can hit next turn: the darker the hex, the more damage is waiting there."
11. Moment: after battle 1, the first reward offer screen. Trigger: reward screen opens, c_boon unseen. Where: campaign card on the reward screen. Text (lint-corrected, title "Protocol offers"): "A core offers protocols: bonuses lasting until this run ends. Rarer ones are stronger. Decline for credits. If another offer is available and you can pay, replace the choices once; the price rises with each replacement this run."
12. Moment: the first wreck strip step after a fight. Trigger: strip screen opens, c_salvage unseen. Where: campaign card on the strip screen. Text (lint-corrected): "After a fight, work one wreck for a refit or credits if offered. Hullbreakers may also tow an eligible small wreck into their fleet at 35% hull if they have room."
13. Moment: battle 2, round 2, when the reinforcement warning posts. Trigger: new condition event_warned; battle 2 of a first run always carries enemy reinforcements (section 4), and the rules already toast and log the warning a round ahead (battle.js:22-31). Where: battle tip card, after the warning toast. Text (new): "More enemy ships are coming. The warning names the round: at the start of that round, an enemy ship jumps in on their side of the field. Use the time before it to finish damaged enemies or pull exposed ships back." The arrival needs no card; the F1 banner announces it.
14. Moment: first time the admiral Command can be used. Trigger: existing condition resolve. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "resolve", unchanged): "Your admiral's signature move is ready: it glows gold in the top bar. You get it once per battle. It grows each round you wait and when you lose a ship, so pick your moment."
15. Moment: first time the selected ship can ram or board. Trigger: existing condition close. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "close", unchanged): "Ramming speed (X) charges the first enemy dead ahead. Boarding (G) sends crews into an adjacent enemy; win and the ship is yours."
16. Moment: first fight whose goal is not wiping out the enemy fleet. Trigger: existing condition objective. Where: battle tip card. Text (shipped "objective", unchanged): "Not every fight is a slugging match. The top bar shows this fight's goal."
17. Moment: the first shipyard screen. Trigger: shipyard screen opens, c_shipyard unseen. A shipyard is guaranteed in the middle column of every sector (core.js, genSector), so this lands around battle 3. Where: campaign card on the shipyard screen. Text (lint-corrected): "Buy ships with credits if they fit within your fleet command-point limit. Pay to repair each ship in full for less than its replacement price. Fit equipment upgrades you have stored here."

Why more than 12: nine of these seventeen are shipped cards with shipped text, and five more are campaign cards already written and lint-corrected; the only new writing is the positioning card (a rewrite) and the reinforcement card. The count is high because the teaching surface is wide. Cutting further means dropping a screen the firstrun audit called untaught (map, reward, shipyard) or the reinforcement card that answers a named owner confusion. Left out on purpose: the Nemesis hunt meter card (c_clock) waits until the meter first fills, because after battle 1 it cannot yet threaten the player; prize, Heat, renown, and run-loss cards wait for their own first screens in later sectors.

## 3. Fade rules

Battle cards keep the current retirement rule, which the code already implements: a card is marked seen only when the player presses "Got it" (battle.gd, the tip_ok action writes the id into the seen set and saves), and nothing retires because its moment passed; an unread card stays pending until read once. Campaign cards retire the same way, once per profile, stored under their own key. Nothing in this design is per-run: re-teaching the map at the start of every run would punish exactly the players who learned it.

Returning players: battle_opts.json gains a lastPlayed timestamp, written on every save. If the gap since the last save is over 30 days, four ids are cleared from the seen set at load: turns, target, fired, and reinf. Those four answer "what happens next" and "what do I do after firing", the confusions the owner himself reported; each re-fires once, under its normal trigger, in the next run. The opening card does not re-fire, because the bottom card repeats its content every turn. All other cards stay retired.

## 4. First battle setup

Enemy faction: Hegemony. Two reasons from the code. First, every Hegemony roster hull carries shields (data.js:74-77: picket sh 10, interdictor sh 18, vanguard sh 24, dominion sh 32), so the player's first targeted shots visibly drain a shield bar before hull, setting up the shields card (entry 9) in the same fight. Second, home ground: FAC_MAP gives Hegemony the station map, while Hullbreakers get belt or graveyard (battle.js:59). A station is one built structure to read; asteroid and wreck fields would add terrain reading to the first fight, and the owner already found move hexes hard to tell from terrain. Sector factions are picked from SECTOR_POOL (data.js:130) at core.js:276, and most battle locations take the sector's main faction (core.js:273), so a first run sets sector 1's main faction to Hegemony.

Map shape: leave shape generation alone. startBattle already turns an "open" shape into "compact" for small fleets in rout-type fights, so battle 1 is close quarters with no special case, and the dead star is already impossible in sector 1 (battle.js:311-312).

Fleet sizes: change nothing, per R62. The player brings their chosen starting fleet of 4 to 7 command points (2 to 4 ships). The enemy budget at the first column is (4.2 + 0.9) times the sector growth of 1.2, then scaled by the player's fleet power inside a 0.75 to 1.3 clamp (battle.js, genEnemyFleet): roughly 3 to 4 enemy ships. Teaching comes from order and text, never from a weaker enemy.

Reinforcements: none in battle 1, forced in battle 2, unforced from battle 3 on. Mid-battle events are rolled per fight (45 percent chance, never in boss fights) and announced a round ahead (battle.js:22-31, rollBattleEvent and eventTick). Battle 1 of a first run suppresses the roll, so the first fight teaches only the core loop. Battle 2 forces enemy reinforcements: the warning posts at round 2, the ship jumps in at the start of round 3 or 4, entry 13 fires at the warning, and the F1 banner covers the arrival. Enemy rather than friendly, because the owner's confusion was about enemy arrivals, and a friendly ship would soften the fight, which R62 forbids. The forced ship is the enemy's cheapest roster hull at sector 1 scaling (battle.js, eventTick), so battle 2 gets harder by one small ship, not easier.

## 5. Between battles

The campaign texts used are entries 1, 2, 3, 11, 12, and 17 above, all quoted there in their lint-corrected forms (contrib/opus/compute/content_lint_2026_11_01.md, items 106 to 119); the assembled originals must not be used, since they carry retired words and two factual errors the lint names. The first map choice gets entries 1 and 2, the first reward entry 11, the first strip step entry 12, and the first shipyard entry 17. Rest stops need no new text in this window: the first rest stop sits in the column before the boss, and entry 17 already teaches repair; the after-boss regroup card belongs to the sector 2 window.

Smallest delivery mechanism: copy the battle checker's pattern, not its code path. godot/ui/campaign/campaign_ui.gd gains a CAMPAIGN_TIPS table in the same [id, title, body] shape and a check function called from the four places that open the map, reward, strip, and shipyard screens; it shows the first unseen card whose screen matches, one at a time, built from the campaign UI's own panel and button helpers with the same two buttons as the battle card (hud.gd, show_tipcard): "Got it" marks the id seen, "Hide tips" sets the shared off flag. Campaign seen ids persist in the same user://battle_opts.json under a new "ctips" key; tipsOff stays shared. One catch: battle.gd's _save_opts currently writes only its own three keys (tips, tipsOff, speed), which would erase ctips on the next battle save; both writers must read the file, change their keys, and write the whole dictionary back.

## 6. The off switch

The existing provision is closer to enough than it looks: every card already carries a "Hide tips" button that turns all tips off permanently (battle.gd, the tip_off action), and battle options holds the "Battle tips: on/off" toggle. Two gaps remain. First, the toggle lives only inside battle options, so a player cannot switch campaign cards off from the map before they start; the same toggle must appear in the campaign screen's menu, driving the same flag. Second, there is no way back from a hasty "Hide tips"; add a separate "Show tips again" action in battle options that clears the seen sets (tips and ctips) without touching the off flag.

What "off" must mean: no tip cards, in battle or on campaign screens. It must never touch the F1 banners (reinforcements, enemy turn) or the bottom card. Those are the interface the owner asked for when he could not tell what was happening; they are information, not teaching overlays.


## 7. Build list

1. battle.gd, TIPS table: rename the id "select" to "position" with the new body in entry 6, and rename "move" to "shields" (body unchanged); existing profiles will see each renamed card once more.
2. battle.gd, _tutorial: add conditions enemy_acted, first_move, shield_hit, and event_warned; point turns at enemy_acted, position at first_move, shields at shield_hit; add the "reinf" card on event_warned.
3. battle.js, rollBattleEvent, and campaign3d.js, where the battle config is built: honour a new noEvent config flag by returning null; on a first run, set it for the first battle and pass the existing force argument for the second, forcing reinf_enemy.
5. core.js, run and sector setup: on a profile with no finished run, set sector 1's main faction to Hegemony where RUN.foes is picked (core.js:276).
6. battle.gd, _load_opts and _save_opts: preserve unknown keys on write, and add ctips and lastPlayed, including the 30-day re-arm of turns, target, fired, and reinf.
7. godot/ui/campaign/campaign_ui.gd: add CAMPAIGN_TIPS with the lint-corrected texts, the screen-open checks, and the card wired to the shared store.
8. Battle options and the campaign menu: share the one tips toggle across both, and add "Show tips again" in battle options.
9. Verification: on a fresh profile, script the first three battles and confirm from the F1 play log that cards fire in schedule order; if the log does not record tip ids, add the id to the log line when a card shows.

## 8. Not checked

- Whether the admiral Command can first become ready during battle 1; the pack gives no starting charge, so entry 14 is trigger-based and may land in battle 1 rather than battle 2.
- Whether battles 2 and 3 of a sector 1 run offer a ram or boarding chance, or a goal other than wiping out the enemy; entries 15 and 16 are trigger-based and may first fire after battle 3. The design does not force them, because forcing encounters would change the fights, not just teach them.
- Whether a wreck strip step follows every won battle or only some; entry 12 fires at the first strip step whenever that happens.
- Whether the warned flag on a battle event is visible through the same rules view the Godot battle scene reads; entry 13 assumes event_warned can be built from state the scene already joins.
- Whether a settings menu exists outside the battle and campaign screens; section 6 assumes the campaign screen has, or gains, a small menu for the shared toggle.
- Hullbreaker hull shield values were not read hull by hull; the Hegemony pick rests on Hegemony's shields and home ground, not on any claim about Hullbreaker hulls.
