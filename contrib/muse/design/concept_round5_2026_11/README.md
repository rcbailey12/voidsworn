# Concept round 5: the Glasswrights fresh from the lore (issue #476), the Hullbreaker mixes (issue #477)

Ryan scrapped the Glasswright grammar on Nov 11, 2026 (FINDINGS R183): four rounds began with a crystal and tried to make it a ship, and all of them are dropped. This round begins with the people: the finest optical engineers there ever were, and a ship that is a precision instrument built as a warship. Polished silver, clear glass, stored light inside, weapons as lens domes. No crystal block, no prism, no shard, no gem anywhere in these three routes. They run against round 4's G9 Wedge, which Ryan called the best Glasswright so far (R184); the Board and Ryan compare.

Two gate rules are new this round and apply to every route: (1) the forward weapon is built the faction's own way, never a long barrel or prongs on the nose tip (for the Glasswrights, twin lens apertures in the shoulders of the bow); (2) no two factions share an outline from above, and the identity must show from above, because the battle camera looks straight down.

## The lens fault from round 4 is fixed, with a new fault in exchange

Round 4's lenses rendered as spoked fan discs. The Board's instruction was to describe each lens as a smooth dome of clear glass in a plain silver ring and never mention iris parts at all. That worked: every lens in this round is a smooth dome, countable, no spokes anywhere. The new fault: on G10 the domes render as polished CHROME, reflective metal rather than clear glass. On G11 they read as glass; on G12 they read as iridescent glass with more rainbow than the thin fringe the brief allows.

## The G12 swap (chair's call, stated per the brief)

The brief named three routes and allowed the chair to swap one if the blind leg found better. The blind leg argued the Board's third route, the Racing Hull, was the weakest of the three (a polished blade whose top view is a glowing line in a metal bar, one step from the prism this round exists to escape) and proposed the Beam Line in its place: the ship drawn as the beam line the Glasswrights actually built. Grok and Flash both endorsed the swap in their legs; the writer leg wrote prompts for both routes, preserved in leg_deepseek.md. G12 below is the Beam Line. The Racing Hull was not drawn.

## G10. The Unfinished (open frame round stored light)

Sheet: ithar_G10_sheet.jpg. Beauty: ithar_G10_beauty.jpg.

The flagship's name taken at its word: the aft half is a finished polished silver hull with the engine block; the forward half is open structure, slender polished ribs and longerons with no skin, holding one long clear glass vessel of soft white stored light, plain to see from above. The two bow lenses sit at the front ends of the outer longerons.

Chair verdict: the strongest route of the three, and the beauty is the round's best picture. In three dimensions the open frame reads as an instrument mount, machined and deliberate, never wreckage; the light is visibly the ship and the frame its mount, which is the flagship's story (built round the largest surviving piece of the Lattice) told in one image. The escort is a true smaller family ship: short frame, one dome, its own small vessel.

Marked failures: the sheet's frame is a regular GRID, graph paper regularity, rather than varied ribs and longerons, so a mild cage read survives in plan (lines 8 and 12, partial). The bow domes render chrome, not clear glass (line 12). The three stern drive rings render as stacked glowing pads on the sheet and as a glowing band in the beauty (line 3). One extra white disc fitting sits on the beauty's frame. Side view moves the gun turret to the hull joint and shows one bow dome.

## G11. The Mirror Deck

Sheet: ithar_G11_sheet.jpg (try 2). Beauty: ithar_G11_beauty.jpg.

The upper surface is one great mirror: a long spearhead of flat hexagonal mirror tiles lying flat to the battle camera, one piece with the hull, the silver hull and a glass keel of stored light beneath. The five weapons sit in round cut-outs in the mirror, the two small turrets in dark mounts so they stay countable.

Chair verdict: the strongest TOP VIEW of the round, which is the view the game is judged from: a bright tiled spearhead is an outline no other faction owns, and in the beauty the tiles catch starlight with rainbow only as sparkles at the edges. Try 1 was rejected: the bow dome pair was duplicated at the stern, five domes and seven mounts in all (line 1 FAIL, recorded in prompts.md).

Marked failures: the stern renders six glowing ports in a 2x3 grid instead of three drive rings (line 3). One unexplained horizontal cylinder lies across the stern deck in plan. The beauty's ESCORT IS A SHRUNK COPY of the hero, full mirror deck, own turrets, same stern grid: the rounds 2 and 3 escort fault returning on this one picture.

## G12. The Beam Line (swapped in for the Racing Hull)

Sheet: ithar_G12_sheet.jpg (try 2). Beauty: ithar_G12_beauty.jpg.

The ship as the beam line the Glasswrights built: a long polished silver bench of a hull with a blunt instrument head, a row of optical stations on the centre line (the stored light vessel at the stern third feeding the line, the dome turret as a ring station amidships, ring collars toward the bow, the twin lenses in the head's shoulders), a real engine block, command block and armoured shoulders so it reads as a ship first.

Chair verdict: the weakest drawing of the three, kept because the route's identity is real and the failure is in the rendering, not the idea. Try 1 was rejected on format (portrait sheet, plan rotated bow up, hull about 7:1), but its station line was the clearest of any try this round: rings and collars reading as a ruled diagram. Try 2 fixed the format and gave the bench a real hull beneath it, at the cost of the plan reading as a chrome bar with fittings (line 8, partial), and the beauty reads as industrial pipe with iridescent bubble domes, rainbow heavier than a fringe (line 12, partial). Escort is a shrunk copy. Three small lamps in an open trough at the very bow are extra fittings. Build note: if this route is ever built, the stations must read as try 1's ruled line, not try 2's rings on a tube.
