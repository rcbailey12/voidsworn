# Blind Rey leg, issue #477 (written before any picture)

The question: which parts of A7 and which parts of A9 make the Hullbreaker flagship, and how is the bow built without A7's giant grey block?

## What makes the ship, part by part

**From A7: the body and the weight.** The broad stacked-slab hull is the flagship. Three things in A7 carry it: (1) the WIDTH, a barge read at a glance, roughly 3:1 or broader, which no other faction owns from above; (2) the deck density, plates on plates with tanks, drums, vents and patches, so the top view is busy everywhere and no large plain surface exists anywhere (this matters for the bow fix below); (3) the three big engine bells, mismatched, which sell "built from wrecks" at the stern where the camera rarely lingers but the silhouette shows.

**From A9: the dress and the silhouette.** (1) The chains and cages down one flank: the single most Hullbreaker object in either picture, because it is cargo handling as identity, and it breaks the outline on one side only (lopsided in add-ons, exactly what the gate allows). (2) The pale trophy slab: one flat pale note on a red and black ship, a story in one object. (3) The stepped side profile, which A9's side view has and A7's is flatter.

**From neither:** A7's bow plate and both ships' nose torpedo bundles. The nose bundle is now banned by gate rule for every faction, and Ryan singled the grey block out by name.

## The bow without the grey block

The grey block failed because it was ONE PLAIN SURFACE on a ship whose whole grammar is many small surfaces. The fix is not a different plate; it is no plate at all:

1. Build the bow the way the rest of the ship is built: three or four red and black slabs stepped down toward the front, overlapping, each narrower than the one behind, so the bow reads as the front of the same stack, blunt because the stack ends, not because a plate caps it.
2. The four torpedo mouths go LOW in the bottom bow slab, flush, a row of dark circles near the waterline of the hull (in side view) and visible in plan as the front slab's dark mouths. Flush is the point: nothing protrudes, the armour is the weapon's housing.
3. Put one small thing on the bow that is pure Hullbreakers: a welded patch or a short length of chain across the top slab, so the eye reads "patched" before it reads "plain".

## The launchers

The rack-doubling fault from rounds 3 and 4 has a cause worth naming: a "rack of tubes on each flank" invites the model to draw the rack it sees in the reference twice (fore and aft), and A9's own sheet carries a rack plus a top tube row. On TOP, as Ryan ruled, the same wording risk changes shape: the model may draw a row of launchers along the deck. The prompt must fix the launchers as OBJECTS, not rows: one box, port, angled out; one box, starboard, angled out; different frames, different paint; each ONE cast block; "two launchers on the whole ship, no other tube rows anywhere, no tubes on the flanks". My prediction: the flanks are where the doubling will try to live this time, off the A7/A9 references.

## A11 against A12

A11 (A7's body, A9's dress) is the safer mix: A7's body already has the width and the deck, and A9's additions are surface dress that image-to-image handles well. Its risk is that the redrawn bow stays a plate in a new colour. A12 (A9's body widened to A7's weight) is the riskier edit, because widening fights the reference's proportions; its side profile and chains come free, but the plan may come out as A9 stretched, steps and all, at the wrong ratio. My blind pick is A11, with A12 as the comparison Ryan asked for.

## Escort

One salvaged section, one launcher on top, one bell, one short chain. If the escort grows a second launcher or a rack row, it fails line 1 the same way the heroes do.
