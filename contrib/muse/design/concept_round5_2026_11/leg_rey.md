# Blind Rey leg, issue #476 (written before any picture and before the other legs)

The question the Board set: from the lore alone, what does a warship built by the finest optical engineers look like from directly above, with no crystal block and no prism in it?

The lore's load-bearing facts, as I read them: these are the people who built the collectors, mirrors and beam lines. Their ship is "an instrument for light, built as a warship". The flagship is built round the largest surviving piece of the Lattice. Their hulls are optical memory, drifting lit only by stored light. In play: fastest in the game, deep shields, thin hulls, crystal vanes that defend after moving, and a great mirror on the flagship.

## The argument

Four rounds failed by starting from a MATERIAL (glass, crystal) and trying to make it ship-shaped. The lore starts from a PROFESSION. An optician's warship is not made of glass; it is made of optical FUNCTIONS, each one a part with a job, assembled with an instrument maker's finish:

1. **Apertures.** The weapons are openings that gather and throw light: round, recessed, set in polished mounts. From above they are the biggest circles on the ship. Two in the bow shoulders (the new gate rule puts them there), one dome on the centre line facing up.
2. **A light path.** An instrument is organised around the path its light travels. From above, the ship should show a clear centre line of components, collector to emitter, like a telescope tube or a beam line drawn in plan: this is the spine, and it is also the stored light's route, so the glow inside follows a line, not a pool.
3. **A surface that works.** Mirrors and collectors are flat or gently curved panels whose job is catching light. A working deck of mirror, flat to the camera, gives the faction an outline no other faction has: a bright spearhead or leaf of reflection on a darker field.
4. **Thin structure, exactly joined.** Instrument makers do not cast slabs; they machine frames, rings and longerons, polished, with visible precision joints. Thin is not a weakness in the read, it is the signature: this family builds like a watchmaker, everyone else builds like a shipyard.

So from above, the identity is: a slender polished frame organised on one light path, two big round apertures in the bow shoulders, a third glass dome amidships, a working mirror surface, and stored light running inside the structure on the centre line. Nothing faceted, nothing gem-like. If a viewer covers the colour and asks "who built this", the answer is "whoever builds telescopes".

## What I would draw, route by route

The Board named three routes in the brief; my blind read of each, from the lore:

**The Unfinished (open frame round stored light)** is the most literal reading of the flagship's name and the Lattice story: the ship is literally built round a piece of the Lattice, and the open frame says the building never stopped. Risk: line 11 and the wreckage read. Open structure in pictures wants to become scaffolding or damage. The guard that matters is FINISH: every rib polished, every joint exact, the frame clearly machined, more watch than scaffold. Second risk: from above, an open frame over a glowing vessel can read as a cage (round 4's G8 try 1 fault in a new form). The vessel must be big enough to dominate its frame: the light is the ship, the frame is its mount.

**The Mirror Deck** has the strongest top-view identity of the three, and top view is where this game is judged (the Board's new rule 2 is written for exactly this). A flat mirror spearhead is an outline no other faction owns, and it is a working surface, not decoration. Risks: line 3 (the mirror must not grow side mirrors or booms; it is one surface, one piece with the hull) and the "solar sail / solar panel" read, which is a sea-and-air cousin fault: the guard is that the mirror is a DECK, thick at its edge with the hull visibly beneath it, tiled like an instrument, not a film on a frame. Second risk: the lenses in cut-outs must stay countable; a busy mirror will swallow small turrets, so the two small turrets need dark mounts that contrast with the mirror.

**The Racing Hull** is the safest ship and the weakest identity. A polished blade with canopy strips reads "fast silver ship" before it reads "optical instrument", and the canopy strip down the back is one step from the prism bar this round exists to escape (a glowing line in a metal bar instead of light in a glass one). Its crystal vanes are the lore's own exception and the part most likely to be drawn as wings whatever the prompt says (line 12). If this route wins, it wins on the vanes and the domes, so those must be the picture's loudest elements, and the hull should be shaped by them, not by a blade first.

## My swap call

The brief lets me swap a better route in for G12. The Racing Hull is the one I would swap, and the route I would put in its place is the **Beam Line**: the ship drawn as the beam line the Glasswrights actually built, in plan. A long polished silver bench, dead straight, carrying a row of optical stations on the centre line: the stored light vessel at the stern third feeding the line, the dome turret amidships, apertures and mounts as rings and collars round the bench, the two bow lenses in the shoulders of a blunt instrument head. It is the most lore-true object available (their civilisation's signature machine), its top view is unmistakable (a ruled line of rings and stations, like a diagram of an experiment), and it cannot be mistaken for a prism because the light is in ONE vessel feeding a metal bench, not in the hull itself. Its risk is the round 1 Beam Line fault the brief names: a feature drawn instead of a ship. The cure is the same as the Unfinished's: a real engine block, a real command block, armour on the shoulders, and the bench thick enough to be a hull, not a rail.

Whether the chair keeps the swap is a chair decision after the legs; my blind vote is: G10 Unfinished, G11 Mirror Deck, G12 Beam Line, with the Racing Hull as the fallback if the Beam Line cannot be kept a ship.

## Escorts

All three routes: the escort is a smaller instrument of the same family with ONE lens aperture, a short bench or frame, a small vessel of stored light and one drive ring or small bloom. What it must not have: the flagship's signature (no open frame on the Unfinished's escort? No: the escort may share the family construction but not the flagship's one great feature: no Lattice vessel, no full mirror deck, no vane set). One aperture and stored light is the family; the flagship feature is the flagship's.

## Predictions, to be scored after generation

1. The Mirror Deck's small turrets will be swallowed by the mirror at least once (line 1).
2. The Unfinished will be drawn as wreckage or scaffolding at least once (line 12).
3. The Racing Hull, if drawn, will grow wings at least once (line 12) and read as a generic silver ship in the beauty (line 8).
4. The lens prompt fix (smooth glass dome, no blades, no spokes) will work better than round 4's iris wording; domes are a shape the model owns.
5. Stored light will try to become flame or lightning in at least one beauty.
