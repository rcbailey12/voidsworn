# Concept round 4: new routes for the Glasswrights, Hullbreakers and Chorus

Issue #467. Three new routes for each of the three factions Ryan was not yet happy with after round 3 (R180): the Glasswrights as a full prism, the Hullbreakers back to the original Pyre-Barge concept, and the Chorus grown, not built. Sunward, Hegemony and Eclipse are settled and are Codex's to redraw in #468.

Method, same as rounds 2 and 3: a design sheet first (top view above, side view below, flat on, same scale, plain dark grey), then a beauty view built image-to-image from the accepted sheet with one escort. Every picture was drawn image-to-image from its named reference: the Glasswrights from round 1's ithar_B (glass and light only, not its layout), the Hullbreakers from the original concept docs/ship_pipeline/concepts/C19_51.webp, the Chorus from round 3's C4 sheet. Council: a blind Rey leg written before any picture and before the other legs (leg_rey.md), DeepSeek as writer leg, Flash and Grok ideation only, Qwen sat this round out after round 3, Rey chairs. The chair overruled all three outside legs on one point: DeepSeek, Grok and Flash each independently reached for yellow-green or bile tones for the Chorus interior glow. The picked C4 base carries amber throats and amber is the Chorus accent on every delivered sheet, so the chair kept amber everywhere. If the Board prefers bile, it is a one-word change on a future build, but the delivered set is consistent with the picked base.

## Marked failures, up front

- The Glasswright lens fault is now the round's standing fault: in all three sheets the pulsar lenses render as spoked discs that read as fans or vents at small sizes, not as flat iris apertures. The weapon PLACEMENT is right everywhere (two on the bow collar, one turret on the top beam, order kept); the lens FACE is what the model will not draw. A build will need the iris drawn as geometry, not prompted.
- Hullbreaker scatter racks doubled again on A7 and A8 (two rack groups per flank instead of one). A9 held the count in plan but grew an extra short tube row along the top edge. Three rounds, three rack doublings or near-doublings: the rack wants to repeat, and a build brief should state the rack as one cast block per flank in the model, not as a prompt.
- G8 line 9: the plan body measures about 2.7:1 against the 5:1 grammar (the side view holds about 5.5:1). The hex cross-section shows wide from above; the Board should judge G8 on the side view and the beauty, where it is the strongest Glasswright of the round.
- Chorus bodies all run lean, about 5:1 against the 3.5:1 grammar, the same drift the picked C4 base had. The set is consistent with its base; if the Board wants the grammar's ratio back, that is a C7-first redraw decision, not a per-variation fix.
- G9 beauty try 1 was rejected for blue lenses (a palette break) and C9 beauty try 1 for a third throat opening in the bloom. Both were redrawn and the replacements delivered.

## Glasswrights: full prism (the ship is the optic)

The grammar, from the issue: one long prism of clear glass with warm white light moving inside it, a silver frame on its long edges carrying everything, the bow as emitter with two forward pulsar lenses in iris mounts on the bow collar, three mounts in order on the top beam (pulsar turret, gun turret, flak), a silver engine block with three white drive rings at the stern. Five weapons and no others. Hero mounts (i_boss): pulsar F, pulsar F, pulsar T, turret T, flak T, hangar none.

### G7 Long Prism (ithar_G7_sheet.jpg, ithar_G7_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
Three-sided prism, one ridge up, silver beams on the three edges with slim ribs. Sheet verdict: glass, moving light, frame and thin edge fringe all right; a true side view with the mount row in order and three drive rings at the stern. Marked: the plan tapers toward the bow where a constant section was asked (it reads part-wedge), the bow lenses are the spoked fan discs named above, and one extra small round fitting sits on the spine in plan (lines 1 and 9 marked, the rest pass). A second try fixed the constant width but lost the side view and moved the guns to flank turrets, so try 1 stands. Beauty try 1: the prism and its escort spar read well in space; the bow lens pair is duplicated toward the stern end and the escort carries three small stern rings instead of one.

### G8 Hex Shard (ithar_G8_sheet.jpg, ithar_G8_beauty.jpg), delivered try 2
Six-sided column coming to a pointed bow like a quartz point, two silver collars, six heavy beams. Sheet verdict: the point is glass and is the emitter face, the collar behind it carries the bow lenses (rendered small, as cylinders, marked on line 1), the turret row is in order in both views and the stern shows three big glowing rings. Try 1 was rejected: the frame read as a cage of thin rails and the rings became white bars. The heavy-beam guard in try 2 is the fix worth keeping for any redraw. Line 9 marked as above. Beauty try 1 is the strongest Glasswright picture of the round: the shard, its light burst and the collar lenses all read, and the escort is a true small shard (it carries small top turrets, noted).

### G9 Wedge (ithar_G9_sheet.jpg, ithar_G9_beauty.jpg), delivered sheet try 1, beauty try 2
Three-sided wedge, widest at the stern, narrowing to the bow emitter, about 4:1 as specified. Sheet verdict: mount row in order, three rings across the wide stern in the side view, light streaks inside the glass; the three lens objects are the standing spoked-disc fault. Beauty try 1 drew the lenses as blue fans and was rejected; try 2 is warm throughout, though in perspective the wedge reads as a framed bar and the sheet carries the wedge read.

## Ashfall Hullbreakers: the original, patched together

The grammar, from the issue: back to the original Pyre-Barge concept (C19_51), stacked mismatched armour slabs, red oxide and black, racks, stepped lopsided outline, only the sea parts changed (ram prow for the boat bow, vents for funnels, no masts or rails). Hero mounts (c_boss): scatter P, scatter S, heavy torpedo F, flak T, hangar 2. No camouflage.

### A7 The original, made a space ship (corsair_A7_sheet.jpg, corsair_A7_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
The C19 character is back at first try: stepped mismatched slabs, blunt prow with a four-tube torpedo cluster, one flak turret, two hangar doors in the side view, three big bells in plan, no masts, no funnels, no camo. Marked: line 1 (racks doubled, two groups per flank), line 3 partial (the side view grows extra small bells below the main three), small red lit strips along the side, and a skull motif on the deck that reads as a trophy marking. Beauty try 1: the barge and a proper one-section escort; racks multiplied in the beauty as on the sheet.

### A8 Three hulls welded (corsair_A8_sheet.jpg, corsair_A8_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
Three salvaged sections end to end: red oxide bow, bare steel freighter middle offset a little, black iron stern, both weld collars heavy and bolted, a continuous keel through both joins (the misaligned-Frankenstein fault DeepSeek and Flash both predicted did not happen). Marked: line 1 (racks doubled again), line 6 (the two hangar mouths render as open square pits in plan, not doors), a small fin under the stern in the side view and one thin antenna spike on the flak turret. Beauty try 1: sections and collars read in space, escort is a single section as briefed; the hero reads slightly tube-like in perspective, where the sheet carries the stepped read.

### A9 Chained barge (corsair_A9_sheet.jpg, corsair_A9_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
A7's barge plus A6's chains: cargo chains with hanging cages down one flank below the gun line, and one pale captured hull slab welded and chained flat on top as a trophy, inert. Marked: line 1 partial (one rack block per flank held, plus an extra short tube row along the top edge), and the hangar doors are easy to lose among the pits and panels. Beauty try 1 is the most characterful Hullbreaker picture of the round; the escort carries its own short chain and single cage.

## The Chorus: grown, not built

The grammar, from the issue: base is round 3's C4 pushed fully organic. No straight lines, no seams, no bands; folds, scars, pores, wet sheen, veins glowing under thinner skin; whale-scale. Weapons are organs: two bioplasma throats in the bow bloom, a tendril crown, a spore vent field, three brood sacs. Hero mounts (b_boss): bioplasma F, bioplasma F, tendril T, spores T, hangar 3 (the sacs). Glow colour: amber, per the chair ruling above.

### C7 The base, grown (chorus_C7_sheet.jpg, chorus_C7_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
The cleanest Chorus sheet of any round: both throats huge and plain in the bloom, the crown from one root, pore fields on both flanks, three smooth sacs, amber veins, nothing built on it anywhere. Marked: line 9 (about 5:1, leaner than the grammar, as its base), a slight fish read in the stern tip membranes, and a near-symmetric plan against the grammar's no-perfect-match line. Beauty try 1: the same animal lit in space with a closed-bloom juvenile escort; the stern membranes flare into a fin-like fan in the beauty only.

### C8 Bone through skin (chorus_C8_sheet.jpg, chorus_C8_beauty.jpg), delivered try 1
The variation every leg called most likely to fail, C6's idea done as growth: irregular pale bone plates and spines grow out through the hide along the back, tilted at uneven angles with torn healed skin at their bases, bare hide between, no rows. It did not fail: no ribcage, no vertebra row, no skull, in either picture. Organs all plain. Marked: the largest plates sit close to bleached in tone, and the body runs lean like the rest of the set. Beauty try 1: the escort is a juvenile pod with two or three small spines just breaking its back (DeepSeek's escort call, adopted over Flash's boneless escort).

### C9 Old leviathan (chorus_C9_sheet.jpg, chorus_C9_beauty.jpg), delivered sheet try 1, beauty try 2
The same animal grown old: scar patches, small barnacle crusts, weathered grey-brown hide, one larger bloom, and three brood sacs of clearly different sizes. The texture does not bury the organs (the line 1 risk Flash named). Beauty try 1 grew a third amber opening in the bloom and was rejected; try 2 holds exactly two throats and delivers a clean juvenile escort.

## What the model kept getting wrong, across the round

1. Lens faces: spoked fan discs instead of iris apertures, on all three Glasswright sheets and carried into the beauties. Placement and count discipline held; the face did not.
2. Scatter rack doubling (A7, A8), with A9's extra top row the near-miss version. Hullbreaker racks want to repeat fore and aft.
3. Chorus leanness: all three bodies run about 5:1 off a base that already ran lean. Consistent within the set, off the grammar's number.
4. Stern membranes on the Chorus beauties flare toward fins (C7 beauty worst). The sheets keep them small.
5. Escorts were the round's quiet success: defined by absences (one lens and no turret row, one salvaged section, a closed bloom), all nine delivered escorts are smaller ships of the family rather than shrunk heroes, the fault that ran through rounds 2 and 3.
