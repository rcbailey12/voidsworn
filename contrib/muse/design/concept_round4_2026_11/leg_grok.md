G7

RISK: Gate line 1 (armed read at thumbnail) is most likely to fail because a pure glass prism reads as a glowing bar or crystal object rather than a warship; without the silver frame, bow collar, and five clearly distinct mounts (two forward iris lenses, top pulsar turret, gun turret, small flak) the top view collapses into an unarmed light stick.

SHEET: Orthographic design sheet, top view above side view below, same scale, plain dark grey background, evenly lit, no text no labels no border. Long three-sided prism of clear glass, one ridge up, length about 5:1:1, warm white light moving slowly inside the glass only. Three silver structural beams run the full length along the three edges, joined by a few thin silver ribs. Silver engine block caps the flat stern with exactly three white drive rings. Bow is the optical emitter face of the prism; two large forward pulsar lenses sit in armoured iris mounts on a silver bow collar, one each side, plain and dark-mouthed. On the top silver beam in strict order from bow: one large pulsar lens turret, one gun turret, one small flak turret, then a low silver command block aft. Exactly those five weapons, nothing else that looks like a gun. Gun ports dark, never lit. Rainbow only as a thin fringe on the glass edges. No metal hull shaped like crystal, no crystal bolted to a hull, no faceted grey armour, nothing insect-like, no wings, no petals. From above it must read as a long glowing glass bar with silver edges and five clear weapon installations.

BEAUTY: Three-quarter view from front and above, near black background. The long three-sided glass prism hero with its silver frame, three stern drive rings, bow collar with two iris lenses, and the four mounts on the top beam. Beside it one smaller escort of the same family: a shorter three-sided glass prism carried in a simpler silver frame, only two weapons (one forward iris lens and one small flak), no full top-beam suite, no command block. Escort is never a shrunk copy of the hero and never a sea ship.
