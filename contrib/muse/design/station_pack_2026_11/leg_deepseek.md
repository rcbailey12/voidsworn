Design pack: the Vesper Reach station, T2 (Voidsworn issue 654)

1. The shape of record

T2 is an abandoned, damaged Hegemony fleet station. From above, its identity is the cross of four docking arms at two heights, the circular habitat ring, the central stack of nine deck drums, and the five towers. At the default battle camera (r 170), the silhouette is roughly 50 units across (a little over twice the length of the 23.2-unit A17 Pack Hunter cruiser). The station must read without the orange blocker outlines. The silhouette is defined by the contrast between the pale grey structure catching hard directional light on one side, and the dark, frosted shadow side. The core stack and ring remain the dominant mass; the torn arm stub and leaning tower break the symmetry and tell the story of the damage.

2. Rulings that bind this build

- R109: The station must be a built set piece with dense surface detail, not plain geometric shapes. This forces the designer to include paneling, greebles, structural ribs, and machinery.
- R206: T2 (the damaged state) is the accepted build reference. The build must pass the build-sheet gate (R203).
- R98 and R209: Broken pieces must never be visibly empty. Torn holes, the broken end of the arm stub, and the buckled spoke must show deck levels, bulkheads, frame stations, and machinery spaces. No flat caps or hollow shells.
- R113: No triangle caps on the station. The built station is priced in milliseconds on the scene gate (R116: 12.5 ms, 75 percent of the 16.7 ms frame). Aggressive LODs are required for a 50-unit object.
- R151: The station uses the Hegemony faction kit. Invisible plumbing (mounts, LOD, bake pipeline) can be shared, but the visual language is strictly Hegemony.
- R207 and R233: The builder corrects physical impossibilities and lists each change on the build sheet. The Board makes the design calls between concept and build.
- R236: Game data, the lore bible, and the faction guide govern; the concept is a base.
- R217: Every part is modelled as the thing it is. A berth collar has depth, a lip, and a dark bore; a tower cap is its system, not a box. The fit check applies: no part inside another, arcs and cones clear. The builder must check the seized ring, the buckled spoke, and the leaning tower for clearance.
- Hegemony faction guide: Machined, modular, horizontal. Flush panels, straight seams, repeated modules. Colours pale grey, steel-blue trim, implant-blue light. Never rust, never warm light, no ornament. Hegemony breaks in modules at bulkhead rings; inside, white corridors, blue emergency light, and racks of identical equipment; breach anchors sit on module joints. The orange battery strips are addressed in Section 4.

3. Shared build rules

- Recipe: The station is built through the ship recipe once its build sheet passes (Issue 373, R203). The built station is sized from the hexes it blocks: at least three hexes wide, and taller than any ship. Current blocker visuals are 14.0 units per hex.
- Insides: Per R98 and R209, every breakable piece must have built insides: deck levels, bulkheads, frame stations, and machinery spaces. Hits, breaks, and deaths must show structure, lit or burning spaces.
- Fit check: Per R217, no part inside another. The seized off-angle ring, the buckled spoke, and the leaning tower must all clear their neighbours. The debris held against the arms and ring must not intersect the station.
- Gate: Priced in milliseconds on the scene gate like a ship (R116: 12.5 ms, 75 percent of the 16.7 ms frame). Gate scene at 1080p median and 4K GPU.

4. Lore and kit

T2 is a Hegemony fleet station of the Vesper Reach, built as a fleet support and docking station for warships on long-range patrol, abandoned after a write-off after a single catastrophic event (see the damage story in the Design Calls section).

The Hegemony kit language is machined, modular, horizontal: pale grey panels, steel-blue trim, implant-blue light. Straight seams, flush panels, repeated modules. No ornament, no rust, no warm light.

Orange battery strips: [Call] The few dull orange strips at torn frames are damage-state emergency strips, not the faction's lit language. They are a state, not a light colour for the faction. They are dull, low-intensity, and only at torn frames and breach points. They must not be used as general lighting, and must not be read as warm light in the Hegemony palette. The living station light remains implant-blue.

T2 inherits the same Hegemony kit as T1. The family resemblance is carried by the ring, the drum core, and the arms. Both directions share one parts kit at build time.

5. Parts inventory

- Core drums: 9 deck drums, stacked vertically. Each drum is a module with its own bulkhead rings at top and bottom, flush panels, straight seams, and a mix of older panels and fresh replacement panels.
- Ring and bearing track: 1 habitat ring, seized off-angle. 1 segmented bearing track with one open gap segment (the gap segment has moved with the seized ring). 10 bearing segments, 9 in place, 1 gap segment.
- Spokes: 4 spokes connecting the ring to the core. 1 spoke is buckled, bent out of plane but still attached at both ends.
- Docking arms and berth collars: 4 docking arms at crossed angles and two heights. 1 arm is torn down to a stub, showing deck frames inside. 3 arms are intact. Each intact arm has 1 berth collar, with depth, a lip, and a dark bore, and guide lights. One berth is empty, with guide lights on. Cargo pods queued at the intact berths.
- Towers and caps: 5 towers at five heights with five different caps. All 5 towers are dead. 1 tower is leaning. Each cap is its system, not a box.
- Radiator banks: Radiator banks under the core. They are flat banks, cold, not lit. They read slightly like solar arrays in the concept; they are the same banks as T1's.
- Lights: About 90 lights left, in scattered stubborn clusters. In service, T1 had about 650 implant-blue lights in clusters. The T2 lights are implant-blue, with a few dull orange battery strips at torn frames only.
- Debris: Debris held where it drifted to a stop against the arms and the ring. Includes torn panel fragments, a bent arm segment, and small wreckage. Frost and dust on the shadow side.
- The moving part: One slow tumbling debris chunk, a torn panel section, held in a slow tumble near the torn arm stub. See Call 1 in the Design Calls section.

6. How it breaks

Break lines are at the bulkhead rings between modules, at the ring spoke joints, and at the docking arm roots.

What comes away, and what the insides show:

- A core drum breaks away at its bulkhead rings. The inside shows deck levels, bulkheads, frame stations, and machinery spaces, with white corridors and blue emergency light, and racks of identical equipment.
- The ring breaks at a spoke joint. The inside of the spoke shows a hollow tube with a frame, decking, and conduits, lit or burning, never flat or empty.
- A docking arm breaks at its root. The inside shows the arm's internal structure, deck levels, and the berth collar mechanism, with torn and scorched rims.
- A tower breaks at its base. The inside shows machinery spaces and its cap's system internals, lit or burning.
- At every break, per R98/R209, there are no flat caps and no hollow shells. Every break shows structure, lit or burning spaces.

See the damage story in the Design Calls section for the cross-reference.

7. Must-keep and must-avoid

Must-keep:

- The cross silhouette from above: four docking arms at crossed angles and two heights, the circular habitat ring, and the central stack of nine deck drums.
- The five towers at five heights with five different caps, one leaning, all dead.
- The seized off-angle ring with the moved gap segment, the buckled spoke, and the torn arm stub.
- About 90 implant-blue lights in scattered stubborn clusters, plus a few dull orange battery strips at torn frames only.
- Frost and dust on the shadow side, torn holes showing deck frames inside.
- The station must read as a single built set piece at r 170, without the orange blocker outlines.

Must-avoid:

- No triangle caps on the station.
- No flat caps, no hollow shells, no visibly empty breaks.
- No weapons, no engine or exhaust visuals, no hull/prow language, no sea-ship details.
- No rust, no warm light (except the dull orange battery strips, as a damage state only), no ornament.
- No repaint of plain geometric shapes; dense surface detail is required per R109.

8. Stale words to ignore

None known. The only point to flag is R109: the old station repaint is superseded by the built set piece approach. Do not follow any older document that treats the station as a repaint, or that uses triangle caps.

Design Calls:

Call 1: The slow moving part

[Call] One slow tumbling debris chunk. A torn panel section, about 2 to 3 units across, in a slow tumble, held in a drift loop near the torn arm stub, on the dark side of the station. It moves slowly enough to be read as drift, not as a ship. At the battle camera (r 170), the player reads it as a single pale grey fragment turning end over end against the dark background, catching light on one edge as it turns. It is the only moving part: the ring is seized, the towers are dead, and the radiators are cold. This satisfies Issue 373's done test for lights and one slow moving part.

Call 2: The damage story

[Call] A single, sudden structural failure killed the station. A power surge or impact event (the exact cause is not in the record) severed the main power trunk and tore the station's spin, seizing the ring off-angle and buckling one spoke. The shock tore one docking arm down to a stub, killed all five towers, and vented the core, leaving frost and dust on the shadow side. The half of the station on the shadow side went dark first, and the power loss cascaded to the rest of the station, leaving only the modules with independent battery backup lit: the core drum housing the emergency power store, the ring segment with the surviving light clusters, and the intact berths, which retain guide lights. About 90 lights remain, in scattered stubborn clusters, powered by those batteries. Dull orange battery strips mark the torn frames where the backup circuits are exposed. Nobody repaired it; nobody is aboard. The station is a write-off, held in place.

Call 3: How it breaks in battle

[Call] The station is a blocker set piece ships fight around. It breaks at the bulkhead rings between modules and at the module joints, per the Hegemony rule. A drum breaks away at its bulkhead rings, showing deck levels and machinery spaces inside. The ring breaks at a spoke joint, showing the spoke's internal frame and conduits. An arm breaks at its root, showing deck levels and the berth collar mechanism. A tower breaks at its base, showing machinery spaces and its cap internals. At every break, per R98/R209, the insides show structure, lit or burning spaces, never flat caps or a hollow shell. See Section 6 for the full break list.

Call 4: Fleet or refinery first

Fleet first. T2 is the fleet station, and it is the accepted build reference (R206). The refinery is T3, a different plan and a different kit, not a variant of T2. Building the fleet station first gives the game its primary blocker and docking station, and the refinery can follow on its own plan and kit.
