# Design pack: the Vesper Reach station (station_T2)

## 1. The shape of record

A Hegemony fleet station drawn from above as an open C: a vertical core stack of 9 deck drums, a habitat ring around it on a segmented bearing track with one open gap segment, four docking arms at crossed angles and two heights, five towers of five heights. In service (T1) it turns its ring slowly and burns about 650 implant-blue lights. T2 is that station after the write-off: the ring seized off-angle so the gap sits where no plan view of T1 puts it, one spoke buckled, one arm torn to a stub, about 90 lights left. Across a little over twice a cruiser's length (cruiser 23.2 units; the station about 50 units across and taller than any ship), sized from the three-plus hexes it blocks. The silhouette that must survive the build: drum core, broken ring with a moved gap, crossed arms of unequal fate, tower cluster of five heights. If a viewer cannot tell it from T1's plan at a glance, the damage has not been built.

- R109 (Ryan, Nov 5, 2026, on the old repaint): "we need WAY more surface detail, it looks super plain and just like regular shapes right now." Forces: one built set piece with parts at three scale bands (hex-scale core and ring, cruiser-scale arms, metre-scale fittings), never assembled slabs.
- R206 (Ryan, Nov 11, 2026): T2 accepted ("T2 damaged"); forces: this concept, this damage state, build-sheet gate before the build (R203).
- R98, R209 (Ryan, Nov 4 and Nov 11, 2026): insides are built, breaks never show an empty husk; forces: the INSIDE section of the sheet (deck levels per drum, bulkhead rings, machinery spaces) and authored break lines with interior faces.
- R113, R116: no triangle cap; cost reported in triangles and priced in ms on the scene gate (12.5 ms of the 16.7 ms frame).
- R151: the kit is a Hegemony kit, not a shared kit; only plumbing (sockets, scale rules, LOD, bake, export) is shared.
- R207, R233: the concept guides, the builder designs; identity comes from the pictures, corrections are listed on the sheet.
- R236: game data, the lore bible and the faction guide govern over the concept where they conflict.
- R217: every part modelled as the thing it is; the fit check runs (no part inside another; nothing blocks a berth's approach).
- Issue 373's done test: at least three hexes wide, taller than any ship, core, ring or docking arms, towers, lights, one slow moving part, break lines and insides, gate scene run on a station map, bench sheets at camera 170 and at a low camera beside a cruiser, Ryan's look before the swap.

## 3. Shared build rules

The station goes through the ship recipe with its own parts kit under tools/blender/kits (issue 373), at station scale: stage discipline, INSIDE section, fit view and fit report, paint stack, and the scene gate are the ship rules read at 50 units instead of 23. Colour is the Hegemony lock: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue, with the single damage-state exception in section 7. No weapons exist on this station and none are drawn, sheeted or fitted.

## 4. Lore and kit

The Vesper Reach station is a Hegemony Navy fleet station: berthing, cargo staging and habitat for the Reach's patrol squadrons, built to the fleet's modular standard (repeated modules, flush panels, straight seams, everything machined and identical where the fleet repeats it). The kit language is the Hegemony ship language at building scale: drum modules joined at bulkhead rings, berth collars as deep docking sockets with guide-light rings, tower caps that are sensor and comms systems (five different systems, five caps), radiator banks as finned panels under the core. It was written off after the damage in section 6 and never repaired; it is uncrewed, and nothing on it is maintained. Frost and dust gather on the shadow side; no fresh panels exist anywhere on T2 (fresh panels belong to T1).

## 5. Parts inventory

- Core stack: 9 deck drums on a vertical axis, joined at bulkhead rings (the break lines live there). Jobs: habitat, command, machinery, storage by drum; the sheet assigns each drum its function and its inside (decks, corridor ring, machinery).
- Habitat ring: segmented ring on a bearing track around the core at mid-height, one open gap segment. In T2 seized off-angle, gap moved from its T1 position. Job in service: rotating habitat; in T2: the station's signature ruin.
- Spokes: ring-to-core spokes; one buckled (bent, its casing split at the bend, interior frame showing).
- Docking arms: 4, at crossed angles and two heights, lengths in the 18 to 24 unit band, each ending in a berth collar (deep socket, lip, dark bore, guide-light ring). In T2: one arm torn to a stub at its root collar, torn rim scorched; one berth on a surviving arm still shows its collar lights among the ~90.
- The moving part (chair call, marked): the torn arm's severed outer section, still caught on a snagged cable a few units off the stub, in a very slow tumble. It needs no power, it is the only motion on the station, and at the battle camera it tells the player the place is dead but not frozen in a diorama. The ring must not move; seized is its state.
- Towers: 5, five heights, five caps (sensor, comms, beacon, docking control, observation), all dead in T2; one leans a few degrees off its mount, its base ring cracked.
- Radiator banks: finned banks under the core, cold in T2. They must be modelled as banks of thin finned panels so they do not read as solar arrays (the concept round's mark).
- Lights: about 650 in service (T1); about 90 in T2, in scattered clusters where the surviving bus still feeds (core drums and the tower cluster), all implant blue. Plus a few dull orange battery strips at torn frames: emergency battery circuits, a damage state, not the faction's lit language (see section 7).
- Debris: a held field of torn pieces (panel sections, a collar ring, frame lengths) stopped against the arms and the ring where they drifted; static except for the tumbling arm section.
- Insides (per drum and ring segment): deck levels, white corridors, racks of identical equipment, machinery spaces in the machinery drums, the core shaft; in T2 mostly dark, with blue emergency light only where power survives.

## 6. How it breaks

Break lines follow the Hegemony rule: at module joints. The drums part at their bulkhead rings, rims torn and scorched; arms separate at their root collars; ring segments separate along the bearing track's segment joints; towers come away at their base rings. Every broken face shows the insides of section 5: deck edges, corridor rings, machinery, never a flat cap (R98, R209).
The damage story (the write-off, one event): something heavy struck the ring quadrant, or was struck by it: the spoke buckled under the load, the ring seized off-angle on its track, and the shock tore the nearest docking arm out at its root. The core's power bus severed with that quadrant, so the half of the station past the buckled spoke went dark at once and stayed dark: dead towers, cold radiators, no ring drive. What still burns is the near half's emergency bus: about 90 lights around the core and one tower, and battery strips at the torn frames where local circuits never got the word. Frost and dust settled on the shadow side afterwards. Nobody repaired any of it; that is the difference between T2 and a battle-damaged station still in service.

## 7. Must-keep and must-avoid

Must keep: the counts (9 drums, 4 arms, 5 towers, the one gap segment, about 90 lit points in T2); the plan that cannot be superimposed on T1's (moved gap, stub arm, buckled spoke); the Hegemony colour lock; debris resting against structure, not floating in a ring; the tumbling arm section as the only motion.
Must avoid: rust and warm light as a design language (the orange battery strips are small, few, and at torn frames only; they are a state, not a palette); solar-array radiator banks; any weapon, engine bell, prow or thrust axis; fresh panels; a turning ring; symmetry that reads as a logo from above.

## 8. Stale words to ignore

The old blocker pieces (14-unit slabs per cell in blocker_visuals.gd) are the placeholder this build replaces; any older document describing the station as a repaint or a detail pass on those pieces (the issue 199 line) is superseded by issue 373. The station triangle figures in the old #200 standard (about 20k target, 40k to 45k ceiling) were retired by R113 and are reported figures only, not caps.

## 9. Open questions for the Board

1. The moving part: the chair's call is the tumbling severed arm section. If the Board would rather keep every part of the station rigid, the fallback is a slow debris drift along the ring track; the ring itself should stay seized either way.
2. Does T2 replace the blocker on all station maps, or does the in-service T1 state also get built from this kit now (the kit is shared; the states are two sheet variants)? The pack assumes T2 first, T1 as a later variant of the same sheet.
3. Battle behaviour: can ships' fire break pieces off T2 in play (its break lines are authored for it), or is it scenery that only its death state uses? The pack authors the break lines either way; the Board owns the gameplay call.
