Design Pack: Station T2 (Voidsworn Issue 654)

1. The Shape of Record

Station T2 reads from above and at the default battle camera (r 170) as a massive, asymmetric set piece over twice the length of a standard cruiser (approx. 50+ units across). Its silhouette is anchored by a vertical core stack of nine drum levels, surrounded by a seized, off-angle habitat ring on a segmented bearing track with an open gap segment. Radiating outward are four docking arms (one torn down to a stub, one buckled spoke) and five distinctive capped towers at varying heights. Flat, cold radiator banks sit below the core stack, reading slightly like solar arrays. The overall layout maintains strict family resemblance to T1 through the core, ring, and remaining arm structures, but presents as a heavily damaged, half-dark wreck choked with frozen debris clouds and dead systems.

2. Rulings That Bind This Build

R98 & R209 (Ryan, Nov 4 & 11, 2026): Broken pieces must never be visibly empty; insides (deck levels, bulkheads, frame stations, machinery spaces) must be modelled so hits and breaks show structure, lit or burning spaces, never flat caps or hollow shells.

R109 (Ryan, Nov 5, 2026): Requires high surface detail; the station is built as a complete set piece rather than a simple repaint.

R113 (Ryan, Nov 7, 2026): No triangle caps on the station; priced in milliseconds on the scene gate like a ship (12.5 ms, 75 percent of the 16.7 ms frame at 1080p median and 4K GPU).

R151 (Ryan, Nov 9, 2026): Parts kits are strictly per faction (Hegemony kit), never shared; only invisible plumbing (mount sockets, scale rules, LOD/bake pipeline, export) is shared.

R203 & R206 (Ryan, Nov 11, 2026): T2 accepted as a build reference following the build-sheet gate process.

R217 (Build Sheets): Every part must be modelled as its true physical object (berth collars with depth, bore, and lips; tower caps as functional systems); strict fit checks apply (no overlapping parts, clear arcs and cones).

R236: Governed by game data, the lore bible, and the faction guide.

Hegemony Faction Guide: Machined, modular, horizontal; flush panels, straight seams, repeated modules; pale grey, steel-blue trim, implant-blue light; never rust, never warm light, no ornament. Breaks occur at bulkhead rings and module joints with rims torn and scorched. Insides feature white corridors, blue emergency lights, and identical equipment racks. (Design Call: Damage-state dull orange battery strips are treated as an emergency state overlay rather than a violation of the faction's lit language).

3. Shared Build Rules

Recipe & Gate: Built as a single station set piece through the ship recipe and scene gate, budgeted at 12.5 ms frame time.

Insides & Breaks: All structural fractures expose detailed interior bulkheads, deck frames, and machinery spaces per R98/R209.

Fit Check: Zero geometric interpenetration; all docking paths and collision volumes verified clear.

4. Lore and Kit

Builder & Origin: Built by the Hegemony fleet as an operational hub in the Vesper Reach, subsequently abandoned following an official write-off.

Kit Language: Hegemony industrial aesthetic - modular steel construction, flush paneling, pale grey plating with steel-blue trim, and signature implant-blue lighting clusters. Unserviced and un-repaired since abandonment.

5. Parts Inventory

Core Drums: 9 vertical deck drums stacked centrally, featuring structural seam lines and exposed interior cutaways at damage points.

Habitat Ring & Bearing Track: 1 segmented turning ring seized off-angle, featuring a visible open gap segment and track damage.

Spokes: Structural radial connectors joining the core to the ring, including 1 buckled/fractured spoke.

Docking Arms & Berth Collars: 4 crossed arms at dual heights; 1 arm intact with cargo pods queued and guide lights active, 1 arm torn down to a scorched structural stub, and 2 remaining secondary arms with partial power. Berth collars feature full physical depth, dark bores, and lips.

Towers & Caps: 5 towers at 5 distinct heights featuring 5 unique functional system caps (communications arrays, sensor domes, crane assemblies).

Radiator Banks: Flat, cold radiator banks mounted below the core stack, reading visually like inactive solar arrays.

Lighting Infrastructure: Approximately 90 surviving implant-blue lights grouped in stubborn clusters, supplemented by dull orange battery strips marking torn structural frames.

Debris & Moving Part (Design Call 1): Static and drifting debris fields caught against the arms and ring. Design Call: The slow-moving part is a dense cluster of torn hull plating and frame fragments held in a slow, gravitational libration drift just off the stub-arm fracture, easily read as rotating debris at the battle camera.

Environmental Textures: Heavy frost and fine dust coating all shadow-facing surfaces.

6. How It Breaks

Break Lines: Structural failures and combat separation cleanly follow Hegemony modular design standards - breaking precisely along bulkhead rings between modules and at primary module joints.

Insides Display: Fractured junctions expose reinforced internal bulkheads, white-walled internal corridors, exposed equipment racks, and flickering blue emergency lighting fixtures.

Damage Story (Design Call 2): Design Call: The damage is the result of a cataclysmic orbital overload event during the abandonment write-off. Power cores in the lower-left quadrant suffered a cataclysmic containment breach, blowing out the lower docking arm to a stub, seizing the habitat ring off-axis, and plunging the shadow hemisphere into total blackout while emergency capacitors fed a dim residual trickle to roughly 90 stubborn clusters and emergency battery strips.

7. Must-Keep and Must-Avoid

Must-Keep: The exact 9-deck core count, 4-arm arrangement, 5-tower configuration, seized ring profile, and the asymmetric damage silhouette matching the approved visual reference.

Must-Avoid: Hollow shells or flat structural caps (violating R98/R209), triangle cap geometries (violating R113), rust, warm interior lighting outside of authorized emergency strips, and weapons integration.
