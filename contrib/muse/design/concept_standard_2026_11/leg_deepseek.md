## 1. Concept sheet standard (its draft, condensed only for repetition)

Sheet: three views of ONE design plus one stated length. If the three pictures could be three different ships, the sheet fails.

Cameras: three-quarter, port bow, 45 deg off the centreline toward port, 15 deg above the deck plane, aimed at amidships, bow left, perspective allowed, no fisheye. True side: port beam, 0 deg elevation, perpendicular, orthographic, bow left. True top: directly overhead, orthographic, bow at top, centreline vertical, port side on the left. Side and top at the same scale; the three-quarter is for shape and colour, not measuring.

Light: key from forward-port-above (45 deg above the deck plane, 45 deg to port of the bow), soft fill from aft-starboard at about 20 percent, black background, no other lights; with the prescribed bow directions this reads as upper-left light in every frame.

Must show, all views: the whole ship uncropped with margin; stated length in world units from build data plus a scale bar ("Length: N world units (build data)"); faction, class, variant, sheet version and date; the deck edge line traceable; every gun with mount, barrel count, station from the prow tip, offset from centreline and pointing direction, traversable guns drawn stowed forward with the arc noted, no aft-facing barrels; prow overhang dimensioned per its definition; belly (side authoritative, top notes it is not visible); engine end (nozzle count, arrangement, diameter, recessed or flush) somewhere on the sheet, by inset if the three-quarter cannot see it; colour scheme and break lines identical in all views; Hullbreaker patching allowed but the same patches in all three views, new damage per view is a fail.

Per view: three-quarter shows bow profile and overhang, near-side deck edge, near-side guns, foredeck fittings, bow belly and keel blade, bridge and mast, engine end or inset. Side shows length and height, deck edge in profile, full belly and keel, near-side guns and elevations, far-side guns dashed and noted, engine nozzles, superstructure height. Top shows plan outline and beam, both deck edge lines, every gun's plan position with an azimuth arrow, prow outline, deck fittings, engine nozzle plan arrangement, and a note that the keel blade is not visible.

Fail rules: a required view missing or a true view in perspective; different outlines, gun counts, feature counts or colour breaks across views; length not from build data or scale bar missing; overhang not dimensioned; engine end shown nowhere; any gun pointing aft or ambiguously; a required feature missing where a view should show it, without note or inset; dimensions disagreeing beyond its proposed 1 percent of stated length or 0.5 world units, angles beyond 5 deg; a side or top view that is the three-quarter re-projected or traced; a feature silently omitted instead of marked not visible. A sheet passes only when a person with the three pictures and a ruler can measure the same design three ways and get the same answer.

## 2. Disagreement protocol (its draft)

The view that sees the fact most squarely and orthographically wins; the three-quarter is never the authority for a measurement. Length: side. Heights: side. Beam and plan outline: top. Gun placement: top for station and offset, side for mount height and elevation, split by axis. Gun azimuth: top; elevation: side. Colour: three-quarter for identity, but if a large flat side area is clearly a different colour that is a contradiction and the sheet fails rather than anyone picking. Surface detail: the most square-on view of that surface (top for deck, side for flank, three-quarter for bow and foredeck); if two square-on views disagree, fail.

Missing features: first check legitimate invisibility (keel blade and belly in top, far-side guns in side, engine end in three-quarter); if the sheet notes it or gives an inset, it is not a disagreement. If the missing view should show the feature, the sheet fails; do not average, infer, or let the build side choose. If two views disagree on a measurement, the winning view gives the number for judgement, but the sheet still fails until corrected: the build side needs one design, not a vote.

## 3. Honest note (its draft, condensed)

Models hold a single three-quarter silhouette and colour scheme passably; they do not reliably hold one design across three views. Typical drift, per the leg: length and beam change between views; top views get beam, plan outline and gun azimuths wrong; gun count and station shift; barrels drift sideways or backward; engine nozzles are invented, omitted or moved; prow overhang and belly disappear in side view; the colour break moves; patching is hallucinated differently per view; far-side features vanish; true side and top often come out as another perspective instead. Build side: side is authority for length and height, top for beam, plan and gun azimuth, three-quarter for colour and detail only; state length from build data, never the drawing; reject failing sheets instead of negotiating compromises; use an orthographic blockout or guide where possible.
