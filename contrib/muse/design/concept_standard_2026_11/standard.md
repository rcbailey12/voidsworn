# Concept sheet standard (Voidsworn, from issue 398)

One design, three views, drawn to be measured from. If the three pictures could be three different ships, the sheet fails. Chaired from the council legs in this folder (leg_rey, leg_deepseek, leg_qwen, leg_flash); the C15s trial that tested it is in trial.md.

## The three views

All orthographic except the three-quarter. Same ship, same scale on side and top.

- Three-quarter: 45 degrees off the bow centreline toward port, 30 degrees above the deck plane, bow pointing left. The identity view: colour, material, massing. Never a measurement source.
- True side: on the port beam, 0 degrees elevation, perpendicular to the centreline, bow left. The length and height master.
- True top: directly overhead, bow pointing up the page, port side on the left of the page. The beam and placement master. The belly and any keel fin must not appear in it; if they do, the sheet fails.

## One light, one background

One key light for all three views: from forward, port and above (45 degrees up, 45 degrees to port of the bow), with a soft fill at about a fifth of the key from aft-starboard. Shadows fall the same way in every view; nobody relights one view. Background: flat neutral dark grey in all three (chair ruling over the legs' black: the original C15s concept's black ground ate the dark aft hull and fringed the silhouette; the trial's grey sheets kept every edge readable).

## What every sheet carries in writing

Pictures alone are not a sheet. Every sheet also carries, printed on it or attached to it:

- The ship's length in world units, from the build data, never estimated from the drawing, plus a 5-unit scale bar on the side and top views.
- Faction, class, variant, and the sheet's version or date.
- A feature table: every gun and major fitting with its count, its station as a percentage of the length from the prow tip, its offset from the centreline, and which way it points. On counts, the table outranks the pictures.

## What each view must show

- Side: the bow profile and the prow overhang, dimensioned (overhang is the horizontal distance from the forward-most hull point to the forward end of the deck edge line; if the deck edge runs to the tip, the overhang is 0 and the sheet gives the prow rake instead). The deck edge line in one unbroken read. Every near-side gun at its rest angle, far-side guns dashed and noted. The full belly line and any keel fin. The engine end in profile, nozzles drawn. Colour breaks at their stations.
- Top: the plan outline and the beam at its widest, with where that falls. Every gun's plan position and pointing direction drawn as the barrel itself. Both deck edge lines. The prow outline from above. Deck fittings and the bridge footprint.
- Three-quarter: the colour zones and where their boundaries sit, the material read, the features that name the ship in one glance.

The engine end must appear somewhere on the sheet. If the concept a sheet is drawn from cannot show it, the sheet carries an inset, or marks the engine end TO BE DESIGNED where a builder will see it. It is never silently invented and never silently dropped.

## How a sheet fails

Any one of these sends the sheet back:

- A required view is missing, cropped, or drawn in perspective where the standard asks for orthographic.
- A side or top view that is the three-quarter re-projected or traced, carrying its distortion with it.
- Dimensions disagreeing between views by more than 2 percent of the stated length, or a view disagreeing with the stated length.
- Gun counts, feature counts, hull outlines or colour-break stations differing between views, or between a view and the feature table.
- Any gun pointing aft, or drawn so its direction is ambiguous, or drawn from the wrong aspect (a turret face looking at the viewer in a profile view is a fail, not a style).
- A feature missing from a view that should show it, with no note or inset.
- No stated length, no scale bar, or no feature table.
- Anything that reads as a sea vessel. These are space ships.

## When the views disagree

The build needs one design, not a vote. The winning view gives the number; the sheet is still corrected before it is built from.

- Length, prow overhang, heights, stations along the length: the side view wins.
- Beam, lateral placement, gun azimuth: the top view wins.
- Gun placement overall: top for plan position, side for mount height and elevation.
- Colour-break stations: the side view wins. Colour identity and surface detail: the three-quarter wins, except that a large flat area in a clearly different colour on a square-on view is a contradiction, and contradictions fail the sheet rather than get picked through.
- Missing features: a feature in the three-quarter but in neither orthographic view does not exist for the build. A feature in one orthographic view but missing where the other should show it fails the sheet until it is resolved. Belly-only features are exempt from the top view and carry a note saying so.

## Building from a sheet

Geometry comes from the side and top views together, paint from the three-quarter. Nobody traces a three-quarter for a measurement; that is the failure this standard exists to end (rounds 7 and 8, issue 209). If it is not pinned by a view or the feature table, it is not agreed, and the builder asks before assuming.
