Mandatory checklist: stated length in text on the sheet ("LENGTH: 24 WORLD UNITS" as its cruiser example) with a segmented 10-unit scale bar; prow overhang read against the keel line in the side view; deck edge line legible in all three; every weapon visible in at least two views with facing angles and arc limits; belly delineated in the side view; engine end with nozzle counts in all views. Failure criteria: station drift between top and side under projected guide lines, hardpoints missing from the plan, ghost modules switching sides, naval carve-outs.

Disagreement protocol (table): side wins longitudinal lengths and vertical heights; top wins beam, gun mount coordinates and gun azimuth; side wins barrel pitch; three-quarter wins livery, markings and surface detail, with side and top setting the colour separation boundaries. Missing-feature rule, its best contribution: orthographic absence overrules perspective (a macro feature in the three-quarter but in neither orthographic view does not exist and is removed from the build); a feature one orthographic view legitimately cannot show (a ventral fin in plan) needs only the other orthographic view to exist.

Drift note: models render each view independently with no shared 3D model; named vulnerabilities are prow stretch and compression between views, asymmetry amnesia (racks and clusters mirrored, moved or dropped), and stern/thruster hallucination. Builder directives: never model off the three-quarter; block out from side and top planes aligned to shared stations first; apply the hierarchy mechanically and measure displacements instead of eyeballing.
