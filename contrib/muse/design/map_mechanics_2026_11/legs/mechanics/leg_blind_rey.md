# Blind Rey leg (written before any council leg was seen)

## A. Mechanics

1. Evasion by distance moved (BattleTech transplant). A ship gains a to-hit penalty against it scaled by hexes travelled this round (for example -5% per hex beyond the first, capped near -30%), expiring at its next activation; dedicated sensor ships can strip it. Causal path: standing still becomes measurably dangerous, moving far becomes defense, fast hulls gain a survival identity distinct from armour. The bot can price it because its offense and threat terms already estimate expected damage; evasion just enters those estimates. Test: mirror battles, metric is post-contact distance per move and rounds survived by the fastest hull class; pass if distance per move rises toward the speed budget and fast hull win share stops lagging. Risk: slows killing, battles lengthen; cap and the strip ability are the counters.
2. Move-or-fire for siege weapons (Advance Wars transplant). The heaviest weapon class (long-range lances and siege guns) may not fire on an activation in which the ship moved more than 1 hex; light weapons are unrestricted. Causal path: creates two movement cultures in one fleet: brawlers keep flying, artillery repositions between shots and needs screens, and rushing inside a siege ship's comfort zone becomes a tactic. Test: battles with mixed fleets; metric is mean distance between opposing fleets over rounds and siege hull reposition frequency. Risk: feel-bad turns for the artillery player; mitigate by letting it fire after a 1-hex adjust.
3. Crossfire. A target engaged in the same round from two or more distinct facings takes bonus damage or degraded shield routing (the pooled shield cannot face two ways). Causal path: splitting your fleet around a target pays in damage, which is the one currency the current scoring already understands; spread emerges from offense, not from an objective term. Test: shot clustering and the angular spread of attackers per target; pass if cluster share falls and multi-axis engagements appear. Risk: focus fire is already strong; the bonus must reward axes, not just concentration, or it makes the blob deadlier.

## B. AI design

4. Threat and value fields (influence maps). Replace the point threat calculation with a propagated field: each enemy weapon paints threat over the hexes it can reach next activation, decaying with distance; friendly value paints coverage. Destination scoring reads the field. Causal path: the bot gains spatial awareness it currently lacks; it can skirt threat edges, hold the frontier, and avoid stacking the whole fleet in one threat cell. This is the standard technique Voidsworn is missing, and the evidence (nothing invisible to scoring moves ships) says spatial information must enter through scoring or not at all. Test: coverage and cluster share in rout battles with no objective involved, isolating the field effect. Risk: field computation cost per activation; the board is small (about 1,000 hexes), so it is affordable.
5. Assignment before movement. A light coordination pass per round: assign each ship a target or a site before individual scoring, with a penalty for choosing a target already assigned to two allies (unless it is the focus call). Causal path: kills the whole fleet independently picking the same nearest target, which is the mechanical cause of the blob. Test: distinct targets engaged per round, cluster share. Risk: rigid assignments feel dumb when the battle turns; keep it a soft penalty, never a lock.

## C. Bot approach

6. Price movement in the offense term, not beside it. Instead of adding new bonus terms (which the pull-crank experiment showed degrades into transit turns), fold the new mechanics (evasion expectation, crossfire axes) into the existing expected-damage and expected-threat estimates, so movement competes inside the same currency as shooting. Causal path: every failed experiment added a term next to offense; the one lever never tried is changing what offense and threat themselves estimate. Test: one mechanic at a time (evasion first), same metrics as its own test. Risk: estimate complexity grows; keep each fold a single multiplier.

Ranking: 4 (fields) first because it is the missing capability every other idea leans on; 1 (evasion) second because it is one rule, human-legible, and directly answers the owner's complaint; 3 (crossfire) third because it pays spread in damage, the currency that already rules the bot.
