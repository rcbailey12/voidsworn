# Glasswright (ithar) Paint Stack - Design Spec Leg

## 1. Colour Zones

Every zone below is defined by its sRGB (lo, hi) pair (the 25th and 75th percentile of the hue class sampled from the approved CD5 concept, 1536 × 1024, background excluded), its lighting role, and how it is driven. Pairs are stored in CONCEPT exactly as in the Hegemony precedent (tools/blender/kits/hegemony/stack.py:40-47).

**Glow discipline.** Following the Hegemony precedent (hegemony/stack.py:331-344): nothing on the intact Glasswright hull crosses the 0.8 glow threshold (look.gd:120-126) except the six zones above. The glass, pearl, vein, amber, violet, green, and edge zones are purely albedo/ORM and must have zero emissive contribution in the baked output. The earlier burnout incident (emissive above threshold over a quarter of the hull, look.gd:18-19) is the cautionary baseline.

## 2. Faking Glass

The bake path produces albedo, ORM, and a hull-detail normal (paint_stack.py, header items 4-5). Baked images are created with alpha=False (paint_stack.py:142). zone_material builds a plain Principled BSDF (paint_stack.py:392). Nothing in the shared file sets transmission, transparency, or subsurface scattering. The concept image is never projected; only sampled (paint_stack.py, header, last lines).

### What the stack does (bake-time, Blender)

**Value and saturation.** The glass zone is very high value, near-zero saturation (#dccacf-#fff4f4). On an opaque Principled BSDF with low roughness this reads as a bright, reflective surface that the eye interprets as glass rather than painted metal. The pearl zone drops to mid value, giving the impression of thicker, milkier glass.

**Roughness gradient as "thickness."** Roughness in the ORM bake is not uniform. It runs from 0.08 at the thinnest blown regions (driven by B long-AO, which correlates with convexity and thinness on a leaf-shaped hull) to 0.35 at the thickest ridges. This gradient mimics the way real blown glass goes from transparent-thin to translucent-thick without any actual transmission.

**Specular kept at default.** The Principled BSDF's specular parameter is left at its default (0.5, giving ~4 % F₀). Combined with the low roughness this gives a visible environment reflection that sells the glass. No custom specular boost is needed; raising it would push the surface toward chrome.

**Internal glow as emissive surfaces behind the albedo.** The stained panes (ipane_a, ipane_v, ipane_g) are separate mesh regions sorted into the glow set by split() (paint_stack.py:42). They sit geometrically flush with the hull (R196: one continuous skin, no seam) but are distinct objects so they receive emissive materials from glow_material. The surrounding hull albedo (the glass or pearl zone) frames them. The visual effect is a coloured glow surrounded by glass, reading as "light inside the pane."

**Depth cue via long AO.** The B mask (long-reach ambient occlusion, header item 2) darkens concavities and the interior of the leaf's twist. On the albedo this is multiplied subtly (5-10 %) into the glass zone to give the impression that the glass has depth and the light is attenuating through it. This is the single strongest "glass thickness" cue available in an opaque pipeline.

**Edge brightness.** The G mask (bevel-normal vs. face-normal, header item 2) drives the edge zone. Cut edges get a very bright, low-roughness strip with the hue-cycle tint (§3). This mimics the way a cut glass edge catches and spreads light.

### What the game side must add (Godot runtime)

**Fresnel rim in the hull shader.** The Godot hull shader should add a view-dependent fresnel term that lifts the brightness of the glass and pearl zones at grazing angles. This is the single most important runtime addition; without it the hull reads as glazed ceramic. The fresnel should feed into the albedo channel, not emission, so it does not cross the glow threshold. The faction's accent colour [0.88, 0.90, 0.96] from faction_looks.json is a suitable fresnel tint.

**Screen-space or reflection-probe environment reflection.** The Principled BSDF specular relies on an environment to reflect. If the Godot scene lacks a reflection probe or SSR pass, the glass zones will read flat. At minimum, a low-resolution cubemap or the existing sky reflection must be available to the hull shader.

### What is honestly out of reach

**True refraction and caustics.** No bake in the pipeline produces a refraction map or caustic pattern, and the opaque Principled BSDF cannot refract. The rainbow fringe is a surface tint, not spectral dispersion.

**See-through transparency.** alpha=False on baked images (paint_stack.py:142) and the absence of any transmission node mean the hull is fully opaque. The player will never see internal structure through the glass. The "stored light glowing deep inside" is represented by emissive panes, not by visible internal geometry.

**Subsurface scattering.** The milky quality of pearl glass is faked with roughness and AO, not SSS. Close-up inspection will show the surface as opaque satin, not translucent.

**Spectral rendering.** The "rainbow fringe" is a hue-cycle approximation, not a physically accurate dispersion.

## 3. Masks and Procedural Drivers

The shared machinery bakes three masks into each atlas (header, item 2): R = short-reach AO, G = edge (bevel normal vs. face normal), B = long-reach AO. Layers are additionally driven by island id, noise, world position, and face normal (header, item 3). Below is the full driver map for every layer in the Glasswright stack.

### Existing masks

| Mask | Layers driven | How |
|---|---|---|
| R - short AO | Silver veins: veins sit in shallow channels on the glass surface. Short AO darkens the recesses, giving each vein a subtle shadow on one side. Also used to lightly darken the border where a stained pane meets the surrounding glass (a 2-3 px soft shadow, no visible seam per R196, but enough to seat the pane). | Multiply into albedo at 8-12 % intensity. |
| G - edges | Rainbow fringe: the G mask identifies every cut edge on the hull. The edge zone is applied where G > threshold. The hue-cycle tint (§1.1, edge) is mapped over this mask. Also drives a thin bright line at the junction between glass and pearl regions where the blown form has a fold. | G mask gates the edge layer; face-normal angle around the local edge tangent drives the hue cycle (see below). |
| B - long AO | Glass depth cue (§2, item 5): darkens the deep concavities of the twisted leaf. Also drives the internal-glow gradient for the stained panes: the B mask is inverted and used as a multiplier on the emissive map so the glow is strongest where the glass is thinnest (convex, low long-AO) and fades where it thickens. | Multiply into albedo at 5-10 %. Multiply into pane emissive maps at 30-50 %. |

### Procedural drivers

| Driver | Layers driven | How |
|---|---|---|
| Island id | Two-tone assignment for glass/pearl. Each plate island on the hull atlas receives either the lo or hi tone, following the shared two-tone mechanism (header, item 3). For the Glasswright the two tones are the clear-glass pair and the pearly pair, so islands alternate between bright clear and mid pearly. | Direct lookup; no blending between islands (the blown form has soft transitions modelled in the geometry, not painted). |
| Noise (procedural, stack-defined) | (a) Silver vein layout: a ridged-multifractal noise generates the vein network. The noise is thresholded to produce thin, branching lines. The veins follow the long axis of the leaf, biased by world position (below). (b) Blown-glass surface irregularity: a low-frequency, low-amplitude noise perturbs the roughness in the ORM bake by ±0.03, breaking up the uniformity so the glass does not read as flat plastic. (c) Stern bloom variation: a very low-frequency noise modulates the ibloom emission map to give the bloom a faintly organic, breathing quality without flicker. | Noise is generated in the faction stack's layer definitions and composited in the procedural material (header, item 3). No new bake pass is needed. |
| World position | (a) Stored-light gradient: the ship's internal glow is brightest at the core (mid-hull) and falls toward bow and stern. A gradient keyed to the long-axis coordinate (local X) multiplies the emissive maps of the three pane zones. This makes the panes near the core glow more warmly, as if fed by the stored light. (b) Silver vein density: veins are denser near the centre of the leaf and sparser toward the tips, driven by a world-position-based density ramp on the noise threshold. (c) Lance glow axis: the ilance emission is keyed to the bow optic's world position so the radial falloff is centred correctly. | World-position lookup in the procedural material, remapped to 0-1 along the relevant axis. |
| Face normal | (a) Rainbow fringe hue: the hue of the edge tint cycles as a function of the angle between the face normal and the view direction (baked as a fixed approximation using the face normal's azimuth around the hull's long axis). This gives adjacent edges slightly different hues, approximating dispersion. (b) Glass brightness: faces pointing toward the "key light" direction (a fixed direction baked into the stack, matching the game's dominant light) get a 5 % value boost to simulate the way blown glass catches light directionally. | Dot-product and atan2 of the face normal, computed in the procedural material. |

### New mask required: T - Thickness / Curvature

The existing three masks do not capture local curvature in a way that distinguishes thin blown shell from thick structural ridges. The Glasswright stack needs a T mask: a per-texel estimate of local shell thickness, derived from the signed distance between the outer hull surface and the inner hull surface (the interior liner).

**What it measures:** Approximate glass thickness. Thin regions (the broad faces of the leaf) get low T; thick regions (the spine, the optic housings, the lance barrel) get high T.

**How it is baked:** The shared machinery already bakes AO by raycasting from the surface (paint_stack.py, header item 2). The T mask uses the same raycasting infrastructure but casts inward along the face normal and records the distance to the first interior hit (the liner mesh). This is a single additional bake pass per atlas, identical in setup to the existing AO bakes but with a different measurement. The result is stored in the alpha channel of the ORM texture (currently unused, since alpha=False on baked images at paint_stack.py:142 would need a faction-level override to enable for this one map; alternatively, T is packed into a fourth channel of a dedicated mask texture if the shared file's alpha constraint is absolute).

**Driven layers:** Glass roughness (thin → lower roughness, thick → higher); pane emissive multiplier (thin glass glows more); vein visibility (veins are only visible where glass is thin, T below a threshold).

If the alpha-channel override is too invasive for the shared file, the fallback is to approximate T with an inverted, heavily blurred B mask (long AO correlates loosely with concavity and therefore thickness). This is less accurate but requires no changes to paint_stack.py.

## 4. Material Names (Zone Sets)

The build sheet assigns each object's material as its zone name. Names follow the existing short-noun style (soot, iron, pale, brass, glow, ember).

**ZONES (surface, baked into hull/parts atlas, opaque Principled BSDF)**

- glass - clear blown-glass faces
- pearl - pearly / milky mid-glass
- vein - silver vein inclusions
- amber - amber stained pane, unlit albedo
- violet - violet stained pane, unlit albedo
- green - green stained pane, unlit albedo
- edge - cut-edge highlight / rainbow fringe base

Seven zones. No soot, rust, iron, or grime zones: the Glasswright hull does not corrode, and the shared soot/rust/grime layers are disabled in the faction stack (see §5). No blood or warhead: weapons are optical, not ballistic.

**GLOW (emissive, sorted by split() at paint_stack.py:42, materials from glow_material)**

- ilance - prism-lance emission at the bow optic
- ibloom - stern engine bloom
- ipane_a - amber pane internal glow
- ipane_v - violet pane internal glow
- ipane_g - green pane internal glow
- idamage - breach / shatter glow (runtime-driven, slot reserved)

Six glow zones. The i prefix follows the Hegemony convention (hblue, hlens, etc., hegemony/stack.py:331-344). The pane glow zones are separate from the pane albedo zones because the pane objects must be sorted into the glow set by split() to receive emissive materials; their albedo is provided by the corresponding surface zone (amber, violet, green) on the hull atlas where the pane border meets the surrounding glass.

**Interior**

Per the recipe, interiors get one dark liner material per room. The Glasswright core "keeps its light": the core room's liner is a very dark glass (#1a1520 to #2a2230) with a single reserved emissive strip (icore, treated as part of the interior set, not the GLOW set) representing the stored-light reservoir. This follows the recipe's note that "the core keeps its light."

## 5. Trim Sheet and Decals

**Trim sheet: disabled.**

The shared machinery's trim sheet provides "rivets, scuffs, stripes, box projected" (header, item 3). None of these apply to the Glasswright:

- **No rivets.** R196 mandates one continuous skin of glass and metal with no visible seam, bracket, or mount. Rivets would violate the faction's first structural rule.
- **No scuffs in the conventional sense.** Blown glass does not scuff; it chips, cracks, and frosts. Edge wear is handled by the G-mask edge layer (§3), not a scuff decal. The ORM roughness noise (§3, noise driver) provides the surface variation that a scuff layer would give on a metal hull.
- **No stripes.** R192: "every structural part is shaped with care and is also the ornament." Applied stripes are decoration, not structure. The Glasswright's ornament is the form of the glass itself, the vein pattern, and the colour of the panes. Stripes would be an applied decoration on top of a surface that is already the ornament.

The faction stack sets the trim-sheet layer weight to 0 for all zones. The box-projection pass is skipped.

**Decals: disabled.**

No faction insignia, no hull numbers, no warning labels. The Glasswright aesthetic (R192) treats the ship as a single crafted instrument; applied decals would read as stickers on a lens. Any identifying mark would be ground into the glass as part of the vein pattern or the pane layout, which is a geometry/material concern on the build sheet, not a decal pass.

If a future content need arises (e.g., a fleet identification glyph), it should be added as a modification to the vein noise pattern for that specific ship variant, not as a decal layer.

**What replaces them**

The visual richness that trim sheets and decals provide on other factions is carried by:

1. The silver vein network (procedural noise, §3).
2. The rainbow edge fringe (G mask, §3).
3. The roughness variation of blown glass (noise on ORM, §3).
4. The colour variation of the stained panes.
5. The long-AO depth shading.

Together these give a dense, crafted surface without any applied industrial detail.

## 6. Risks and Fallbacks

**Risk 1 - Glow burnout across the hull**

The risk. The Glasswright ship is covered in emissive surfaces: three pane colours, a lance, a stern bloom, and a damage-glow slot. If the pane emissive maps are too aggressive, or if the world-position gradient (§3) pushes too many panes above the 0.8 threshold (look.gd:120-126), the ship will bloom across a large fraction of its surface, repeating the incident where "its baked emissive map sat above threshold over a quarter of the hull" (look.gd:18-19). The glTF emissive strength of 4.0 (look.gd:47-49) means even a modest base emissive of 0.25 reaches 1.0 effective, well above threshold.

**Fallback.** The pane glow zones (ipane_a, ipane_v, ipane_g) are clamped in the faction stack's glow_material to a maximum base emissive of 0.18 (× 4.0 = 0.72, below the 0.8 threshold). Only ilance and ibloom are permitted to exceed threshold, and their emission maps are spatially confined to the optic discs. A validation step in the stack asserts that no more than 12 % of the total glow-atlas texels exceed 0.8 before the bake is finalised. If the art direction later demands brighter panes, the glow threshold for the ithar faction can be raised in faction_looks.json as a per-faction override rather than loosening the emissive clamp.

**Risk 2 - Glass reads as glazed plastic or ceramic**

The risk. Without transmission, refraction, or SSS (§2), the opaque Principled BSDF with a bright, low-roughness albedo can read as glossy plastic or porcelain, especially in evenly lit scenes where there is no environment contrast to produce reflections. The fresnel rim and reflection probe identified in §2 as game-side requirements may be delayed or deprioritised in the Godot shader pass, leaving the hull flat.

**Fallback.** If the game-side fresnel is not available at first integration, the faction stack bakes a fixed view-direction fresnel approximation into the albedo: the face-normal driver (§3) brightens edges of each glass panel by 8-12 % and darkens the centre by 3-5 %, producing a static rim effect that mimics fresnel without runtime support. This is visually inferior (it does not respond to camera angle) but prevents the plastic reading. The ORM roughness is also raised by a flat +0.05 across all glass zones as a compensating measure, since a slightly rougher surface is more forgiving of missing reflections. This fallback is removed once the Godot fresnel is confirmed.

**Risk 3 - Rainbow fringe aliases and becomes noise at game resolution**

The risk. The edge zone's hue-cycle tint is driven by the G mask (bevel-normal vs. face-normal, header item 2), which produces a 1-3 texel-wide line at each cut edge. At game rendering resolution (the ship may occupy 200-400 px on screen), a 1-texel line with a rapidly cycling hue will alias, flicker, and read as chromatic noise rather than a clean prismatic fringe. The effect is worst on the twisted-leaf geometry where many edges converge.

**Fallback.** The hue cycle is computed at quarter resolution in the bake (each edge texel samples the hue from a 4×4 neighbourhood average), producing a smooth, slow gradient along each edge rather than a per-texel hue jump. The fringe width is widened from the raw G-mask edge to a 3-texel feathered band by dilating the G mask before applying the tint. If aliasing persists at typical gameplay camera distances, the fringe is reduced to a fixed two-colour gradient (warm at one end of the edge, cool at the other, sampled from the lance-tip orange #e26833 and the engine blue [0.82, 0.94, 1.0]) rather than a full spectrum cycle. This trades physical accuracy for readability. The full hue cycle is retained in the bake file at full resolution for cinematic / screenshot use.
