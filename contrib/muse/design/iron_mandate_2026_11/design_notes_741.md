# The Iron Mandate (issue 741): three concept directions for h_boss

Refs #708. The Hegemony flagship (super-dreadnought, 31.0 units, boss) gets its own design inside the wedge language, not a scaled Dominion (R232; Ryan, Nov 12, 2026: "I like it being unique from existing ships"). Pictures: IM1.jpg, IM2.jpg, IM3.jpg, each 1080 x 720, in the realistic Scrap Brick style (R197) with rigid clean Hegemony panelling (R243).

## The data every direction carries (R241: exactly these weapons, nothing decorative)

From data.js, h_boss: Heavy pulse laser battery, port arc (hturbo); Heavy pulse laser battery, starboard arc (hturbo); Pulse laser turret (turbo); Ion cannon, forward arc (ion), which in this faction is always the recessed emitter trench in the bow face, never a barrel or prongs; Flak battery turret (flak). Ability: nova. No hangar in the data, so no drone bays on any direction, whatever the faction sheet's general line says. The hegemony pack's older weapon names (Heavy plasma lance and friends) name these same slots; data.js names govern.

Standing language all three hold: heavy wedge about 3:1, low sunk shoulder mounts with short thick barrels (never tall round turrets), one low sensor fin (never a tower or mast), flush rigid panels, a flat row of rectangular blue-white engine nozzles at the stern, implant-blue light strips pulsing in sync, satin pale grey with steel-blue trim. Never rust, asymmetry, ornament or warm light. R244: no flat-box hull (facets, chamfers, steps), no flat disc openings (recesses with real depth). Guns never face backward at rest.

## IM1: The Terraced Fortress (chair's pick)

A broad blunt wedge built as terraced armour modules stepping down toward the bow, separated by deep bulkhead rings, the flanks chamfered and banded with implant-blue light lines. The bow face is dominated by the ion emitter trench, a deep recessed slot with baffles and the blue glow deep inside. The heavy pulse batteries sit on the shoulders as low armoured mounts; the pulse laser turret is a single low mount on the dorsal centreline; the flak cluster sits aft of the sensor fin; a flat row of rectangular blue-white nozzles closes the stern between deep rail outriggers.

Weapon callouts (everything pictured that is a weapon):
- Shoulder mount on each side, matching pair: the Heavy pulse laser batteries (port and starboard slots).
- Single low dorsal turret amidships: the Pulse laser turret.
- Recessed trench in the bow face: the Ion cannon.
- The flak cluster and sensor fin are weak in this render (see production notes).

Why this one: it is where the council converged. The blind leg's terraced slab, Flash's Fortress Monolith and DeepSeek's Bulwark are the same architecture from three independent legs: mass through terracing rather than length, a fortress the Dominion's sleek wedge cannot be mistaken for.

## IM2: The Split-Spine Anvil

A low flat wedge split by a deep dorsal channel running the full length of the hull between two raised spine-ridges; the channel is a real shadowed recess with decking and capacitor housing at its floor, and the twin rails of the faction carried forward as the architecture itself. The batteries ride the outer faces of the ridges as repeated matching low mounts; the ion trench sits in the bow face's lower centre; the flak mount closes the channel at its stern end; the engine row runs across the stern between the ridge roots.

Weapon callouts:
- Matching low barrel mounts along both ridge faces: the Heavy pulse laser batteries (one slot per side; the mounts of a slot match each other, per the SAME discipline in ships.md rule 11).
- Recessed trench in the bow face: the Ion cannon.
- Small cluster mount at the channel's stern end: the Flak battery.
- The Pulse laser turret is ventral in DeepSeek's leg and so not visible from this angle; the sensor fin reads at the bow deck.

Chair's note: this is DeepSeek's pick and the most original silhouette of the three; from the battle camera's top-down read, nothing else in the fleet has a channel. It is the chair's close second. One correction the chair made to DeepSeek's leg before rendering: the leg put the sensor fin on the port ridge, which breaks the faction's symmetry rule; the fin is centred.

## IM3: The Catamaran Mandate

Two full-length parallel wedge hulls joined by a central command spine, the gap between the hulls open along the mid-body. One broad blunt bow face spans both hulls with the ion trench recessed across its centre. The heavy pulse batteries sit on the outer shoulders, one matching mount per hull; the pulse laser turret and the flak cluster ride the spine; each hull's stern carries its own row of rectangular blue-white nozzles.

Weapon callouts:
- Four-barrel armoured mount on each hull's outer shoulder, matching pair: the Heavy pulse laser batteries.
- Low mount on the spine amidships: the Pulse laser turret.
- Small cluster at the spine's aft end: the Flak battery.
- Recessed trench spanning the bow centre: the Ion cannon.

Production note: the render mixes its ends at the near face, with nozzle-like blue blocks visible on the hull ends beside the bow trench. The build sheet must fix the ends: trench at the bow, nozzles only at the stern. The architecture itself (twin hulls, spine, open gaps) rendered exactly as briefed.

## Production notes applying to all three

- Barrel counts inside one battery mount vary between renders (pairs, triples, a four-barrel row). R241 pins the slots, not the barrel count of a battery; the build sheet should settle one barrel arrangement per battery and hold it across the fleet's Hegemony sheets.
- Light strips in the renders sit as broken dashes along seams and chamfers, matching the pack's pulsing-strip language; keep them out of panel centres on the sheets.
- No direction carries drone bays, towers, masts, rust or warm light; the chair checked each render against the faction sheet's never list.

## Chair's pick

IM1, the Terraced Fortress: the council's convergence, the lowest-risk read as the flagship (unmistakably Hegemony, unmistakably not the Dominion), and the architecture that scales down the fleet's language least when the smaller classes borrow from it. IM2 is the bolder choice if Ryan wants the flagship to own a silhouette no other hull shares; IM3 is the outsider, the only twin-hulled ship in the game if picked. The blind leg leaned to the catamaran before the council; the convergence on the fortress, and its cleaner fit with the settled H10 line, moved the chair's pick. Recorded so the shift is on the record.
