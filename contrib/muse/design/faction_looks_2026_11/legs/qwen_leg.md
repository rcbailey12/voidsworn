THE GLASSWRIGHTS
Species Base / People
One species base. Organic beings who evolved to shape glass. They are not made of glass, not crystal constructs, not assembled from separate materials (R66, owner verdict Nov 2). Glass and glass like matter is grown and woven into living tissue as one continuous substance system: the same prismatic material threads through flesh, forms the lens eyes, grows the sail vanes, and draws out as spinneret glass thread. A single material logic governs every visible element (owner verdict Nov 2, issue 48).
Body plan: tall, slender, long necked. Four arms: two primary working arms and two smaller precision arms (owner verdict Nov 2). Wrist spinnerets on the precision arms draw and weave glass thread onto a workpiece. Carriage is spider weaver: segmented thorax, low wide stance, support limbs. Full spider body is too monstrous for the base; the weaver carriage reads as posture and proportion, not as a literal arachnid. Long tapering fingers on all four hands.
Face (Nov 1 portrait ruling, owner): two forward grown lens eyes set side by side, deep green, flush in the flesh. Lids and a heavy segmented brow carry expression. A short reed grille sits flush low in the face as the speaking mouth. Speech is a normal voice. No vertical eye stacks, no rings of bead eyes, no secondary eye arcs, no extra brow magnifier lenses. No camera aperture rims, no brass fittings, no jewellers loupes. The face sits on a young to old spectrum: young Glasswrights have rooted, near Starwright faces with craft grown lens eyes just forming; old Glasswrights show deep lens eye infusion where the glass has grown further into the orbital structure over centuries.
Prism Sails (owner verdict Nov 2): grown crystal sail vanes on the back and shoulders. Practical, modest in size, jointed with tendon and cartilage (grown, no hardware, no brackets). They furl at the work bench and one edge focuses light onto the piece being worked. These are working glasswrights shown at their craft. Age and admiral variety live in the sail plan: larger sails, bloom edged, worn for elders. No literal naval sails on a mast.
Emotion tell: light runs along the glass in the body and splits into prismatic colour at its edges. It catches brighter when the being feels strongly. This is involuntary. It is not speech. They speak normally through the reed grille (Nov 1 portrait ruling). They are markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse.
Clothing and adornment: layered crystal and mirror thread fabrics. Small light cores worn as jewellery. Clothing integrates with the one substance system: woven glass thread, not bolted on hardware.
Package direction pick: A Threaded (fine crystal threads woven through flesh like fibre optics under skin, body reads as a person first) as base. C Bloomed (crystal clusters growing outward at joints, spine, crown like frost flowers) allowed for one admiral only (chair pick, R78 Q2 notes this is inspiration not locked).
Lifespan: 300 to 400 years.
Admiral variation (data.js:229 to 232, R68):
Tamsa of the Clear Aperture: youngest at ninety, wing admiral, Starling flight, raised in the Ringing Field salvage crews. Young face, lens eyes newly grown, smaller practical sails.
Saro of Long Focus: oldest after the Lens keeper, lance admiral, Burning glass, sixty years on the Astra watch, speaks least. Deep lens eye infusion, large worn sails, bloom edged.
Vell of the Third Mirror: duellist and mirror smith, polishes the flagship great mirror by hand, Scatter the light. Mid age, focused expression, sails angled for precision light work.
Brin of the Fourth Lens: ward admiral, Prism ward, shield engineer, apologises to enemy fleets in writing before battles. Mid age, thoughtful, sails modest and functional.
Nemesis: the Lens keeper duellist. Keeps range. Returns with the same four elite ships upgraded, never enlarged. Quiet, precise, mournful. Apologies meant. No fixed single Nemesis hunter; assignments are symmetric and variable by story point (R78, issue 45).
Ships
Hull language: clear crystal and silver hulls with faceted edges that split light into rainbow fringes. Mirrors and lenses mounted on the hull turn to catch light. Crystal vanes extend from the hull. Stored light glows deep inside the ship. The family shape at every size is a slender, faceted, light catching form identifiable from outline alone at battle distance (Starsector and Endless Space 2 lesson).
Silhouette: elongated, angular, faceted. Flat armoured bellies with chines. Sensor housings and antenna arrays. Equipment pods. Recessed lights. Main engine bells and thruster pods. Docking hatches. Heat radiators. No boat keels, no raked stems, no jackstaffs, no bollards, no anchors, no crows nests, no yardarms with rigging, no mast ladders, no lifeboats, no portholes in rows along a waterline, no crossbar masts, no smokestack funnels.
Parts: snapped onto the real hull surface on visible mounts. Guns aimed forward or out to the side, never backward. Placed in matched symmetric pairs at sensible hard points. Shields keep faction colours: silver white shield flicker.
Weapons: Prism lance, Prism shards, Needle beam, Star torpedo, Mirror veil.

Do
Keep the Knuckle Stalker species base: low and wide, knuckles down at rest, shoulder hump above the head (chair pick, R66).
Face: bare plated horn, short blunt overlapping plates, no fur on face, no snout, no nose (R64).
Jaw splits sideways into two halves, lined with teeth (R64).
Four small eyes across the brow, shine in the dark, heat sensing pits beside them (R64).
Ridged plates down back and shoulders (R64).
Coarse mane over hump, neck and shoulders only. Tough dark hide everywhere else (package).
Posture varies by character: Grax upright, Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies (package).
One hero trophy per portrait: machines and hull plates, never skulls or bodies (R78 Q5).
Battle trophy plates on ships: flagship only (R78 Q5).
Ships are slow and deliberate in fiction, move one at a time in pack order (R64 feel rulings).
Haphazard and junky is on brand for Hullbreaker ships (ship rules exception).
Space ship form: armoured flat bellies, chines, sensor housings, antenna arrays, engine bells, thruster pods, docking hatches, heat radiators.
No lances at all in the weapon loadout.
Oil paint style throughout.
R68: every portrait gets its own setting, pose, framing, lighting.
Faction identifiable from silhouette alone at battle distance (Starsector lesson).
Faction read survives zoomed out battle view (Homeworld 3 lesson).
Display names only in all text.
Hullbreaker Nemesis always raids: debrief credit loss plus one raider line, universal (R78, issue 45).
Do not
Do not make them look like the predator from Alien vs Predator: no quill dreadlocks, no mouth tusks, no helmet look (R66).
Do not make them bears with gear (package).
Do not use skull necklaces or mask face (package).
Do not use dreadlocks of any kind (package).
Do not put fur on the face. Do not add a snout or nose (R64).
Do not use skulls or bodies as trophies. Trophies are machines and hull plates only (R78 Q5).
Do not write fast sleek ships. The package Identity section calling ships fast is overridden by the ruling. Fiction movement is slow and deliberate (R64 feel, chair review flag).
Do not give them lances. No lances at all (package weapons list).
Do not use naval ship elements: boat keels, raked stems, jackstaffs, bollards, anchors, crows nests, yardarms with rigging, mast ladders, lifeboats, portholes in rows along a waterline, crossbar masts, smokestack funnels (ship rules).
Do not aim guns backward (ship rules).
Do not put battle trophy plates on any ship other than the flagship in battle scenes (R78 Q5).
Do not add text or watermark in prompts.
Do not use em dashes or en dashes in any text.
Do not state data ids as display text.
Do not make them sleek or clean or symmetric in ship design. Lopsided and junky is the brand.
