# The Glasswrights

## Species base / People

One substance system governs the whole being: living flesh that grows glass. A Glasswright is a tall, slender, long necked person of pale, half translucent flesh, and the glass in the body is the same flesh in a grown, hardened form, never a separate material bolted on. Fine crystal threads run through the flesh like fibre optics under skin, surfacing at the joints, the spine and the crown as small grown clusters of the same glass. The body reads as a person first, a glasswright second. They are organic beings who evolved to shape glass. They are not made of glass, not crystal constructs, and nothing like grey aliens.

Face. Two forward grown lens eyes set side by side, deep green, sitting flush in the flesh, modest in size. Lids and a heavy segmented brow carry all expression. A short reed grille sits flush low in the face as the speaking mouth; the voice that comes through it is a normal speaking voice. No tubes, no held mouthpieces, nothing worn over the face.

Age spectrum, young to old, is the faction's main variation axis. The young are rooted and near Starwright in the face: smooth pale flesh, lens eyes freshly grown and clean edged, glass threading fine and mostly under the skin. With age the lens eyes infuse deeper, the rims of grown glass thicken around them, the brow segments grow heavier, and the joint and crown clusters bloom larger and more worn. The old look deep infused, not different in kind. Glasswrights live 300 to 400 years, so even the youngest admiral is old by human measure; show age in glass growth, not in wrinkles alone.

Sails. Each Glasswright carries grown crystal sail vanes behind the shoulders, echoing the clear crystal and silver vanes of their ships. The sails are part of the body: jointed on tendon and cartilage, grown, no hardware, no brackets, no fittings. They are modest and practical in size. They furl flat at the bench and open at work, and one edge of an opened sail focuses light down onto the workpiece like a lens. Sail plan carries age and admiral variety: elders carry larger sails with bloom edged, worn tips; the young carry smaller, cleaner vanes.

Arms and craft. Four arms: two full working arms and two smaller precision arms below them, all with long tapering fingers. Wrist spinnerets on the working arms draw and weave glass thread directly onto the workpiece. The carriage holds a little of the spider: a segmented thorax section, a low, wide weaver stance at the bench, and support limbs that brace the body while the hands work. Keep it a hint in the posture and midsection only. A full spider body is too monstrous for the species base and is reserved as a possible ancient elder extreme.

Light and colour. Stored light runs along the glass threading and splits into prismatic colour at its edges. It catches brighter when the Glasswright feels strongly. This is an involuntary emotion tell only. It is never speech, never a signal language, never a deliberate display.

Clothing. Layered fabrics of crystal thread and mirror thread, woven by the wearer's own spinnerets, worn over the torso and lower body, leaving the arms, sails and glass growths clear. Small light cores are worn as jewellery, grown into a collar or a wrist band, never carried as separate gadgets.

## Ships

Silhouette first: every Glasswright ship is a faceted crystal dart built around a clear crystal spine, with silver vane fins toward the rear and a lens or mirror array toward the bow. The family shape holds at every size, so the faction reads from outline alone at battle distance: long, fine, faceted, bright. Hulls are clear crystal over a silver frame, hull colour #eef3f1, with faceted edges that split light into rainbow fringes. Deep inside each hull, stored light glows pale green white. Mirrors and lenses mounted on the hull surface turn to catch light. Crystal vanes spread from the rear hull like the people's sails, grown looking and jointed, never rigged, never on a mast. Engines are recessed silver bells at the stern with pale exhaust. Weapons sit in matched symmetric pairs on visible mounts, aimed forward or out to the side, never backward: prism lance housings along the spine, shard racks at the flanks, a needle beam emitter at the bow. Shields flicker silver white in the faction glow colour #9fffd2. Trim is a pale gold #d7c88a, used sparingly on frame lines and vane roots. No naval tells anywhere: no keel, no raked stem, no porthole rows, no funnels, no masts or rigging. Sensor housings and antenna arrays cluster near the bow lens array. These are the fastest, most delicate looking ships in the game: fine boned, bright, fragile, precise.

## Palette

- Primary hull: clear crystal white, #eef3f1. The body of every ship and the base tone of skin and sail glass.
- Frame and structure: bright silver. Ship frames, vane roots, engine bells; on people, mirror thread in the clothing.
- Trim: pale gold, #d7c88a. Frame lines, vane root edging, jewellery settings.
- Glow: mint stored light, #9fffd2. Shield flicker, weapon charge, jewellery light cores, light inside hulls.
- Light: pale green white stored light. The light inside the body glass and inside the hulls; portrait key light in most settings.
- Edge effect: rainbow fringe. Prismatic splitting at faceted edges of sails, hulls and glass growths; an effect, not a base colour.
- Eye colour: deep green. The grown lens eyes, darker and more saturated than the glow.
- Background: deep dark blue green workshop and void tones, dark enough that the pale bodies and stored light read first. Portraits stay dark behind the figure per the faction's stored light look.

## Do

- Build every Glasswright from the one substance: living flesh that grows its own glass, threaded first, blooming only with age.
- Keep the face constant: two forward deep green lens eyes side by side, flush, with lids and a heavy segmented brow doing the acting, and the reed grille mouth set flush.
- Vary admirals by age on the spectrum, sail plan, pose, setting and lighting, never by inventing a new anatomy.
- Make the sails practical: modest, jointed, grown, furled at rest, one edge focusing light at work.
- Echo the people in the ships: crystal spine, silver frame, rear vanes, bow lens array, stored light inside.
- Keep stored light pale green white and the glow mint #9fffd2 across people, ships, jewellery and effects.
- Write them quiet, precise and mournful; their Nemesis returns with the same four elite ships, upgraded, never enlarged.

## Do not

- Do not make them faceless, and do not use colour in the glass as speech; it is an emotion tell only.
- Do not use human, grey alien or elf bases however dressed up.
- Do not stack eyes or lenses vertically, ring the head with bead eyes, or add secondary eye arcs or brow magnifier lenses.
- Do not add steampunk or hardware: no camera aperture rims, brass fittings, brackets, jewellers loupes, or a voice grille held in the mouth as a tube.
- Do not make crystal construct or robot bodies; that is Eclipse ground.
- Do not put literal naval sails on masts on the people or the ships; vanes are grown crystal, jointed, unrigged.
- Do not give the base a full spider body; the spider stays a hint in carriage and stance, elder extreme only.
- Do not aim any ship gun backward, and do not use naval hull tells: no keels, raked stems, porthole rows or funnels.

## Flags

- R64 describes the Glasswrights as faceless with colour pulse speech, and the Nov 1 owner portrait ruling in the same pack overrides exactly that: they were never ruled faceless and never ruled colour pulse speech. This leg follows the owner ruling, per the pack's own note.
- The package people section says no large eyes, while the Nov 2 face direction specifies two forward grown lens eyes. Read together: the lens eyes stay modest and flush, not large alien eyes; flagged because a prompt writer could take either line alone and drift.
- The package offers three directions (Threaded, Inlaid, Bloomed) with a chair pick of Threaded plus Bloomed for one admiral, while the Nov 2 owner verdict demands one substance system and calls the three substance concepts a failure. This leg resolves it as one substance, flesh grown glass, with bloom as age growth of the same glass rather than a second material; the Inlaid direction is dropped, since set in plates read as a second, placed material.
- The shipped data lists the Glasswright ship style as sail. Under the ship rules and the Nov 2 drop list, that word must mean grown crystal vanes only, never rigged sails on a mast; flagged so the ship pipeline does not take the style word literally.

# Ashfall Hullbreakers

## Species base / People

One species base: the Knuckle Stalker. A huge hunting beast from a cold, heavy gravity belt, wolf and bear inspired in mass and movement but never a bear with gear. The body is low and wide: a big shoulder hump rises above the head, the forearms are thick and heavy, the hands carry heavy claws, and at rest the beast walks knuckles down. A coarse mane covers the hump, neck and shoulders only, in grey, black or rust brown. Everywhere else is tough dark hide. Ridged plates run down the back and shoulders.

Face. Bare plated horn: short, blunt, overlapping plates of dull horn, no fur on the face at all, no snout, no nose. The jaw splits down the middle of the chin into two halves that open sideways, lined with teeth. Four small eyes sit across the brow and shine in the dark. Heat sensing pits sit beside the eyes and flare when the beast is listening or has caught a trail. The face is a bare plated face, never a mask, never a helmet.

Posture is character, set per admiral by ruling: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies, and the Warlord always upright. The upright challenge posture is the Den alpha read, towering with the ridged back crest up; it is an allowed stance of the same base, not a second species. The Long Skulker read, lean and low with heat pits flared, is Sable's stance of the same base.

Hands and damage. Hands are heavy clawed working hands. Scars and losses are part of the admirals: Grax has one hand broken, Vesh has lost three fingers on one hand. Re-derive scars from this base each time; do not carry scars over from older, different bases.

Trophies. One hero trophy per portrait, and trophies are machines and hull plates only, never skulls, never bodies, never worn as necklaces or gear. A hero trophy is a single taken machine part or hull plate, large enough to stand over or beside.

Voice and manner. Short, flat, brutal. They hunt by ion trail and by listening, count what they take, and strip every wreck.

## Ships

Silhouette first: a Hullbreaker ship is a low, heavy, lopsided mass of matte soot black and rust plate, built slow and deliberate, never sleek, never fast looking. The family read at battle distance is bulk, asymmetry and red light: few windows, dim red running lights, red orange engine glow. The bow carries the hunting gear: big listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops, open and forward. Boarding claws fold along the forward hull. Chains run between hull sections. Armour is patched from plain torn scrap plate, bolted on rough. Under ruling R78 Q5, plates torn from other factions in their old colours appear as battle trophy plates on the flagship only; the rest of the fleet patches with unmarked scrap. Hull colour #6a2c22, a rust red brown, trim #b8402a, glow #e0402c, style junk. Hullbreakers are the ship rules exception: haphazard and junky is on brand, so parts need not sit in matched pairs, but guns still aim forward or out to the side, never backward, and the parts still mount on the real hull surface. Weapons are scrap guns, autocannon and junk turrets on the forward hull, rattle and heavy rocket pods in clusters, chaff guns at the flanks; no lances of any kind. Effects: dull red tracers, white red rocket exhaust, red orange engine bells at the stern. No naval tells: no keel, no funnel stacks, no porthole rows; engine bells, thruster pods, sensor dishes and docking hatches instead. Their own ships run silent and dark; the red lights stay dim.

## Palette

- Primary hull: rust red brown, #6a2c22. Matte, soot darkened, the body of every Hullbreaker ship.
- Secondary hull: soot black. Patch plate, shadowed hull sections, boarding gear.
- Trim: bright rust red, #b8402a. Edges, claw tips, dish rims, warning marks.
- Glow: hot red, #e0402c. Running lights (kept dim), engine glow reads red orange in flame, tracer fire dull red.
- Exhaust: white red rocket exhaust. The brightest note in the fleet, used sparingly.
- Mane colours (people): grey, black, rust brown. One mane colour per individual, over hump, neck and shoulders only.
- Hide and horn (people): tough dark hide; dull horn plates on the face and ridged back plates, grey brown and unpolished.
- Trophy colours: the old faction colours of taken plates, flagship only in battle, one hero trophy per portrait.
- Background: cold dark belt tones, near black blues and greys, so the red lights and eye shine carry.

## Do

- Build every Hullbreaker on the Knuckle Stalker base: low and wide, hump above the head, knuckles down at rest, mane on hump, neck and shoulders only.
