# The Glasswrights

## Species base / People

- **Origins and Lifespan:** The Glasswrights are the Starwrights who remained flesh, specifically the Lattice optical engineers who attempted to stop the Upload and failed (Source: The Glasswrights). They live 300 to 400 years, remain markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse, and crew the caged core A-4 Astra to hunt down and shut off the other five cores before shutting down Astra last (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **One Substance System and Core Anatomy:** Every Glasswright is built from a single integrated organic substance system rather than bolted-on parts (Source: Binding rulings, Owner verdict Nov 2). They are tall, slender, long-necked organic beings of pale, half-translucent flesh who evolved to shape glass with fine, glass-like grown threads and crystal clusters, not to build glass constructions. The substance itself is grown, organic, and woven in; its visible elements include subsurface fibre-optic crystal threads woven under the skin, Direction C Bloomed frost-flower crystal clusters at the joints, spine, and crown reserved for one elder admiral per faction, and small glowing light cores worn as jewellery rather than as a body power source (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling).

- **Spider Weaver Craft and Carriage:** Glasswrights are spider-weaver people in the same substance-system sense as the Pack, not the bear-like Hullbreakers, and their craft and carriage come from that substance system and from the weaver's stance: low, wide, grounded, with a segmented thorax and multiple arms working independently to weave, adjust, and shape. Their work is lens and mirror work, not construction work (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2).

- **Prism Sails Framing:** Prism sails are framed as grown crystal sail vanes, not naval sails, masts, rigging, or cloth, and are a personal and ship-level sail system: large jointed crystal sail vanes grow from the back and shoulders, remain close to the body when not in use, and open slowly into a precise prismatic flare. On the back, they should read as a pair of large jointed crystal sail vanes that can fold down into a fan behind the shoulders; on ships, they are big, light-catching fan-blades (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **Face, Eyes, and Voice:** Every Glasswright has exactly two forward-grown, deep green lens eyes (#9fffd2) set side by side flush in the flesh, with expressive eyelids and a heavy segmented brow (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling). Eyes must never be vertical stacks, rings of bead eyes, or extra brow lenses, and apertures must never be mechanical, because aperture geometry belongs to Eclipse. The mouth is a short reed grille set flush low in the face, and the voice is not speech by colour pulse (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2).

- **Age, Scars, and Character Variation:** All Glasswrights in this leg are aged 90 to 110 years, the founding admiralty excepted, and their faces and bodies are aged, experienced, and worn without becoming withered or frail: Saro, as the founding admiralty exception, is ancient, the oldest after the admiralty's founding, and carries deep, heavy wear. A Glasswright at 90 is still a young admiral, but an old person (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2).

- **Clothing and Harness:** Glasswrights wear layered clear crystal and pale silver fabric (#eef3f1) woven with fine subsurface fibre-optic crystal threads, with layered mirror-thread harness straps in warm mirror gold (#d7c88a) as a unifying system across all Glasswrights, and jewellery made of small glowing light cores (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2). They do not use brass (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling).

## Ships

- **Hull Language and Silhouette:** Glasswright hulls are grown crystal and glass, with the ship itself a lens and a mirror: large crystal sail-vanes and a mirrored lens array dominate the silhouette, while the overall profile remains slim, glassy, and crystalline, with sharp, translucent edges (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights). Ships are not constructed from glass panels, not separate glass constructs, and not naval hulls with masts, rigging, or sails (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **Space Architecture and Parts That Belong:** Glasswright space architecture must use parts that belong to the species: mirrored lens arrays, long focusing lenses, a Prism ward lens assembly, mirror-thin hull panels, a clear crystal workbench hall, an observatory with a huge clear crystal lens, and a ward generator chamber with silver-white shield flicker (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; R66; R68). Ships must not include engine rooms, boiler rooms, or generic industrial machinery, because those elements make the ship read as steampunk (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **Fleet Role, Roster, and Weapons:** Glasswrights are an artillery faction: long range, slow, fragile, and low in brawling (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights). Their roster, including Saro, Tamsa, and Brin, is artillery, and their flagship The Unfinished is the largest surviving Glasswright ship, described as a floating cathedral lens (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights). The ships move like light: slow, drifting, and turning deliberately (Source: The Glasswrights). Weapons are stored light, released as a slow, intense beam, and a beam that goes off only when it hits, a property that should appear in prompts as slow, intense, and precise, not as fast projectile fire (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **Palette in Words:** Glasswright hulls use clear crystal and pale silver (#eef3f1), with warm mirror-gold trim (#d7c88a), pale green-white stored light (#9fffd2), and an involuntary prismatic rainbow fringe from the glass threads and an intense white-core beam with a sharp rainbow spectrum (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

## Palette

- **Primary (#eef3f1, Clear Crystal and Pale Silver):** A clear, bright, slightly icy white-silver used for the main hull material, sails, and robes, and the main glass colour (Source: Binding rulings; R66; The Glasswrights). No blue tone is to be assigned to glass.

- **Trim (#d7c88a, Mirror Gold and Silver Trim):** A warm, clear, mirror gold used for harness trim, mirror bezels, and mirror-thread detailing, with silver as a secondary trim and a mirror-polished finish (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; R66; The Glasswrights). Brass is not to be used (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling).

- **Glow (#9fffd2, Pale Green-White Stored Light):** A pale, green-white glow used for stored light, lens eyes, glowing light cores, and the slow, intense beam (Source: Binding rulings; R66; The Glasswrights). The deep green of the lens eyes is the same colour family as the stored light, and lens eyes and light cores must read as the same colour family, with the colour pulse speech banned for Glasswrights (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling).

- **Light (White Core with Prismatic Rainbow Fringe and Silver-White Flicker):** The beam is an intense white core splitting into a sharp rainbow spectrum; prismatic rainbow fringe runs involuntarily along woven glass threads, and shield flicker is silver-white (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

- **Background (Varied Faction Environments per R68):** Each portrait and ship prompt in this leg uses a distinct background (Source: R68), for example a bright clear crystal workbench hall with a mirrored lens array, a dim ancient observatory with a huge clear crystal lens, a sunlit mirror-polishing workshop with a row of standing mirrors, a quiet ward generator chamber, and the floating cathedral lens interior of The Unfinished (Source: Binding rulings; The Glasswrights).

## Do

- Base every Glasswright on the single organic substance system: tall, slender, long-necked, pale, half-translucent flesh, with subsurface fibre-optic crystal threads woven under the skin, small glowing light cores as jewellery, and, for one elder admiral per faction, Direction C Bloomed frost-flower crystal clusters at the joints, spine, and crown.

- Give every Glasswright exactly two forward-grown deep green lens eyes (#9fffd2) set side by side flush in the flesh, with expressive eyelids and a heavy segmented brow, and a short reed grille mouth set flush low in the face.

- Frame prism sails as grown crystal sail vanes: large jointed crystal sail vanes growing from the back and shoulders, furled closely when not in use, and opening slowly into a precise prismatic flare.

- Use layered clear crystal and pale silver fabric (#eef3f1) with layered mirror-thread harness straps in warm mirror gold (#d7c88a) as a unifying system across all Glasswrights.

- Use parts that belong to the species: mirrored lens arrays, long focusing lenses, a Prism ward lens assembly, mirror-thin hull panels, a clear crystal workbench hall, an observatory with a huge clear crystal lens, and a ward generator chamber with silver-white shield flicker.

- Make weapons stored light, released as a slow, intense beam, and a beam that goes off only when it hits.

- Make ships move like light: slow, drifting, and turning deliberately.

## Do not

- Never show a Glasswright as faceless, with colour pulse speech, or with a mechanical aperture, because aperture geometry belongs to Eclipse.

- Never make a Glasswright literally out of glass, a separate glass construct, or a cut crystal construct.

- Never base a Glasswright on a human, grey alien, or elf, with large black eyes, grey skin, or elf ears.

- Never use vertical eye or lens stacks, rings of bead eyes, or extra brow lenses.

- Never use steampunk, brass, engine rooms, boiler rooms, or generic industrial machinery.

- Never show a Glasswright as a full spider body, with a spider face, or as an insectoid.

- Never use naval tells: masts, rigging, cloth sails, or a boat keel.

# Ashfall Hullbreakers

## Species base / People

- **Origins and Hunting Way:** The Hullbreakers are the Starwrights who did not Upload, specifically the Pack engineers who refused it, and they are hunters of the uploaded (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). They hunt the uploaded cores, including Glasswright-descended Eclipse, and their hunting way is to break hulls and take trophies from wrecks (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **One Species Base and Body Anatomy:** Every Hullbreaker is built from a single integrated organic substance system, not bolted-on parts, and every Hullbreaker is bear-like: huge, broad, shaggy, and powerful, with a thick, shaggy coat, heavy paws, and a massive, broad frame (Source: Binding rulings, Owner verdict Nov 2; The Hullbreakers). They are not a separate bear species, and they are not plain bears (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). Their substance system is the same integrated organic system as the Glasswrights, but expressed as a bear-like hunter, not a spider-weaver (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **Face and Jaw:** Every Hullbreaker has a broad, bear-like face with a heavy jaw, a short, powerful muzzle, and small, deep-set eyes, with a heavy brow and a wide, powerful jaw that can crush hull plating (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). Eyes are small and deep-set, not large, black, or alien, and the face must read as a bear-like hunter, not as a Predator or a plain bear (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **Anti-Predator and Anti-Bear Guardrails:** Hullbreakers must never be drawn as Predators, with dreadlocks, a mandible mouth, or a Predator-style face, and must never be drawn as plain bears, with a bear snout, bear ears, or a bear body without a hunter's harness, tools, and trophies (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). They must always carry a hunter's harness, tools, and trophies, and their bear-like anatomy must be framed as a hunter's anatomy, not as a wild animal's (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **Posture, Age, and Variation:** Hullbreakers vary in posture, age, and character, but all remain huge, broad, and shaggy: Grax is a massive, broad warlord in a dominant, upright stance; Sable is a lean, fast scout in a low, crouched stance; Old Mott is an ancient, scarred veteran in a slow, heavy stance; and Vesh is a young, eager raider in a forward-leaning, aggressive stance (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). All Hullbreakers in this leg are aged 90 to 110 years, the founding warlord excepted, and their faces and bodies are aged, experienced, and worn without becoming withered or frail (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2).

- **Harness and Trophy Rule:** Hullbreakers wear a heavy, practical hunter's harness in dark, worn leather and iron, with tools, straps, and pouches, and carry exactly one hero trophy per portrait, chosen from a wreck: a single hull plate, a single lens, a single engine part, or a single weapon part (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers). They do not wear layered crystal or pale silver fabric, and they do not use mirror-gold trim (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

## Do

- Base every Hullbreaker on the single integrated organic substance system, not bolted-on parts, and make every Hullbreaker bear-like: huge, broad, shaggy, and powerful, with a thick, shaggy coat, heavy paws, and a massive, broad frame.

- Give every Hullbreaker a broad, bear-like face with a heavy jaw, a short, powerful muzzle, and small, deep-set eyes, with a heavy brow and a wide, powerful jaw that can crush hull plating.

- Vary posture, age, and character, but keep all Hullbreakers huge, broad, and shaggy: Grax in a dominant, upright stance; Sable in a low, crouched stance; Old Mott in a slow, heavy stance; and Vesh in a forward-leaning, aggressive stance.

- Include exactly one hero trophy per portrait, chosen from a wreck: a single hull plate, a single lens, a single engine part, or a single weapon part, and wear a heavy, practical hunter's harness in dark, worn leather and iron, with tools, straps, and pouches.

- Design all Hullbreaker ships as heavy, armoured, and brutal, built from salvaged hull plating, iron, and dark, worn metal, with a massive, broad, and brutal silhouette, thick armour, and a heavy, forward-leaning bow, and make them move like hunters: fast, aggressive, and forward-leaning.

## Do not

- Never draw a Hullbreaker as a Predator, with dreadlocks, a mandible mouth, or a Predator-style face.

- Never draw a Hullbreaker as a plain bear, with a bear snout, bear ears, or a bear body without a hunter's harness, tools, and trophies.

- Never use skulls, bones, or totems as Hullbreaker decoration, because those elements are not in the pack.

- Never give a non-flagship ship trophy plates from multiple factions; only the Warlord's Pyre Barge may carry trophies from multiple factions.

- Never make a Hullbreaker ship fast, sleek, or lance-like, or use long-range beams or fast projectile fire.

- Never use naval tells: masts, rigging, cloth sails, or a boat keel.

## Flags

- **Fixed Nemesis pairing:** Nemesis is the Hullbreaker traitor and fifth core, shown half in light and half in Eclipse darkness, with small, deep-set eyes on the light side and a single black Eclipse aperture on the dark side, and must never be shown as fully Eclipse or fully Hullbreaker (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **Flagship-only trophy plates:** Only the Warlord's Pyre Barge may carry trophy plates from multiple factions, because it is the warlord's ship; all other Hullbreaker ships carry trophies from a single faction only, or no trophies at all (Source: Binding rulings; The Hullbreakers).

- **Age, face, and body variation:** All Hullbreakers in this leg are aged 90 to 110 years, the founding warlord excepted, and their faces and bodies are aged, experienced, and worn without becoming withered or frail, with variation in posture, age, and character across Grax, Sable, Old Mott, and Vesh (Source: Binding rulings, Nov 1 portrait ruling; Owner verdict Nov 2).
