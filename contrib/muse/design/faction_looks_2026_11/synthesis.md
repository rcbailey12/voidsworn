# The Glasswrights

## Species base and people

One substance system governs the whole being: flesh grown glass. A Glasswright is a tall, slender, long necked person of pale, half translucent flesh, and the glass in the body is the same flesh in a grown, hardened form, never a separate material bolted on. Fine crystal threads run through the flesh like fibre optics under skin. The body reads as a person first, a glasswright second. They are organic beings who evolved to shape glass. They are not made of glass, not crystal constructs, and nothing like grey aliens. This is the Threaded read, and it is the base for every Glasswright.

Bloom is age growth of the same glass: crystal clusters growing outward at the joints, spine, and crown like frost flowers. It is allowed for ONE admiral: Saro. The Inlaid direction is dropped: large crystal plates set into the body read as a second, placed material, and the one substance rule forbids it.

Face. Two forward grown lens eyes set side by side, deep green, sitting flush in the flesh, modest in size. Lids and a heavy segmented brow carry all expression. A short reed grille sits flush low in the face as the speaking mouth; the voice that comes through it is a normal speaking voice. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell only: stored light runs along the glass threading, splits into prismatic colour at its edges, and catches brighter when the Glasswright feels strongly. It is never speech and never a deliberate display.

Age spectrum, young to old, is the faction's main variation axis. Glasswrights live 300 to 400 years. The young are rooted and near Starwright in the face: smooth pale flesh, lens eyes freshly grown and clean edged, glass threading fine and mostly under the skin. With age the lens eyes infuse deeper, the grown glass thickens around them, the brow segments grow heavier, and the sail plan grows larger and more worn. The old look deep infused, not different in kind. They are markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse.

Spider weaver carriage. Four arms: two full working arms and two smaller precision arms below them, all with long tapering fingers. Wrist spinnerets on the working arms draw and weave glass thread directly onto the workpiece. The carriage holds a little of the spider: a segmented thorax section, a low, wide weaver stance at the bench, and support limbs that brace the body while the hands work. Keep it a read of posture and proportion. A full spider body is NOT the species base; the one exception is the Lens keeper portrait prompt, written on the chair pick for owner question Q1.

Prism sails. Each Glasswright carries grown crystal sail vanes behind the shoulders, echoing the clear crystal and silver vanes of their ships. The sails are part of the body: jointed on tendon and cartilage, grown, no hardware, no brackets, no fittings. They are modest and practical in size. They furl flat at the bench and open at work, and one edge of an opened sail focuses light down onto the workpiece. Age and admiral variety live in the sail plan: elders carry larger sails with bloom edged, worn tips; the young carry smaller, cleaner vanes.

Clothing. Layered fabrics of crystal thread and mirror thread, woven by the wearer's own spinnerets, worn over the torso and lower body, leaving the arms, sails, and glass growths clear. Small light cores are worn as jewellery, grown into a collar or a wrist band, never carried as separate gadgets.

Voice and manner. Quiet, precise, mournful. Apologies meant.

The admirals:

- Tamsa of the Clear Aperture: youngest at ninety, wing admiral, Starling flight, raised in the Ringing Field salvage crews. Young face, lens eyes newly grown, smaller clean sails.
- Saro of Long Focus: oldest after the Lens keeper, lance admiral, Burning glass, sixty years on the Astra watch, speaks least. Deep lens infusion, large worn sails, and the faction's one Bloom admiral: frost flower clusters at joints, spine, and crown.
- Vell of the Third Mirror: duellist and mirror smith, Scatter the light, polishes the flagship great mirror by hand. Mid age, focused, sails angled for precision light work.
- Brin of the Fourth Lens: ward admiral, Prism ward, shield engineer, apologises to enemy fleets in writing before battles. Mid age, gentle, sails modest and functional.

The Glasswrights as Nemesis: the Lens keeper duellist, the oldest of the faction, who keeps range and returns with the same four elite ships upgraded, never enlarged. Per the Nemesis redesign, hunter assignments are symmetric and variable by story point; the Lens keeper is the carrier of the Glasswright behaviour package wherever it appears.

## Ships

Silhouette first: every Glasswright ship is a slender, faceted crystal form built around a clear crystal spine, with silver vane fins toward the rear and a lens or mirror array toward the bow. The family shape holds at every size, so the faction is identifiable from outline alone at battle distance: long, fine, faceted, bright. Hulls are clear crystal over a silver frame, hull colour #eef3f1, with faceted edges that split light into rainbow fringes. Deep inside each hull, stored light glows pale green white. Mirrors and lenses mounted on the hull surface turn to catch light. Crystal vanes spread from the rear hull like the people's sails, grown looking and jointed, never rigged, never on a mast.

Full space architecture, per the ship rules: armoured flat bellies with chines, sensor housings and antenna arrays, equipment pods, recessed lights, main engine bells and thruster pods, docking hatches, heat radiators. Parts sit snapped onto the real hull surface on visible mounts. Guns sit in matched symmetric pairs at sensible hard points, aimed forward or out to the side, never backward: prism lance housings along the spine, shard racks at the flanks, a needle beam emitter at the bow. Shields flicker silver white in the faction glow colour. Trim is pale gold #d7c88a, used sparingly on frame lines and vane roots.

The shipped style word sail means grown crystal vanes only, on people and on ships. Never rigged sails, never a mast.

The fleet: Glasswright sliver (Frigate), Glasswright lancet (Destroyer), Glasswright prism (Cruiser), Glasswright aperture (Carrier), The Unfinished (Ark, flagship, built around the largest surviving piece of the Lattice). Weapons: Prism lance, Prism shards, Needle beam, Star torpedo, Mirror veil. Effects: white core Prism lance beams with rainbow fringe, pale green white stored light, silver white shield flicker. In play this is the fastest fleet, with deep shields, fragile hulls, and range control: fine boned, bright, fragile, precise.

## Palette

- Primary hull: clear crystal white, #eef3f1. The body of every ship, and the base tone of skin and sail glass.
- Trim: pale gold, #d7c88a. Frame lines, vane root edging, jewellery settings, mirror thread in clothing.
- Glow: mint stored light, #9fffd2. Stored light inside hulls and body glass, shield flicker, weapon charge, light core jewellery.
- Silver: frames, mirror surfaces, engine bells. Part of the hull family, word only.
- Light: pale green white stored light. Rainbow fringe at faceted edges is an effect, not a base colour.
- Eyes: deep green, darker and more saturated than the glow.
- Background: dark workshop and void tones, deep dark blue green and near black, so the pale bodies and stored light read first.

## Do

- Build every Glasswright from the one substance system: living flesh that grows its own glass, fine crystal threads woven through pale half translucent flesh, the body reading as a person first.
- Keep the face constant on every figure: two forward grown lens eyes side by side, deep green, flush; lids and a heavy segmented brow carrying expression; a short reed grille set flush low as the mouth; a normal speaking voice.
- Treat colour in the glass as an involuntary emotion tell only.
- Give every Glasswright four arms, two working and two precision, with wrist spinnerets drawing and weaving glass thread onto the workpiece, and long tapering fingers.
- Keep the spider weaver carriage as posture and proportion: segmented thorax, low wide weaver stance, support limbs.
- Use prism sails as grown crystal sail vanes: modest, practical, jointed with tendon and cartilage, no hardware, furled at the bench, one edge focusing light onto the work.
- Put age and admiral variety in the sail plan and the lens infusion: young near Starwright, old deep infused. Bloom frost flower clusters for Saro only.
- Use layered crystal and mirror thread fabrics, and small light cores worn as jewellery.
- Give every portrait its own setting, pose, framing, and lighting within the faction palette (R68). Oil paint style throughout.
- Build the ships clear crystal and silver: faceted edges with rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, stored light glowing deep inside, and a slender faceted family silhouette identifiable from outline alone at battle distance.
- Use full space architecture with parts on visible mounts: armoured flat bellies with chines, sensor housings, antenna arrays, equipment pods, recessed lights, main engine bells, thruster pods, docking hatches, heat radiators.
- Place guns in matched symmetric pairs, aimed forward or out to the side, and keep shields in faction colours.
- Use the shipped palette exactly: hull #eef3f1, trim #d7c88a, glow #9fffd2.

## Do not

- Do not make them faceless, and do not use colour pulse speech. Colour in the glass is an emotion tell only.
- Do not make them literally glass: no crystal construct or robot bodies. That is Eclipse ground.
- Do not assemble the being from separate substances: no cut crystal block head over a veined flesh body over webbed membrane sails.
- Do not use human, grey alien, or elf bases however dressed up. No large black eyes, no grey skin.
- Do not use vertical eye or lens stacks, rings of identical bead eyes, secondary eye arcs, or extra brow magnifier lenses.
- Do not use steampunk or hardware: no camera aperture rims, brass fittings, brackets, or jewellers loupes. The reed grille must sit flush, never a tube held in the mouth.
- Do not use the Inlaid direction: no crystal plates set into the body as a second, placed material.
- Do not give the species base a full spider body. The spider stays a carriage read, with the single Lens keeper prompt exception noted at Q1.
- Do not put literal naval sails on a mast on the people or the ships. The style word sail means grown crystal vanes only.
- Do not use naval ship tells: no boat keels, raked stems, jackstaffs, bollards, anchors, crows nests, yardarms with rigging, mast ladders, lifeboats, porthole rows along a waterline, crossbar masts, or smokestack funnels.
- Do not aim any gun backward.

# Ashfall Hullbreakers

## Species base and people

One species base: the Knuckle Stalker. A huge hunting beast from a cold, heavy gravity belt, wolf and bear inspired in mass and movement but never a bear with gear. The body is low and wide: a big shoulder hump rises above the head, the forearms are thick and heavy, the hands carry heavy claws, and at rest the beast walks knuckles down. A coarse mane covers the hump, neck, and shoulders only, in grey, black, or rust brown. Everywhere else is tough dark hide. Ridged plates run down the back and shoulders.

Face, per the R64 void hunter ruling: bare plated horn. Short, blunt, overlapping plates of dull horn, no fur on the face at all, no snout, no nose. The jaw splits down the middle of the chin into two halves that open sideways, lined with teeth. Four small eyes sit across the brow and shine in the dark. Heat sensing pits sit beside the eyes and flare when the beast is listening or has caught a trail. The face is a bare plated face, never a mask, never a helmet.

Posture is character, set per admiral by ruling: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies, and the Warlord always upright. Den alpha Upright is a posture of this same base for Warlord portraits, towering with the ridged back plates raised; it is not a second species. Long Skulker is not a base either: Sable's lean, low, listening read with heat pits flared is her posture of the same base.

Hands and damage. Hands are heavy clawed working hands. Scars and losses belong to the admirals and are re-derived from this base each time, never carried over from older, different bases: Grax has one hand broken, Vesh has lost three fingers on one hand.

Trophies. One hero trophy per portrait, and trophies are machines and hull plates only, never skulls, never bodies, never worn as necklaces or gear. A hero trophy is a single taken machine part or hull plate, large enough to stand over or beside.

Voice and manner. Short, flat, brutal. They hunt in space by ion trail and by listening, count what they take, and strip every wreck. They came for the biggest fight in known space, and they hunt R-5 Rook.

The admirals:

- Warlord Grax Ironmaw: Warlord, Full burn, upright in challenge, took the Pyre Barge by challenge, one hand broken in a spotlight beat.
- Sable the Smiling Knife: lone hunter, Knife in the dark, low and skulking, split jaw hangs open when she has found a trail, hunts alone ahead of the pack.
- Old Mott: swarm keeper, Release the swarm, low and wide, oldest alive, outlived forty captains, keeps the hulk carrier and the young.
- Vesh the Collector: collector, Patch and weld, upright over trophies, fights with a quartermasters eye, lost three fingers on one hand in a spotlight beat.

The Hullbreakers as Nemesis: the raider Warlord. Always raids, against any player faction: debrief credit loss plus one raider line, universal. Per the Nemesis redesign, hunter assignments are symmetric and variable by story point; the raider Warlord is the carrier of the Hullbreaker behaviour package wherever it appears.

## Ships

Silhouette first: a Hullbreaker ship is a low, heavy, lopsided mass of matte soot black and rust plate, built slow and deliberate, never sleek, never fast looking. Movement is slow and deliberate, ships moving one at a time in pack order. The family read at battle distance is bulk, asymmetry, and red light: few windows, dim red running lights, red orange engine glow. The bow carries the hunting gear: big listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops, open and forward. Boarding claws fold along the forward hull. Chains run between hull sections. Armour is patched from plain torn scrap plate, bolted on rough. Under R78 Q5, plates torn from other factions in their old colours appear as battle trophy plates on the flagship only; the rest of the fleet patches with unmarked scrap.

Hullbreakers are the ship rules exception: haphazard and junky is on brand, so the matched pairs rule is relaxed for them. Parts still sit on visible mounts on the real hull surface, and guns still aim forward or out to the side, never backward. Weapons are scrap guns, autocannon and junk turrets on the forward hull, rattle and heavy rocket pods in clusters, chaff guns at the flanks; far too many rockets; no lances of any kind. Space architecture underneath the junk: armoured flat bellies with chines, sensor housings, antenna arrays, equipment pods, recessed lights, main engine bells and thruster pods, docking hatches, heat radiators. Effects: dull red tracers, white red rocket exhaust, red orange engine bells at the stern. Their own ships run silent and dark; the red lights stay dim.

The fleet: Hullbreaker raider (Frigate), Hullbreaker gutship (Destroyer), Hullbreaker wrecker (Cruiser), Hullbreaker hulk carrier (Carrier), Warlords Pyre Barge (Battle barge, flagship; the name Pyre Barge is kept). Weapons: Autocannon, Scrap scatter gun, Junk turret, Rattle rocket pod, Heavy rocket pod, Chaff guns. In play: a close range fleet with thin shields and scrap guns with wild extra shots. The Pyre Barge's look inside this ship set (trophy plating, chains, dishes) is direction only; its in game graphical upgrade and sign off stay a separate owner call, per Q4.
