# Hegemony Navy class family: chair designs (issue 1146)

Run 3 (Nov 22, 2026) revises the three escorts (Picket, Cordon,
Vanguard) with new outlines. The Convoy and Relay sections are run 1's,
unchanged; both ships are accepted and built. Chair: Rey, from a blind
Rey leg written first, then DeepSeek, Qwen and Gemini legs (the Gemini
seat ran on Hydrogen, the disclosed picker deviation of record). Qwen
answered in its comparison mode with two full variants; both were
recorded and chaired as one leg. Grok's seat is empty: the cloud
browser hit the bot wall before sign-in, as on Nov 17 to 21. Fit order
throughout: data.js first, then the faction sheet, then the anchor
builds, then the pictures. Where a picture and a table disagree, the
table governs. Picture slips are listed per ship.

## What run 3 changed, and why

Ryan's ruling on the run 1 escorts (Nov 20, 2026): the three read as
one wedge at three sizes, and the builds wait on a redraw he can pick
from. Run 3 therefore amends the family law below: sameness now binds
the surface and the parts, NOT the outline. Each escort class gets one
hull-level silhouette event of its own:

- Picket: no event. A single solid convex hull, the only escort with
  no notch, no slot and no concave corner anywhere.
- Cordon: one hard transverse step at 35 percent, co-registered in
  plan and profile at the same station.
- Vanguard: one piece of negative space, the open well between its
  rails, 15 to 58 percent.

Chair fail-safes (from the Qwen leg, adopted): the Cordon's shoulder
steps in at least 20 percent of beam per side and its aft body keeps
at least 50 percent of the bow beam; the Vanguard's slot is at least
35 percent of max beam wide and 40 percent of length long (as tabled:
2.2 wide against a 4.8 beam, 43 percent of length); no ship relies on
its armament for identification, because recesses and fittings do not
survive a solid black fill.

Two leg proposals were considered and not taken. Qwen's second
variant would move the Vanguard's ion trench to the root of a
forward-open slot at 44 percent; the faction sheet fixes the ion
weapon as a recessed trench in a blunt bow face, both anchors carry
it there, and the chaired slot starts aft of the bow block, so the
claimed contradiction does not arise. Gemini would draw the Cordon's
trench as a notch cutting into the bow in plan; a plan notch forks
the bow, and the faction sheet bans prongs. The trench stays a face
recess on both ships.

## The family, from the anchors

Anchors: H10 Dominion (battleship, 21.0, data key h_dominion) and IM5
Iron Mandate (super-dreadnought, 31.0), both built. The family look is
unchanged: pale machined grey plate, flush panels and straight seams,
steel-blue trim, one implant-blue flank light line, about 3:1. What
changed in run 3 is only that the repeated parts now sit on three
different outlines.

Never change across the family:
1. The blunt bow face: flat deck, vertical flank band carrying the
   implant-blue light line, flat worked belly. Never a pointed prow,
   never prongs.
2. The ion weapon, where data fits one, is a recessed emitter trench
   in the blunt bow face: blue lens, stepped capacitor rings, inset
   muzzle lip. Capacitor ring count scales with hull size: Cordon 3,
   Vanguard 4, Dominion 6.
3. Guns sit low and sunk: armoured mounts in shoulder or centreline
   terraces, roofs barely over deck, short thick barrels. One low
   sensor fin on the centreline, never a tower. Engines are a flat
   row of rectangular blue-white nozzle pods at the stern. Breaks
   fall at the bulkhead rings between modules; breach anchors sit on
   the module joints.

Never copy from the anchors:
1. Dominion's exact terrace station band (38 to 46 percent) and its
   shoulder-bay beam as a default for smaller hulls.
2. The six-ring trench and the four-nozzle row as fleet defaults;
   smaller hulls step both down (see the ring ladder above; Picket
   and Cordon carry two nozzle pods).
3. Dominion's solid midsection. The Vanguard's open well is the
   deliberate opposite, and it is what keeps the cruiser from reading
   as a scaled Dominion.

Weapon conventions (from H10's weapon match):
- A turret slot is ONE low terrace mount. Where a hull carries two
  turret slots, the mounts mirror port and starboard at the same
  station (the Vanguard, as Dominion's two heavy slots sit one a
  side). Where a hull carries one turret slot, the mount sits on the
  centreline (the Cordon).
- A forward gun that is not the ion cannon is a low casemate in the
  bow face, barrels visible, no rings (the Picket).
- The flak slot on the smallest hull is one low dorsal barbette with
  three short emitters, one per shot (the Picket).
- Shots are volleys distributed over the mount's barrels and
  emitters.

gone; this is the only escort with no rails at all, and the only one
widest aft of middle.

## Silhouette checks

silhouette_check_top.webp and silhouette_check_side.webp draw the
three run 3 escorts as solid black shapes at the same length beside
the run 1 trio drawn the same way, and silhouette_check_scale.webp
draws the whole family in plan at true relative scale with H10, O2
and O3. All three are drawn from these tables, not traced from the
generated pictures; the check tests the tabled outlines, which are
what the builders build from.

Verdict: no run 3 pair can be confused. In plan the ladder is solid
convex (Picket), solid stepped (Cordon), holed (Vanguard). In
profile it is one band with a chamfered tail (Picket), a cliff at 35
percent (Cordon), one flat rail line (Vanguard). The run 1 rows show
the failure this run fixes: three wedges, separable only by size.
family_lineup.webp is the generated beauty line-up; its drawn scale
is approximate, and silhouette_check_scale.webp carries the true
relative lengths (21.0 / 14.5 / 11.0 / 9.5 / 7.5 / 6.5).
