# #429 blind leg: Rey (chair), written before any picture and before the other legs

Per faction: the variation most likely to break the gate, the line it breaks, and the guard I will use.

## Sunward Crusade (S1 Oath Blade, S2 Blade and Hilt, S3 Forge Blade)

Most at risk: S1. Round 1's Oath Blade failed exactly here: the model scatters barrels along every edge because "gun decks" invites rows of guns. Gate line 1 (weapons counted) and line 10 (one scale). Guard: the prompt names the broadside galleries as recessed port galleries INSIDE the flanks, flush, with no barrels anywhere, and states the complete weapon list as a closed set: one lance at the bow, two flank galleries, one flak cluster, nothing else that reads as a gun. The radiator bank is one graded bank on the rear third, named as fins in a row like organ pipes, not spires scattered along the spine.

## Hegemony Navy (H1 Kit Wedge, H2 Monolith, H3 Twin Rail)

Most at risk: H3. Two hulls invite the model to draw a catamaran boat or two submarines, and the drone grid in the gap invites aircraft. Gate lines 11 (space, not sea, not air) and 3 (one thrust axis: two engine rows must still read as one axis). Guard: name the cross-bridges as flat armoured bridges, the rails as flat slab hulls with the shared flat rectangular engine row across the whole stern, and the drones as faceted blocks in a geometric grid, the hegemony_C lesson from round 1 (specify drone SHAPE, not just formation). H2's risk is the opposite: a bare slab reads as a building or a tombstone with nothing on it; the guard is to name every fitting's place (prongs, two turrets, hangar slot, blue line) even on the monolith.

## Ashfall Hullbreakers (A1 Pyre-Barge and Pack Hunter escort, A2 Jaw Brick, A3 Trophy Hulk)

Most at risk: A2. Mandibles that open sideways drew a literal animal head in round 1 (corsair_B try 1). Gate line 11 and the faction Never list. Guard: the jaw blocks are named as flat armoured grapple blocks, hinged plates with hydraulic rams, no teeth, no eyes, no head shape; the torpedo cluster sits in the throat between them as visible tubes. A1's quieter risk: the escort must be a different animal (lean Pack Hunter frigate), not the barge shrunk; the beauty prompt describes the escort's own grammar separately. A3's risk: the welded-in Hegemony module and Crusade blade section read as three ships collided, not one hulk; guard: one blunt silhouette first, the trophies as sections of PLATING and one module embedded in the mass, chained.

## The Glasswrights (G1 Spinal Prism, G2 Twin Prism, G3 Cut Hull)

Most at risk: G1, because round 1's Prism was a crystal with rails, an object, not a ship: gate line 4 (one spine) and the grammar's ratio (crystal a third of what you see, armour two thirds). Guard: the prompt builds the silver arrowhead WARSHIP first (spine, command block, engines, lens turrets), then lays the six-sided crystal along its back in silver ribs; the word order matters, hull nouns before crystal nouns. G2's risk: outrigger prisms read as wings or pontoons; guard: prisms slung tight against the hull sides as armoured outrigger bars, light arcing to the bow focus lens. G3 is the safest and the control case.

## Eclipse (E1 Ring and Fan, E2 Crescent, E3 Double Arc)

Most at risk: E1. Round 1's Annulus parked a stock warship behind a ring (gate line 4, one spine; and the ring read as a portal). Guard: the ring is a broken ring of SHADE PLATES on a hub the bow passes through, the body fan-plated and joined, core burning at the hub; the ring faces forward like a shield boss, never flat, never a smooth portal ring. E2's risk: the crescent reads as a logo or a sickle; guard: the body joined to the inner curve's middle by the spine, turrets at the horns, so it reads as a shield with a ship behind it. All three: the green stays a thin rim line and the core, named in every prompt, because pooled green is the model's favourite failure for this faction.

## The Chorus (C1 Bloom Mouth, C2 Crowned Leviathan, C3 Nine Throats)

Most at risk: C3. Nine openings at a bow invite a face, and a face is the faction's hardest Never (gate line 12). Guard: the openings are named as throats and feeding mouths of uneven sizes in a cluster, explicitly asymmetrical in detail, no two alike, no eye-like pair, no arrangement that reads as features; the two lit weapon throats sit low and wide apart. C1's risk: petal-jaws read as a flower; guard: thick blunt petal-JAWS of hide and chitin, folded shut like a fist, not petals opening to the sun. All Chorus prompts carry: no segments, no legs, no eyes, no metal, olive and ochre only (round 1's gold palette failure), and the stern is vents and trailing membranes, never fins (round 1's fish escort failure).

## Cross-cutting chair rules for the whole round

1. Sheet first, beauty second, beauty generated image-to-image from the accepted sheet; the pipeline supports reference images (proven in the #398 trial), so both pictures show the same ship by construction, and README.md will say so.
2. Every prompt states the closed weapon list with places, the proportions as numbers, and the one accent light. Counts failed in every #398 trial even when stated twice, so the sheet gets judged against the mount table before the beauty view is even attempted; a sheet with extra guns is redrawn, never carried forward.
3. Design sheets are flat views on dark grey; perspective creep is the sheet failure to watch (the #398 side views drew casemates frontally). Prompts say orthographic, flat on, no perspective, top view above, side view below, same scale.
