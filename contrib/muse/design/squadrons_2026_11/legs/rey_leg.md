# Rey leg: squadron design spec (issue 70)

Written blind from the evidence pack and the leg prompt only.

## 1. Comparator breakdown

Homeworld 2. Strike craft fly as squadrons of individual ships: fighters in flights, bombers in their own squadrons, each craft a real unit that launches from a carrier or production ship, transits in formation, makes firing passes at its target, and returns to dock to rearm, refuel and repair (game manual and the game's own squadron and docking behaviour; the scar and hardpoint detail is covered in Simon Schreibt's Homeworld 2 rendering write-ups). Losses are per craft: a squadron visibly thins as it fights, and a mauled squadron that docks comes back whole. At tactical distance the read is the formation's shape and its engine trails moving against the capital ships; individual craft detail is a zoom reward. Lesson: the wing is a count, not a token, and the return trip is half the life of the system.

Homeworld 3. Squadrons persist from the carrier, fly attack runs in formation, and the surface damage system means the capital ships they attack carry the visible record of the fight [IMPRESSION for the strike craft specifics; the Splat Damage system itself is from Blackbird's Unreal Fest 2023 material]. Craft read at distance as moving formation lights with trails; the mothership and carriers anchor where wings come from and go back to. Lesson: wings need a home they visibly use, or they read as free-floating tokens.

Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 1 and 2. Squadrons are ordnance: waves of fighters, bombers and assault boats launched from carriers, which cross the board, make their attack run against a hull (bombers and boats) or intercept enemy ordnance and craft (fighters), and are then spent or return depending on type; turrets and point defence are the counter, and a squadron's strength is worn down by defensive fire on the way in (Tindalos's game design as documented in the games' own ordnance rules and coverage of the launch economy [IMPRESSION on exact per-type return behaviour]). At tactical distance a wave reads as a swarm icon mass with a clear heading: you read where it is going, not what each craft is. Lesson: an attack run has a shape: approach, a committed pass through the point defence, effect, and an exit. Commitment is what makes it read as an attack rather than travel.

Sins of a Solar Empire 2. Strike craft are hosted per carrier and capital ship, built and replaced over time at a cost in antimatter-like resources, and fly as individual craft that orbit their host, strike out at targets in range, and return; hangars and flak are the counters (Ironclad's developer journals on strike craft and the game's squadron mechanics [IMPRESSION on the resource detail]). At fleet distance, strike craft read as orbiting motes and streams between fleets; nobody reads a silhouette. Lesson: on-station behaviour (orbiting the host) is what keeps craft alive on screen between orders, and it is cheap: a holding pattern, not a simulation.

Transferable lessons. (1) A wing is a visible count of craft that can thin (Homeworld 2). (2) Craft need a home they launch from and return toward, even if the return is only visual (Homeworld 3, Sins 2). (3) The attack run is a committed pass with an entry, a firing window and an exit, flown through the flak (BFG). (4) Between orders, craft hold a moving pattern around their station, never a parked formation (Sins 2). (5) At board distance the read is formation shape, motion and trails; silhouette is for zoom (all four).

## 2. Per-faction craft spec

Sizes: I shrink the craft. Today's 3.84 unit craft (30 px at 1080p, 59 px at 4K) is over a third of a frigate and reads as a small ship, not a strike craft; that is part of why wings read as static tokens, because at that size the eye expects ship behaviour. Spec: fighter-class craft 2.6 units long (20 px at 1080p, 40 px at 4K), bomber-class 3.0 units (23 px, 46 px), Chorus spores 2.8 units. The formation, at the spacing in section 3, spans about 14 units, which keeps the wing the readable unit at board distance while each craft gains a believable size against the hulls (a fighter at a quarter of a frigate's length, not a third).

Sunward Crusade: Hearth-darts. Black iron dart craft with a frost-white chalk stripe and an ember-orange drive, a small radiator fin echoing the capital ships' spires. Two kinds: dart (fighter role, guns) and brand-bomber (bomber role, heavier nose, twin ember drives). Engine: ember-orange, dim at rest per R55, flaring on the attack run.

Hegemony Navy: Screen drones. The bible calls them drone escorts, so Hegemony craft are uncrewed: identical pale grey wedges, steel-blue trim, implant-blue light at the nose, flying exact geometry. Bomber role is the same wedge with an ion pod slung under. Engine: blue-white, and the whole wing's lights pulse in time, the fleet sync made visible at small scale.

Ashfall Hullbreakers: Pack-runners. Bolted, asymmetrical strike craft that look welded from salvage, rust-red and scrap tones, one big overbored engine that burns dirty orange-red. There is no bomber variant silhouette: Hullbreaker bombers are runners with rocket racks bolted on, which keeps the pack one animal with two loads. They fly loose and ragged (section 3).

The Chorus: Spore motes and gnats. Grown creatures, never craft: a spore mote is a chitin-shelled sac with fin clusters and a bile yellow-green glow in its seams (fighter role, sporeb weapons read as stinger spines); the bomber role is a bloated sac-gnat that carries visible spore bladders and bursts them over the target. Dark olive-brown flesh, ochre chitin, per the bible. They do not have engines: propulsion sacs pulse, and the glow brightens as the sac contracts. They are budded from the brood-sac's pores at launch, visibly swelling out rather than streaking.

The Glasswrights: Starlings. Small crystal shards with two vanes, clear glass over silver, stored light glowing inside the body in pale green-white. Fighter and bomber are the same shard at two sizes, the bomber carrying a brighter core (its star torpedo). No engine flame: they move on light pressure, shown as a faint prismatic shimmer trail. Their flight is the most precise after Hegemony's, but curved where Hegemony is straight.

Eclipse (dependent on a future carrier or drone bay decision; no Eclipse ship launches wings today): Warden motes. Machine drones: dark violet-black platelets around a white-gold point of trapped light, no cockpit, no exhaust, moving by fold skips in short blinks at the end of each transit leg. If Eclipse ever fields them, they should be repair-and-escort drones first in fiction, strike craft second, matching the bible's tiny repair drones.

## 3. Formation and movement behaviour

Craft per wing on screen: six, in two elements of three, for fighter-role wings; five for bomber-role wings (larger craft); Chorus swarms show eight smaller motes. Visible count tracks wing hp in steps, because the rules treat the wing as one pool: above two thirds hp, full count; between one third and two thirds, two craft missing; below one third, half the craft missing. The missing craft are simply not drawn (they were lost on earlier runs; no mid-run explosion bookkeeping). This gives the board a damage read that costs nothing and answers the owner's static complaint every time a wing comes back thin. The R80 smoke ribbon below half HP stacks on top of this.

Formation geometry: a shallow wedge, lead element forward, second element offset back and wide. Spacing 2.2 units between craft in an element, elements 3.5 units apart, total footprint about 14 units wide and 11 long. Hegemony flies it at half spacing variance (near perfect), Crusade standard, Glasswrights in a curved arc instead of a wedge, Hullbreakers at double variance with constant small position noise, Chorus in a loose ball that slowly churns (craft exchanging places), Eclipse (if built) in a flat ring.

Movement model, one system with per-faction parameters:
- Launch: craft leave the carrier over 1.2 seconds in element order, accelerating along a curving path to the formation's station point beside the carrier, drives flaring then settling to the R55 dim glow. Chorus launch is a budding: motes swell from the brood-sac over 1.6 seconds. No sprite streaks; the meshes fly this.
- Transit: when the wing changes hexes, the formation flies the same spline the ships use, at the same duration, but each craft flies its own offset copy of the spline with a lag of 0.06 seconds per position in the formation and a bank into the turn proportional to the spline's curvature. Craft arrive staggered over about half a second, not as one block.
- On station: the formation holds a slow patrol: a flat oval circuit around its hex centre, 8 units by 5, one lap in about 9 seconds, craft keeping their wedge on the move. This is the single biggest anti-static change: a wing is never parked. Screen-stance wings move their circuit to between their carrier and the nearest threat and tighten it; hunt-stance wings drift theirs toward the hunted ship.
- Attack run: the meshes fly it. From station, the wing accelerates to the target over about 1.4 seconds on a converging curve, elements splitting to pass on two sides of the target hex, firing window in the middle 0.5 seconds (tracers, bombs or spore sacs from the actual craft positions, keeping the existing per-weapon sprite payloads), overshoot of about 12 units past the target, then a wide turn and return to station over about 1.6 seconds. Total run about 3.5 seconds. Flak fire during the run is already the rules' gauntlet; visually, point defence tracers target individual craft positions during the pass.
- Dogfight: when opposing wings are in adjacent hexes or cross during transits, elements pair off into turning circles of about 4 units radius for the duration of the engagement animation, with tracer exchanges; losses, when the rules inflict them, show as one craft breaking away trailing smoke and falling dark (then the count step in the next formation covers the bookkeeping). No new rules: this is presentation of the existing wing-versus-wing fire.
- Loss, return, replacement: a destroyed wing's remaining craft scatter and go dark on the spot (no explosion bigger than a capital ship's small kill, per the flash rules). Replacement wings launch fresh from the carrier with the launch behaviour above. Wings do not land to rearm (the rules have no rearm); the return leg of every attack run brings them back to their station circuit, which is the visual home the comparators teach.

Speeds, relative: transit uses the ship flight duration as the base; craft spline speed within the formation is the same average, with the lag and stagger giving the impression of faster, lighter machines. On station circuit speed is about 1.6 units per second. Attack run peak speed about 30 units per second (the strafe sprites today run at an equivalent pace; the meshes now match it).

## 4. Readability at the battle camera

At full board view a wing must read as three things: a formation shape in the faction's geometry, a moving patrol circuit, and faction light (engine glow colour and, for Hegemony, the synced pulse; for Chorus, pulsing sacs). A fresh wing versus a mauled one reads by count: six craft versus three is obvious at 100 px of formation width, and below half HP the ruled smoke ribbon confirms it. Craft silhouette, chalk stripes, crystal vanes and chitin detail are zoom reads, honest at 40 px per craft at 4K and marginal at 1080p, which is fine: they reward the zoom the owner asked for ("more views") without carrying the board read. The minimum anti-static package is: patrol circuit always on, per-craft spline lag in transit, drive glow breathing at rest (dim, per R55, brightening on burns). If a wing is not doing one of those three at any given second, it will read as parked, so the build should treat the circuit as the wing's idle state, not an embellishment. Attack runs read at board distance by the formation's convergence and the firing window's tracers converging on the target, the BFG committed-pass shape; the two-element split around the target is the signature to protect in the build.

## 5. Frame budget at 20 ships plus wings

Worst case from the pack: 14 wings. At this spec's counts (six, five or eight per wing, call it six average) that is about 84 craft meshes, against the pack's 56 at today's fixed four. Draw behaviour: craft must be one shared mesh per faction-kind (12 meshes total: two kinds times six factions, Chorus and Eclipse included) with per-faction materials, instanced or at worst one draw per craft; 84 small draws is acceptable, 84 unique scenes is not. Animation cost drivers are per-craft spline evaluation and banking, which are cheap transforms; the patrol circuit is a parametric offset, no physics. Sprite and particle traffic: attack runs reuse the existing strafe payloads (tracers, bombs, vapour trails), now anchored to craft positions; launches drop the six-sprite streak and spend nothing new; dogfight tracers reuse wing weapon sprites. Caps: at most two wings run attack animations at full craft fidelity at once per side (stagger further runs by a beat, which reads as waves, a BFG behaviour); beyond 40 units of camera distance in a future zoom-out, or at low settings, per-craft lag and banking switch off and the formation flies as one block with the circuit kept; in safe mode, craft draw as the lead mesh plus count, no trails, circuit kept (motion is the point of the spec). What must be measured, not promised: frame cost of 84 instanced craft with per-craft transforms on the owner's RTX 5080 at 4K, in the same capture harness the damage spec uses, before the counts above are treated as final. If the measurement fails, the first degradation is craft per wing (six to five to four), never the patrol circuit.

## 6. Owner questions

1. Visible craft count tracking wing hp. A (pick): count steps down as hp falls (full, minus two, half), so a mauled wing looks mauled. B: always full count, damage read only from the smoke ribbon and the UI. Stakes: A is the cheapest strong damage read on the board but makes wing strength public information at a glance, including the enemy's.
2. Craft size. A (pick): shrink to 2.6 to 3.0 units, formation carries the read. B: keep today's 3.84 units, each craft reads individually at 1080p. Stakes: A risks wings feeling small at 1080p; B keeps craft reading as mini ships that compete with frigates.
3. How far faction flight character goes. A (pick): full set, one system, per-faction geometry, spacing variance and launch behaviour (Chorus budding, Hegemony pulse sync, Hullbreaker ragged pack). B: shared behaviour, faction difference only in mesh and colour. Stakes: A is the direct answer to "wrong craft" but costs per-faction tuning in one build; B is safer and blander.
4. Eclipse craft. A: spec and build the warden motes now, dormant until an Eclipse carrier or drone bay exists. B (pick): spec only (as here), build when the data change is ruled. Stakes: A spends asset time on craft no ship can launch; B keeps the design ready at zero build cost.
5. Dogfight presentation. A (pick): paired turning circles with tracer exchanges whenever wings engage, pure presentation over existing rules. B: wings exchange fire from their station circuits, no special dogfight state. Stakes: A is the most alive moment two wings can have and the most new animation code in this spec; B is nearly free.
