## 1. Comparator breakdown

**Homeworld 2.** Strike craft are individual ships grouped into squadrons at build time, with squadron sizes fixed per hull: 3 for scouts, 5 for interceptors, 6 for bombers, 7 for assault craft. They launch from carriers and motherships, fly as individual craft in formation, and dock to rearm and repair. Docked squadrons replace lost craft free of charge, so losses are recovered only by returning to the hangar. Losses show as individual craft disappearing from the formation, and the squadron persists as a reduced group until it docks or is wiped out. At tactical distance the read is carried by engine trails and the shape of the formation; trails describe motion even when the craft themselves are small. The squadron is the unit of control, but the individual craft is the unit of simulation and of visual loss.

**Homeworld 3.** The representation is still individual craft in squadrons, but the formation is more dynamic: squadrons reflow spacing around obstacles and execute 3D maneuvers including barrel rolls and high-G turns. Strike craft make strafing runs and then retreat to cover before another pass. Formations were explicitly improved after demo feedback to be more strategically valuable, and the non-linear inverse perspective scaling was fixed so fighters remain visible and selectable when zoomed out. Losses are still individual craft lost from the squadron. The tactical read is formation behavior and strafing arcs, with engine trails secondary to the visible maneuver.

**Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 1 and 2.** Strike craft are closer to ordnance waves than to persistent individual ships. A launch bay puts a squadron token onto the table, and that token moves across the battlespace to make an attack run against a hull. Turrets are the counter: a failed turret roll can remove the entire wave, and fighters are removed before other ordnance in a mixed wave. In Armada 2, fighters and bombers restore their charge only if they return intact, so the wave persists as a reusable munition rather than as a group of individually simulated craft. Losses show as the token being removed or the squadron failing to return. At tactical distance the read is the token itself, its course, and the turret fire meeting it. Individual craft are not the unit of readability.

**Sins of a Solar Empire 2.** Strike craft are hosted per carrier as squadrons, with per-craft models that orbit and strike. A TEC hangar hosts two squadrons of six fighters or four bombers, and those craft can move anywhere in the gravity well to engage enemies. They cannot be targeted by most weapons, which means the visual read is not about being shot at craft by craft; the read is the swarm leaving the carrier, orbiting, and striking. Losses are per craft and squadrons rebuild for free over time, so the carrier slowly replenishes its air group. At tactical distance the read is the moving cloud of small craft and their engine lights, not any single model.

**Transferable lessons.** Homeworld 2: the squadron is the control unit but the individual craft must visibly exist, because loss and motion read at craft level (lesson: keep four visible craft and let them move independently). Homeworld 3: formation reflow and strafing runs are what make a wing feel alive; a rigid block will always read static (lesson: replace the rigid duplicate offsets with a reflowing formation and fly the attack run with meshes). BFG Armada: turret fire and the attack run are a single readable event, and a wave that survives returns (lesson: make the flak gauntlet and the run one staged visual event, and make replacement a visible launch, not a pop-in). Sins 2: a carrier's air group reads as a cloud of lights at distance, and per-craft detail matters only on zoom (lesson: prioritize formation shape, engine lights, and trails over panel detail). None of these games solve Voidsworn's specific problem of a wing that is one rules unit with one hp pool, so the design must borrow motion and readability without borrowing per-craft loss simulation.

## 2. Per-faction craft spec

Today's craft is 3.84 world units, which is about 30 px at 1080p and 59 px at 4K at the battle camera (pack, ship_view.gd:72; hull_table.gd:26-28). A frigate draws at 10.4 units and a battleship at 33.6, so the craft is roughly 37 percent of a frigate's length. The formation footprint is about 13 by 12 units, or about 100 by 92 px at 1080p. I keep the craft near 3.84 units for most factions because the silhouette is already at the readability floor; shrinking below about 3.0 units risks losing the craft silhouette entirely at 1080p. I vary size only where the faction idiom demands it.

**Sunward Crusade: Ember Dart.** A black iron dart with a single tall radiator spire, two swept fins, and a frost-white oath marking along the spine. Silhouette is a narrow forward wedge with a bright ember-orange drive at the tail. Size: keep 3.84 units (30 px at 1080p, 59 px at 4K). Colours: hull #4a4440, trim #c8501f, glow #ff7a2a, with frost-white chalk markings. Engine: ember-orange, dim at rest per R55, brightening in a short ramp on launch and transit. The radiator spire is the faction tell and should be visible on zoom but not relied on at board distance.

**Hegemony Navy: Registry Lance.** A pale grey and steel-blue faceted arrowhead, flush panels, no fins, identical across the wing. Silhouette is a tight geometric dart with a single blue light at the nose and a recessed engine block. Size: shrink to 3.5 units (27 px at 1080p, 54 px at 4K) to sell the tight registry geometry and allow a closer formation without overlap. Colours: #c4ccd5, #3a78d0, #5fa0ff. Engine: blue lights that pulse in time across the wing, dim at rest, synchronized on launch. The pulse synchronization is the faction read: all four craft blink together.

**Ashfall Hullbreakers: Scrap Hound.** A bolted-together strike craft with asymmetric welded plates, an exposed engine block, a jagged nose, and trophy scraps lashed to the hull. Silhouette is irregular and slightly nose-heavy, with visible gaps and mismatched fins. Size: grow to 4.2 units (32 px at 1080p, 65 px at 4K) so the scrap detail reads on zoom and the craft looks heavier than a fighter. Colours: #6a2c22, #b8402a, #e0402c, with bare metal patches. Engine: red-orange, uneven, sputtering at rest. The irregular silhouette is the faction read and should never fly in a clean parade line.

**The Chorus: Brood Spore.** This replaces the current spore mesh (hull_table.gd:15 uses B23_51, which is a bomber underneath, sq:'bomber' at data.js:70-72). The Brood Spore is a living creature: a bulbous ochre-ridged sac with trailing tendrils, bile yellow-green bioluminescent veins, and a pulsing glow sac at the core. It is hatched or budded from the brood-sac, never built. Silhouette is rounded and organic, with no hard edges and no wings. Size: keep 3.84 units (30 px at 1080p, 59 px at 4K), because the creature needs volume to read as a creature rather than a dot. Colours: dark olive-brown flesh, ochre chitin trim #a4913a, bile yellow-green glow #d6d24a; the bible's flesh palette governs where the data palette differs. Engine: a slow bioluminescent pulse, dim at rest, brighter on launch. This directly answers the owner's complaint about regular fighters launching from Chorus ships.

**The Glasswrights: Starling Shard.** A thin clear-crystal shard with two silver vanes and stored light glowing inside the facets. Silhouette is a narrow faceted sliver with a bright internal core and two swept crystal wings. Size: shrink to 3.1 units (24 px at 1080p, 48 px at 4K), because these are "small crystal shards or vaned motes" and the starling read depends on many small lights moving together rather than on individual bulk. Colours: #eef3f1, #d7c88a, #9fffd2. Engine: stored light, a pale green-white glow that dims at rest and flares on launch. The internal light is the faction tell and must be visible at board distance as a moving point of light.

**Eclipse: Corona Drone (fenced).** A dark violet-black angular machine body with a ring of white-gold corona light and a single white-gold eye. Silhouette is a compact geometric drone with a halo, not a fighter shape. Size: 2.8 units (22 px at 1080p, 43 px at 4K), the smallest craft, because these are "tiny repair drones" per the lore bible. Colours: violet-black plates and white-gold corona light, using the bible palette rather than the stale data green. Engine: steady white-gold corona, dim at rest. This spec is dependent on a future carrier or drone bay decision: Eclipse has sq null and no hangar today (data.js FACTIONS; pack), so this craft is design-only until that data change is ruled.

## 3. Formation and movement behaviour

The base model is one system with faction parameters. Every wing shows four craft. The visible count does not track hp, because R80 rules stage 0 damage surfaces and a smoke ribbon below half total HP and forbids per-craft damage states beyond that. Dropping a craft from the formation would imply an individual loss that the rules do not simulate, and would contradict the one-pool rule (pack, battle.js:1064, 1157). Instead, at below 50 percent hp the formation tightens by 15 percent and the smoke ribbon appears. The wing always looks like a wing; damage is read through smoke and surface scorch, not through missing craft.

Formation geometry is faction-specific but uses the same four slot offsets in world units, rotated to the wing's heading. The Crusade flies a diamond: lead at (0,0,0), wingmen at (-4.0, +1.5, +3.0), (-4.0, -1.5, -3.0), and (-8.0, 0, 0). Hegemony flies line-abreast: lead at (0,0,0), wingmen at (-3.2, 0, +3.6), (-3.2, 0, -3.6), (-6.4, 0, 0). Hullbreakers fly a loose irregular pack with offsets jittered by ±1.2 units per craft and a 10 percent spacing variance. Chorus flies a cloud: offsets at roughly (±3.5, ±1.0, ±3.5) with slow individual drift. Glasswrights fly a starling V: lead at (0,0,0), wingmen at (-3.6, +1.2, +2.8), (-3.6, -1.2, -2.8), (-7.2, 0, 0). Eclipse flies a halo: lead at (0,0,0), wingmen on a 3.0-unit radius circle around the lead. Footprint is about 8 to 10 units across, slightly tighter than today's 13 by 12, which keeps the formation as the readable unit without letting it blur into a single blob.

Launch is flown by the meshes, replacing the sprite-only streaks at fx_weapons.gd:969-983. The four craft leave the carrier's hangar in a staggered sequence, 0.12 s apart, and fly an eased curve to the wing's hex. Transit time is 0.9 s for a carrier three hexes away and 1.4 s for eight hexes. Engine glow ramps from dim to full over the first 0.3 s. The lead craft follows the carrier-to-hex spline; wingmen hold formation with a 0.1 s lag so the group ripples rather than moves as a block.

Transit between hexes uses the same eased flight as a ship, but the wing flies at 1.4 times the parent ship's spline speed. The whole formation banks into turns at up to 25 degrees, and each wingman banks with a 0.08 s delay, which produces a visible ripple through the formation. This is the core fix for the "rigid duplicates" problem: the wingmen are no longer rigid offsets, they are independent transforms following a formation target.

On station, the wing never freezes. It flies a slow patrol orbit around its hex centre, radius 1.5 units, period 6 s, while individual craft weave ±0.3 units on their own phases. Engines stay dim at rest per R55. This gives constant motion without changing the rules position. At board distance the wing reads as a drifting cluster of lights, not a parked diamond.

The attack run is flown by the meshes and replaces the sprite-only strafe at fx_weapons.gd:609-640. When the wing attacks, it breaks formation into two pairs, flies to the target over 0.8 s, fires during a 0.3 s pass, then pulls up and loops back to the hex over 1.2 s. Total event is 2.0 s, with the return leg overlapping the next ready state. Engine glow and weapon sprites are timed to the mesh pass, so the lights no longer attack while the craft stay parked. The flak gauntlet (battle.js:774) is represented by turret fire meeting the incoming craft during the 0.8 s approach, and craft that "die" in the run are abstracted as the wing taking hp, not as individual removals.

Dogfighting occurs when an enemy wing is within 4 units. The two wings break formation and pair off, each craft flying a 1.5 s weave loop around its opposite number with crossing passes. The engagement lasts 2 to 4 s, then both wings reform. This is presentation-only: the rules still resolve the wing as one unit. If the frame budget is tight, dogfight weaving degrades to simple head-on passes before any other motion is cut.

Loss, return, and replacement follow the rules: wings never return to rearm, and a destroyed wing is replaced from spares as a new launch (pack, data.js:136). Death is the standard unit death plus a scatter-and-fade of the four craft, and the smoke ribbon persists for a short beat before the unit is removed. A replacement wing launches from the carrier with the same launch sequence, so the player sees a new wing fly out rather than a token popping into the hex.

Faction variation lives in the parameters: Hegemony tightens spacing by 15 percent and synchronizes banking; Crusade keeps the diamond and banks as a unit; Hullbreakers use jittered spacing and lag bank delays by 0.2 s; Chorus uses a cloud formation with continuous individual drift even in transit; Glasswrights fly the V and weave on station with the widest amplitude; Eclipse holds the halo and flies the most precise lines.

## 4. Readability at the battle camera

At 1080p the battle camera is about 7.7 px per world unit, and at 4K it is 15.4 px per unit. A 3.84-unit craft is 30 px at 1080p and 59 px at 4K. The formation is about 100 by 92 px at 1080p. The formation, not the craft, is the unit of readability at full board view (pack, camera section).

What reads at full board view: the formation silhouette as a cluster, the direction of motion through banking, engine trails as short bright streaks, the launch arc from carrier to hex, the attack run's out-and-back path, and the smoke ribbon below half hp. What only reads on zoom: individual panel lines, radiator spires, crystal facets, weld seams, dogfight weave detail, and per-craft banking. Internal detail under about 0.5 units (4 px at 1080p) is not worth building for board view and should be treated as zoom-only ornament.

The minimum motion to never read as static is a constant on-station drift: the 6 s patrol orbit plus per-craft weave means no wing is ever a frozen diamond. Even at rest, engines flicker dimly and the formation breathes. In transit, the banking ripple and the 0.1 s wingman lag make the group read as four craft, not one block. In the attack run, the mesh pass and return loop are the strongest motion events and should be the most legible thing a wing does.

The damage read is deliberately narrow because R80 settles it. A fresh wing has a clean formation, clean stage 0 surfaces, and dim engines. A mauled wing below 50 percent hp has the smoke ribbon and a 15 percent tighter formation. At board distance the smoke ribbon is the primary read because it is the only high-contrast mark; the tighter formation is secondary and supports the read without contradicting the one-pool rule. There are no missing craft and no per-craft scorch states, so the player cannot mistake a mauled wing for a wing that has lost individual craft. On zoom, stage 0 surfaces add scorch and pitting, but board-view readability does not depend on them.

## 5. Frame budget at 20 ships plus wings

The pack's worst case is 14 wings on the board, 56 craft meshes, plus launch and strafe sprite traffic. I accept that case and add a 15 percent headroom for dogfight weaving. Today those 56 meshes already exist as 14 ship_view units with four meshes each (pack, ship_view.gd:116-122). The new cost is per-craft animation, not new mesh count.

Craft meshes and draw behavior: 56 craft meshes. Use one MultiMesh per wing for the three wingmen where the faction finish allows it, and a separate mesh for the lead if it needs unique damage surfaces. Draw calls target 14 to 28 for craft, plus one ribbon mesh per wing for trails. Per-craft animation drivers are transform updates (position, rotation, bank), engine glow shader parameters, and a trail segment count. At 60 fps that is 3360 transform updates per second, which is not a bottleneck by itself; the risk is overdraw and particle traffic.

Sprite and particle traffic: launch uses six engine sprites per wing (84 if all 14 launch at once) and the attack run uses three sprites per craft pass (up to 168 per wing attack). Pool these at 256 active sprites and cap per-wing launch sprites at six. Trails are one ribbon mesh per wing with 8 to 12 segments, so 14 ribbons and at most 168 segments, which is well within budget if the ribbons are unlit and share a material.

What degrades first: at distance, per-craft trails collapse to one trail per wing and per-craft banking is disabled; the formation still moves as a group. At low settings, trail segments drop to four, dogfight weaving becomes head-on passes, and wingman transforms update at 30 Hz instead of 60 Hz. In safe mode, the system falls back to today's rigid four-craft block with lead-only bob and sprite-based launch and strafe, so the game remains playable on the minimum spec. No millisecond promises are made here: the build must measure GPU time for trail ribbons, draw calls for MultiMesh batches, and frame time on the RTX 5080 at 1080p and 4K with 56 craft and 256 sprites. The acceptance gate is the 40 fps floor from the damage spec, and the first thing to cut if the gate fails is dogfight weaving, then per-craft trails, then banking.

## 6. Owner questions

1. Craft size. (a) Keep 3.84 units for most factions, with per-faction exceptions for Hegemony and Glasswrights (my pick). (b) Shrink all craft to 3.0 units to increase formation spacing. (c) Grow all craft to 4.2 units for detail. Stakes: readability at 1080p board view versus how much faction detail survives at distance. Option (a) protects the 30 px silhouette while letting idiom drive the exceptions.

2. Visible craft count versus hp. (a) Fixed four craft, no count tracking, with formation tightening and the R80 smoke ribbon (my pick). (b) Drop to three craft below 50 percent hp and two below 25 percent. (c) Keep four craft but scatter them as hp falls. Stakes: option (b) gives a strong damage read but implies per-craft loss that the rules do not simulate and risks contradicting R80. Option (a) is the only one fully consistent with the one-pool rule and the ruled damage surfaces.

3. How far faction variation goes. (a) Distinct silhouette, formation, and engine behavior for all six factions (my pick). (b) Shared silhouette per class with faction finish and glow only. (c) Distinct silhouettes for four factions and shared for two. Stakes: the owner complaint names wrong craft from Chorus ships, so shared silhouettes repeat the problem. Option (a) is the direct fix, but it is the largest art cost and should be staged: Chorus and Crusade first, then Hullbreakers, then Hegemony and Glasswrights, with Eclipse design-only.

4. Eclipse craft. (a) Spec the Corona Drone now but do not build it until a carrier or drone bay is ruled (my pick). (b) Spec and build a drone bay now, accepting the data and balance change. (c) Do not spec Eclipse craft. Stakes: Eclipse has no launch ship and no hangar today, so building craft without a carrier is wasted work. Option (a) keeps the design complete without expanding scope.

5. Dogfight presentation. (a) Full pairing and weave loops when wings meet, degrading to head-on passes at low settings (my pick). (b) Simple head-on passes only. (c) No dogfight presentation; wings pass through each other abstractly. Stakes: full weaving is the strongest answer to "super static," but it is also the first thing that must degrade under frame pressure. Option (a) gives the owner the motion he asked for while keeping a documented fallback.
