Squadron Design Spec: Design Leg
1. Comparator Breakdown
Homeworld 2 (Relic, 2003). Strike craft are individual ship models grouped into squadrons of roughly five to seven craft. They physically launch from a carrier's hangar bay, fly a transit path to the target, make a strafing pass, then return to the carrier to rearm and repair before going out again. Losses show as individual craft exploding and being removed from the group; a damaged squadron visibly thins. At tactical distance, the read comes from the cluster of small engine glows moving as a loose group against the dark of space, plus the carrier's hangar activity. The return-to-rearm cycle is the core loop and is visible on screen (Relic developer commentary and the in-game manual describe the dock-and-rearm cycle; individual craft counts per squadron are documented in the Homeworld 2 manual).
Homeworld 3 (Blackbird Interactive, 2024). The model carries forward from HW2: individual craft in squadrons, launch and recovery cycles, visible attrition as craft are destroyed one by one. The visual fidelity is higher; craft leave short engine trails and bank through turns. At the tactical camera the read is carried by the formation's motion silhouette and by attack-run contrails. Squadrons still return to rearm. The count per squadron is in the same five-to-seven range [IMPRESSION from launch-window gameplay coverage; no developer talk transcript verified here].
Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 1 and 2 (Tindalos, 2016 and 2019). Squadrons are closer to ordnance waves than to individual craft. A launch sends a visible cluster of small sprites toward a target; the cluster makes an attack run against the hull while point-defence turrets fire at it. Losses show as the cluster shrinking or failing to reach the target. There is no individual craft model to speak of at combat distance; the read is the streak of the wave and the flak bursting around it. Squadrons do not return; they are spent ammunition. The BFG tabletop rules describe squadrons as attack waves resolved against turret defence, and the digital games mirror that (the in-game codex and tutorial text describe the wave-and-flak interaction; individual craft are not modelled as separate entities in the Armada games [IMPRESSION for the visual scale, confirmed by the tabletop rulebook structure]).
Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (Ironclad, 2024). Each carrier hosts a complement of individual strike-craft models. Craft orbit the carrier at rest, peel off to make attack runs on a target, and return. The orbit is the on-station idle and it reads clearly at the tactical camera as a ring of small dots circling the carrier. Attack runs show as streams of craft diving toward the target. Losses remove individual craft from the orbit. The read at distance is carried by the orbit ring itself and by the dive vectors during an attack (Ironclad's pre-release developer updates describe the per-carrier craft hosting and the orbit-strike loop; individual craft counts scale with carrier level).
Transferable lessons for Voidsworn.
From Homeworld 2 and 3: the return-to-rearm cycle gives wings a visible life cycle, but Voidsworn's rules say wings never return (pack, data.js:136). The lesson to keep is the visible launch and the visible thinning, not the docking. From BFG Armada: the flak gauntlet is a moment of spectacle and the wave shape reads at distance even without individual craft detail; Voidsworn already has the flak gauntlet at battle.js:774, and the attack run should borrow that drama. From Sins 2: the on-station orbit is the single most effective anti-static behaviour and the cheapest to read at distance; Voidsworn's wings currently have no on-station animation at all (pack, ship_view.gd:1280 to 1282), and adding an orbit or patrol loop is the highest-value fix.
2. Per-Faction Craft Spec
Today all five launching factions share three meshes (hull_table.gd:15). The spore is the only faction-flavoured hull. This section gives each faction its own craft identity. Craft length stays at 3.84 world units (approximately 30 px at 1080p, 59 px at 4K) for all factions. Growing the craft would push the four-craft formation past one hex footprint; shrinking it would drop the silhouette below the 10 px readability floor at 1080p. Keep 3.84.
Sunward Crusade. Name: Ember Dart. A black-iron wedge with a tall, narrow radiator spine and frost-white chalk markings on the flank. Silhouette: a sharp, tall-fin dart, asymmetric top-to-bottom so the spine breaks the outline. Colours: hull #4a4440, trim #c8501f, engine glow #ff7a2a (data.js FACTIONS palette). Frost-white markings catch the light on the radiator spine so the craft reads even when the hull colour blends into a dark background.
Hegemony Navy. Name: Registry Drone. A compact, symmetrical hexagonal craft, pale grey with steel-blue panel lines and a single implant-blue light strip running nose to tail. Silhouette: a flat, wide chevron, identical across the fleet by design. Colours: hull #c4ccd5, trim #3a78d0, glow #5fa0ff. The identical geometry is the point: Hegemony craft should look interchangeable and locked in formation.
Ashfall Hullbreakers. Name: Scrap Fang. A lopsided, bolted-together strike craft: one oversized engine pod on one side, a crude ram nose, rust and patch-weld scars. The Chorus. Name: Spore Hatchling. A living creature, not a built craft: a ridged, bulbous body with chitin-limb stubs and a glowing bile sac. Colours: dark olive-brown flesh #57364c base, ochre chitin trim #a4913a, bile yellow-green glow #d6d24a (bible line 339 governs over the data palette where they differ). Engine: no engine; the creature pulses and its glow sacs brighten when it moves, dimming at rest per R55. The Glasswrights. Name: Starling Vane. A small crystal shard with two swept, faceted vanes and a core of stored light glowing inside the translucent body. 3. Formation and Movement Behaviour
One base system, parameterised per faction.
Craft per wing and hp tracking. Four visible craft per wing today (ship_view.gd:116 to 122). Keep four. The rules treat the wing as one hp pool and never lose individual craft separately (pack, wing rules). Visible craft count should not track hp; removing meshes at thresholds would contradict the single-pool rule and create a false read. All damage expression goes through the R80 surfaces and smoke ribbon.
Formation geometry. Replace the rigid diamond offsets (ship_view.gd:116 to 122) with a faction-shaped layout at the same spacing scale (roughly 1.3 to 2.5 craft lengths between craft). Crusade and Hegemony fly tight and symmetric (a wedge and a box respectively). Hullbreakers fly a loose, staggered scatter with randomised offsets per wing so no two look identical. Chorus flies a loose, slowly undulating cluster; the offsets drift by plus or minus 0.3 units on a slow sine. Glasswrights fly a shallow V, the "starling" shape. All offsets are per-wing random seeds within the faction's allowed spread, so the system is one table of spread parameters.
Launch (2.5 seconds). The meshes now fly the launch. Craft spawn at the carrier's hangar position, accelerate outward over 1.2 seconds, spread into formation over the next 0.8 seconds, and settle into the transit spline in the final 0.5 seconds. The current six-sprite streak (fx_weapons.gd:969 to 983) is replaced. Engine glow ramps from the R55 dim idle to full over the first 0.6 seconds.
Transit. Wings fly the same spline flight as capital ships when changing hexes. The duplicates no longer hold rigid offsets: each craft follows the spline with its own offset target and a per-craft lag of 0.1 to 0.25 seconds, producing a natural stagger through turns. Banking: each craft rolls into turns up to 25 degrees (Hegemony) or 40 degrees (Hullbreakers, Chorus), easing out over 0.4 seconds. This replaces the current block-yaw-only behaviour.
On-station behaviour. This is the core anti-static fix. Wings on station (no active target) execute a slow patrol behaviour tied to the carrier. Default: a loose orbit at 3 to 5 hex radius around the carrier, period roughly 14 seconds, altitude varying by plus or minus 0.5 units. The orbit is cheap (one trig call per craft per frame) and reads at board distance as motion around the carrier. Faction variation: Hegemony orbits in a locked ring, Hullbreakers patrol in a wide, irregular sweep, Chorus drifts in a slow cloud, Glasswrights fly figure-eight loops, Crusade holds a slow racetrack. Leash is unchanged at 8 hexes (battle.js:1128).
Attack run (3.5 seconds total). Replaces the sprite-only strafe (fx_weapons.gd:609 to 640). Phase 1, dive (1.0 s): the formation tightens and the craft pitch nose-down toward the target hex. Phase 2, pass (1.0 s): craft streak across the target at 2.2 times transit speed, weapons fire (tracer, bomb sprite, or spore-sac sprite depending on the wing's weapon type). Phase 3, break and reform (1.5 s): craft pull up, bank hard, and re-enter the orbit. Flak gauntlet visuals (battle.js:774) play during phase 2. The craft meshes are present and moving for the entire run.
Dogfighting. When enemy wings occupy the same or adjacent hexes, both wings enter a close-orbit around the shared hex at half orbit radius and 1.5 times orbit speed. Craft weave with a small sine offset. Weapons fire as normal per the swarm phase (battle.js:1064, 1157). No per-craft targeting animation is needed; the visual is two interweaving groups.
Loss, return, replacement. Wings fight until destroyed and are replaced from spares (data.js:136). Death plays the existing unit death. Replacement: the launch sequence plays from the carrier. No rearm cycle. The R80 smoke ribbon below half hp is the only in-flight damage read and is handled in the readability section.
Scramble Command. All four scramble Commands share one shape: extra wing out, then all wings strike again at plus 50 percent damage (pack, battle.js:1080). Visually: the extra wing launches over 1.5 seconds while all existing wings break orbit simultaneously and dive. A brief camera shake and a flash on the carrier mark the Command.
4. Readability at the Battle Camera
The formation is the unit of readability, not the single craft (pack, camera section). At 1080p the formation is roughly 100 by 92 px; at 4K, roughly 200 by 184 px. The owner plays at 4K.
What reads at full board view (170 units out). The formation silhouette and its motion. The orbit patrol makes a wing a moving cluster rather than a frozen diamond, which is the primary fix for the "super static" complaint. Engine glow and the faction glow colour carry the faction identity at this distance: ember-orange for Crusade, pulsing blue for Hegemony, flickering red for Hullbreakers, bile-green pulse for Chorus, pale green-white for Glasswrights. The smoke ribbon below half hp (R80) reads as a grey-brown trail behind the formation. Attack runs read as a sudden dive vector and a bright streak at the target.
What reads only on zoom (below 80 units). Craft silhouette detail: the Crusade radiator spine, the Hullbreaker asymmetry, the Chorus tendrils, the Glasswright vanes. Stage 0 damage surfaces (R80): scorch marks and panel displacement on the craft hulls. The frost-white chalk markings on Crusade craft. These are affordances for the zoom view, not board-distance reads.
Minimum motion to never read as static. The on-station orbit at 3 to 5 hex radius with a 14-second period moves each craft roughly 4 to 7 world units per second. At 1080p that is 30 to 54 px per second of continuous motion, well above the threshold where a player registers movement. The per-craft offset drift adds a secondary wobble. At rest (engines dim per R55), the orbit still runs; the craft never parks.
Damage read within R80. Below half total wing hp, the smoke ribbon appears: a particle trail behind the formation, grey-brown, roughly 2 units wide, fading over 3 units. Stage 0 damage surfaces (scorching, panel cracks) appear on the craft meshes. A fresh wing orbits cleanly with bright engines; a mauled wing trails smoke and shows darkened hull surfaces. At board distance the smoke ribbon is the tell. At zoom the surfaces confirm it. No per-craft destruction, no formation thinning; the wing is whole until it dies, per the rules.
5. Frame Budget at 20 Ships Plus Wings
Worst case. 20 capital ships on the board (R1) plus 14 wings, 56 craft meshes (pack worst case). I accept the pack's number. A carrier-heavy player fleet fields 5 to 7 wings; enemy carriers add the rest.
Craft meshes. 56 craft, each a single mesh instance. Each faction craft is one draw call with one material (vertex colour or a single small texture). 56 draw calls for all wings combined. No per-craft skeleton animation; all motion is transform-based (position, rotation). The per-craft cost drivers are: one trig call for orbit position, one easing interpolation for banking, one glow-intensity uniform update. These are trivial per frame.
Capital ships. 20 ships with existing mesh and material costs, unchanged by this spec.
Particles and sprites. Launch: one burst of roughly 8 particles per craft for 1.2 seconds, so 32 particles per wing launch, 224 in a worst-case simultaneous scramble. Attack run: weapon sprites per pass, matching today's three-sprite-per-craft cost (fx_weapons.gd:624), so 12 sprites per wing per pass, 168 across 14 wings. Smoke ribbon: one particle emitter per damaged wing, capped at 30 particles. Total particle ceiling in the worst simultaneous frame: roughly 500 sprites. This is within the existing fx budget; no new particle system is required.
Degradation tiers. First to degrade at distance (beyond 120 units): per-craft banking and offset drift are frozen; wings move as a single offset block. Second to degrade at low settings: the orbit is replaced by a simple back-and-forth patrol on a line (no trig, cheaper). Third, in safe mode: wings are drawn as a single billboard sprite per wing (one quad, faction-coloured), and the orbit becomes a circular translation of the billboard. The billboard is the absolute floor: one quad per wing, 14 quads.
Caps and measurement. This spec sets caps: 56 craft draw calls, 500 concurrent particles, one orbit trig call per craft per frame. Actual millisecond cost must be measured on the RTX 5080 target (pack, damage spec reference) with the 20-ship-plus-14-wing scene. The measurement must cover: full orbit at board distance, a simultaneous 14-wing scramble, and a mixed state with 6 wings in attack runs while 8 orbit. The 40 fps floor is the pass criterion. No unmeasured millisecond promises are made here; the caps above are the design constraints, and the profiler run is the gate.
6. Owner Questions
Q1. Visible craft count and hp. The rules say the wing is one pool and craft are never lost individually. (a) Keep four craft always, express damage only through R80 surfaces and smoke. (b) Drop to three craft at 50 percent hp and two at 25 percent, accepting the rules fiction. (c) Keep four but dim and slow the "ghost" craft below thresholds as a compromise. Pick: (a). Stakes: (b) contradicts the single-pool rule and risks the owner seeing craft vanish that the rules say are still alive; (c) adds a visual state the rules do not track.
Q2. How far faction variation goes in formation shape. (a) Faction spread parameters only (tight box versus loose scatter), same orbit and attack logic for all. (b) Faction-specific on-station behaviours (orbit, sweep, cloud, figure-eight) as described in section 3. (c) Fully bespoke per-faction flight scripts. Pick: (b). Stakes: (a) is cheapest to build but the Chorus cloud and Hullbreaker scatter are strong identity beats; (c) multiplies animation QA and makes the one-system goal harder.
Q3. Eclipse craft. (a) Spec them now, park them, build only if a carrier ships. (b) Leave Eclipse out entirely and revisit with a balance pass. (c) Give them a minimal drone visual now so the faction does not look empty in any future carrier preview. Pick: (b). Stakes: building a mesh and animation for a faction with no carrier is wasted art time; but the faction's absence from squadron content should be a conscious choice, not an oversight.
Q4. Attack-run camera behaviour. (a) No camera change; the run plays at board distance. (b) A brief 0.8-second soft zoom toward the target hex during the dive phase. (c) A full cinematic cut to the run, as in BFG Armada. Pick: (a). Stakes: (b) and (c) add spectacle but fight the turn-based board view; the owner may find a camera nudge disruptive across many simultaneous wing attacks in the swarm phase.
Q5. Dogfight visual intensity. (a) Two interweaving orbits with weapons fire, as specced. (b) A tighter, faster melee with per-craft target tracking. (c) A brief slow-motion beat when wings first clash. Pick: (a). Stakes: (b) needs per-craft targeting logic the rules do not support; (c) is a presentation flourish that may feel overwrought at 14 wings.
