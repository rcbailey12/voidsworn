Part 3 - The Three Complaints
Complaint | What causes it | Cure
"Thin and weak" | Sounds lack energy below 200 Hz. Short, bright samples with no body. Single-layer sounds with no sub component. Over-use of high-pass filtering. Everything sits in the 1-6 kHz range and nothing is felt. | Add a dedicated sub layer (sine or filtered thump at 30-90 Hz) to every impact and explosion. Keep body in the 80-400 Hz range. Use multi-layer construction: transient + body + tail. Let big events (torpedo, death) reach down to 30 Hz. Reference: Sins of a Solar Empire - you feel a capital ship die in your chest, not your ears.
"Cheesy or arcade-like" | Stock "pew", "zap", rising whooshes, and cartoon explosion samples. Over-use of pitch sweeps and LFO wobble. Sounds that reference other games or toys instead of physical sources. Too-bright, too-clean synth presets with no noise or grit. | Source from real mechanical and electrical recordings: relays, capacitors, breech mechanisms, pressure valves, hull groans, metal impacts. Pitch sounds down. Remove rising sweeps. Ground every weapon in a physical action (a latch opening, a charge building, a shell seating). If synthesising, start from noise and distortion, not from clean sines. Rule: if it could be in a mobile puzzle game, delete it.
"Harsh or annoying" | Excess energy above 4 kHz. Long, bright reverb tails. Clipping or hard limiting. Too many simultaneous high-frequency sounds. Sharp, unfiltered transients. No dynamic range management - everything at the same loudness. | Low-pass everything above what the sound needs (most SFX can roll off above 6-8 kHz; ambience above 3 kHz). Keep tails short and dry. Use a limiter on the master bus set to -1 dB, but mix individual sounds well below it (-12 to -18 dBFS for common hits, -6 for big events). Apply gentle compression (2:1 to 4:1 ratio) instead of hard limiting. Duck ambience and music by 3-6 dB during heavy combat. Enforce the "quiet bed" rule: nothing under the fight should compete with a torpedo.
