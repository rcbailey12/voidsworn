# #422 blind leg: Rey (chair), written before any other leg and before chair web research

## Sourcing priors (to be verified or refuted by the writer legs and the chair's licence checks)

The families to source: weapon fire, hits, ship deaths, engines, shields, buttons and menus, map and battle ambience, music.

Candidate sources I expect to survive licence checking:

- Sonniss GDC bundles: large professional libraries given away yearly; the licence has historically allowed use in commercial games with no attribution, redistribution of the raw files banned. Ryan has already ruled Sonniss fine for this project. If the current terms still say that, this is the spine of the found-sound half.
- freesound.org, filtered to CC0 only: the biggest raw-material pool (metal stress, machinery, impacts, servos, room tones). CC-BY is usable with credits; CC-BY-NC is a trap for a shipped game and must be excluded by filter, not by memory.
- Kenney audio packs: CC0, clean UI and interface sounds, a good fit for buttons and menus where the target is soft and dry.
- Pixabay and ZapSplat free tiers: usable commercially on their current terms, attribution rules differ (Pixabay none, ZapSplat attribution on the free tier). Both are mixed quality; treat as gap fillers, not the spine.
- OpenGameArt: CC0 and CC-BY material is fine; CC-BY-SA is a trap of a different kind (share-alike on the audio asset) and should be avoided for a shipped game unless the Board rules otherwise.
- NASA audio and video: public domain; engine and space ambience raw material.
- BBC Sound Effects: expect this to FAIL the licence check (its RemArc licence is personal and educational, not commercial games). It will get proposed by someone; the synthesis should name it as excluded so nobody builds on it.

Synthesis route priors: most ladder sounds are better made than found. Free tools: Audacity (editing), Surge XT and Vital (free synthesizers, capacitor charges, electrical arcs, sub thumps), LMMS (sequencing and layering), ffmpeg (pitch, stretch, layering in batch), PaulStretch-type extreme stretch for ambience beds and the Eclipse held note. A hit is three layers (transient click or crack, low-mid body, short tail) and the ladder is one recipe at five weights, matching deaths_and_hits.md: same family, each step plainly heavier.

## Palette priors (one line each, to be tested by the legs)

- Crusade: iron and fire - breech blocks, chains under tension, forge bellows, a choir-free organ-like furnace roar kept low.
- Hegemony: clean electrical - capacitor whine that stays polite, relay clicks in unison, filtered hum, everything quantized and identical.
- Hullbreakers: rattling junk - loose bolts in a drum, mismatched calibres, a cough before the bang, chains snapping at deaths.
- Glasswrights: glass and tone - struck crystal, sine tones with harmonics, fracture races; no explosion body at all, light leaving is the sound.
- Eclipse: a low held note - one tone under everything, corona crackle at the rim, silence as a weapon; its death is the note stopping.
- Chorus: wet and breathing - bellows, guts, sacs popping, breath cycles for engines; never a roar, it exhales.

## Thin, arcade, harsh: my priors on causes

Thin: single-layer sounds with no low-mid body; high-passed or phone-recorded sources; the same take repeated; loudness raised instead of weight added. Arcade: synthesized zaps and pitch-sweeps (the ear reads a falling sine as a toy laser), stock explosion recordings everyone has heard, sounds that start at full volume with no mechanical cause before them. Harsh: distortion and clipping used as a shortcut to power, energy stacked at 2 to 5 kHz, long tails fighting the next sound, no variation so fatigue builds. Cures in one line each: layer for body, give every big sound a mechanical tell before it (the deaths playbook's tell beat, in sound), cap the top, vary takes, keep tails short and dry.
