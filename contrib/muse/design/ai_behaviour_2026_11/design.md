# AI behaviour improvements: what a player notices, and what to fix first

Issue 1048. Type: brainstorm and recommendation (proposals only, no code, no rule changes). Chair: Rey. Legs: blind Rey (written first), DeepSeek, Qwen, Flash. Grok seat empty: grok.com returned "Your request was blocked" on the probe and on a fresh route; recorded, not substituted. Evidence: battle.js on current main, contrib/muse/tools/findings_tests/README.md (AI-1, AI-3, AI-4, AI-5), and the map/mechanics package sim results (contrib/muse/design/map_mechanics_2026_11/, about 4,000 battles, Nov 17, 2026).

## The problems a player actually notices

The council was unanimous on the list; every item is tied to a tested finding or a sim result, nothing is vibes.

1. Veteran ships die unbraced when their own numbers say brace (AI-1, BLOCKER at the findings pin). A veteran at 63% hull with a spent gun moved toward the enemy line, fired nothing, and ended unbraced under 32 expected threat. The brace decision lives in at least three places with three different condition sets, so whether a hurt ship braces depends on which path its activation took. Chair note: current main's aiUnit contains a mid-move brace check whose comment cites this finding's patch pack, so main may have moved since the pin. Whether the AI-1 test passes on current main is NOT CHECKED, and checking it is step zero before any brace work is scoped.
2. A frigate and a capital price death identically (AI-3, measured). The death cliff is a flat -25; in the sweep, cp1 and cp5 movers with identical normalised numbers first accepted the same lethal hex at the same kill value (40). Capitals take screen-level risks; cheap ships are no braver than expensive ones.
3. Ships with nothing loaded charge anyway (AI-4, measured). The approach leash reads the minimum range over all weapons, spent or not, so a torpedo boat with its only gun reloading closed 10 to 6 hexes against unarmed targets instead of holding or kiting.
4. Carriers launch wings into kill zones and sail away from them (AI-5, measured). Launch happens in the pre-move ability step, from hexes sorted by distance only: into 9 expected threat with safe hexes available, then the carrier moves and strands the wing.
5. Fleets beeline and clump; objectives are decoration (sim). The movement scorer prices combat value for the moving ship only and cannot see off-fleet objective value. Plural sites, rent, pull weights to 4.0 and majority scoring all failed to spread the battle under this scorer. On big maps the fight is two mobs colliding at one attractor.
6. The slugfest is small moves, not no moves (sim feel data). 91 to 94% of post-contact activations already move, but only 2.3 to 2.8 hexes. The scorer does price the motion defence bonus per candidate hex; the magnitude is too small to make repositioning feel real.
7. Damage spreads instead of finishing (weakest evidence, flagged by the blind Rey leg only). The veteran sticky focus is checked once per activation and nothing prices a kill-securing shot above fresh damage elsewhere. No leg could tie this to a test; the harness can measure overkill waste and time-to-first-kill before anyone builds on it.

## The ideas, ranked by how much a player would feel them

### 1. One self-preservation decision, priced by ship value (fixes AI-1 and AI-3)
Collapse the three brace sites into a single decision every activation path routes through, and scale the death cliff by ship value (CP-anchored), so a capital refuses a lethal hex a frigate may accept and a hurt veteran's brace no longer depends on which code path fired. References: XCOM 2's single survival gate (hurt Advent visibly fall back and hunker), BattleTech's value-aware retreat. Cost: small to medium (the logic exists three times; the work is consolidation plus one scaled constant). Sim proof: AI-1 and AI-3 tests pass; capital loss rate falls while frigate losses hold; player win rate and median rounds inside the guardrails below.

### 2. An objective-aware movement scorer (the attractor fix)
Add a capped objective-value term to the movement score: what a hex is worth to the fleet (unheld site, rent, blocking value), discounted by what fleet-mates already cover, so ships distribute instead of stacking one attractor. This is the untested step the map package pointed at: the scorer is the problem, not the objective types. References: Company of Heroes and Dawn of War (squads fan across capture points because the points carry the value), XCOM 2 objective maps (units peel off to contest the relay). Cost: large (new term, tuning, and the harness work is the real price). Sim proof: the package's own metrics on 34x24 and 38x26: coverage up, clustering down, with the guardrails: player win rate must not repeat the multiplicative retest (5/24 to 13/24), median rounds must not repeat 9.5 to 11.0, damage per round stable. If it pays those costs, it fails its own bar and does not ship.

### 3. Launch with the move, into the safest good hex (fixes AI-5)
Score launch hexes by threat first and distance second, and let the carrier launch from its post-move position (the ability step already re-runs after movement). Qwen's variant keeps the pre-move step but makes it consult the movement plan and allows a one-round delay when no safe hex exists; DeepSeek and Flash prefer the straight post-move launch. Either way the wing stops being a free kill and stops being stranded. References: Battlestar Galactica Deadlock's post-positioning squadron deployment, Homeworld's carrier-coupled production. Cost: small to medium. Sim proof: AI-5 test passes; wing survival in the first two rounds after launch rises; launches into 5+ expected threat fall to near zero.

### 4. The leash reads ready weapons only (fixes AI-4)
Compute the approach pull from weapons that can fire this activation; a ship with nothing bearing holds at its longest ready range or kites. References: StarCraft II cooldown kiting, BattleTech's missile boats refusing to close, Total War's skirmish mode. Cost: small (the ready list already exists in the activation). Sim proof: AI-4 test passes; closing-while-unloaded goes to zero; torpedo shots per battle and median rounds unchanged (the kite-forever guard).

### 5. Spread without the tax: soft spacing and role stances
Two additive routes to distribution that avoid the tested failures. Qwen's soft repulsion: a small penalty for ending adjacent to or stacked with allies, faction-scaled (swarm weak, capital fleets strong), generalising what Hegemony's syncPull already does for one faction. DeepSeek's role stances: per-class positioning weights (capital stand-off, screen escort, torpedo kite, carrier rear) inside the same scorer. The blind Rey leg's alternative, multiplicative scoring with an offense floor, is recorded but ranked below both: the retest showed multiplicative's spread is real and its damage tax is real, and the floor variant is untested. References: Homeworld formations as soft constraints, StarCraft II role micro. Cost: small to medium each. Sim proof: clustering down and coverage up on big maps at three weight settings, inside the same win-rate and rounds guardrails as idea 2.

### 6. Veteran focus fire, with a tell (veteran only)
Extend the sticky focus: veteran ships gain score for hexes that let them fire on the fleet's focus target, and finishing a nearly dead ship is priced above fresh damage (the kill removes a whole activation). Gated to veteran; at normal the nudge is zero. The convergence must be legible: ships converge spatially before their fire converges, giving the player a round to react, the way XCOM 2's officers visibly mark targets. References: XCOM 2 focus marking, FTL's system-concentrated fire. Cost: small to medium. Sim proof: distinct targets per AI round falls at veteran, time-to-first-kill falls, normal-difficulty numbers unchanged by construction and verified.

### 7. Hurt ships reposition like they mean it
Raise the scorer's weight on the motion defence bonus for ships that are hurt (under about 60% hull) or outgunned, so a 4 to 5 hex reposition beats a 2 hex shuffle for exactly the ships that need it. Qwen's version is a single multiplier on an existing term. The blind Rey leg's variant, a one-turn threat memory so a faded ship does not drift back into the same kill zone, is the natural follow-on if the metric moves. DeepSeek's narrowed two-pass (a bounded finish-move only for ships that fired nothing) also lives here as a variant: the broad two-pass failed its acceptance bar, the no-shot-only restriction is the untested remainder. References: BattleTech and Darkest Dungeon damage-state movement weighting, XCOM 2's no-shot fallback to cover. Cost: small (weight) to medium (variants). Sim proof: mean move distance for sub-60% hull ships rises out of the 2.3 to 2.8 band toward 3.5 to 4.5; battle length inside guardrails.

## Recommended first three (ship order, which is not the feel order)

By pure feel, idea 2 is the biggest change a player would notice. It should still ship fourth, because everything else on this list either clears a tested defect or de-risks it:

1. Ready-weapons leash (idea 4, AI-4). Pure defect removal, smallest build, zero difficulty change. Every leg put it in or near their first three; Qwen's argument carries: it builds trust and clears the ground before any scorer work.
2. One self-preservation decision with the value-scaled cliff (idea 1, AI-1 and AI-3). The BLOCKER plus the maintenance hazard: every later scorer change (ideas 2 and 5) adds paths that must route through one correct survival gate, or the bypass class of bug returns. Step zero is re-running the AI-1 test on current main, since main may already carry part of the fix.
3. Launch sequencing (idea 3, AI-5). The last tested defect, self-contained in the ability step, with a narrow proof (wing survival) that does not contaminate the coverage metrics idea 2 will be judged by.

Then idea 2 as a scoped experiment with its guardrails written down before it runs, and ideas 5 to 7 as seasoning in any order the Board prefers.

## What would make the AI feel worse (all legs, merged)

Multiplicative scoring adopted as-is (tested: fights worse, battles longer). Broad two-pass (tested: pays damage for motion, and unreadable). An over-scaled death cliff (capitals kite forever, battles stall past 12 rounds). Focus fire leaking into normal difficulty (mechanical deletion of one ship per round reads as punishment, not play). Objective weights high enough to camp (the AI ignores the player's fleet to sit on a well). A leash fix with no engagement floor (torpedo boats kite forever). Any veteran sharpness without a visible tell.
