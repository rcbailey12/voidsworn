# AI behaviour improvements, issue 1048. Model's response, verbatim.

Design Council Leg - AI Behaviour Improvements (Issue 1048)
1. Problems a Player Would Actually Notice
Each item below is grounded in the evidence pack. I'm listing what the player sees on screen, then tying it to the finding.
A. Ships charge into death for no reason
Evidence: AI-1 (BLOCKER). "A veteran ship at 63% hull with a spent gun moved 8->4 hexes toward the enemy line, fired nothing, and ended unbraced under 32 expected threat." The mid-move volley path returned before the veteran brace decision could fire, even though its own thresholds were met. Compounding this, the brace logic is fragmented: the evidence notes it "lives in at least three places with three different condition sets" (mid-move check, post-volley fallback at hp < 50% / threat > 10, and aiVetBrace at threat > 45% hp / hull < 70%). A player sees a ship lurch forward, do nothing, and sit there getting shredded. It reads as a bug, not a tactic.
B. A frigate and a capital price their own death identically
Evidence: AI-3 (FIX). "Two movers identical in every normalised number, differing only in hull value (cp1 vs cp5), first accept the same lethal hex at exactly the same kill value (40)." The death cliff is a flat -25, unscaled to ship cost. A player watching a cp5 capital wade into the same kill-zone a cp1 scout would accept gets no sense that the AI values its expensive assets differently. The fleet feels homogeneous.
C. Ships close distance when they literally cannot shoot
Evidence: AI-4 (FIX). "A ship whose only gun is a reloading torpedo closed 10->6 hexes against unarmed targets instead of holding or kiting: the leash runs off the spent gun's minimum range even when nothing can bear." The approach leash computes minR over all weapons, spent or not. The player sees a ship bearing down on them, clearly unable to fire, and it just looks broken-like the AI doesn't know what its own loadout is.
D. Carriers launch wings into danger and then leave them behind
Evidence: AI-5 (FIX). "The carrier launches in its pre-move step into 9 expected threat on the launch hex nearest the enemy while threat-free launch hexes exist; the wing is then left behind when the carrier moves." Launch hexes are sorted only by distance. Because abilities fire in step 1 (before movement), the carrier is locked into its launch position before it can reposition. A player sees a carrier dump its wing into a hot zone and then sail away, abandoning them. It's one of the most visible "the AI doesn't plan ahead" moments in the game.
E. On large maps both fleets beeline into a single clump
Evidence: Sim, Nov 17. "On big maps both fleets beeline and clump (coverage and clustering measured per size). No objective variant tested… spread the battle under the current scorer: the scorer prices combat value for the moving ship and cannot see off-fleet objective value." The movement scorer is a single attractor. A player on a big map sees two blobs collide in the centre and nothing else happens spatially. There is no flanking, no splitting, no reason to care about the edges of the board.
F. Ships move, but barely-the perceived slugfest
Evidence: Sim feel data. "91-94% of post-contact activations already move; the perceived slugfest is small moves (2.3-2.8 hexes), not no moves." And the defence bonus already exists: "a ship that moved 3+ hexes is harder to hit, sitting still is easier." The scorer sees the defence bonus per candidate hex but the magnitude is too small to pull ships into meaningful repositioning. The player feels a grinding melee where nobody is really going anywhere.
G. The AI ignores objectives entirely
Evidence: Sim, objective variants. Plural sites, rent-for-holding, pull weights up to 4.0, majority scoring-none spread the battle because "the scorer prices combat value for the moving ship and cannot see off-fleet objective value." A player contesting a well or a site watches the enemy fleet sail straight past it to punch the player's flagship. The objectives feel decorative.
2. Improvement Proposals, Ranked by Player Feel
Proposal 1 - Unified, tiered self-preservation (fixes AI-1, AI-3, and the three-place brace)
What changes. Collapse the three brace/survival checks into a single decision point that runs after all movement scoring is final. The check scales its thresholds to ship value: a cp1 scout braces at a lower absolute threat than a cp5 capital, but the capital's death-cliff penalty is proportionally larger. Concretely, the flat -25 becomes a curve anchored to the ship's cp cost (e.g. proportional to cp × k), and the brace conditions (threat-vs-hp ratio, hull percentage, offense shortfall) are evaluated once, in one place, after the ship has committed to a hex. The mid-move volley path no longer short-circuits past the check.
Game reference. XCOM 2's squad AI evaluates "will this kill me?" as a gating pass before any action, and its Advent units visibly fall back to cover when damaged-the player reads "that unit is hurt and playing safe." FTL: Faster Than Light layers this: enemy ships prioritise shield-breaking and system damage, but their AI will retreat a crew member from a room about to be breached. The key mechanic in both is a single survival gate evaluated after all other decisions, not three scattered checks.
Build cost. Medium. The logic already exists in three places; the work is consolidating conditions, parameterising the cliff to cp, and ensuring the mid-move path routes through the same gate. No new scorer architecture needed.
Sim proof. Re-run the paired harness at the findings pin. AI-1 and AI-3 tests must pass. Then: on the 4,000-battle suite, veteran survivor count should rise (fewer ships dying to the bypass), player win rate at normal difficulty should hold or shift ≤1 game per 24 (the AI is preserving assets, not getting smarter offensively), and median battle length should not increase by more than 0.5 rounds (guard against stall).
Proposal 2 - Fix the approach leash to respect loaded weapons only (AI-4)
What changes. minR in the leash calculation considers only weapons that can actually bear this turn (loaded, in arc, not on cooldown beyond a threshold). If every weapon is spent or out of arc, the leash term zeroes out and is replaced by a gentle hold-or-kite bias: the ship prefers to stay at or widen current range rather than close. This is a small conditional on the existing leash code, not a new scorer term.
Game reference. StarCraft II's Marine and Stalker micro at higher AI levels: the AI kites with ranged units that cannot fire, pulling back to max range rather than closing to melee. The specific mechanic is weapon-readiness gating on approach vectors-the AI checks "can I actually shoot from closer?" before committing to a closer hex. BattleTech (HBS, 2018) does the same with its engagement-range AI: a mech with only long-range missiles will not close to short-range band.
Build cost. Small. One conditional branch in the leash term; the weapon-state data is already available to the scorer.
Sim proof. AI-4 test passes. In the harness, filter battles where at least one AI ship has a torpedo-only or single-weapon loadout: the mean closing distance for those ships should drop (no more 10->6 on spent guns), and player win rate in those specific matchups should not change significantly (the ship is no longer gifting free approach, but it also isn't fighting smarter).
Proposal 3 - Carrier launch timing and hex selection (AI-5)
What changes. Carriers evaluate launch as part of movement rather than as a blind pre-move ability. Two sub-changes within the existing activation structure: (a) the carrier's pre-move step selects a launch hex from among the hexes it can reach this activation, not just the hexes adjacent to its current position; (b) launch-hex scoring includes expected threat at the launch hex and expected threat at the carrier's destination hex, so the wing isn't orphaned. If no safe launch hex exists in reachable range, the carrier delays the launch (the wing stays aboard) and repositions. This keeps the ability -> move -> volley structure; the ability step simply becomes a planning step that consults the movement scorer.
Game reference. Homeworld (1999 / Remastered) carrier production: the player picks a build location and the carrier positions itself before launching fighters; the AI in Homeworld: Deserts of Kharak does the same, moving its carrier to a defensible position before spawning units. The mechanic is production-coupled-to-positioning: the launch decision and the movement decision are one decision. Sins of a Solar Empire's hangar ships similarly reposition before deploying squadrons.
Build cost. Medium. Requires the ability step to query the movement scorer's candidate-hex list (already computed or easily hoisted), and the launch-hex sort to include a threat term. No new activation phases.
Sim proof. AI-5 test passes. In the harness, track carrier activations: wing survival rate in the first two rounds after launch should rise, carrier deaths attributable to "launched into threat and couldn't move" should drop to near zero, and wing DPS contribution should increase (wings are alive longer). Player win rate should be flat or slightly down (the AI is no longer gifting free wing kills).
Proposal 4 - Objective-aware movement scoring
What changes. The movement scorer gains an objective-value term: for each candidate hex, the scorer estimates the off-fleet value of being there (proximity to a contested well, rent accrual, blocking an enemy scoring path). This is additive to the existing offense - threat + situational terms, weighted by a faction-profile parameter. Crucially, the sim evidence says "the untested fix is an objective-aware scorer, not more objective types"-so this proposal does not add new objective mechanics; it makes the existing scorer see the ones already on the map. The term is capped so it cannot override survival (a ship never walks into a death hex for a well point).
Game reference. XCOM 2's AI on mission-objective maps: Advent units don't just beeline to the squad; they peel off to defend the relay, contest the extraction zone, or hold a chokepoint. The mechanic is spatial goal weighting in the utility function-each tile's score includes a mission-value component alongside combat value. Total War: Warhammer III does this at the army level: AI armies will divert to contest a settlement even mid-battle if the strategic value outweighs the local fight.
Build cost. Medium-large. The scorer needs an objective-proximity lookup per candidate hex (spatial query, not per-ship combat math), a faction-profile weight, and a cap. The sim harness already has objective variants plumbed (plural sites, rent, pull weights up to 4.0), so the test infrastructure exists.
Sim proof. Re-run the objective variants that currently fail to spread the battle. Coverage and clustering metrics should move: coverage up, clustering down, without the win-rate penalty seen in the multiplicative-scoring test. The key acceptance bar: player win rate does not rise by more than 2 games per 24 (the AI is contesting space, not getting dumber in fights). Battle length should not exceed +0.5 rounds median.
Proposal 5 - Fleet spatial distribution via soft formation pull
What changes. Add a mild repulsion term between allied ships in the movement scorer: if two friendly ships would end on adjacent or overlapping hexes, the second ship's score for that hex takes a small penalty. This is not the multiplicative-scoring restructure (which the sim showed makes the AI fight worse); it is a single additive nudge, tuned small (-1 to -2), applied after the main combat terms. The faction profile controls the strength: swarm factions (many small ships) get a weaker repulsion; capital-heavy factions get a stronger one. The Hegemony syncPull already does something analogous for formation; this generalises the idea.
Game reference. Homeworld's formation system: ships in a fleet maintain relative positions and the AI redistributes them when the formation is broken. The specific mechanic is inter-unit spacing as a soft constraint in the movement solve, not a hard formation lock. Sins of a Solar Empire fleet AI similarly spaces ships to avoid overlap and present distributed firing arcs.
Build cost. Small-medium. One new additive term in the scorer, a distance check against already-moved allies (the activation order gives us this), and a faction-profile knob. No structural change.
Sim proof. Clustering metric on big maps should drop; coverage should rise. The critical guard rail from the sim evidence: player win rate must not rise beyond the +2-per-24 bar. If the repulsion weight causes the AI to spread so much it loses focus-fire efficiency, the weight is too high. Run the paired retest at three weight values (-1, -2, -3) and pick the one that improves coverage without crossing the win-rate bar.
Proposal 6 - Focus-fire escalation on veteran difficulty
What changes. The existing sticky focus (veteran only) is extended: when two or more veteran ships can fire on the same target in the same round, they get a small bonus to scoring hexes that allow firing on that shared target. This is not a new targeting rule; it is a scoring nudge that makes converged fire slightly more attractive than spread fire. At normal difficulty, the nudge is zero. At veteran, it scales with the number of ships already focused.
Game reference. FTL's enemy ships concentrate fire on one system (shields, then weapons, then engines) rather than spraying damage across the hull. The mechanic is convergent target selection as a scoring bonus, not a hard override. XCOM 2's Advent officers call out focus targets, and the AI visibly funnels fire onto the marked unit.
Build cost. Small-medium. The sticky focus already exists; this adds a "how many allies are already scoring this target" lookup to the hex scorer. The data (ally positions, targets) is available during the activation sequence.
Sim proof. On veteran-difficulty sim battles, the mean number of distinct targets hit per AI round should drop (fire is concentrating), time-to-kill on the player's first ship lost should decrease, and player win rate at veteran should drop by 1-3 games per 24 (the AI is sharper, as intended for veteran). Normal-difficulty numbers must be unchanged.
Proposal 7 - Meaningful repositioning via defence-bonus awareness tuning
What changes. The sim shows the scorer already sees the 3+-hex movement defence bonus per candidate hex, but the magnitude is too small to motivate large moves. This proposal increases the weight of the defence bonus in the scorer for ships that are hurt (hull < 60%) or outgunned (offense < threat), making a 4-5 hex reposition genuinely attractive. Healthy, offensive ships are unaffected. This is not two-pass movement (which the sim showed costs damage); it is a single-pass weight adjustment that makes the existing defence bonus matter more in specific situations.
Game reference. Darkest Dungeon's AI repositions its back-line enemies when they take damage, pulling them out of melee range. The mechanic is damage-state-dependent movement weighting: hurt entities value distance more, healthy entities value aggression. BattleTech (HBS) similarly has its AI prefer repositioning to brace when a mech is critically damaged.
Build cost. Small. One multiplier on an existing term, gated on hull/offense conditions.
Sim proof. Mean movement distance for ships below 60% hull should rise from the 2.3-2.8 hex band toward 3.5-4.5. Player win rate at normal should hold (hurt ships repositioning is fair, not oppressive). Veteran survivors should rise slightly (hurt ships survive longer by using the defence bonus the game already provides). Battle length must not increase beyond +0.5 rounds.
3. Recommended First Three, In Order
| Order | Proposal | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Proposal 2 - Approach leash fix (AI-4) | It is the smallest build (small, one conditional), it fixes a finding that is trivially visible to every player, and it has zero risk of making the AI harder or changing battle shape. It is a pure bug-removal. Shipping this first builds trust in the council's process and clears a defect before any scoring work begins. |
| 2nd | Proposal 1 - Unified self-preservation (AI-1 + AI-3 + brace consolidation) | This is the next-smallest build that touches the most findings. AI-1 is tagged BLOCKER. The three-place brace is a maintenance hazard that will break again the next time anyone touches aiUnit. Fixing this before Proposal 4 or 5 matters because the objective-aware scorer and the repulsion term both add new scoring paths that must route through a single, correct survival gate. If the brace is still fragmented when those terms land, we will get new bypass bugs. |
| 3rd | Proposal 3 - Carrier launch timing (AI-5) | This is the last of the four named defects and it is self-contained: it touches the ability step and the launch-hex sort, not the main scorer. Doing it third means the scorer work in Proposals 4-7 starts from a clean baseline where all four findings pass. It also has a clear, narrow sim proof (wing survival rate) that doesn't interfere with the broader coverage/clustering metrics needed for Proposals 4 and 5. |
Why the order matters. Proposals 4-7 all modify the movement scorer. If we do them before consolidating the brace logic (Proposal 1), every new scoring term is another path that can bypass a brace check. If we do them before fixing the leash (Proposal 2), the leash will fight the new terms (e.g. the repulsion pulls a ship away, the leash pulls it back). And if the carrier still launches blind into threat (Proposal 3), objective-aware scoring will try to protect a wing that was deployed into a hex the carrier never intended to defend. Clear the defects first; build new behaviour on clean ground.
4. What Would Make the AI Feel Worse
These are the traps to flag now, before anyone proposes them in a follow-up:
- Multiplicative scoring for the AI seat. The sim already tested this: "the player win rate against a multiplicative AI rose from 5/24 to 13/24 on identical seeds." It spreads fleets but makes the AI fight noticeably worse. A player would feel the AI got dumber, not more interesting. Do not ship this. If spatial spread is the goal, use the soft repulsion in Proposal 5, which preserves additive scoring.
- Two-pass movement (move-fire-finish-move). Tested: "buys 20-23% more displacement and pays for it in damage and wins… failed the 'no damage loss' acceptance bar." It would also make the AI harder to read: the player can't predict where a ship will end up because it fires mid-path and then adjusts. Unreadable beats clever.
- Over-cautious death-cliff scaling. Proposal 1 scales the cliff to cp cost, but if the multiplier is too aggressive, capital ships will kite forever and never commit. The player experiences this as a stall: the AI won't engage, battles drag past 12 rounds, and the game feels like it's refusing to fight. The cap must be tuned so a cp5 capital still accepts a favourable trade; it just stops accepting a suicidal one.
- Perfect or near-perfect focus fire at normal difficulty. Proposal 6 gates the focus nudge to veteran. If it leaks into normal, the AI will delete one player ship per round with mechanical precision, and the player will feel punished for fielding a fleet rather than rewarded for positioning it. Readable and fair beats optimal: at normal, the AI should still occasionally split fire in ways the player can exploit.
- Objective scoring without a combat floor. If the objective term in Proposal 4 is weighted too high, the AI will sail past the player's fleet to sit on a well, and the player will feel like the AI is ignoring them. The combat term must remain dominant; the objective term is a tiebreaker and a nudge, not an override.
- Carrier launch delays that feel like the AI is doing nothing. Proposal 3 lets the carrier delay a launch if no safe hex exists. If the delay condition is too conservative, the carrier sits for three rounds with a full hangar and the player wonders why the AI isn't using its units. The delay cap should be one round maximum; after that, the carrier launches from the best available hex even if it's not ideal.
- Veteran focus-fire escalation without a tell. If veteran AI starts converging fire without any visible signal (a target marker, a brief pause, a formation shift), the player will experience it as "my ship just melted for no reason." The focus should be legible: the ships converge spatially before they converge their fire, giving the player one round to react. XCOM 2's Advent officers mark targets visibly; the AI should be at least that readable.
This leg supports Proposals 1-7 as scoped. The first three are defect clearance; the rest are behaviour additions that should enter the sim harness only after the first three are green at the findings pin. No proposal here changes the activation structure (abilities -> move -> volley -> orders); Proposal 3 adjusts what the ability step consults, not when it runs. If the council later finds that the ability-before-movement ordering is itself the binding constraint on carrier play, that is a structural conversation for a separate issue.
