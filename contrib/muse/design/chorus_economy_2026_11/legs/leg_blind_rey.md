# Blind leg: Rey (written before any council leg, from the evidence pack only)

## Approach 1: The Brood Stop (shipyards become gestation reefs)

How it works: for a Chorus run, shipyard nodes are Brood Reefs. The fleet does not spend credits there; it spends biomass, a pool earned from battle salvage, wreck nodes and prizes (credits the run would have earned convert to biomass at the source, so reward tables do not change). At a reef the player picks per visit: regrow the fleet (heal all ships, cost scales with missing hull, priced like repairs today), gestate a new hull (the yard's stock of 3 becomes 3 gestatable forms at biomass prices mirroring the credit ladder, so Chorus stays cheap-small and dear-capital), or pupate a Mark (a ship grows a stage: biomass instead of credits, same +20% hull / +15% damage, no shield component since Chorus has none). Drydocks become Deep Reefs (free full regrowth, same rarity), field nodes become Feeding Grounds (free 20% regrow, unchanged numbers).

Other games: the Zerg hatchery is the template: production and healing live in the organism, larvae are the pacing resource. Stellaris hive minds grow fleets from food at what are still shipyards in UI terms, which teaches the cheap version of this idea: the screen can stay a shipyard screen while the fiction and currency change.

What the player decides: the same decisions as a yard visit today (heal vs grow vs upgrade vs save), which is the point: the run structure survives intact. The new decision is biomass budgeting across the whole run, since one pool pays for healing and growth both.

What it breaks: least of any approach here, but also changes least; if biomass is just credits renamed, the asymmetry test from the decision page (does the faction actually play differently?) may fail. Node parity is safe. Captured hulls at Cinder yards need a Chorus answer (eat them for biomass at a good rate is the flavourful one, and it removes the captured-hull fantasy for Chorus).

Build size: medium. Node behaviour swap, one resource rename at the reward source, shipyard screen relabelled with three verbs. No battle-layer changes.

## Approach 2: The Fleet Is Its Own Shipyard (no repair stops at all)

How it works: Chorus ships regrow between battles the way they already do inside them. Each jump, every ship heals a percentage of its missing hull (a campaign echo of its regen stat: drone-spawn mends fastest, the Maw slowest in proportion). There is no repair purchase at any price. Replacement and growth come from budding: at any node, a ship at high health can bud a smaller hull of the class below (a brood-sac buds drones freely but slowly; any capital can bud once per sector), paying a chunk of the parent's current hull. Marks become growth: a ship that survives a battle well-fed (fleet won without losing a ship, or it personally killed) gains growth toward its next Mark instead of buying it.

Other games: Slay the Spire is the lesson: healing tied to progress rather than purchase makes every fight's damage permanent-ish and route choice becomes the healing decision. Zerg regeneration across a match is the fiction. FTL shows the pressure version: when repair is scarce, hull is the run's real health bar.

What the player decides: routing becomes sustainment (longer routes heal more but the Nemesis clock and sector escalation punish dawdling, if movement is free enough to dawdle), when to bud (a budded parent enters the next fight wounded), and whether to protect a nearly-grown veteran or spend it.

What it breaks: the yard decision layer disappears for Chorus, which is a real loss of texture; if the map allows free backtracking, per-jump healing is farmable and the whole thing collapses (needs the map's existing movement constraints to be real); a fleet crippled early can death-spiral with no purchasable recovery before the boss. Budding math needs care or the brood-sac becomes a drone printer that ignores the CP cap's intent (CP still caps total size, but free CP-efficient hulls distort the price ladder's cheap-small shape into free-small).

Build size: small to medium. A jump hook, a bud action, a growth counter. No new nodes, no new currency. The balancing, not the building, is the cost.

## Approach 3: Eat the Wrecks (biomass earned only by destruction)

How it works: Chorus runs do not earn credits. Kills, wreck nodes, scrapped prizes and consumed refits yield biomass (Gorge, generalised from an admiral power into the economy itself: anything in the hold can be eaten). Biomass is spent anywhere between battles, not just at yards: heal any ship at a biomass rate mirroring today's repair price, grow a new hull from the run's unlocked roster at yard-equivalent prices when at a shipyard node (a reef in fiction), or feed a ship a Mark. The floor: lore says the Chorus lives on starlight, so the fleet also gains a small biomass trickle per jump, enough to mend a frigate over a few jumps, never enough to fund a capital. A starving fleet must fight to heal, which is the identity: aggression is the sustainment loop.

Other games: the Cravers in Endless Space teach both halves: an economy that must keep consuming is thrilling, and an economy that can stall completely is miserable, hence the starlight floor. FTL's scrap is the proof that a single currency for repair, growth and upgrades creates constant real trade-offs. The asymmetry decision page's feed mechanic is this idea's in-battle cousin (kills restore hull via the avenge logic); this is its campaign form.

What the player decides: eat the refit or keep it (Gorge already asks this at small scale), scrap a captured hull for a feast or field it, route toward fights when hungry versus toward safety when hurt, and spend the trickle on the frigate now or bank it toward a razor.

What it breaks: run variance widens in both directions (a winning fleet feasts and snowballs; a losing fleet starves, the classic death spiral, only partly capped by the trickle). Event and exploration rewards priced in credits need a Chorus conversion table or Chorus runs get poorer from non-combat routes. Boss fights after a lean sector are the danger case. It also touches the reward layer, not just the yard.

Build size: medium. Currency conversion at reward sources, a spend-anywhere healing/growth UI (the shipyard screen's actions unbound from the node, with hull growth still node-gated), wreck yields. No battle-layer changes except reading the kill ledger the game already keeps.

## Approach 4: Metamorphosis (nothing is bought; ships are grown through stages)

How it works: Chorus has no hull purchases at all. The fleet grows by pupation: each hull has a growth track fed by biomass or battle experience; a fed drone-spawn can pupate into a spitter, a spitter into a bladeback, a bladeback toward a brood-sac. Pupation restores the ship to full hull in its new form (healing and growth are the same act), and Marks fold into the track as growth stages within a form. New frigates enter only by budding from a brood-sac or the flagship between battles, costing the parent hull. Losing a grown bladeback is losing the whole track invested in it; an egg reserve (one unhatched frigate slot earned per sector) softens total losses.

Other games: Zerg morphing (a cheap unit becomes a different unit, never a refund) and any evolution-line game teach the core pleasure: the fleet's shape is a history of what survived and what you fed. Endless Space 2's Vodyani show a faction whose growth unit (essence) is also its expansion gate.

What the player decides: feeding priorities (one great bladeback or three fed spitters), pupation timing (a veteran maxed drone traded for a green cruiser), and how much of the fleet's future to risk in each fight, since replacement is slow and earned, never bought.

What it breaks: the class unlock structure (carrier and capital unlocks) does not map onto growth tracks without redesign; the price ladder work for Chorus becomes mostly moot; run recovery after a bad fight is the slowest of any approach here; and it is the only approach that needs a new progression system rather than a new economy. It also touches battle-adjacent systems if brood-sac battle spawns are ever allowed to persist (flagged as a touch, not proposed here).

Build size: large. New progression track, new UI, rebalanced roster entry. Highest ceiling, highest cost.

## Recommendation

Approach 3 as the spine, with Approach 1's reef as its spending place for hulls and Marks, and the starlight trickle as the floor. It is the only approach that gives Chorus an economy no other faction shares (the asymmetry audits' own test of whether a faction is real), it reuses pieces the game already has (Gorge, the spawning-field feed, the avenge ledger, wreck nodes), and it keeps every shipyard decision while changing what the decisions are made of. Approach 2 is the fallback if the Board wants the smallest build; Approach 4 is the best long-term fantasy and the wrong next step.

## Biggest risk across all approaches

Variance. Every eat-to-grow loop amplifies the run's existing luck: fleets that win get healthier and bigger, fleets that bleed starve. The tuning knobs that decide whether Chorus feels distinct or merely swingy are the floor (starlight trickle size), the heal price relative to kill income, and one bad-sector recovery path. Any adopted version should be judged on the distribution of run outcomes, not the average run.
