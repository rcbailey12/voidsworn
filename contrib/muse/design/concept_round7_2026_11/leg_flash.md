1. Glasswrights Craft Additions (G14, G15, G16)

Route G14: The Instrument

Named real craft or object to borrow from: 16th-century brass planispheric astrolabe.

Specific detail to borrow: Interlocking pierced rete rings and graduated limb markings engraved into the silver framework that seat and calibrate the optics.

Detail to avoid: Steampunk brass gear trains, exposed clockwork cogs, and loose dangling pointer needles.

Route G15: Leaded Glass

Named real craft or object to borrow from: Hector Guimard Art Nouveau cast iron and leaded art glass canopies.

Specific detail to borrow: Organic whiplash cames (silver structural framing lines) that branch across the hull to cradle each weapon lens within the structural load lines.

Detail to avoid: Repetitive wire-mesh honeycomb tiling or ecclesiastical church window medallions with figurative imagery.

Route G16: The Lighthouse

Named real craft or object to borrow from: First-order hyperradiant Fresnel lighthouse lens.

Specific detail to borrow: Stepped concentric annular prisms ground directly into the transparent hull plates to collimate and direct internal luminescence toward the forward bow lenses.

Detail to avoid: External rotating mirror cages, beacon searchlight towers, or spherical glass bulb enclosures.

2. Hullbreakers Verifiable Checklist and Drift Risk

Editor Verification Checklist (Against C19_51.jpg)

Mast and Rigging Replacement: Confirm that the tall wire mast, thin crossbars, and rigging atop the red oxide pyramid are entirely removed and replaced by a low, squat cluster of sensor arrays and antennas.

Bow Post Removal: Confirm that the vertical jackstaff post at the forward tip of the nose is absent, leaving the leading edge clean of vertical poles.

Ram Face and Underside Conversion: Confirm that the pointed ship prow, surface foredeck, and rounded V-keel boat hull are replaced by a blunt, angular armoured ram face assembled from heavy bolted plates, and the lower underside forms a flat, slabbed belly fitted with recessed thruster pods.

Mass and Asymmetry Preservation: Confirm that the central red oxide stepped pyramid, the heavy dark armour slabs along the starboard flank, the patched grey riveted plate, the top rocket batteries, and the glowing stern engines match the silhouette and volume of C19_51.jpg without slimming or stretching.

Most Expected Drift

The image generation model will attempt to streamline the hull into a symmetrical, tapered gunmetal bar or naval cruiser, losing the lopsided red-oxide pyramid mass and replacing the welded slab ram with an aerodynamic or naval wedge.

3. Hegemony Render Checklist and Ranked Cartoon Tells

Untouchable Design Features (Keep from H9)

Geometry: Flat, heavy slab wedge chassis supported on twin parallel propulsion rails.

Recesses: Central rectangular hangar bay located between the rails.

Shoulder Mounts: Sunk weapon emplacements on both shoulders sporting twin short, thick barrels.

Centerline Features: Recessed auxiliary mounts and sensor arrays along the central spine.

Bow: Flat, blunt nose profile incorporating the recessed horizontal emitter trench.

Render Attributes to Change (Style Pass)

Hull Value and Material: Replace the pale, chalky off-white with dark navy grey battle plate displaying panel tone variation, brushed grain, and edge friction wear.

Surface Density: Introduce dense micro-scale surface greebling, including maintenance hatches, pressure vents, structural seams, and stencil hull numbers, to scale the ship to capital size.

Illumination: Subdue and narrow the thick neon glow strips to hairline seam light and pinpoint formation beacons, concentrating intense light strictly inside the rear thrusters and the bow emitter trench.

Lighting and Contrast: Replace ambient flat shading with harsh directional sun lighting casting deep, hard shadows across hull facets.

Ranked Cartoon Tells in H9 (Worst to Least)

Uniform thick cyan neon edge piping outlining hull segments like a glowing arcade model.

Flat, diffuse ambient fill with zero deep shadow casting across plate steps.

Pale, unweathered plastic white surface finish lacking panel tonal variety or grit.

Oversized, sparse hull features (such as giant rectangular deck vents) that destroy visual scale and make the ship look twenty meters long instead of a kilometer.
