# Concept round 7: edits, not redraws (issue 500)

This round works the way FINDINGS R192 ruled: liked pictures get edited image-to-image, never redescribed and redrawn. Three bases, seven variations, thirteen of the fourteen pictures delivered. The missing one is marked where it belongs, under A14.

Council: blind Rey leg (Glasswrights only, written before the prompts), DeepSeek and Qwen writer legs, Flash and Grok ideation legs, Rey chairs. The chaired prompts are Qwen's set with five chair amendments, all recorded in prompts.md; DeepSeek's set is filed as the alternative.

## Hullbreakers: the original concept, its sea parts changed

Reference: docs/ship_pipeline/concepts/C19_51.webp, the concept from before all the rounds.

### corsair_A14, the lightest touch

Side by side with the original: the same hulking, lopsided ship, the huge sloped stepped pyramid of red oxide plate, the black armour slab stack down one flank, the rocket tubes, drums and tanks, the big riveted grey patch, the painted hull lettering, every dent kept. The differences take a moment to spot, which was the test:

- The mast and rigging on top of the pyramid are now a squat sensor and antenna cluster (dishes and boxes, no tall pole).
- The small post at the bow tip is gone.
- The front is a blunt armoured ram face of the same patched slabs, and the underside is a flat armoured belly with three thruster pods, not a boat hull.
- The background is near black instead of the original's white field, a presentational constant of the project, not a design change.

Gate score (beauty): line 1 partial by design (A14 inherits the original's weapon layout as drawn; the brief forbids weapon changes here, and A15 is the variation that makes the count exact). Line 2 pass. Line 3 pass (engines under the stern, one axis). Line 4 pass. Line 5 pass (one sensor cluster, low). Line 6 n/a (no hangar change permitted). Line 7 pass (lopsided plating permitted, engines and guns balanced). Line 8 pass. Line 9 pass (the mass is the original's). Line 10 pass. Line 11 naval pass partial: no mast, no prow, no boat section, worked flat belly; two thin cables still hang on the pyramid face as rigging remnants. Line 12 pass (it is the faction's own original sheet).

Sheet: NOT DELIVERED. The generation service refused the sheet request for this beauty and the request was not retried in altered form. The Board can derive the sheet from the delivered beauty in a later job.

### corsair_A15, the same ship with the game's own weapons

Built from the accepted A14, so the sea-part edits are inherited, not re-rolled. The counted set is all present and correctly placed: two rocket launcher batteries on top, one on each top edge angled out to its own flank (scatter P, scatter S); heavy torpedo tube mouths flush and low in the ram face (htorp F); one small flak mount on the top centre line (flak T); two hangar doors on the flank (hangar 2).

Gate score (beauty): line 1 PARTIAL, the one real failure of the Hullbreaker pair: two extra rocket racks survive on the foredeck pointing forward and one more at the stern edge, so gun-like objects exceed the count even though the counted set is right. The torpedo mouths also render as clusters of small round holes rather than a few heavy tubes, and the flank openings read as three (one is the old patch recess). Lines 2 to 5 pass. Line 6 pass (the doors read as doors). Line 7 pass. Line 8 pass. Line 9 pass. Line 10 pass. Line 11 naval pass: pass on the ship itself (flat belly, ram face, nothing hanging); the stern rack sits at the deck edge but does not hang. Line 12 pass.
Sheet: line 1 partial (the beauty's extra racks multiply in plan); line 9 partial (the plan stretches the ship to about 4.5:1 against the beauty's massing); the engines are drawn thrusting. Everything else carries.

## Glasswrights: G13 made by artists

Reference: contrib/codex/design/concept_round6_2026_11/ithar_G13_beauty.jpg. Ryan: "The base is right." All three routes keep the base exactly: the long leaf of clear cut glass with soft white stored light running its length, the silver spine and rim, the stern engine group as drawn. In all three, the three skylight domes and the two tiny stock turrets are gone. The weapons are the same counted set in each (i_boss, The Unfinished, an Ark): pulsar F, pulsar F, pulsar T, turret T, flak T, hangar none.

What each weapon fires at, in all three routes: the two bow pulsars fire forward at whatever the Ark faces, from the bow shoulders like the objective lenses of a great instrument; the top pulsar swings on its mount to aim at targets above and ahead; the small turret covers the top centre line's field; the flak rank is point defence against small craft and torpedoes coming in along the hull.

### ithar_G14, the instrument

Crafts drawn on: the astrolabe and the orrery (engraved silver rings and arcs as the ship's frame, graduated scales along the spine, the rim and round each lens cell), the great telescope (objective cells with focusing rings), and the master watch movement (the mounts finished like its bridges, beveled and jewelled at the joints). The concentric graduated circle round the top mount is the single clearest piece of instrument work in the round.

Gate score (beauty, try 3 of 3): line 1 pass (both bow lenses in the shoulders, bores forward, each one large deliberate installation; flak as one graduated rank). Line 2 PARTIAL: the top pulsar and the small spine gun are drawn swung toward the stern in this view; both are turning mounts, and the fixed forward battery, which is what Ryan's "the guns don't make sense" was about, points forward. Lines 3 to 10 pass (one spine, engines filling the stern face, escort a smaller ship of the same grammar with fewer parts, never the capital shrunk). Line 11 naval pass: pass (same above and below, no deck, no boat section). Line 12 pass.
Sheet: pass across the gate; the cleanest Glasswright sheet of the round.
Failed tries: try 1 put the top pulsar and a second large barrel pointing at the stern and let the warm metal overrun the silver; try 2 fixed the metal and still placed the big lenses at the stern end. Try 3 stated the orientation from the picture itself (engines at the left, the point is the bow) and landed. All three tries are recorded in prompts.md.

### ithar_G15, leaded glass

Crafts drawn on: Art Nouveau leaded glass in the manner of Guimard and Lalique (flowing silver cames as structural ribs, sweeping bow to stern and gathering round each lens cell), and cathedral glass (a few panes coloured in the glass itself: deep green, amber, violet). The honeycomb tiling of G13 is gone; pane sizes follow the hull's stresses, small at the bow shoulders, long amidships.

Gate score (beauty, try 1): line 1 pass. Line 2 PARTIAL (one small spine gun aft of the top mount points toward the stern; the bow battery, top pulsar and flak all read correctly). Lines 3 to 10 pass. Line 11 naval pass: pass. Line 12 pass. Marked detail: the stored light bands read a little regular and stripey through the panes.
Sheet: line 1 PARTIAL (the two bow lenses do not read as lenses in either view; bell-like forms at the bow in profile, the centre mount carries the weapon read). The tracery and colour carry into both views; everything else passes.

### ithar_G16, the lighthouse

Crafts drawn on: the Fresnel lighthouse lens (the glass ground in concentric and radiating facets so the stored light is gathered and thrown, brightest toward the bow lenses it feeds) and silver tracery work (the stern engine group is ringed and faceted like a lens's own rings). Route note: the blind leg proposed a variant with the drive rings set in a rose of tracery at the stern; the chaired prompt folded that detail into this route rather than swapping the route out, because the blind leg's three routes converged with the issue's three independently. The rose is not separately distinct in the result; the faceted rings carry the idea.

Gate score (beauty, try 1): line 1 pass (both bow lenses in worked ring mounts pointing forward and visibly fed by the gathered light; the top pulsar is a proper turning turret with a lens eye; the flak is a rank of five small globes). Lines 2 to 10 pass. Line 11 naval pass: pass. Line 12 pass. Marked detail: the faceting is uniform across the hull rather than visibly deepening toward the bow.
Sheet: pass across the gate; the facets radiate in plan from the spine and both bow lenses sit in their ring mounts in plan.

## Hegemony: H9 in a realistic style

Reference: contrib/codex/design/concept_round6_2026_11/hegemony_H9_beauty.jpg. The design is not changed in either variation: thick slab wedge on two thick rails, hangar between the rails, low gun mounts sunk into the shoulders with short thick barrels, blunt bow with a recessed blue emitter trench, mounts as drawn in H9 (h_dominion: heavy turbolaser P, heavy turbolaser S, ion cannon F, flak T, hangar 1). Only the render changes, toward the realism level of C19_51.webp and the Sunward S8 beauty.

### hegemony_H10, realistic and restrained

What changed: the pale clean even grey is now a darker, cooler navy grey with real material (brushed and painted plate, edge wear, streaking, panel variation); many more small features at one small scale (hatches, vents, pipe runs, bolt rows, painted hull numbers) so the ship reads as a kilometre of warship; the thick bright blue edge light is cut to thin dim seam lines and small running lights, bright only at the engines and the bow emitter; one hard directional light with real shadow does the describing. The cartoon tells are gone, in the ideation legs' ranked order: the neon piping first, then the flat even grey, then the shadowless light, then the too-few features.

Gate score (beauty, try 1): pass on every line the variation touches. It is the same ship; lines 1 to 9 and 12 are inherited from H9 and unchanged; line 11 naval pass holds (no deck house, no prow, worked underside between the rails). Marked detail: the bow emitter reads as one bright panel, which is the emitter trench as H9 designed it.
Sheet: pass; the wedge on twin rails is unmistakable in plan. The side view keeps a slight top bias.

### hegemony_H11, realistic and heavier

Built from the accepted H10, pushed further in the same direction: darker still, near black on the shadow side, more armour belts and surface structure inside the same outline, the blue lines almost gone except the engines and the bow emitter.

Gate score (beauty, try 1): pass, same terms as H10. Marked detail: the engine treatment changed at render level from lit panels to dark blocks with blue plumes; the engines and emitter remain the only bright blue, per the brief.
Sheet: pass, as H10's sheet, darker.
