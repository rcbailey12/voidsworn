Grok ideation leg, Voidsworn issue 500, concepts round 7

(1) Glasswrights craft additions per route

G14 instrument

Borrow: the graduated chapter ring and vernier scale from a 16th-century astrolabe, applied as a continuous engraved band along the silver spine that also serves as the structural ruler.

Avoid: any free-floating decorative rings or arcs that do not carry load or motion; they read as jewellery, not mechanism.

G15 leaded glass

Borrow: the flowing came lines and varying pane sizes of Art Nouveau leaded glass (Guimard or Lalique window work), where the silver tracery sweeps continuously from bow shoulders to stern and gathers around each lens mount.

Avoid: regular hexagonal or honeycomb tiling of the glass skin; it reads insectile and flat, not hand-laid.

G16 lighthouse

Borrow: the concentric prismatic rings of a Fresnel lighthouse lens, ground into the hull glass so the internal stored light is visibly gathered and directed toward the two forward pulsar lenses.

Avoid: a full radial facet wrap that turns the entire hull into a glowing lamp; keep the facets quiet enough that the ship still reads as a long leaf with a clear thrust axis.

(2) Hullbreakers checklist and expected drift

Verifiable checklist for the three permitted edits on C19_51:

Mast and rigging on the pyramid top are gone; in their place sits only a low, squat sensor and antenna cluster of the same dark metal family, no tall thin pole remaining.
The small vertical post at the tip of the bow is completely removed.
The front is now a blunt armoured ram face built from the same patched red and grey slabs; the underside is a flat armoured belly carrying a few thruster pods, with no boat-hull curve or foredeck step.

Most expected drift: the image model will smooth the lopsided stepped pyramid and the stacked black armour slabs into a more symmetrical long bar or wedge, losing the hulking, rebuilt-from-scrap mass that is the entire identity of the original.

(3) Hegemony render checklist

Design (untouchable, keep exactly as drawn in H9):

thick slab wedge body on two thick parallel rails; hangar bay between the rails; low gun mounts sunk into the shoulders with short thick barrels; blunt bow with the recessed blue emitter trench; overall proportions and silhouette.

Render (to change):

replace the pale even grey with darker cooler navy grey showing brushed and painted armour plate, edge wear, streaking and panel variation; multiply the surface detail at one consistent small scale (hatches, vents, lights, hull numbers) so the ship reads as a kilometre-class vessel; reduce the blue light to thin dim edge lines and small running lights, bright only at the engines and the bow emitter; apply hard directional light with real cast shadows.

Cartoon tells ranked by how strongly they make it read as a toy:

1. thick bright blue neon outlining every edge
2. pale clean even grey with almost no material variation
3. flat shadowless lighting
4. few large simplified surface features that shrink the perceived scale
