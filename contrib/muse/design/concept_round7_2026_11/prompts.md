# #500 prompts as run (chaired)

Chair base: Qwen's writer leg, adopted nearly whole (it carried the background-swap catch, the A15 conversion rule and the escort rule). DeepSeek's leg is the filed alternative; where the two differed the chair took Qwen's wording except as noted below.

Chair amendments, applied to every affected prompt and recorded here:
1. Sheets are hero only: top view above side view, both flat on at the same scale, plain dark grey background, evenly lit, no text. Qwen's Glasswright and Hegemony sheet prompts added the escort in two more views at a shared scale; the issue's sheet spec and the standing sheet standard (issue 398) are hero only, so the escort stays in the beauties only.
2. A15 is generated from the accepted A14 beauty, not from the original (Qwen's own chair note offered both; the chair takes the chained version, so the sea-part edits are inherited, not re-rolled).
3. H11 is generated from the accepted H10 beauty (DeepSeek's chaining), with Qwen's H11 text naming H10 as its reference, so the heavier pass cannot drift the design a second time.
4. G16 keeps the issue's lighthouse route. The blind leg's one distinct element, the stern drive rings set in a rose of silver tracery, was already folded into Qwen's G16 text and is kept; no route swap was needed, because the blind leg's three routes converged with the issue's three (instrument, leaded glass, lighthouse) independently.
5. In the Glasswright prompts, "three white drive rings" follows the issue's words for the G13 base; the reference picture draws the stern engine group as three cylinders. The prompts keep the base "as drawn", so the wording cannot invent rings the picture does not have.

Subject: the same hulking, lopsided, scrap-built warship, kept exactly in mass, silhouette and character. Make exactly three edits and no others. Edit two: delete the small vertical post at the tip of the bow, leaving clean plate. Edit three: rebuild the front as a blunt, armoured ram face built from the same patched and riveted slabs, and replace the boat-like underside with a flat armoured belly carrying two or three squat thruster pods, so that no boat hull section remains anywhere on the ship. Also lift away any chain, cable or cage lying on or hanging off the hull, and remove every green tint from the lower plate, repainting it dark grey to black. Change nothing else. Keep the huge sloped stepped pyramid of red oxide plate rising amidships, the stack of heavy black armour slabs stepped down one flank, the rocket tube block, drums and tanks on top, the big riveted grey patch plate on the side, the low armoured wedge of the front, the orange engine glow under the stern, the painted hull lettering, and every scratch, dent and soot mark. Materials: worn red oxide painted plate, black armour slabs, bare grey patched steel, rust streaks, soot. Lighting: the same soft even key light as the reference, with the orange engine glow as the only strong coloured light. Background: plain near-black empty space, replacing the reference's plain white field; this background swap is a presentational constant of the round, not a design change. Framing: the same three-quarter view from the front and above as the reference, ship at the same size and angle in frame. No weapon is added, removed or re-aimed in this variation, and nothing aims backward. No part hangs off the hull. ## G14 beauty (from ithar_G13_beauty.jpg)

Image-to-image edit of the attached reference picture ithar_G13_beauty.jpg, the Glasswright Ark base from Codex round 6. Subject: the same long leaf of clear cut glass lit from inside by soft white stored light running its length, with its silver spine, its silver rim, its stern engine group as drawn, and its smaller escort leaf below. Keep that base exactly: same leaf silhouette, same bow tip and stern, same spine line, same rim, same stern engines, same stored light, same camera angle and same placement of capital and escort. Change only the craft and the weapons. Weapons: replace the two forward glass domes with two large objective lenses set into the shoulders of the bow, one each side of the centre line, their bores plainly on the ship's forward axis so a viewer says at once that they fire forward, each lens in a worked polished silver cell with engraved focusing rings like the objective cell of a great telescope. Replace the mid dome with one lens in a turning mount on the spine, held in two or three engraved silver rings set at angles so it plainly swings and tilts to aim. Replace the first tiny generic turret with a small lens gun in a low worked silver mount of the same family on the top centre line with a clear field. Replace the second tiny generic turret with the flak, a rank of four to six small lenses and bells in graduated sizes along the spine, one tuned family, obviously point defence. Craft, the instrument route: the frame becomes astrolabe and orrery work, engraved silver rings and arcs that are the ship's structure rather than hoops stuck on it, with graduated scales engraved along the spine and around each lens cell, and the lens mounts finished as the finest part of the ship, like a master watch movement's bridges with beveled edges. Replace the honeycomb pane tiling with smooth clear panes sized to the hull, small near the bow shoulders and long amidships, held in the ring and arc frame. Materials: polished silver as the main metal, with a warm dark bronze accent only at moving joints and mount rings; clear glass carrying soft white stored light, colour in the glass only where a craft would put it. Lighting: soft key from above plus the ship's own stored light glowing through the panes. Background: plain near-black empty space, as in the reference. Framing: the same three-quarter view from the front and above as the reference, capital as hero with the escort at the same relative size and place. The escort receives the same instrument treatment in miniature with its fewer parts, one top lens in a ring mount, one small mount, its own smaller stern engines, never the capital's full mount set and never the capital shrunk. A viewer must be able to say what each mount fires at. Ornament stays structural and restrained: ornament a navy would be proud of, not a jewellery box, and never a crystal block, prism bar, wings, cloth sails, petals or anything insect-like. Still a warship and a space ship: one spine, a clear bow and stern, real engines, the same shape above and below, no boat section, no deck.

## Retries as run (chair amendments at generation time)

### G14 beauty try 2 (rejected: the big lenses rendered at the stern, pointing aft)
As the chaired G14 prompt, plus these sentences in place of the plain weapon directions: "shown aimed forward over the bow" for the top pulsar, and the added guards "No other barrel or tube anywhere on the ship, and no barrel of any size points toward the stern: every weapon aims forward, up and forward, or outward" and "a warm bronze accent appears only on thin rings and moving joints, never on whole barrels or hull parts". Try 2 fixed the metal and kept the craft but still placed the two large lenses at the stern end.

### G14 beauty try 3 (accepted)
The prompt was rewritten to state the orientation from the picture itself: "Orientation, read from the picture: the three glowing engine rings are at the LEFT end, which is the stern; the long POINTED end at the RIGHT is the bow." The bow lenses are then specified as sitting "in the shoulders of the POINTED BOW at the right end" with "their round glass faces and muzzles pointing RIGHT, toward and past the pointed tip", with "No gun, barrel or lens of any size placed near the engines at the left end, and nothing anywhere points left toward the engines." Full text as run is in the generation record; the route content (astrolabe arcs, graduated scales, watch-movement mounts, flak rank, escort in miniature) is unchanged from the chaired prompt.

### A15 beauty try 2 (accepted)
As the chaired A15 prompt, restructured into numbered removals and placements, with the added sentences: "Remove every other rocket tube block, tube rack and tube cluster anywhere else on the ship, including on the foredeck and behind the pyramid, turning those spots into plain plate, drums or vents"; "No thin whip antennae anywhere; sensors are boxes and dishes only"; "Nothing is firing: no muzzle flash, no flame except the orange engine glow under the stern." The counted set landed; two foredeck racks and one stern rack still survived, so the variation is scored partial on line 1 (see README).

### A14 sheet (not generated)
The sheet request for the accepted A14 beauty was refused by the generation service and was not retried in altered form. The other six sheets ran on the common form above, each with its variation's specifics, from its accepted beauty.
