hegemony_H12

Risks in the brief:

The reference style piece, corsair_A.jpg, relies on scrap, visible wear, and salvage patches to sell physical reality. The primary brief risk is that the image model imports that scrap vocabulary directly onto hegemony_H12, compromising the discipline and orderly character specified for the faction.

Attempting to show scale without changing outline risks keeping the low-relief, printed-on panel graphics of H10, resulting in an illustrated ship with fine lines rather than a massive assembled structure with physical thickness.

Specific failure modes:

Hullbreaker drift: Applying mismatched plating, uneven patches, rust streaks, or haphazard greebles that belong to salvage raiders rather than an orderly fleet.

Scale collapse via oversized fittings: Adding access hatches, sensor blisters, or pipe clusters that are too large relative to the hull, causing the ship to read like a fighter or patrol craft rather than a flagship.

Printed detail without relief: Retaining flat, single-tone slab faces where panel edges lack stepped depth, fastener rows, shadow gaps, and edge highlights.

Geometry drift: Altering weapon mount counts, shifting engine nozzles, or reshaping block silhouettes during the pass instead of executing a pure surface rendering edit.

Guard checks and sentences for the chair:

Visual check: Confirm that every weapon mount from hegemony_H10 is present in the exact coordinate position, with zero additions or deletions, and that plate joints show highlight and shadow lines indicating true plate thickness.

Vocabulary check: Reject any image showing rust, jagged patches, irregular scrap welds, or mismatched plate alloys.

Guard sentence: "Render the hull as orderly, manufactured plate with stepped seams, visible fasteners, and service sheen, maintaining every block, engine, and weapon mount from hegemony_H10 without rust, patches, or hull modifications."

chorus_C10

Risks in the brief:

Translating corsair_A.jpg into biological form requires translating mechanical seams into multi-scale living anatomy. The brief risks prompting the model into horror tropes, adding decorative bio-greebles or monstrous features not present on C9.

Retaining the soft, painted feel of C9 by failing to apply the hard directional lighting and wet versus matte specular response.

Specific failure modes:

Horror drift: Sprouting extra mouths, asymmetrical fangs, eyes, or random tendril tangles that violate the original anatomy of chorus_C9.

Speckle-on-smooth failure: Applying a uniform speckled or noisy texture across broad, rubbery surfaces instead of multi-scale grain, anatomical folds, and tension lines.

Lighting and environment contamination: Retaining the soft ambient fill and painted nebula backdrop of C9 instead of placing the creature under one hard directional light in stark black space with faint stars.

Symbolic weapon organs: Treating the weapon organs as plain hollow tubes rather than working biological structures with muscular sphincters, internal ridges, and firing discharge wear.

Guard checks and sentences for the chair:

Visual check: Verify that the two glowing amber mouths at the bow, the exact count of hanging sacs, stern fins, and weapon organs precisely mirror chorus_C9, set against an unlit black space background.

Specular check: Confirm distinct specular highlights across damp membranes and taut hide, contrasting with matte bone and thick dermal calluses.

Guard sentence: "Render the living anatomy of chorus_C9 under a single harsh light against deep black space, giving hide multi-scale folds, tension grain, and wet specular highlights while preserving the exact silhouette, sac count, tendril cluster, and two bow mouths without extra eyes, mouths, or horror elements."

chorus_C11

Because chorus_C11 is a smaller companion class (bladeback cruiser) sharing the same anatomical vocabulary as C10, the brief risks the model homogenizing both ships into identical forms or scaling down C10 features instead of respecting C11's distinct anatomical silhouette.

The prominent dorsal bone ridge and twin throats risk being rendered as bolted-on mechanical armor or detached decorative pieces rather than organic skeletal tissue emerging through living flesh.

Specific failure modes:

Class conflation: Blurring the distinct outline of C8 so that its top-down plan view fails the side-by-side silhouette check against C10 in the README deliverable.

Mechanical bone transition: Rendering the pale dorsal bone ridge with hard, socketed seam lines resembling metal plating rather than bone breaking through transitional living tissue.

Loss of internal throat depth: Painting the two dark throats as flat black stickers or voids rather than recessed organic passages with internal muscular structure and rim tissue.

Flank sac symmetry error: Rendering the flank sacs as smooth, identical spheres rather than weighted, fluid-filled biological organs showing skin tension and anatomical growth marks.

Guard checks and sentences for the chair:

Visual check: Ensure the top-down outline matches chorus_C8 exactly, keeping the two dark throats, flank sacs, and dorsal bone ridge clearly distinguishable from C10's architecture.

Transition check: Confirm that the pale dorsal ridge emerges through gradual fleshy margins, exhibiting growth rings and matte bone density.

Guard sentence: "Depict chorus_C8 as a photographed living entity in black space, preserving its specific bladeback ridge, twin dark throats, and flank sacs with realistic tissue transitions and directional light, avoiding mechanical seams, decorative changes, or silhouette drift."
