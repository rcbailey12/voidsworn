hegemony_H12

Risks in the brief

The shape reference (hegemony_H10_beauty) already carries the correct mass, outline, mount count and orderly character, but its detail is drawn on smooth surfaces. The style pass must add real plate thickness, stepped edges, fasteners, hatches, pipes and sensor housings at door scale while preserving every block, mount, engine and light exactly where it sits. The Hegemony character is discipline and service wear only: panel-to-panel tone variation, polish and darkening at touch points, faint exhaust staining. Any slide toward scrap entropy or toward remaining showroom-smooth fails the faction and the style line.

Specific failure modes the transformation invites

Adding large, sparse detail (a few big hatches or pipe runs) instead of dense door-scale fittings, so the ship still reads small and drawn.
Introducing mismatched plates, rust, patches or random irregularity that belongs to the Hullbreakers.
Leaving large flat single-tone faces or printed-looking panel lines that catch no edge light.
Softening the single hard light or adding fill, so edges and joints lose the photographic shadow and specular behaviour.
Changing the background from plain black space with faint stars.
Slight drift in turret race rings, barrel proportions or engine clustering that the model treats as "improvement."

Guard sentences and checks for the chair

"Every block, mount, engine and light stays exactly where it is on the shape reference; only surface rendering changes."
"Detail must be numerous and small enough that the eye can find door-sized fittings across hull and turret faces; count them."
"Service sheen and staining only: polish and darkening at touch points, faint exhaust fans; never rust, never patches, never mismatched plates."
"One hard light, plain black space, few faint stars; reject any nebula or soft fill."
Gate check: does the result still read as the same orderly Hegemony flagship, only now built of real plate under hard light?

chorus_C10

Risks in the brief

The shape reference (chorus_C9_beauty) defines the body, the two glowing amber mouths at the bow, the tendril cluster, the hanging sacs, the stern fins and every weapon organ. The pass must turn that into a photograph of a city-scale living creature: multi-scale hide (folds, tension lines, creases, scars, fine pore texture), growth records in anatomically sensible places, wet/taut/matte specular behaviour, bone and tissue drawn small enough to convey size. The painted nebula background must become plain black space. The creature itself must not become prettier, softer or more monstrous.

Specific failure modes the transformation invites

Treating the hide as a smooth shaded volume with a uniform speckle overlay, so it still reads as a toy creature.
Adding extra mouths, spikes, tendrils or horror anatomy the shape reference does not have.
Smoothing tone variation or decorative patterning that removes anatomical consequence.
Clean socket lines where bone meets flesh instead of transition tissue.
Weapon organs remaining smooth tubes or symbolic holes rather than structured openings with folds, sheen and use-staining.
Soft light or residual nebula atmosphere that destroys the single hard-light photographic read.
Any change to the two amber mouths, tendril cluster, sacs or stern fins.

Guard sentences and checks for the chair

"The same creature, photographed: body, two glowing amber mouths, tendril cluster, hanging sacs, stern fins and every weapon organ stay exactly where they are."
"Hide must show grain at several scales plus growth records in sensible places; reject speckle-over-smooth."
"Bone emerges through transition tissue, not a clean socket line; organs show internal structure and use-wear."
"Wet/taut/matte variation under one hard light; plain black space, few faint stars; no nebula."
Gate check: does the result still read as the identical Chorus flagship, only now a real living object of city scale?

chorus_C11

Risks in the brief

Identical style constraints to C10, applied to the bladeback cruiser shape reference (chorus_C8_beauty). The pale bone ridge through the back, the two dark throats and the flank sacs must remain exactly as given. The risk is the same anatomical transformation, compounded by the need to keep the two Chorus ships reading as distinct classes once both are rendered in the same photographic living style.

Specific failure modes the transformation invites

The same biological failures listed for C10: smooth hide plus speckle, added anatomy, clean bone sockets, symbolic weapon organs, soft light or residual atmosphere.
Softening or over-detailing the pale bone ridge so it loses its structural character or merges with surrounding tissue.
Making the two dark throats or flank sacs more or less prominent than the shape reference, or giving them mechanical rather than organic structure.
Drift that causes C11's outline from above to converge with C10's, erasing class distinction.
Applying Hegemony-style panel or fastener language by accident.

Guard sentences and checks for the chair

"Pale bone ridge, two dark throats and flank sacs stay exactly as on the shape reference; only surface rendering changes."
"Same multi-scale hide, growth records, transition tissue and organ structure rules as C10; no added anatomy."
"One hard light, plain black space, few faint stars."
"After both ships are accepted, place their outlines from above side by side and confirm they still read as two distinct classes."
Gate check: does the result still read as the identical bladeback cruiser, only now a real living object under the same photographic conditions as C10?
