# Concepts round 8b: Hegemony H10 and the Chorus pair in the Scrap Brick style

Issue #517, the follow-on to round 8 (issue #515). Method per FINDINGS R192: image-to-image edits of the named pictures, rendering only. Style reference for the round: corsair_A.jpg, the round 1 Scrap Brick (R197). The owner's words behind the job: H10 picked as the Hegemony flagship but "semi realistic but not quite" beside the Hullbreakers (R198); "Chorus should be redrawn too" (R199). Every result is scored below against the twelve-line gate (docs/playbooks/concepts_round2.md) and the style line. Failures and partials are marked where they stand.

The style line, as scored: (1) a photograph of a real, very large built (or grown) object, not an illustration, a toy or a game icon; (2) the surface made of many separate parts small enough to give the ship its size, no large flat single-colour area; (3) real materials, the faction's own, under one hard light on plain black space with a few faint stars; (4) weapons, engines and fittings drawn as working machinery or working organs, not smooth symbols.

Council: blind Rey leg written first (leg_rey.md): realism is consequence, not damage; a clean ship's vocabulary is manufacture and service, a living ship's is anatomy, and neither borrows the Hullbreakers' rust. Two writer sets were delivered (leg_deepseek.md, leg_qwen.md). The chaired prompts are Qwen's set, because it read the actual pictures (it counts H10's mounts and the Chorus sacs from the beauties), with one chair amendment (the sheets carry the escort, per the round 8 rule) and DeepSeek's H12 guard sentence appended. Flash and Grok ideation legs converged with the blind leg (leg_flash.md, leg_grok.md). Argon seat empty, blocked as standing.

## hegemony_H12, Hegemony flagship (style pass on H10)

Change made: style only. Every block, mount, engine and light stays where H10 put it: wedge plan, hangar mouth amidships, two twin turrets, the mid-hull twin point-defense mount (six barrels in all), the blue engine panels, the HD-14 marking, the escort with its own turret.

Gate: lines 1 to 12 pass on both pictures. The sheet is the cleanest of the round: plan and profile agree, the hangar mouth opens in the flank in profile, the escort sits at scale in both views.

Style line: 1 PARTIAL, 2 PARTIAL, 3 pass, 4 pass. The delivered pass (try 1) hardened the light, deepened the panel tone variation and gave the plate real edges; the ship reads heavier and more built than H10. It does not reach the Scrap Brick's density: fittings still sit in low relief on several faces and the broad bow faces stay comparatively smooth. Small liberties in try 1: faint exhaust trails stream from the stern pods and the escort, and the background carries faint dust at the frame edges.

The honest method finding, recorded because the Board will hit it again: a second, escalated try (deeper relief, rivet rows, grime in the seams) bought surface at the price of shape. The angular turret housings became round domed barbettes, the hangar mouth shrank, the long grille strips vanished, the engine panels gained grid covers, and the tone went lighter. Under R192 that is a failed edit, and it was rejected. With this image model, H10's ceiling under a pure rendering edit is the try 1 gain; going further means accepting drift or redrawing, which is a different job than this brief allows.

Drift on the delivered pictures: none in mass, outline, mounts or character.

Tries: beauty 2 (try 1 delivered, try 2 rejected on drift), sheet 1.

## chorus_C10, Chorus flagship (style pass on C9)

Change made: style only. The same creature, photographed: two glowing amber mouths at the bow, the dorsal tendril cluster, the hanging sacs as drawn (three large, one smaller at the stern root), the stern fins, the escort in its pose. The reference's painted nebula background is gone, plain black space as the style line requires.

Gate: lines 1 to 12 pass on the beauty. On the sheet, one projection mark: in plan the tendril cluster splays flat and wide where the beauty curls it; same organ, same count. The profile carries the anatomy correctly and the plan carries the outline.

Style line: 1 pass, 2 pass, 3 pass with a note, 4 pass. The hide is now terrain: scarred and callused patches at the flex points and sac roots, cracked callus on the sacs, fine pore grain, dry matte variation set against the wet folds of the mouths. The note on 3: the pass desaturated the creature. C9's amber vein tracery across the flank is much fainter here and the palette sits greyer overall; the faction's amber now lives mostly in the mouths and the stern. The Board may want that tracery strengthened in a future pass; it was not chased here because a colour chase risks the anatomy the pass got right.

Drift: none in anatomy or counts.

Tries: beauty 1, sheet 1.

Change made: style only, no redesign, as the brief orders. The pale bone ridge and its blade row, the two dark throats, the three flank sacs, the stern filament plume with its amber nodes, the escort in its pose: all as drawn in C8.

Gate: lines 1 to 12 pass on the beauty. On the sheet, one projection mark: in plan the bone ridge reads as separated pale plates rather than one continuous row; the profile carries the row correctly and is the view of record for it.

Drift: none in anatomy, blade count or spacing.

Tries: beauty 1, sheet 1.

## The class question the brief asks: do C10 and C11 read as two classes?

Yes, from above they read as two classes, plainly. See chorus_outlines_compare.jpg (the plan views from the two sheets; the crops are each ship's own sheet scale, not a common scale, so judge the outlines, not the relative sizes).

- The bow decides it first: C10 ends in a folded double mouth mass, a single fused structure; C11 ends in two separate open throats side by side.
- The spine decides it second: C10 carries the curled tendril cluster amidships and a smooth ridge line; C11 carries the pale bone blade row down its centre, which nothing else in the faction has.
- The proportions back both up: C10 is long and tapering to a stern spike; C11 is shorter in proportion and broader, with its sacs carried as domes along the back rather than hanging bulges along the flanks.

What they share is the family grammar (sac rows, hide, amber organs), which is what makes them one faction rather than two strangers. Ryan's R184 note stands as the design direction for later work, not this round: when the bladeback's own fleet sheet is built, the single-throat change belongs there. This round changed no anatomy, per the brief.
