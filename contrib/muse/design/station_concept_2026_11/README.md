# Station concept, three directions (issue 523)

Three directions for the battle board's station set piece, for Ryan's pick before the build (issue 373). Each direction has a beauty view (high three-quarter, as the battle camera sees it) and a scale view (the same station from above with an A17 Pack Hunter cruiser beside it at true scale: cruiser about 23 units, station a little over twice that across or more). Code name on every picture; no picture is taller than 720 px. Style reference for all six: corsair_A.jpg, the Scrap Brick.

The council: blind Rey leg first (why the current station reads as blocks: uniform part size, no system with a nameable job, no record of time). Qwen and DeepSeek wrote prompt sets; the chaired prompts are Qwen's inventory discipline at DeepSeek's proportions, with Grok's guard sentences and Flash's catches folded in. Flash caught two real faults in the blind leg's own direction, both fixed before generation: the refinery needed the turning part the brief requires (it now has a rotating ore-grading drum), and its furnaces are kiln drums with slag channels, never bells (bells on a spine would draw engines and hand the station a thrust axis). Hauler docking sits on a transverse arm at mid-spine for the same reason. Qwen's leg this round was clean and complete; its earlier derailments track open-ended briefs, and this pack was written closed.

Gate scoring uses the twelve lines of docs/playbooks/concepts_round2.md where they apply to a station (lines 1, 2, 6 and 9 are ship weapon and class lines; the station carries no weapons) plus the style line. Lines 3 to 5 and 7 are read for a station as: no thrust axis, one core, one turning part, no accidental symmetry that reads as a logo. Line 11 (space, not sea) and line 10 (one scale) are scored in full, as is line 8 (designed from above first: the scale views are the plans).

## station_T1: the fleet station in service

Beauty try 1 accepted. Scale try 1 accepted. A Hegemony fleet station of the Vesper Reach, well kept: a core stack of 9 deck drums, a habitat ring turning on a segmented bearing track with one open gap segment, four docking arms at crossed angles and two heights with lit berth collars (one berth empty, guide lights on, cargo pods queued), five towers at five heights with five different caps, radiator banks under the core, about 650 implant-blue lights in clusters, fresh replacement panels among older ones.

Style line, point by point: photograph of a real built object, pass. Many small parts giving the size, pass (three scale bands visible at once: hex-scale core and ring, cruiser-scale arms, metre-scale fittings). No large flat single-colour area, pass. One hard light on plain black, pass. Systems as working machinery, connected, pass. Colour lock held: pale grey, steel blue, implant-blue only.

Gate: line 11 pass (no hull, no prow, no engine bells anywhere, stack ends in collars and platforms). Line 10 pass (the scale view proves it: the cruiser reads small). Line 8 pass (plan reads as an open C with crossed spokes and an off-centre tower cluster). Marks: the four arms read closer in length than the specified 20, 20, 18, 24, and the rebuilt shorter arm is not obvious at this distance; the plan is more symmetrical than the beauty. Drift: none in systems or counts that matter. Tries: 1 and 1.

## station_T2: the same station abandoned

Beauty try 1 accepted with marks. Scale try 1 accepted. The same kind of station after the write-off: the ring seized off-angle with its gap moved, one spoke buckled, one docking arm torn to a stub, debris held where it drifted to a stop against the arms and ring, towers dead with one leaning, radiators cold, about 90 lights left in scattered stubborn clusters plus a few dull orange battery strips at torn frames.

Style line: pass on the same points as T1, with the time record inverted (no fresh panels anywhere, frost and dust on the shadow side, torn holes showing deck frames inside, so it reads as decks and frames, not a solid lump). Colour lock held.

Gate: line 11 pass. Line 10 pass (same ratio in the scale view, and the plan cannot be superimposed on T1's: the gap has moved and one arm is a stub). Marks: (1) the flat radiator banks hanging below read slightly like solar arrays; they are the same banks as T1's, cold. (2) The family resemblance to T1 is carried by the ring, the drum core and the arms rather than an identical plan; at build time both directions share one parts kit (issue 373), so this is a concept-stage note, not a failure. Tries: 1 and 1.

## station_T3: the Reach refinery (the chair's third direction)

Beauty try 1 accepted. Scale try 1 rejected (it came back as a three-quarter side view, not a plan); try 2 accepted with a mark, and is the delivered scale view. Issue 373 asks which station kind comes first, fleet or refinery; this is the refinery, the Reach's older industrial kind: a captured rock at one end, clamped in a steel cage with drill heads and cable runs in its face and a rotating ore-grading drum turning round the cage, an open-frame processing spine, smelting kiln drums hung under the spine at spaced points with slag channels glowing dull ember-orange, a row of radiator sails on one side, ore hoppers and container racks on deck, a short control block, hauler docking on a transverse arm at mid-spine, and a silo group closing the far end. Total length about 66 units, near three cruiser lengths.

Style line: pass. The rock is the one unbuilt mass and stays unplated; everything else is frames, voids and fittings at three scale bands; orange is locked to molten channels, kiln mouths and work floods, blue to windows and markers.

Gate: line 11 pass with emphasis: no thrust axis anywhere (the kilns hang downward at spaced points and point at nothing), no front and no rear (the dock juts sideways). Line 10 pass (in the scale view the cruiser is about a third of the station's length). Line 8 pass (plan: a bar with a comb of sail blades on one side, a spoked rock cage at one end, dock fingers at mid-spine; shares nothing with T1 and T2's radial plans). Marks: in the beauty the radiator sails read as large box panels amidships rather than banks of thin panels; the molten slag runs are theatrical; in the scale view the deck's middle band reads as one long slab from directly above, flatter than the beauty, so the beauty is the view of record for the deck and the plan for the outline. Tries: beauty 1, scale 2.

## The pick

The Board's build (issue 373) takes one direction first. T1 and T2 are one station in two times, so picking either buys the other cheaply later from the same parts kit. T3 is a different plan and a different kit, and it is the direction that answers the refinery half of issue 373's question. Nothing here is built until Ryan picks.
