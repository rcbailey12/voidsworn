# Chaired prompts, issue 523 (station concept)

Chair note: built from Qwen's writer set (inventory discipline and the T1/T2 shared plan) with DeepSeek's proportions, Grok's guard sentences, and Flash's catches applied to both writer sets: the refinery's furnaces are kiln drums and hearth basins, never bells; T3 gets its turning part (a rotating ore-grading drum round the rock cage); hauler docking sits on a transverse arm at mid-spine so the refinery has no fore-to-aft axis; T2 carries a few dull orange battery strips among its blue. Code names are captioned by the chair at delivery, never generated into a picture. Lights are always clusters and irregular groupings, never rows: a row of lights is the waterline failure in miniature. Beauty input order: corsair_A.jpg first (style only, no shape carried over), then the prompt. Scale input order: the accepted beauty first, corsair_A17_beauty.jpg second (the cruiser as itself, in its own colours), then the prompt.

## T1 beauty: the Vesper Reach fleet station in service

A photograph of a real, very large built object, not an illustration, a toy or a game icon. Plain black space with a few faint stars, no nebula. One hard light from upper left, high three-quarter view from above the ring plane, as the battle camera sees it. A Hegemony fleet station in service and well kept, in the Vesper Reach. Colour lock: pale grey plate, steel blue secondary structure and pipework, implant-blue lights only; no red, no orange anywhere.

Systems, largest to smallest. A core stack of 9 stacked deck drums, about 34 units across and 40 units tall, each drum a separate structure with visible frames and deck plates between drums, about 200 small square window lights on the core in irregular clusters. A habitat ring about 48 units in diameter and 6 units deep at core mid height, turning slowly for gravity on a visible bearing track with joint plates and drive housings, joined to the core by 3 spoke tubes; the ring has one open gap segment of about 40 degrees; its outer face carries about 400 small windows in clusters, all lit. Open space between the core, the spokes and the ring: the structure is frames and voids, not a solid body. Four docking arms at crossed angles and two heights, lengths 20, 20, 18 and 24 units, the 18 unit arm visibly rebuilt shorter after an accident with brighter plating; each arm ends in a berth collar with 12 guide lights, all lit; one berth empty with its guide lights on and a queue of 3 small cargo pods waiting along its approach; pipe runs, walkways and cable trays along every arm at metre scale, feeding the core. Five towers in an off-centre cluster on the core top deck, heights 10, 14, 18, 22 and 26 units, every cap different: a sensor dome, a comm array of 3 dishes, a beacon head, a crane jib, a flat landing pad. Four radiator panels in 2 pairs hung off the core's hot section, fed by steel blue header pipes, each panel a bank of many thin plates. About 900 separate metre-scale plates and panels over core, ring and arms, about 30 replacement panels a shade brighter than their neighbours, bolt rows, seams and conduit runs everywhere, no large flat single-colour area anywhere. About 650 lit points in total, in clusters, never a single dotted row along any side. The central stack ends in docking collars, sensor platforms and truss caps: no engine nozzles, no thrust bells, no exhaust on any axis. The station spans about 52 units across and stands about 52 units tall, taller than any ship. It is a place, not a ship: no streamlined body, no pointed front end, no centreline ridge, no sailing rig, no round windows in a row; its parts point in several directions because it stays in place.

Most expected failure: the logo station, perfect symmetry, identical towers, one clean ring. Guard: five towers at five heights with five caps, four arms of unequal length, one arm rebuilt shorter, the ring riding a segmented track with joint detail.

## T1 scale: edit of the accepted T1 beauty

Keep every system, count, colour and light exactly as accepted. Move the camera to directly above the station, a true plan view straight down, no tilt: the ring reads as an open C around the core, the four arms as crossed spokes of unequal length, the tower cluster off-centre. Place one A17 Pack Hunter cruiser beside the station, clear of the longest arm, parallel to the shortest arm, at true scale, drawn as itself in its own rusted scrap colours: the cruiser about 23 units long against the station's span of about 52 units, a little over twice the cruiser's length, so the cruiser reads small beside the station. Same hard light, plain black space, no nebula, no text, no grid, no labels in the picture.

## T2 beauty: the same station abandoned

A photograph of a real, very large built object, not an illustration, a toy or a game icon. Plain black space with a few faint stars, no nebula. One hard light from upper right, high three-quarter view from the side that shows the damage. The same Vesper Reach fleet station family as T1, abandoned after the write-off and half dark. Colour lock: dust grey plate where pale grey used to be, faded steel blue structure, implant-blue light only in a few stubborn clusters, plus a few dull orange battery strips rim-lighting torn frames; no red.

The silhouette changes before the lighting does. The core stack of 9 drums stands, its top drum torn open at one edge; of its 200 windows, 170 are dark and 30 stay lit in 2 stubborn clusters. The habitat ring is seized 30 degrees off its service angle, stopped mid-turn on a rusted bearing track with joint plates sprung open, so its 40 degree gap now sits over one arm's line; one of its 3 spoke tubes is buckled inward; of its 400 windows, 360 are dark and 40 stay lit in 3 clusters. Four arms: three intact at 20, 20 and 18 units, the fourth torn to a bent stub of 10 units, its berth collar gone and frame ribs showing at the break. Debris held where it drifted to a stop: 3 cargo pods, 2 caught in an arm's cradle and 1 against the ring's outer face, and one torn plate of 4 units folded over a walkway. The five towers stand dead at the same heights with the same cap family: sensor dome cracked, comm dishes pitted, beacon dark, crane jib frozen half raised, landing pad buckled, the tallest tower leaning 5 degrees. The 4 radiator panels are cold, dust on their upper edges, one hanging open on a single hinge. About 900 metre-scale plates, none of them fresh; 4 torn holes of 2 to 4 units show deck frames and ribs inside, so the structure reads as decks and frames, not a solid lump; frost and dust dull the whole side away from the light; markings worn half away. About 90 lit points in total, in 6 scattered clusters. This plan cannot be superimposed on the service station's plan: the gap has moved, one arm is a stub, and two limbs are widened by held debris. It is a place, not a ship, on the same terms as T1.

Most expected failure: the dimmer switch, T1 redrawn with the lights off. Guard: the breaks come first (torn stub arm, seized ring, buckled spoke, held debris), the darkness second.

## T2 scale: edit of the accepted T2 beauty

Place one A17 Pack Hunter cruiser beside the station, at true scale, drawn as itself in its own rusted scrap colours: the cruiser about 23 units long against the station's span of about 52 units, a little over twice its length, reading small beside the dead station. Same hard light, plain black space, no nebula, no text, no grid, no labels in the picture.

## T3 beauty: the Reach refinery

A photograph of a real, very large built object, not an illustration, a toy or a game icon. Plain black space with a few faint stars, no nebula. One hard light from high above the sail side, high three-quarter view along the spine from the sail side, rock end near camera. A working industrial refinery in space in the Vesper Reach, rough-kept by the Reach's own people, older than the fleet station. Colour lock: dust grey and steel blue plate, implant-blue window and marker lights, ember-orange only at molten channels, kiln mouths and sodium work floods; no red.

Systems, largest to smallest. At one end, one irregular captured rock about 16 units across, the one piece nobody built: pitted grey-brown stone with no plate on it, clamped inside a cage of structural steel retention pilings and anchor frames that bite into the rock, with 6 drill heads and 10 cable runs anchored into its face, feeding ore through a throat into the spine. Round the cage turns a rotating ore-grading drum, a wide open frame ring on rollers, the station's one turning part. A horizontal processing spine about 46 units long and 8 units deep, built as 14 open deck frames in a row with trusses and voids between them, pipe runs and catwalks along its length, about 250 small window lights in clusters. Three smelting kiln drums of 5 units across hung under the spine at frames 4, 8 and 12, spaced along its length, each a squat cylinder with a slag tap trough and a run channel angled down and out, the channel mouths glowing dull ember-orange, heat staining and slag baking on the plates below each kiln. Six radiator sails in a row on one side of the spine only, each sail a bank of many separate thin panels totalling 16 units tall and 6 units wide, on steel blue header pipes off one manifold, angled slightly off vertical, the row breaking where a repair stopped. Four ore hopper cones of 4 units across on the top deck at varied positions, and 2 container racks holding 18 containers in mismatched dull paint. One short fat control tower, 9 units tall and 6 wide, at mid-spine top, one band of 20 windows lit implant-blue. Hauler docking on one transverse arm at mid-spine, an open frame jutting sideways with 2 berth collars of 12 guide lights each and a crane jib over the empty berth; the far end of the spine carries a silo group of 5 tanks and no dock, so the plant has no front and no rear. About 840 separate metre-scale plates, about 20 of them mismatched replacements in wrong shades of grey, weld seams and clamp rows visible, no large flat single-colour area anywhere, and the rock stays unplated. About 410 lit points in total: windows and markers in implant-blue, work lights in ember-orange at the kilns, channels and dock only. Total length about 66 units from rock face to silo end, near three cruiser lengths; height about 30 units, taller than any ship. It is a place, not a ship: no streamlined body, no pointed front end, no centreline ridge, no row of thrust bells anywhere; parts point up, down, sideways and along the spine because it stays in place.

Most expected failure: the ship writ large, spine as body, rock as bow, kilns as engines. Guard: the rock is an irregular captured mass with drills in its face, the kilns hang spaced under the spine as furnaces with slag channels pointing down and out, the dock juts sideways at mid-spine, and there is no thrust axis anywhere.

## T3 scale: edit of the accepted T3 beauty

Keep every system, count, colour and light exactly as accepted. Move the camera to directly above, a true plan view straight down, no tilt: the spine reads as a straight bar of open frames, the 6 sails as a row of thin blades on one side only, the rock as an irregular blob at one end inside its cage, the dock arm jutting sideways at mid-spine, the silos closing the far end. Place one A17 Pack Hunter cruiser beside the spine on the side opposite the sails, parallel to the spine, at true scale, drawn as itself in its own rusted scrap colours: the cruiser about 23 units long against the station's 66 units end to end, near three cruiser lengths, reading small beside the refinery. Same hard light, plain black space, no nebula, no text, no grid, no labels in the picture.
