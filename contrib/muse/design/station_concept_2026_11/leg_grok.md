Judgment of the blind leg

The three failure causes are strong and correctly identify why the current station reads as game blocks. Uniform part size is the root mechanical problem and the most important to beat; without violent scale mixing the rest fails. The demand that every major form answer a named job and that systems connect is correct and directly usable in prompts. The third cause, that a place must record time through repairs, wear and uneven lighting, is also correct and is the cleanest way to separate T1 from T2 without relying on a dimmer switch.

The causes are not wrong. The only mild weakness is that the first cause could be stated more sharply for prompt writing: the eye needs at least three clear scale bands visible at once (hex-scale core, cruiser-scale arms or spine, metre-scale fittings and lights) rather than a general call for “orders of magnitude.” That phrasing would make the guard easier to enforce.

T3 (Reach refinery) is the right alternative plan. A horizontal processing spine with a captured rock at one end, radiator sails in a row, hoppers, crucible bells and hauler docking is distinct from the ring-and-arms family of T1 and T2, and the industrial, older character fits the Reach lore given in the pack. The lean toward working-but-rough with ember-orange work lights among the blue is good and keeps it from becoming a third clean Hegemony picture.

Where T3 is weak: the short fat control tower is under-specified and risks becoming a generic block. The rock core needs an explicit statement that it is the one unbuilt mass and that the processing systems are clamped onto and through it, otherwise the model may treat the rock as just another plated volume. The radiator sails should be described as tall, thin, parallel arrays of many panels rather than single solid sheets, or they will read as large flat planes, which the style line forbids. I would also require that the ore hoppers and container racks sit at different heights and angles along the spine so the silhouette from above is broken and not a clean rectangle with fins.

T1 risks, failure modes and guards

Top risks: perfect rotational symmetry around the core and ring; identical tower heights and caps; the turning habitat ring reading as a clean decorative band rather than machinery on a visible track or bearing; docking arms that look identical and undamaged; large uninterrupted plate fields with no panel seams or fittings; lights that form neat rows instead of clustered working markers.

Most expected failure: logo station, one clean ring with matching towers.

Guard sentence to add: “Phase-built skyline of towers at three or more different heights with different caps (sensor dome, array, beacon), one docking arm shorter and visibly rebuilt, habitat ring on an explicit segmented track with joint detail, hundreds of small implant-blue lights in irregular clusters, no large flat single-colour panels.”

T2 risks, failure modes and guards

Top risks: same silhouette as T1 with only lighting reduced; damage limited to surface scorch or missing panels that do not change the outline from above; the seized ring still reading as a continuous circle; debris that floats free instead of being held against the arms; remaining lights still evenly distributed.

Most expected failure: dimmer-switch version of T1.

Guard sentence to add: “Silhouette must differ from the in-service version: one docking arm segment missing or torn, habitat ring stopped mid-turn at an angle, debris field of plates and containers held against the remaining arms, whole sections of lights dead while a few stubborn circuits stay on, frost and dust dulling one face, structure still readable as the same plan family but visibly broken and abandoned.”

T3 risks, failure modes and guards

Top risks: the processing spine becoming a long cylinder with greebles; the captured rock plated over so it loses its unbuilt mass; radiator sails reading as solid walls or single sheets; hoppers and racks arranged in tidy symmetric rows; the whole form acquiring a thrust axis or stern-like end; large flat areas on the sails or spine.

Most expected failure: death-star cylinder or logo industrial block with fins.

Guard sentence to add: “Horizontal processing spine only, no rotational symmetry; one end is an unbuilt captured rock mass with processing frames clamped onto and through it; tall thin radiator sails in a parallel row made of many separate panels that break where repairs stopped; ore hoppers and container racks at varied heights and angles; hauler docking clustered at the far end only; short control tower of different height and cap from any other volume; systems connect (hoppers feed spine, spine feeds crucibles, radiators hang off the hot sections); hundreds of small lights, mix of implant-blue and ember-orange work lights, no large flat single-colour surfaces.”
