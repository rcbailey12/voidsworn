# Issue 1006 round 4, blind Rey leg (written from the pack alone, before the external legs)

## 1. The log: option A, floating right, and the shelf at 248

Verdict: A. The log belongs on the right, floating above the shelf, for three reasons. Its content is mostly about the enemy and the last action (damage done and taken, effects, the enemy marshal's lines), and the right side is where the enemy cluster already lives; the eye is there when the log matters. The top-left cluster is the player's own turn state, and stacking a scrolling narrative under it mixes two jobs in the one corner the player reads at the start of every activation. And B's cost is structural: the turn cluster growing downward eats the board's left edge, where the player's own fleet deploys and moves early in a battle.

Form: a patch about 560 by 150 design px at the right edge, ending about 12 px above the shelf's top edge, on a veil at roughly 55 percent of the shelf veil's strength (the scene stays readable through it; the text carries T2 at full strength so it still reads). Default state: the last 3 lines, newest at the bottom, older lines fading a step. It is optional three ways: a small Log word at its top right corner collapses it to a one-line ticker (the newest line only); clicking or hovering the ticker expands it to about 8 lines with the older lines at full strength; and Controls keeps a show-log option for players who want it gone entirely, with the L key still toggling. The one-line ticker is the floor, not full hiding, because a brand-new player needs outcomes explained somewhere standing; the Controls option is the true off switch.

Share accounting, honest: shelf at 248 of 1080 is 23.0 percent hard veil; top-left and top-right clusters about 3 percent; the log patch is 560 by 150, about 4.0 percent of the screen at 55 percent veil strength, a veil equivalent of about 2.2 percent. Total about 28 percent against today's rail at about 33 percent, and the log portion can be collapsed to about 0.5 percent by a player who knows the game. Option B's accounting for comparison: no right patch, but the left cluster grows to about 700 by 300 (about 10 percent) whenever the log is open, and it pushes over deployment water. A wins on both readings.

The shelf at 248, what the air buys: the silhouette anchor at its full 40 px height with the integrity rows under it at taller pitch; order glyphs at their full 32 px spec with labels beneath instead of beside where width allows; the range bands lengthen by about a third, which is the range band's readability budget; the fleet strip slots get taller mini bars. Nothing new is added to the shelf. That is the point of the expansion: same contents, more air.

## 2. The faction silhouette kit

Fixed per class (the envelope): length and height proportions from round 3, bow position, stern block mass, and the class signature's position. A player identifies class from the envelope before anything else, at both sizes.

Swapped per faction (the language), in three zones plus a detail kit:
- Bow third: Mandate gets a stepped armoured prow with a lower jaw; Glasswright gets a smooth leaf tip, curved, no angles at all; Dominion gets a blunt prow with the cannon housing ridge starting at the bow; Hegemony keeps the clean raked wedge.
- Spine third: Mandate terraces (two steps); Glasswright one unbroken curve with a vein ridge (a contour ridge, not a drawn line); Dominion a heavy raised housing running to the cannon mount amidships, the cannon itself a long parallel ridge on the spine line so it reads as a weapon, not a bay; Hegemony a single raised spine with a command tower.
- Stern third: Mandate a wide stepped drive block; Glasswright the leaf tapering into a soft bloom notch (the engine glow point); Dominion a deep squared block; Hegemony the standard three-nozzle block.
- Anchor-size detail kit (40 px only, contour and negative cuts only): turret bumps on the spine (two, faction-shaped: Mandate square, Hegemony angled, Dominion one large at the cannon mount, Glasswright none, its weapons sit on the optical axis as a nose notch); a stern antenna tick on Mandate and Hegemony; nozzle segmentation as negative ticks in the stern block; a hangar mouth cut on carriers in every language.

The cruiser in four languages, one line each: Mandate cruiser: the round 3 envelope rebuilt as steps, prow jawed, spine in two terraces, stern a wide block; it reads as a fortress that moves. Glasswright cruiser: the same length and height stretched into a leaf, top line one curve from tip to stern bloom, no tower, a nose notch for the axis weapon; it reads as a grown thing, and its damage creep should follow the leaf from the stern bloom forward. Dominion cruiser: blunt prow, the spine dominated by the cannon ridge running half the hull, one large turret bump at the mount; it reads as a gun with a ship around it. Hegemony cruiser: the round 3 Vigil pattern unchanged; it is the reference the others depart from.

16 px honesty: faction survives at 16 px only as massing. Mandate reads stepped, Glasswright reads curved, Dominion reads top-heavy, Hegemony reads clean. If a faction's massing cue does not survive for a given class (small frigates are the risk), the fallback is the class envelope plus the ship's name, which the strip already carries; no faction colours, ever. That is an acceptable loss, stated plainly: the fleet strip identifies ships and classes first, factions second.

Cost and order: the kit is one pass per faction language (envelope mapping plus the three zones plus the detail kit), then one pass per class inside a language is short work because the zones are already decided. Ship the player's own faction first (it anchors the integrity unit, the most-read silhouette in the game), then Hegemony (the E1 enemy and the reference), then Mandate and Dominion, Glasswright last because its curved language needs its own contour pass and should not be rushed into angles by habit.

## 3. The odds split, made plain

Layout: the hit chance stays the big figure ("92%"). Beside it, a small stack: line one, "About 16 damage" in mono, ink, the total the player asked about. Line two, the split as two labelled parts in one row: the Glacier shield diamond, "7 to shields", then the hull chevron, "9 to hull", both at the 14 to 15 px mono label size already used for SHIELD and HULL. The words do the work the glyphs cannot: a bare 7 beside a diamond asks the player to guess; "7 to shields" does not. "About" stays, because the game deals expected values and the word is one syllable of honesty. When the target's shields are down, line two reads "all to hull" and the diamond part disappears rather than showing a zero.

## 4. Refusals

A fully hiding log by default (outcomes must be explained standing for a new player; optional means collapsible, not absent). Faction colours or faction icon variants in the glyph set (round 3 refusal, stands; factions live in silhouettes and ship art, not in the HUD's working glyphs). Interior shading, panel lines or window dots inside silhouettes at any size (contour and negative cuts only; the leaf's inner light is a ship-art question, not a HUD silhouette question). And expanding the shelf past about 248 without a new reason: the declutter case is made at 248, and every further row of height is board the player cannot see.
