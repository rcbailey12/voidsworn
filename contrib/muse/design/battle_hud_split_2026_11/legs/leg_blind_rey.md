# Leg: Rey (blind, from the pack alone)

Three concepts, one pick. All geometry at the 1920 by 1080 design size; shares are of the whole screen. Today for comparison: a full-height left veil band touching about 30% of the screen, text column 19.8% of the width, plus about 3% in the top-right cluster and bottom-right log.

## My pick: LEDGER SPLIT (the brief's concept (a), tightened)

Top bar, 88 px tall, full width (8.1% of the screen): turn header and objective at the left ("Round 1, Your turn, 3 of 3 ships to act" on one line, objective under it), and the existing top-right cluster absorbed into the bar's right end (enemy line, Heat, Nemesis, Menu). Nothing else lives up top.

Bottom bar, 208 px tall, full width (19.3%): two rows. Main row (160 px), left to right in decision order: the ship block (name in display type, class, hull and move lines, the four-facing shield cross compact at the block's right edge, status line under), then the guns table, then target and odds, then the FIRE box at the right with the orders row and Command row stacked under it inside the bar. Fleet row (48 px) along the bar's bottom edge: the fleet as one horizontal line of name-and-hull chips, the acting ship ember-marked, exactly where the eye ends.

Total covered: about 27.4%, barely less than today's veil. The win is not raw area, it is the shape of what is free: one continuous field 784 px tall across the full width, both board flanks clear for the whole battle, and nothing tall standing on the left where the player's fleet deploys. Reading order: context at top left, then the eye drops to the bottom-left and sweeps right through ship, guns, target, odds, FIRE, with orders and Command under the FIRE box and the fleet at the foot of the sweep. The order survives the split as a Z.

At 1440p and 4K the shares are identical (the scale law scales the bars with the window); at 4K the shield cross and the guns table gain room, and the bottom bar could give 16 design px back to the board if the Board wants, but the concept does not depend on it.

Costs, honestly: the bottom bar is a wide slab and 208 px is a lot of height to give the board's lower edge; on the round arena the fight centres low and the bar will sit under the action rather than beside it, which is better than today's left band but not free. The status line and the damage split are the tightest fits and may need the hover layer more often than today.

Occasional items: the phase banner centres on the board as today (it is not rail content). The tree prompt card docks into the bottom bar's centre, replacing the guns and odds block while it is up (same footprint, no new slab). Retreat sits in the top bar at the right, beside Menu.

## Alternative 1: BOOKENDS (my own)

Top strip 64 px (5.9%): turn and objective at the left on two short lines, the fleet as a row of chips in the centre (name and hull, acting ship ember-marked), enemy, Heat, Nemesis and Menu at the right. Bottom strip 144 px (13.3%): ship block at the left with a compact four-number shield diamond, the guns as one row of weapon chips, target and odds, the FIRE box at the right, orders and Command as a key-chip row under the guns. Total about 19.3% covered, the least of the full-coverage concepts, and the closest to Ryan's reference screenshots (fleet as a row in a bar, weapons as a table in the strip).

Costs: the fleet chips cap out at about eight ships before they must shrink or wrap, and player fleets can grow past that late in a run; the 64 px top strip is exactly two text lines at the floor, so the objective truncates on long text; the shield diamond is a new glyph the player must learn, where today the cross reads at a glance. This is the boldest option and the one most likely to need a second iteration.

## Alternative 2: COMMAND DECK (the brief's concept (b))

Bottom-centre strip 1240 by 176 px floating over the board's lower middle with a 24 px margin (10.5%), holding ship, guns, target, odds, FIRE, orders and Command in two rows. Slim corner cards: turn and objective top left (400 by 104, 2.0%), the fleet bottom left (300 by 132, 1.9%), enemy and Nemesis top right and the log bottom right as today (about 3%). Total about 17.4%, the most open edges of any concept: both flanks and the whole top middle stay clear.

Costs: the strip floats over the board itself, not over an edge, so it covers live hexes at the board's lower centre, which is where a pincer or a retreat plays out; the fleet card is divorced from the ship block, breaking the "fleet at the foot of the sheet" adjacency the eye is trained on; and four separate cards are four veils to keep consistent, the most new layout code of the three.

## What I would not do

- Split the decision order across the split (ship up top, guns down below). The order is the design; a split that scatters it trades Ryan's actual complaint (one tall band) for a worse one (hunting).
- Move the log or the Nemesis panel without cause. The brief says keep them; none of the three concepts needs them moved, and the top bar absorbing the top-right cluster is tidier than relocating it.
- Shrink text below the 17 px floor to make a bar thinner. The floor is a ruled constraint (RULEBOOK 9.4), not a style choice.
