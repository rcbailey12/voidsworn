The mockups are the chair's drawings of the three chaired concepts, in the game's real faces (Big Shoulders Display, Barlow Semi Condensed, Azeret Mono) with the palette sampled from the capture itself.

Mockup base, stated plainly: the pictures are drawn over the Board's own 1080p battle capture (docs/shots/ui_battle_ember/full_1080/E1_ledger_1080.png). ## Ryan's notes, Nov 17, 2026 (for the build; these supersede the legs where they conflict)

1. Shield readout: ONE pool, no four-way cross. Ryan ruled the shield strength model on Nov 14, 2026: one universal shield pool per ship, not four independent facing strengths; armor and hull stay side-specific, each side carries a coverage value, and the ship routes shield power toward the side under fire at an efficiency loss. The four-facing cross in the mockups (and in today's build, ledger.gd's _shield_plan) is the pre-ruling readout, carried into this study only because the mockups copy the E1 capture; the Board has not built the pooled readout yet. In every concept, replace the cross with a single pooled readout in the ship block, up with the hull line: the pool figure in Glacier with a slim bar under it (the redrawn KEEL mockup shows it: "Shield 80 / 80", the E1 ship's four 20s summed). Per-side coverage and routing state are expert detail and live behind hover (SAY LESS), not as four standing numbers. The odds line's wording follows the same ruling: "to its prow shield" is pre-ruling text; under the pool it reads "to shields", and how coverage modifies that figure is a Board numbers question, not settled here.
2. No keyboard shortcut labels on the HUD face. The key letters come off the FIRE box, the orders and Command rows, the log header, and the menu label, in every concept. The keys themselves keep working; the full list lives under Controls in the menu. The redrawn KEEL mockup shows the shelf without them. One discovery path for new players is still needed (first-battle hints, or a show-key-hints option in Controls); that choice is a Board follow-up, not a blocker.

## Round 2: the polish pass (Nov 17, 2026), chaired

Ryan's read of the KEEL mockup: the bottom row still seems super unpolished; hull and shield are not together and are approached differently; it is mostly text instead of visual icons; look at the Sins 2, BattleTech and Gothic Armada 2 UIs. The council re-ran on a round 2 pack (evidence_pack_round2.md): blind Rey plus DeepSeek, Qwen, Grok and Flash under their gates (legs_round2/). The verdict is unanimous: all five legs agree with all three criticisms. DeepSeek and blind Rey add the one refinement the chair adopts: names and exact figures stay text or mono, because they are the decision; what changes is that status is drawn and actions become glyphs. The chaired grammar below amends KEEL's shelf internals; KEEL's geometry, decision order, top clusters and screen share stand as delivered. The redrawn mockup is mockups/keel_v3.png; its glyphs are schematic placeholders in the game's line style, and the Board's art pass draws the final set.

1. The integrity unit (the hull and shield fix). One component, two stacked rows of identical geometry (DeepSeek's spec): a glyph slot, a small label, the mono figure at the row's right end, and a 9 to 10 px track under each. Shield row on top (it is spent first, and Qwen, Grok and Flash all stack it there), Glacier fill and figure. Hull row under it, fill in warm ink, with the missing portion and the figure taking the muted damage colour as hull falls. Per-side coverage stays behind hover, as ruled. The unit sits as its own block beside the ship block, which keeps name, class, and Move (Move becomes a hex-arrow glyph plus its figure, DeepSeek).
2. Guns as a checklist. Each row: a weapon-class glyph (battery: three strokes; lance: a spearhead; torpedo and point defence follow the same family), the weapon name, the arc as a small wedge glyph carrying its letter, then the round's one genuinely new element, Qwen's drawn range band: a line with near and far ticks spanning the weapon's band, with the current target's range as a diamond marker on it. In range, the hit chance stands as a mono figure at the row's end; out of range, the row dims and the diamond parks past the band's end. The "1-5 out of range" prose is gone.
3. The status line stays a sentence. Grok and Flash would move it behind hover and DeepSeek would compress it to a glyph and a label; the chair keeps it standing, headed by DeepSeek's focused-fire glyph, because it is the one piece of context that changes the current decision (Brace is its direct answer) and DeepSeek's own cost section flags the risk of hiding it.
4. Odds become figures. A large mono hit figure with a small "to hit, Oath lance" label; the damage split as glyph plus figure pairs (Glacier diamond 7, hull chevron 8) with the total. The odds sentence is cut (DeepSeek, Qwen).
5. Orders and Command become bare glyph plus short label, drawn on the veil with invisible hit areas. No tiles: Flash's outlined hexagon tile is rejected, it is a box by another name. Labels stay standing (Grok's hover-only labels are rejected; a player should not have to hover to learn the controls). "Concentrated fire" shortens to "Focus fire" on the standing surface, full name on hover (DeepSeek). The chair reads the Command row's first element as the resource itself, a star glyph plus its points figure, not a button; the Board should confirm that reading. States, per DeepSeek, adopted whole: ready at full strength; active takes an ember glyph and a 2 px ember underline; unavailable at 35 percent opacity; cooldown as a thin ember arc on the glyph plus a mono turns figure. Ember stays an accent, never a fill.
6. The log keeps two lines. DeepSeek's shrink to a glyph and one line is rejected; the log is how the game explains outcomes, and round 1 already cost it width.
7. The fleet strip becomes slots in the same grammar: ship name, a stacked mini integrity bar (Glacier over ink), the hull figure, and the ember underline for the acting ship. Names stay standing: Qwen's glyph-only strip and Flash's three-letter codes are rejected, because the strip's job is knowing who is who at a glance. At 14 ships the names truncate and hover carries the full name (DeepSeek, Grok); that is the honest cost, stated rather than designed away.

Colour discipline corrections the chair made to the legs: three legs (Qwen, Grok, Flash) filled the hull bar with ember. Rejected. Ember is the accent and the FIRE box's colour; a shelf-wide ember bar floods it and leaves nothing for the active states. Hull is warm ink, damage colour when hurt, as in the palette the pack carried. Flash's red odds reticle is rejected (red is the Nemesis's name only). Qwen's ember ship name for the acting ship is rejected (the name stays ink; the acting marks are the turn header and the fleet underline).

Borrow and refuse, one line each: from Armada 2, the stacked icon-headed bars and the cooldown shown on the action itself; refuse its chrome and density. From BattleTech, status as a picture and the weapon checklist; refuse the paper doll, which would advertise per-location health this game no longer has. From Sins 2, the rule that every value pairs an icon with a number; refuse its tiles and its resource sprawl.

Deferred and flagged for the Board: Qwen's per-class ship silhouettes (a strong anchor for the vitals block and the fleet strip, and a second art pass; adopt once the grammar is proven, not before). The drawn range band needs one Board picture against a real target sitting at a band edge before it is built; it is the only element whose glance-reading is unproven. The glyph set the build needs is the union of blind Rey's and DeepSeek's lists, about sixteen glyphs, one icon pass in agent terms plus the shelf rebuild.

## Round 3: iconography (Nov 17, 2026), chaired

Ryan's direction: the ship silhouettes are in, and the icons are "pretty, pretty simple"; upgrade them. The council re-ran on a round 3 pack (evidence_pack_round3.md): blind Rey plus DeepSeek, Qwen, Grok and Flash under their gates (legs_round3/). Deliverables: mockups/icons.png (the chaired set at 96 px with all detail layers, then at 32, 20 and 12 px with layers dropped, plus the five silhouettes at anchor size and at 16 px) and mockups/keel_v4.png (KEEL with the silhouettes and the upgraded set in place).

Silhouettes (unanimous on the system, chaired on the numbers). One master vector per class, side profile, bow right, solid ink fill, exported at two sizes: the integrity anchor (about 40 px tall) and the fleet strip (16 px tall). Proportions in hull units, length by height, are DeepSeek's table, the most differentiated of the legs: frigate 5.5 by 1.4, destroyer 8 by 1.8, cruiser 10 by 2.2, carrier 9.5 by 3.0, capital 12 by 4.0. Class signatures: frigate, a knife bow and a small dorsal fin set well aft; destroyer, one amidships hump and a twin-nozzle stern; cruiser (the Vigil pattern), a continuous raised spine and a heavy three-nozzle stern block, with a command tower at the spine's forward end as 40 px detail (Qwen); carrier, a flat flight deck top line, an island bump aft, and a hangar mouth cut under the deck as 40 px detail (DeepSeek); capital, a terraced prow with a lower ram jaw and a stepped three-tier top. State, unanimous: the damage colour replaces ink from the stern forward in flat steps as hull falls (at 16 px it simplifies to a two-tone split, Qwen); the acting ship takes the ember underline and never an ember fill; unavailable dims to a third. At 16 px only the outer contour, the stern block and one spine feature may survive; if two classes read alike at 16 px, the fix is a proportion change, never added detail (Qwen).

The glyph grammar (chaired). Two stroke weights only: a main stroke at about 6 percent of glyph height and a detail stroke at about 3 percent, 1 px floor (DeepSeek). Three detail layers, standardized from the legs' competing numberings: contour (always), signature (the one detail that names the glyph; survives to 20 px, and below that only if it changes the contour), micro (32 px and the silhouette anchor only). Below 20 px at most one interior line; below 16 px, none. Every glyph must pass the label-hidden naming test on the real veil at its shelf size before adoption; a glyph that fails drops its signature layer and retests, and falls back to its round 2 form rather than holding the set hostage.

The set, per glyph (chair picks; the legs' alternatives are in legs_round3/):
- Battery: a shared recoil cradle holding three barrels, the centre barrel longer with a muzzle-brake notch at 32 px (DeepSeek). Grok's linking spine and Flash's staggered heights were the runners-up.
- Lance: a tapered projector whose emitter tip forks into two prongs, centreline inside, a capacitor block at the base at 32 px (DeepSeek). Blind Rey's cooling fins are the recorded fallback if the fork closes up at 20 px.
- Torpedo: a blunt notched nose, a transverse warhead band behind the nose, two unequal tail fins (the legs converged here: blind Rey, Qwen and DeepSeek).
- Point defence: a low mount with two barrels at deliberately unequal elevations under a broken sweep arc with range ticks (DeepSeek). The legs flag this as the highest-risk glyph for small-size confusion; it is first in line for the naming test.
- Hull: a keel plate with a raked bow, a stepped stern notch, one transverse frame rib and a centre ridge (DeepSeek). Qwen's rivet dots are rejected: at 1.5 px they break the 2 px minimum detail rule Qwen's own leg states.
- Shield: a hexagon with a single vertical meridian and an inner offset hex at larger sizes (DeepSeek); Glacier only in shield readouts, ink elsewhere.
- Brace: a heavy downward chevron with a truncated vertex and a short hold line beneath it (Qwen), quoting the shield family by mass rather than by shape.
- Focus fire: four open corner brackets with a centre dot (Flash's construction, blind Rey's extended bracket as the 32 px signature). The dot is ink while the order is merely available; ember belongs to the active state, not to standing art. The focused-fire status glyph shares this bracket family at smaller size, so the player learns one shape for attention on a target.
- Turn: an arc with an open chevron head, a pivot dot at the centre, and a fainter inner wake arc at 32 px (Qwen); square caps throughout (Flash).
- Fire for effect: a broken horizontal bracket over three staggered descending impact darts of unequal length (DeepSeek).
- End ship: a short ship-profile bar running into a vertical end-stop, a downward chevron beneath, wake dashes behind (DeepSeek). It is the only order glyph that pictures a ship, which is the point: it ends this ship's activation.
- Command: a four-pointed star with a hollow centre circle and a crosshair through it at 32 px (Qwen, with DeepSeek's orbit arc as a micro detail).
- Move: a hexagon with an arrow crossing it (blind Rey and DeepSeek agree; drawn in the v4 mockup's ship block).

Colour corrections the chair made to the legs: Flash put a standing ember dot inside the lance and the focus-fire glyph; cut. Ember marks states and the acting ship, it is not readiness decoration, and a shelf where every ready weapon glows ember has no accent left. Grok gave Fire for effect a ringed star and Command a bare star, two stars in one row; resolved by giving neither a ring and making Command the four-point star above. Grok's End ship as a bold X is rejected (it reads as close or cancel), and Flash's End ship as a square container is rejected (a box, against its own leg's refusal list). Qwen's costs were stated in agent-hours and minutes; the chair restates the pass in agent terms, following DeepSeek: one pass for the silhouettes and their 16 px reductions, one for the weapons, one for integrity and orders, one for Godot import with state variants and size contact sheets, plus one revision loop after review.

Testing before adoption (merged from the legs): a contact sheet of the whole set on the real veil at 32, 20, 16 and 12 px in ink, dimmed, ember-underlined and damage states; a label-hidden naming check (Qwen's two-second rule, Flash's 90 percent bar); a row scan for the weapons at 20 px; the five silhouettes in a row at 16 px identified by contour alone; one downsample-and-back pass to simulate shelf blur (DeepSeek). The Board should run this sheet before the set is locked, and the point defence glyph goes first.

## Recommendation: KEEL (DeepSeek's leg, chaired as drawn)

Build KEEL. One bottom decision shelf, two small top clusters, no full-width top bar, the log folded into the shelf's right end. It is the only leg that placed every rail item at coordinate level, kept the status line visible, priced its own screen share honestly (hard veil and fade counted separately), and named its real weakness (small windows) instead of waving at it. What KEEL buys, in the study's terms: the full-height left band is gone. ## Considered and not chaired

Grok's LEDGER SPLIT (improved): a full-width top bar (72 px) plus a 220 px full-width bottom bar with the fleet as a footer. Sound, and the closest cousin to KEEL; the chair takes DeepSeek's cluster argument over it (a full-width top veil buys nothing the two clusters do not) and its geometry is otherwise KEEL's with a taller bottom bar.
Blind Rey's BOOKENDS: slim top strip (64 px) with the fleet as chips in the top strip's centre, bottom strip 144 px. The fleet-in-the-top-bar idea matches Ryan's reference screenshots most literally, and it fails on capacity: chips cap out near eight ships and the top strip then crowds the Nemesis line. Worth revisiting if fleet sizes stay small.
Pure COMMAND DECK (the brief's seed (b), unanchored): rejected by every leg that addressed it, the chair included: four separate cards fragment the reading order and the floating strip veils the board's centre-bottom for no edge benefit.
