# Sunward fighter and bomber, round 2: six fresh directions (issue 757)

Refs #742

Ryan ruled Nov 14, 2026 (about 22:54 CST): "Let's try the six fresh too, just for one last sign off". These six are built for that final sign-off ALONGSIDE his round 2 picks SF2 and SB2, not instead of them. The owner's standing rules from his test rounds govern every direction: no canopy glass (an armoured sensor slit or band at most), no wings or tail planes, ordnance internal or tube-carried and never underslung, thrusters as small nozzle clusters inside armoured blocks, fighters sleeker, bombers chunkier and visibly built around what they carry. Reference family: EVE Online, Sins of a Solar Empire, Star Citizen. Sunward colours throughout: matte hammered black iron #4a4440, trim #c8501f, frost-white #f0f4f8 oath stripes, ember-orange #ff7a2a slits and bells dim like banked coals.

Files: SF3.jpg, SF4.jpg, SF5.jpg, SB3.jpg, SB4.jpg, SB5.jpg (each 720 px tall, name on the picture), plus silhouette_sheet.jpg with all six at 32 px tall, their battle-camera size (the bomber is 32.3 px on the camera; the fighter 24.6 px, so the sheet flatters the fighters slightly).

## The fighters (Ember Dart wing: guns T, "Interceptor guns", 3 shots; hp 18; speed 6; 3.2 units)

Every fighter carries exactly that one weapon and nothing else that reads as a weapon.

### SF3, the Spire Dart
One narrow forged blade, thickened only at the stern into an armoured block with two recessed ember bells. A single short radiator spire stands on the spine, ember glow at its root: the flagship's spire row reduced to one at this size. Weapon callout: the Interceptor guns are the three small muzzles recessed as one tight emitter cluster in an armoured recess at the bow (on a shallow traverse ring, DeepSeek's refinement, so the fixed look does not contradict the turret arc). One frost-white oath stripe along the blade edge. From above: a knife with one glowing spine point.

### SF4, the Hammerhead Wedge
A wide flat wedge, much broader than tall, with a broad blunt armoured nose and a recessed sensor band across it. Recessed machinery insets in the flanks (the EVE Punisher cue). Weapon callout: the Interceptor guns are three small muzzles recessed flush in a shallow armoured recess at the centre of the leading edge, on a traverse ring; nothing protrudes. The stern face carries a recessed nozzle cluster. One frost-white oath stripe across one shoulder. From above: a broad arrowhead, the widest fighter silhouette.

### SF5, the Keel Fin
A compact thick hull with a clean, nearly featureless dorsal surface; its signature is a deep ventral keel fin lined with ember glow (the radiator as keel). Weapon callout: the Interceptor guns are three muzzles recessed in an armoured recess in the bow face, between the bow shoulders. Nozzle cluster in the armoured stern block; one frost-white oath stripe along the flank. At the game's 34-degree battle camera the glowing keel is the strongest silhouette hook of the three fighters; at a strict top-down it is the plainest, which the silhouette sheet shows honestly.

## The bombers (Pyre-Lance wing: bombs T, "Bomb payload", 2 shots, pierce; hp 28; speed 5; 4.2 units)

Every bomber carries exactly that one weapon and nothing else that reads as a weapon.

### SB3, the Twin-Hull Cat
Two slim forged hulls joined by a thick central ordnance spine. Weapon callout: the Bomb payload is two large tube mouths recessed side by side in the bow face of the central spine, between the hulls, on a shallow traverse cassette; the ordnance is entirely internal. Frost-white oath stripes on the hulls' outer faces; a nozzle cluster in each hull's stern block; sensor slit on the spine. The catamaran formation read is the named risk; the spine's mass and the single shared bow face are the mitigation.

### SB4, the Magazine Block
One massive faceted block hull built around a central bomb magazine: thick slab sides, heavy armour panels, a deep armoured bow face. Weapon callout: the Bomb payload is two large tube mouths recessed in the bow face with heavy shaped armoured rims, flanking the recessed sensor slit at the centre, on a traverse cassette. Ember bell clusters recessed into the stern faces. One frost-white oath band across one shoulder. The clearest built-around-its-bombs story of the three: the hull IS the magazine.

### SB5, the Sponson Carrier
A slim central dart core (sensor band at the bow, clean spine, nozzle cluster in its stern block) carrying two fat lateral ordnance sponsons: thick rectangular armoured bomb pods, flat-topped, joined to the core by thick armoured connector blocks. Weapon callout: the Bomb payload is one large tube mouth recessed in each sponson's front face, fed from a common central magazine; nothing else. One frost-white oath stripe per sponson. Distinct from round 2's SB2: there the hull is a wide barge with a command block between flank tubes; here the core is a dart and the pods are the mass.

## The chair's picks: SF3 and SB4

Unanimous among the seated legs, and the renders bear the picks out. SF3 is the flagship's forged blade at the smallest size that can carry it: one mass, one spire, one nose cluster, and the cleanest 32 px silhouette of the fighters. SF5 is the close second and the most distinctive at the real camera angle; if Ryan wants the fighter with a signature, SF5 is the one to argue for. SB4 is the bomber the brief asked for in one sentence: a magazine with engines, its two bow mouths the whole story, immediately readable at 32 px and impossible to mistake for a fighter. SB3 and SB5 stay full options; both carry a formation or wing read risk the notes flag rather than hide.

## Production notes (render drift the sheets must correct, not copy)

- SF3's engine bells render oversized and brighter than banked coals; the sheet dims and shrinks them (R55). Its gun cluster renders in a dorsal bow recess rather than the nose face; either placement satisfies the callout, the sheet picks one. Glyph-like hull decals are generator noise, not markings.
- SF4's stern nozzle cluster renders slightly proud of the stern face; the sheet recesses it. An earlier SF4 render carried a dorsal turret and stern tube rows; it was regenerated, and the delivered picture is the corrected one.
- SF5 carries a small machinery stub on one flank near the stern; it is not a wing and the sheet may keep or drop it.
- SB3 renders two small dorsal mounts on the hull sterns that read as minor turrets. The design has NO such fittings (the bomber's only weapon is the spine's bomb cassette); the sheet omits them. Its first render carried dorsal ordnance racks and was regenerated for the same reason.
- SB5's connector blocks render flat; the sheet keeps them thick and armoured so they never read as wings. Its core bells render brighter than banked coals; dim per R55. Its first render carried yellow dorsal blocks and a chimneyed spine; it was regenerated, and the delivered picture is the corrected one.
- All six: panel detail must stay bold and simple at build; fine greeble at this scale reads as a shrunken capital ship (both design legs flagged this independently).
