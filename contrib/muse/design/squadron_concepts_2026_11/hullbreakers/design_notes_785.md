# Hullbreaker bomber, round 1: two directions (issue 785)

Refs #785

Per-faction squadron craft (R253). The Ashfall Hullbreakers launch the bomber wing from the hulk-carrier (hangar 2) and the Warlord's Pyre-Barge (hangar 2). The standing craft rules from the Sunward rounds govern both directions: no canopy glass, no aero wings or tail fins, nothing hung under the hull, ordnance internal or tube-carried, thrusters recessed, weapons exactly the data's and nothing else that reads as a weapon, bombers chunky and visibly built around what they carry. Hullbreaker language: the Scrap Brick of A16 to A20; welded patchwork plate, rust hull (#6a2c22, trim #b8402a, glow #e0402c heat orange), mismatched salvaged sections, exposed weld seams, heat slits. Chains and cages are off the language. Engines are thrusters, never "rockets": the faction's rockets are capital torpedoes, and the word on a bomber's stern invites an extra-weapon read (DeepSeek's terminology point, adopted).

## The bomber (Bomber wing: bombs T, "Bomb payload", 2 shots, pierce; hp 28; speed 5; size 1.6, drawn 3.84)

Every bomber carries exactly that one weapon and nothing else that reads as a weapon. The tube pair sits in a welded traverse cassette in both directions, so the fixed look never contradicts the turret arc (DeepSeek's arc point, adopted as a stated term).

### KB1, the Flying Magazine

One massive welded brick built around its bomb bay: thick mismatched rust plates, one flank plated visibly heavier with a salvaged plate of a different tone, weld seams everywhere, heat slits glowing dull orange along the seams. Weapon callout: the Bomb payload is two large tube mouths recessed side by side in the bow face on a welded traverse cassette, enlarged and separated with a welded bridge between them so they read as two dark mouths at 32 px (DeepSeek's refinement, adopted), heavy irregular rims welded from cut plate; the whole hull is the magazine behind them. A sensor slit under a welded brow plate at the bow. Stern: recessed thruster nozzles inside a cut-down salvaged engine block. From above: a brick with two dark mouths.

### KB2, the Split Hulk

Two mismatched scrap blocks joined by a thick welded ordnance spine that extends forward into one blunt welded prow: the port block a cut-down hauler bow (blunt, taller), the starboard block a lower slab of layered plate, the gaps weld-closed so the craft reads as one mass (DeepSeek's refinement, adopted: spine forward into the prow, blocks pulled back from the bow, asymmetry kept in block height and plate tone, never in the weapon face). Weapon callout: the Bomb payload is two large tube mouths recessed in the prow face on a welded traverse cassette; the spine is the shared magazine. Recessed thruster nozzles in each block's stern. From above: a lopsided mass with one shared bow face. The named risk is the catamaran formation read; the prow's mass and the single weapon face are the mitigation. Flash would DROP this direction outright on the silhouette test; the chair keeps it as a live option with the refinement above, since the brief asks Ryan for two options per faction and the refined shape answers the read it fears.

## The chair's pick: KB1

Unanimous among the seated legs. KB1 is the bomber the brief asks for in one sentence: a magazine with engines, its two bow mouths the whole story, immediately readable at 32 px and impossible to mistake for a fighter or a wreck. KB2 stays a full option; its lopsided scrap character is real, and its first render (a cannon-like side profile with stray circular openings on the rear block) was regenerated into the delivered prow-forward picture.

## Production notes (render drift the sheets must correct, not copy)

- KB1's stern block renders a row of three small circular ports; they are thruster nozzles and the sheet keeps them small, recessed and clearly at the stern, so they never compete with the bow mouths.
- KB2's delivered render is the second generation; the first read as a hand cannon and carried extra circular openings on the rear block's face, which the design does not have. The sheet omits any round opening except the two prow mouths and the stern nozzles.
- Both: weld seams and patch plates stay bold; fine rivet-and-pipe detail saturates at 32 px (Flash's density caution, adopted as a build note).
