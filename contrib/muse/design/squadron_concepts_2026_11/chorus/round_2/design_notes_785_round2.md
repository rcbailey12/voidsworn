# Issue 785, round 2: CS1 variations (Chorus spore swarm)

Owner's commission (Nov 15, 2026): take CS1, the Spore Mother (round 1), and spin off three variations WITHOUT THE GIANT CLEAR SACS: on the base render the two big translucent amber domes read as eyes. Every variation keeps exactly two acid sacs as the weapon (sporeb T, "Acid sacs", 2 shots, pierce, acid; hp 26, speed 5, evasion 25; size 1.6, drawn 3.84), but changes their shape, placement or exposure so the eye read is gone. Chorus colours as round 1 (flesh #57364c, bone #a4913a, amber sacs). One correction carried through all three on DeepSeek's catch: the sacs are translucent fluid, never emissive; no variation's amber glows.

## The three variations

### CS1A, the Seam Sacs (CS1A.jpg)
The sacs grown almost entirely under the skin: each flank carries one long raised blister ridge, flesh-covered and visibly fuller than the pod around it, with translucent amber showing only in a narrow recessed seam slit along the ridge's crest, the fluid's depth visible in the slit (DeepSeek's refinement, adopted: broader recessed seam, exaggerated swelling, so the pair reads as sacs and not decoration). The bone spine, eye-spot row, flesh buds and stern orifice cluster are CS1's.
Weapon callouts: (1) the two seam sacs, one per flank, amber fluid in the crest seam of each swollen ridge, the swarm's only weapon; (2) eye-spots along the bow ridge; (3) the muscle-ringed orifice cluster at the stern.

### CS1B, the Gill Bladders (CS1B.jpg)
The sacs as elongated ridged bladders lying along the flanks like gills: two long low bladders, each a third of its flank, plum-skinned and crossed by thick flesh ribs, with wide deep grooves between the ribs showing the translucent amber fluid (DeepSeek's refinement, adopted: wider, deeper grooves and more bladder volume, so the ribs read as flesh bands over continuous sacs, never vents). Elongated and ribbed, so the eye read is impossible. The rest of the pod is CS1's.
Weapon callouts: (1) the two gill bladders, one per flank, amber fluid in the grooves, the swarm's only weapon; (2) eye-spots along the bow ridge; (3) the muscle-ringed orifice cluster at the stern.

### CS1C, the Keel Pouch (CS1C.jpg)
The sacs carried underneath: a ribbed ventral pouch along the belly holds both sacs side by side, amber showing between the pouch's ribs. The pouch is grown flesh inside the body's own outline: no neck, no gap, no separate volume, and its side walls rise into the lower flanks so the amber ribs break the lower silhouette and read at the game's 34-degree battle camera, not only from below (DeepSeek's refinement, adopted). The flanks stay clean flesh and bone; from directly above there is almost no amber at all.
Weapon callouts: (1) the keel pouch's two sacs, amber fluid between the belly ribs, the swarm's only weapon; (2) eye-spots along the bow ridge; (3) the muscle-ringed orifice cluster at the stern.

## Council

- Blind Rey (filed before the pack went out): leans CS1B; CS1A's seams risk reading as trim; CS1C the boldest, standing or falling on the grown-not-hung distinction.
- DeepSeek (design leg, DeepThink on, Search on, verified both ends): REFINE on all three (the refinements adopted above, plus the translucent-not-emissive correction across the set). Leans CS1B.
- Flash (design leg, Flash-Lite verified both ends): KEEP CS1A, REFINE CS1B (ribs overlapping the grooves tightly, adopted in the sheet's rib weight), DROP CS1C (in its reading, a ventral pouch breaks the nothing-hung-under-the-hull rule even grown). Leans CS1A. The chair records the DROP without adopting it, as in round 1: CS1C's grown-versus-hung distinction is exactly the call the picture exists for the owner to judge.
- Qwen: seat empty (Qwen3.8-Max daily cap, still not reset). Grok: seat empty (bot wall, 39th consecutive).

## Chair's lean

CS1B. DeepSeek and the blind leg agree, and the render settles it: the gill bladders are unmistakably the weapon, they are large and flank-mounted like the base's sacs, and no arrangement of them can read as eyes. CS1A is the most elegant and the riskiest (its delivered render shows how close the seam sits to decoration). CS1C works better than its risk suggested: the render's pouch is continuous with the belly and its amber breaks the lower flank line as specified; it remains the owner's call on the hung rule.
