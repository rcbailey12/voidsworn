# Leg: Rey (blind, from the pack alone)

## Recommendation

Keep the 30 by 22 canvas. Do not grow the board. Spend the change on shape: make the shaped fields (hourglass, corridor, archipelago, lanes) a normal part of the rotation instead of rare rolls, and treat the square-arena approach as the one real pace problem to fix. The bigger board is the only option that made every pace number worse, and speed did not buy it back at +1 or +2. That kills Ryan's floated fix in its stated form, and the pack's dose-response (12.5, 12.0, 12.0 median activations to contact at +0, +1, +2 speed) is the strongest evidence in the study: contact on the big board waits for a full round of movement no matter how fast the ships are, because the fleets start at the edges and the gap grew by half.

## Why

- Pace lives in deployment geometry, not board area. Today the strip opens 16 hexes apart, about one move plus one long gun, so contact lands inside 2 activations on every map kind. The measured slow spots are the square approach (median 9) and defend (16.4% move-only). Neither is fixed by a bigger board; the square approach is an arena weighting and deployment-angle question, and defend is the station footprint plus the crossing it forces.
- Shapes are free texture. On today's canvas the four shaped fields cost nothing in pace (contact 1, move-only 12.6 to 14.9% against 13.1% open) and each changes how a fight feels: corridor is a fast head-on brawl, hourglass gives a chokepoint and two home lobes, archipelago breaks the fight into island duels, lanes makes gates worth holding. That is exactly the "cohesive and space-like, not just more room" quality Ryan asked for, and the generators already exist in both rule copies, so the build cost is weights and encounter tables, not new systems.
- The big board's one honest gain is room for the largest late-sector fleets (up to 20 enemy ships). The pack did not measure a 20-ship battle on either canvas, so I flag that as the missing measurement rather than a reason to grow: if crowding is the real complaint at sector 3 scale, the answer might be a bigger board only for the biggest battles, not for every battle.

## Alternatives

1. Shaped fields plus a deployment fix on square: keep today's canvas and shapes, and pull square-arena deployments inward (or weight strip higher for rout) so the approach round shrinks. This is the smallest change that touches the one measured delay. It was not itself measured, so it should be simmed before it is ruled.
2. The big board, only if a 20-ship crowding measurement shows today's canvas actually breaks down at sector 3 scale, and only with deployment moved inward at the same time. Edge deployment on a 38 by 26 canvas is measured and bad; an inward-deployment variant is unmeasured and would need its own run.

## What I would not do

- Lanes as a default. It produced the longest battles (11.5 rounds) and the study's only timeout; as an occasional roll it is a good gate fight, as a staple it drags.
- Any fleet-wide speed rise. At +2 it bought nothing on contact and it taxes balance everywhere else (torpedoes, ramming, speed-granting admirals and modules).

## Pack criticisms

- Win rates are rightly excluded, but the mirror-fleet bot pairing means the move-only shares are lower bounds for human play; the pack says this and I agree with the direction claim only.
- The defend row uses mirror fleets, but a real defend attacker is generated weaker or stronger by sector; the 16.4% is a geometry signal, not a balance verdict.
- Missing: a sector-3-scale crowding run (14 to 20 ships) on both canvases, and an inward-deployment run on the big canvas. Both are cheap and either could change the size answer.
