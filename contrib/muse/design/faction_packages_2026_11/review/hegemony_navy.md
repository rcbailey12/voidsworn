# The Hegemony Navy

Who they are
The fleet of the Registry: a vast intelligence at Meridian that links every citizen, ship, and officer, plans their careers, predicts their losses, and keeps those losses within acceptable limits.
Its people are safe, comfortable, proudly cybernetic, and counted.
The horror under the polish is Vesper: proof that when the numbers say so, the Registry will archive a whole frontier and call it acceptable. Twenty five years ago its survey fleet captured the core V-2 Vyre, and Hegemony shield and ion technology comes from that core.

How they look
People: composed humans with tasteful implants: a cortical link port at the temple with a faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Pale grey high collared uniforms and gloves, calm to the point of eerie.
Ships: light grey wedge hulls, steel blue trim, blue white windows, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the fleet, small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.
Colours: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

How they play
The network is the weapon: fire syncs near the flagship, so every battle is a geometry problem.
Keep formation tight, strip shields with ion, launch fighter screens from carriers, use cheap retro movement to correct mistakes.
Bad at: the flagship itself. When the anchor is threatened, the formation's reason to exist is threatened.

Admirals
Ilsa Kassar (Synchronized broadside): the formation purist; maximum sync, minimum improvisation.
Dace Vorn (Interdiction strike): the interdictor; cut off stragglers, punish anything that leaves formation.
Corin Reyes (Launch the strike group): the carrier marshal; fighter screens, layered strikes, patience.
Oskar Maren (Shield wall order): the shield wall; deflectors layered, losses priced at zero, ground given one hex at a time.

Decisions

Question 10. Who hunts the Hegemony as their Nemesis? (Drafted in the package: the Hullbreakers.)
A. An Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader who has raided Registry supply lines for a decade. Fights to steal one ship, flees a losing fight, returns stronger with stolen scrap bolted on.
B. An Eclipse commander. A machine civilisation with no oversight, which the Registry finds instructive.
C. A Sunward Crusade admiral, the secession wound come to collect.
Chair pick: A. A thief the projections cannot price is the Registry's truest problem, and the stolen ship returning when the raider dies gives the campaign a personal thread.

Question 11. How present is the Registry in battle?
A. A calm channel voice pricing battles as they happen, plus a quiet UI overlay stating the projected result and updating when the battle leaves the projection. You hear losses called acceptable while you cause them, and you watch the model watch you.
B. The voice only, no overlay. The horror is audible, the screen stays clean.
C. Neither. The Registry speaks only through written Registry Orders between battles; the horror stays on paper.
Chair pick: A. Two legs picked voice and overlay as separate yeses; one leg (Argon) picked B to avoid building a one faction HUD element. The overlay is the faction's fantasy made visible every round, so the chair takes A and notes the build cost is real.

Question 12. Can a Hegemony admiral refuse a Registry order on screen?
A. Yes, once per admiral, as their spotlight beat, at a stated cost. Each admiral gets one visible moment of being a person, and it costs them.
B. No, refusal exists only in backstory and private moments. On screen, everyone obeys.
C. Yes, and repeated refusal changes the run 5 finale.
Chair pick: A. Once each, at a cost, keeps the Registry strong and the admirals human. Surfaced by two legs; it is the package's own question (its printed pick is also A).
