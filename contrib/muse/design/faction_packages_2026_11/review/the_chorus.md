# The Chorus

Who they are
Not a people: a living weapon the Fleshwrights grew to destroy Eclipse, and it worked too well. It merged, multiplied, and ate its makers.
Inside the one swarm, two throats argue: Hunger, which is the swarm itself, and Memory, which is what is left of the Fleshwrights it consumed.
It is coming to the Hollow Sun to eat the other five cores and finish the war it was grown for.

How they look
Beings: grown organisms with no face, eyes, or limbs to recognise: glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, and bile, nothing on the surface that recalls a person.
The four admirals are four unique creatures, each a different aspect of the Chorus as a hero admiral, not variations on one brood pattern (ruling).
Ships: grown, not built: ridges, pores, veins, glowing sacs, tendrils and brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue, no panels or pipes.
Colours: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow green light.

How they play
Appetite and numbers: no shields at all, cheap ships, win by regrowth and mass.
Flesh knits back every round, broods spill more bodies than the enemy can kill, acid eats the shields and armour that stop everyone else.
Bad at: a focused enemy who kills faster than flesh can knit and refuses to spread fire.

Admirals
Matriarch of Nine Throats (Spawning surge): the brood admiral; spawn volume, swarm strikes, pressure everywhere at once.
Razormaw (Blood frenzy): the frenzy admiral; close fast, chew through one ship, let the panic spread.
Mending Throng (Regrowth tide): the regrowth admiral; outlast everything, make damage feel wasted.
Voice of the Deep (Spitting volley): the volley admiral; the swarm as artillery, spitting in one breath from every throat.

Decisions

Question 13. Who hunts the Chorus as its Nemesis? (Drafted in the package: Eclipse.)
A. An Eclipse Archivist who remembers the Fleshwrights. The enemy the swarm was grown to eat, across nine hundred years of study: patient, never retreats, returns once after death.
B. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, the makers' kin. Family, who apologise while they kill you.
C. A Hegemony line admiral. Many, neat, and all the same taste.
Chair pick: A. The Chorus exists for one war, and its Nemesis should be that war's other side. An Archivist that remembers the makers' voices is also the one rival that makes Memory argue with Hunger.

Question 14. Are Chorus admirals the same growths every run, or regrown?
A. The same throats persist, regrown from kept flesh, so levels and scars carry naturally. The Matriarch you level is the Matriarch you keep.
B. Each run grows new admirals, with inherited growths only. New faces of the swarm each run, levels carried as growths.
C. The Matriarch persists, and the other three are regrown.
Chair pick: A. Persistence matches the digestion idiom: what the swarm eats, it keeps, including its commanders. Admiral levelling then reads as the swarm growing a better throat.

Question 15. May the swarm eat a core mid campaign, as a story event?
A. Yes, once, in sector 3: eat a core you choose not to sync with, gain a permanent growth, and lose that core's protocols for the run. One real, costly choice about what the swarm is for.
B. No, cores are eaten only in the finale. The campaign stays clean and the hunger peaks at the end.
C. Yes, but only Crucible grows, with no mechanical effect.
Chair pick: A. A hunger that never gets to choose is only a description; this makes it a decision, and the lost protocols keep the price honest. It is the package's own question (its printed pick is also A). One leg (Argon) picked B to keep core mechanics identical across factions.
