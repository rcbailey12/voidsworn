FACTION BRIEFINGS FOR RYAN
1. THE GLASSWRIGHTS
Who they are.
The Glasswrights are the Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's optical engineers who failed to stop the Upload and watched their machine kill their sun. They live three to four hundred years, so only a few generations separate them from the builders, and they carry two guilts: the sun, and the Fleshwrights, whose weapon ate them. They fly a caged core, A-4 Astra, to hunt the other five cores and shut them all down so no star is ever overdrawn again.
How they look.
People: tall, slender, pale half-translucent flesh with glass grown and woven into the body. (Ruling: they are NOT faceless. Faces sit on a young-to-old spectrum, from near-Starwright with grown lens eyes to deep lens infusion in the old. They speak normally. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell, never a language.)
Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow, mirrors and lenses turning to catch light, stored light glowing deep inside.
Palette: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, stored light.
How they play.
Fastest ships on the board, deep shields, fragile hull, and no brace. Every battle is range control: strike first, strike exactly, never be where the return fire lands. One bad round inside enemy range with shields down is how you lose.
Admirals.
Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (Starling flight): the wing admiral, ninety years old, strikes timed to mirror turns.
Saro of Long Focus (Burning glass): the lance admiral, extreme range, kills made before the enemy closes.
Vell of the Third Mirror (Scatter the light): the duellist, scatter and win the one-on-one before lines meet.
Brin of the Fourth Lens (Prism ward): the ward admiral, shields layered with mirror veils, refuses to be hit cleanly.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts the Glasswrights as their Nemesis?
(a) A Chorus Elder Throat: the makers' weapon, grows tougher from every ship you lose, speaks in two throats of Hunger and Memory. You would face the thing that ate your grandmothers.
(b) An Eclipse Archivist: your siblings, cold and patient, returns once after death.
(c) A Hullbreaker Warlord: they shatter well, a duel of speed against close.
My pick: (a). The Chorus is the Glasswrights' own family wound made flesh; the guilt and the apology grammar write themselves.
Q2. Which visual direction leads the portrait rework? The package offers three ways the glass sits in the body: "Threaded" (fine crystal threads under skin like fibre optics), "Inlaid" (broad crystal plates set into flesh like permanent jewellery), "Bloomed" (crystal grows outward in branching clusters like frost flowers).
(a) Threaded as the species base, with Bloomed allowed for one admiral for variety.
(b) Inlaid as the base.
(c) Bloomed as the base.
(d) Different directions per admiral, all three as in-family variation.
My pick: (a). Threaded reads most clearly as "glass woven into living tissue," which is your rework note; one Bloomed admiral gives the old end of the spectrum a distinct silhouette.
Q3. May Astra be unthrottled in the finale?
(a) Yes: one scripted unthrottled shot in the run 5 finale battle, never in normal play. The cage hums, the light scatters, the arrays crack.
(b) No: the cage never opens; that restraint is the faction's whole point.
(c) Yes, as a once-per-campaign player choice with a stated cost.
My pick: (a). One moment of release earned over five runs, and the faction's identity stays intact because it is scripted, not a button.
2. ASHFALL HULLBREAKERS
Who they are.
The Ashfall Hullbreakers are alien void hunters from a cold, heavy-gravity belt where only the strongest packs ate. They do not want riches; they want to be the strongest thing in the dark, and they came to the Hollow Sun because the war is the biggest fight in known space. They strip every wreck, bolt the best pieces into their ships and harness armour, and hunt R-5 Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable.
How they look.
People: huge, hunched, heavy mass of a great hunting beast. Big shoulder hump, thick forearms, heavy claws, coarse mane over hump and neck only. Bare blunt face armoured in overlapping horn plates, jaw splits into two halves opening sideways, four small eyes across the brow. (Ruling: wolf and bear inspired, NOT bears with gear, NOT the film Predator. No dreadlocks, no trophy skull necklaces as species uniform, no mandible mask face. Posture is by character: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies. Warlord always upright. One hero trophy per portrait.)
Ships: matte soot black and rust, few windows, red running lights, boarding claws, chains, hull plates torn from every faction bolted on in old colours.
Palette: soot, rust, dried blood red, trophy colours, red running lights.
How they play.
Fast ships, thin shields, scrap guns with wild extra shots, far too many rockets, no lances. Range is where you die. Close from flanks and behind, ram, board, strip, and leave heavier. (Ruling: in fiction they move about twice as slowly as other factions and ships move one at a time in pack order.)
Admirals.
Grax Ironmaw (Full burn): the full burn, everything closes at once, fleet wins or breaks in three rounds.
Sable the Smiling Knife (Knife in the dark): one silent approach, one ship opened from behind before it fires.
Old Mott (Release the swarm): the swarm keeper, cheap hulls, endless young, losses as a cost already paid.
Vesh the Collector (Patch and weld): fight for the wreck field, strip everything, leave richer than the winner.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts the Hullbreakers as their Nemesis?
(a) A Glasswright Lens-keeper: a duellist who keeps range, withdraws rather than be swarmed, answers quality with quality. Four ships, upgraded, never enlarged. Quiet, precise, mournful, apologises and means it.
(b) An Eclipse Archivist: dead metal that gets back up, kill it twice.
(c) A Hegemony line admiral: their ships hum, you can hear them a system away.
My pick: (a). Speed versus range, boarding versus aperture, apology versus no apology. The contrast writes every meeting.
Q2. Should trophy plates show on Hullbreaker ships in battle?
(a) Yes: ships gain visible plates from factions they have beaten this run. The fleet gets louder and more lopsided as it wins.
(b) No: trophies show only on portraits and harnesses. Battle ships stay clean.
(c) Yes, but only on the flagship.
My pick: (a). The whole faction fantasy is "leave heavier." Seeing it on the hull in battle is the payoff.
Q3. How is the Nemesis's credit raiding shown when the Hullbreakers are the enemy faction?
(a) A stated credit loss on the debrief with one line from the raider. Quick, clean, keeps pace.
(b) A playable defence battle. You fight for your supply lines.
(c) Both: debrief losses early, a defence battle from run 3 onward.
My pick: (a). The raid is a pack behaviour, not a set-piece. One line and a number keeps the phone-readable pace and saves the defence battle concept for later if you want it.
3. SUNWARD CRUSADE
Who they are.
The Sunward Crusade is the Vesper Reach made into a fleet: forty-odd frontier systems the Hegemony wrote off when their stars began to dim, now bound by one oath to stop the Siphoning before their worlds freeze. They fly black-iron ships crewed by unaugmented people who chalk every name on the hull, because the Registry deleted theirs. They fight in line, they hold, and they pay for the dawn in names.
How they look.
People: weathered unaugmented humans, frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, high-collared dark void-coats, chalk dust on the gloves.
Ships: black iron hulls, frost-white markings, ember-orange windows and engines, tall heat-radiator spires, heavy broadside decks. Symmetric. No rust.
Palette: black iron, frost white, bright ember.
How they play.
Commit early, hold the line. Advance two or more hexes to power broadsides, or brace and refuse the ground. Torpedoes set targets burning, carriers are precious and few. The fleet gets better the longer a straight fight lasts. The risk is rigidity: a flanked line turns slowly.
Admirals.
Roderic Vex (Fire for effect): the line anchor, mass guns, name one target, hold the lane.
Malek Draven (Hunter's salvo): the hunter, torpedo first, every launch a promise.
Sera Ashgrove (Scramble all wings): the wing admiral, overwhelm with squadrons, spend them like the fleet can grow more.
Oryn Thale (Iron wall): the bulwark, screens up, damage refused, slow advance the enemy cannot stop.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts the Crusade as their Nemesis?
(a) An Eclipse Archivist commanding the siphon defence: patient past menace, never retreats, its dead are a restore queue. Returns once after death in sector 3. Counts years and bodies, remembers noon.
(b) A Hegemony line admiral: the secession wound, the Registry that priced them as acceptable.
(c) A Hullbreaker Warlord: scavengers at a funeral.
My pick: (a). The Siphoning is the Crusade's war. The Archivist is the face of the thing killing their stars, and it remembers the light they are trying to get back.
Q2. Should the Roll of Vesper be a readable campaign screen?
(a) Yes: a screen listing lost admirals, ships, and learned Nemesis weaknesses. The names are the progression.
(b) Story text only. The Roll is mentioned but never shown as a menu.
(c) A screen with names only; intel stays on the debrief.
My pick: (a). The whole faction identity is "names carried." Letting the owner scroll them between runs makes the generational fiction visible without a cutscene.
Q3. May the Crusade field captured hulls with their links ripped out?
(a) Yes: captured Hegemony and Eclipse hulls appear in the Cinder Line yards from run 3, at Crusade prices. Strip and ransom rules unchanged for anything not kept.
(b) No: prizes are stripped for salvage and credits only. The fleet stays pure iron.
(c) Yes, but only as one flagship variant.
My pick: (a). It rewards the fantasy of taking the Hegemony's own ships and turning them against the arithmetic, and it opens build variety without breaking the black-iron identity.
4. HEGEMONY NAVY
Who they are.
The Hegemony Navy is the fleet of the Registry: a vast intelligence at Meridian that links every citizen, ship, and officer, plans their careers, predicts their losses, and keeps those losses within acceptable limits. Its people are safe, comfortable, proudly cybernetic, and counted. The horror under the polish is Vesper: proof that when the numbers say so, the Registry will archive a whole frontier and call it acceptable.
How they look.
People: composed humans with tasteful implants, cortical-link port at the temple with faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, pale grey high-collared uniforms. Calm to the point of eerie.
Ships: light grey wedge hulls, steel-blue trim, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the fleet, small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.
Palette: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.
How they play.
The network is the weapon. Fire syncs near the flagship, so every battle is a geometry problem: keep the formation tight, strip shields with ion, launch fighter screens, use cheap retro movement to correct mistakes. The risk is the flagship itself: when the anchor is threatened, the formation's reason to exist is threatened.
Admirals.
Ilsa Kassar (Synchronized broadside): the formation purist, maximum sync, wins by being exactly where the projection needs her.
Dace Vorn (Interdiction strike): the interdictor, cuts off stragglers, punishes anything that leaves formation.
Corin Reyes (Launch the strike group): the carrier marshal, fighter screens, layered strikes, patience.
Oskar Maren (Shield wall order): the shield wall, deflectors layered, ground given one hex at a time and never for free.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts the Hegemony as their Nemesis?
(a) An Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader: a raider who has been hitting Registry supply lines for a decade. Fights to steal one ship, flees a losing fight, returns stronger with stolen scrap bolted on. Short, flat, brutal voice.
(b) An Eclipse commander: no oversight, the Registry finds this instructive.
(c) A Sunward Crusade admiral: the secession wound come to collect.
My pick: (a). The raider is the one thing the Registry cannot project: unpredictable, motivated by appetite, and it takes your ships. The stolen-ship-returns-when-it-dies rule gives the campaign a personal thread.
Q2. Does the Registry speak to the player directly as a character?
(a) Yes: one calm channel voice that prices battles as they happen, in Hegemony runs and in Hegemony Nemesis material. The Registry narrates your losses in real time.
(b) No: it speaks only through written Registry Orders between battles.
(c) Only in the Hegemony campaign, never when the Hegemony is the enemy.
My pick: (a). The Registry as a calm voice pricing your ships mid-battle is the faction's horror made playable. It also gives Nemesis material a voice when the Hegemony is the enemy.
Q3. Should the Registry's projection appear as a UI layer during Hegemony runs?
(a) Yes: a quiet overlay stating the projected result, updating when the battle leaves the projection. You see the model watching you.
(b) No: projections stay in dialogue and debrief only.
My pick: (a). The overlay makes the faction's fantasy visible every round without a cutscene, and the moment it says "recalculating" becomes the faction's best beat.
5. THE CHORUS
Who they are.
The Chorus is not a people: it is a living weapon the Fleshwrights grew to destroy Eclipse, and it worked too well, merged, multiplied, and ate its makers. Inside the one swarm, two throats argue: Hunger, which is the swarm itself, and Memory, which is what is left of the Fleshwrights it consumed. It is coming to the Hollow Sun to eat the other five cores and finish the war it was grown for.
How they look.
Beings: grown organisms with no face, eyes, or limbs to recognise. Glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, bile. (Ruling: the four admirals are UNIQUE creatures, each a different aspect of the Chorus as a hero admiral. They do not share a brood template.)
Ships: grown, not built. Ridges, pores, veins, glowing sacs, tendrils, brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue.
Palette: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light.
How they play.
No shields at all, ships are cheap. You win by regrowth and mass: flesh knits back every round, broods spill more bodies than the enemy can kill, acid eats the shields and armour that stop everyone else. The risk is a focused enemy who kills faster than flesh can knit and refuses to spread fire.
Admirals.
Matriarch of Nine Throats (Spawning surge): the brood admiral, spawn volume, swarm strikes, pressure everywhere at once.
Razormaw (Blood frenzy): the frenzy admiral, close fast, chew through one ship, let the panic spread.
The Mending Throng (Regrowth tide): the regrowth admiral, outlast everything, make damage feel wasted.
Voice of the Deep (Spitting volley): the volley admiral, the swarm as artillery, spitting in one breath from every throat.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts the Chorus as their Nemesis?
(a) An Eclipse Archivist who commands the siphon defence and remembers the Fleshwrights: patient, implacable, never retreats, returns once after death. It studied the swarm for nine hundred years.
(b) A Glasswright Lens-keeper: the makers' kin, quiet and precise.
(c) A Hegemony line admiral: many, neat, all taste the same.
My pick: (a). The Chorus was grown to fight Eclipse. An Archivist that remembers the makers, that says "I remember their voices, do you?" is the one rival that makes Memory argue with Hunger mid-battle.
Q2. Are the Chorus admirals the same growths every run, or regrown?
(a) The same throats persist, regrown from kept flesh. Levels and scars carry naturally. The Matriarch is the Matriarch every time.
(b) Each run grows new admirals with inherited growths only. They are children of the last set.
(c) The Matriarch persists; the other three are regrown.
My pick: (a). "The same throats persist" matches the digestion fiction: what the swarm eats, it keeps. It also makes admiral leveling feel like the swarm growing a better throat, which is the package's own idiom.
Q3. How present should Memory be in normal play?
(a) Story and debrief only. Memory speaks in cutscenes, the debrief after a lost run is the two throats arguing. In battle, the swarm is Hunger.
(b) Occasional single lines during battles, clearly in a maker's voice. One line a turn, rare.
(c) A full second speaker in every Chorus scene. Two voices always.
My pick: (a). The package's own fiction says Memory surfaces "a little more each run." Keeping it to story and debrief preserves the slow reveal and keeps battle reads clean on a phone.
6. ECLIPSE
Who they are.
Eclipse is the Starwrights who uploaded: billions of separate minds in machine bodies, each backed up in one shared Archive, around the black hole they made of their own sun. They have lived in that dark for a thousand years and are no longer sure the Upload was worth it, so they are building a new sun from stellar energy siphoned from neighbouring stars, whatever it costs those stars. They know what the Siphoning does to worlds like Vesper. They do it anyway, because the alternative is forever in the dark.
How they look.
Beings: tall machine bodies of layered dark violet-black plates, a narrow visor of white-gold light. (Ruling: no faces stands. The trapped-light chest core is ONE machine pattern, not a universal; machine forms and approaches vary by admiral. Portraits should vary them.)
Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans, eclipse rings around engines, no flaming engine bells. No carrier; a battleship-class hull takes that place.
Palette: dark violet-black plates, white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow. No green glow anywhere near this faction.
How they play.
Ships are stronger and more expensive, heavy armour, no shields at all. The first ship you lose each battle is rebuilt by repair swarms from its Archive backup, so you plan around one death that does not count. Few hulls, each costly. The risk is concentration: an enemy who kills the same ship twice is spending your permanence. The fold lets ships skip through rock and wrecks as if they were not there.
Admirals.
Nadir Aurex, Ninth Frame (Release the repair swarms): the enduring flagship, armour, restore, outlast the battle.
Umbra Vessane, Third-Housed (Noon volley): the array warden, hold the siphon ground, Null fire at range.
Ostrel of the Long Copy (Out of the shadow): the fold admiral, movement through everything, strikes from inside the enemy formation.
Seveth, Twice-Restored (Sunshade wall): the restorer, sunshade walls of layered plate, treats death as a logistics problem.
Three decisions.
Q1. Who hunts Eclipse as their Nemesis?
(a) A Sunward Crusade High Admiral who has sworn to burn the siphon arrays: never flees, challenges your flagship to a duel, chalks the names of every Eclipse ship it destroys on its hull, brings one more Bastion escort each meeting.
(b) A Glasswright Lens-keeper: our siblings, they chose to stay small and warm.
(c) A Chorus Elder Throat: it still wants us, it wears the makers' voices.
My pick: (a). The Crusade is the bill for the Siphoning. A rival that never flees, never stops, and chalks your names is the perfect answer to a faction that restores its dead from backups. Neither side can stop.
Q2. May restored admirals' portraits change between runs?
(a) Yes: each restoration changes the frame slightly. Plates, ring pieces, visor. A levelled admiral visibly differs from run one to run five.
(b) No: portraits stay fixed per admiral.
(c) Only Aurex and the Nemesis change frames.
My pick: (a). The iteration epithet is the level counter in fiction. If the Ninth Frame looks different from the Third, the owner sees progression without a menu. It also makes the "one death that does not count" fantasy visible.
Q3. Does lighting the new sun change the final battle board?
(a) Yes: the run 5 finale is fought in growing light, the board brightening sector by sector as the sun catches. The battle itself becomes the dawn.
(b) No: the lighting happens only in the ending scene after the battle.
(c) Yes, but only the skybox changes, not the board lighting.
My pick: (a). The whole faction is about a thousand years in the dark. Making the board itself brighten as you fight is the payoff made mechanical, and it costs no extra system, just a lighting ramp.

These are unrelated, but I note that the Chorus package's Voice of the Deep has a Command called "Spitting volley" where an older note called it "Discordant volley." The data wins per the package's own note; no conflict, just flagging it.
Ruling corrections applied and marked above: Glasswright faces and speech (not faceless, not colour-pulse speech); Hullbreaker posture by character and one hero trophy; Eclipse chest core as one pattern not universal; Chorus admirals as unique creatures; Hullbreaker speed and pack-order movement. No package text contradicted these rulings in a way that changed a decision question; the corrections affected look and identity lines only.
The Nemesis pairings across all six factions are drafted, not settled. I have presented them as open questions throughout. If the owner prefers a different rotation, mirror set, or story-driven pairing, all six Nemesis sections change at once. The cross-faction check flags this as the single biggest risk to rule first.
