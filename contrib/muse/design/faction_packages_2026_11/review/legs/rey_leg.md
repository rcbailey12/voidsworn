# Six faction one pagers (Rey leg, blind)

## The Glasswrights

Who they are
The Glasswrights are the Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's own optical engineers.
They tried to stop the Upload, failed, and watched their machine kill their sun.
Now they hunt all six Lattice cores to shut them down for good, their own core, Astra, last.

How they look
People: tall, slender, pale, half translucent beings, with glass grown and woven into living flesh.
They are organic beings who evolved to shape glass, not beings made of glass (ruling).
Faces are real faces, on a young to old spectrum: near Starwright faces with craft grown lens eyes in the young, deep lens infusion in the old (ruling).
They speak normally; colour catching brighter in the glass is an emotion tell only, never speech (ruling).
Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, stored light glowing deep inside.
Colours: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edges, pale green white stored light.

How they play
The fastest fleet on the board: deep shields, fragile hulls underneath, and no brace at all.
They strike first at range with precise lance fire and are never where the return fire lands.
One bad round inside enemy range with shields down is their standing risk.

Admirals
Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (Starling flight): the wing admiral; her wings launch and strike in one motion, as a single flock.
Saro of Long Focus (Burning glass): the lance admiral; every lance in range fires one heavy volley at a single target.
Vell of the Third Mirror (Scatter the light): the duellist; the fleet scatters aside and enemy fire lands where the ships were.
Brin of the Fourth Lens (Prism ward): the ward admiral; bent light scatters incoming fire and shields pay less for it.

Decisions
Question 1. Who hunts the Glasswrights as their Nemesis?
A. A Chorus Elder Throat, the weapon their own splinter faction made.
You face a thing that grows tougher from every ship you lose, anywhere, all campaign.
B. An Eclipse Archivist, their uploaded siblings.
You face a patient rival who never retreats and returns once after death.
C. A Hullbreaker Warlord, who shatters glass fleets for sport.
You face a close range hunter your fragile hulls must never let in.
My pick: A. The makers' weapon is the most personal enemy this faction has.

Question 2. Which look leads the Glasswright portraits?
The package offers three ways the glass sits in the body.
A. Threaded: fine crystal threads woven through the flesh, like fibre optics under skin.
The body reads as a person first, with light moving under the skin.
B. Inlaid: large crystal plates set into shoulders, chest, and hands, like jewellery never removed.
The glass reads as grown ornament on plainly living flesh.
C. Bloomed: crystal clusters growing outward at joints, spine, and crown, like frost flowers.
The body reads as a person with crystal growing on them.
D. A different direction per admiral, all three in one family.
My pick: A, with C allowed for one admiral. Threaded keeps them closest to their Starwright roots, as ruled (ruling).

Question 3. May Astra be unthrottled once, in the run 5 finale only?
A. Yes, one scripted unthrottled shot in the finale battle, never in normal play.
You see the caged core at full power exactly once, at the end.
B. No, the cage never opens.
The faction's whole point stays pure, and the finale is fought with normal weapons.
C. Yes, as a once per campaign choice the player makes, at a stated cost.
My pick: A. One scripted shot gives the finale its moment without breaking the cage rule in play.

## Ashfall Hullbreakers

Who they are
The Hullbreakers are alien void hunters from the cold Ashfall belt, where only the strongest packs ate.
They hunt in space by ion trail and by sound, the way they hunted at home, and their own ships run silent.
They came to the Hollow Sun for the biggest fight in known space, and for the core Rook.

How they look
People: huge hunting beasts, wolf and bear inspired, big shoulder hump, thick forearms, heavy claws (ruling).
Fur is a coarse mane over hump, neck, and shoulders only; the face is bare plated horn, with a jaw that splits sideways and four small eyes.
They are not bears with gear, and not the film Predator: no dreadlocks, no skull necklaces, no mask face (ruling).
Posture is by character: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies; the Warlord is always upright (ruling).
Each portrait carries one hero trophy, no more (ruling).
Ships: dark and lopsided, matte soot black and rust, listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops at the bow, boarding claws, plates torn from other factions bolted on.
Colours: soot, rust, dried blood red, dim red running lights, red orange engine glow.

How they play
A close range fleet: fast ships, thin shields, scrap guns and far too many rockets, and no lances at all.
They close from the flanks and behind, ram, board, and strip the prize ship named before the fight.
In fiction they move about twice as slowly as other factions, and their ships move one at a time in pack order (ruling).

Admirals
Warlord Grax Ironmaw (Full burn): the pack leader; every ship gains full movement and rams and boards at full strength.
Sable the Smiling Knife (Knife in the dark): the lone hunter; one ship slips to an enemy's rear arc and boards before it can fire.
Old Mott (Release the swarm): the swarm keeper; the hulk carrier launches every swarm craft it carries at once.
Vesh the Collector (Patch and weld): the quartermaster; damaged ships regain hull from salvage lying on the field.

Decisions
Question 4. Who hunts the Hullbreakers as their Nemesis?
A. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, a duellist who keeps range.
You must close on a rival who withdraws rather than be swarmed, and returns with the same four ships, upgraded.
B. An Eclipse Archivist, dead metal that gets back up.
You must kill the same enemy twice.
C. A Hegemony line admiral, whose ships hum and can be heard a system away.
You hunt a tight formation that knows you are coming.
My pick: A. Range against the close is the cleanest test this faction can be given.

Question 5. Which look is the species base for the portraits?
A. Knuckle Stalker: low and wide, knuckles down, one eye clouded, animals first and captains second.
The package names this design the species base: "Knuckle Stalker".
B. Den-alpha Upright: towering and standing tall, head low between massive shoulders.
The challenge ritual leads the portraits.
C. Long Skulker: lean, long, and low, head tilted to listen.
Hunting by sound leads the portraits.
My pick: A as the base, with the upright pose kept for Warlord portraits, as ruled (ruling).

Question 6. Should trophy plates show on Hullbreaker ships in battle?
A. Yes, ships gain visible plates from factions they have beaten this run.
Your fleet visibly grows heavier and more patchwork as the run goes on.
B. No, trophies show only on portraits and harnesses.
Ships stay as built, and the trophy story lives in the portraits.
C. Yes, but only on the flagship.
My pick: A. The bolted on plates are this faction's story told on the hull.

## Sunward Crusade

Who they are
The Crusade is forty odd frontier systems in the Vesper Reach that the Hegemony wrote off as their stars dimmed.
They seceded, pooled their navies, and swore one oath: stop the Siphoning before their worlds freeze.
They fly black iron ships crewed by unaugmented people who chalk every name on the hull, because the Registry deleted theirs.

How they look
People: weathered, unaugmented humans, frost scarred skin, scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, chalk dust on the gloves.
Ships: black iron hulls, frost white markings, tall heat radiator spires glowing ember orange, heavy broadside decks, everything symmetric, no rust.
Colours: black iron, frost white, bright ember orange.

How they play
A line fleet: advance two or more hexes to power the broadsides, then hold the ground and fire as one.
Torpedoes set targets burning, and the fleet gets better the longer a straight fight lasts.
The weakness is rigidity: a flanked line turns slowly, and carriers are precious and few.

Admirals
Roderic Vex (Fire for effect): the line anchor; every ship fires a free volley at its best target, now.
Malek Draven (Hunter's salvo): the hunter; torpedoes reload at once and deal 30 percent more damage this round.
Sera Ashgrove (Scramble all wings): the wing admiral; carriers launch past their limit and all squadrons strike again, harder.
Oryn Thale (Iron wall): the bulwark; shields refill and the fleet takes 40 percent less hull damage this round.

Decisions
Question 7. Who hunts the Crusade as their Nemesis?
A. An Eclipse Archivist commanding the siphon defence.
You face the enemy your whole war is about: patient, never retreating, back once after death.
B. A Hegemony line admiral, the secession wound.
You face the state that wrote your worlds off.
C. A Hullbreaker Warlord, the funeral scavenger.
You face a raider picking over your dead.
My pick: A. The Siphoning is their war, and the Nemesis should be the war itself.

Question 8. Should the Roll of Vesper be a readable campaign screen?
A. Yes, a screen listing lost admirals, ships, and learned Nemesis weaknesses.
The names your crusades paid in are always one tap away.
B. No, story text only.
The Roll appears in scenes and is never a screen you browse.
C. Yes, but names only; intel stays on the debrief.
My pick: A. A crusade that pays in names should let the player read the bill.

Question 9. May the Crusade field captured hulls with their links ripped out?
A. Yes, captured Hegemony and Eclipse hulls appear in the Cinder Line yards from run 3, at Crusade prices.
You can fly the enemy's ships, stripped of their links, in Crusade colours.
B. No, prizes are stripped for salvage and credits only.
The fleet stays purely Reach built.
C. Yes, but only as one flagship variant.
My pick: A. A written off frontier flying captured hulls is the arithmetic being answered.

## Hegemony Navy

Who they are
The Hegemony Navy is the fleet of the Registry, a vast intelligence at Meridian that links every citizen, ship, and officer.
It plans careers, predicts losses, and keeps those losses within acceptable limits.
Twenty five years ago its survey fleet captured the core Vyre, and Hegemony shield and ion technology comes from that core.

How they look
People: composed humans with tasteful implants, a link port at the temple with a faint blue light, data moving across the eyes, pale grey uniforms and gloves.
Ships: clean light grey wedges, steel blue trim, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, small drone escorts, lights pulsing in time across the fleet.
Colours: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

How they play
A formation fleet: fire syncs near the flagship, so every battle is a geometry problem.
Ion strips shields, carriers launch fighter screens, and cheap backward movement corrects mistakes.
The weakness is the flagship itself: threaten the anchor, and the formation's reason to exist is threatened.

Admirals
Ilsa Kassar (Synchronized broadside): the formation purist; every ship in formation fires one volley at a single target, at once.
Dace Vorn (Interdiction strike): the interdictor; one enemy ship loses its next move and every friendly ship in range fires on it.
Corin Reyes (Launch the strike group): the carrier marshal; every carrier launches its full fighter screen in one coordinated wave.
Oskar Maren (Shield wall order): the shield wall; every ship raises deflectors to full and counts as braced against the next volley.

Decisions
Question 10. Who hunts the Hegemony as their Nemesis?
A. An Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader who has raided Registry supply lines for a decade.
You face a raider who fights to steal one ship, flees with it if the fight turns, and returns stronger.
B. An Eclipse commander, a machine civilisation with no oversight.
You face the rival the Registry finds instructive.
C. A Sunward Crusade admiral, the secession wound.
You face the frontier you abandoned.
My pick: A. A thief the projections cannot price is the Registry's truest problem.

Question 11. Does the Registry speak to the player directly, as a character?
A. Yes, one calm channel voice that prices battles as they happen.
You hear losses called acceptable while you are causing them.
B. No, it speaks only through written Registry Orders between battles.
The horror stays on paper.
C. Only in the Hegemony campaign, never when the Hegemony is the enemy.
My pick: A. The voice is the faction; without it the Registry is just a navy with good filing.

Question 12. Can a Hegemony admiral refuse an order on screen?
A. Yes, once per admiral, as their spotlight beat, at a stated cost.
Each admiral gets one visible moment of being a person, and it costs them.
B. No, refusal exists only in backstory.
On screen, everyone obeys.
C. Yes, and repeated refusal changes the finale.
My pick: A. Once each, at a cost, keeps the Registry strong and the admirals human.

## The Chorus

Who they are
The Chorus is not a people: it is a living weapon the Fleshwrights grew to destroy Eclipse.
It worked too well, merged, multiplied, and ate its makers.
Inside the one swarm, two throats argue: Hunger, which is the swarm itself, and Memory, what is left of the Fleshwrights it consumed.

How they look
Beings: grown organisms with no face, eyes, or limbs to recognise: glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, and bile.
The four admirals are four unique creatures, each a different aspect of the Chorus, not variations on one pattern (ruling).
Ships: grown, not built: ridges, pores, veins, glowing sacs, tendrils and brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue, no panels or pipes.
Colours: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow green light.

How they play
Appetite and numbers: no shields at all, cheap ships, and flesh that knits back together every round.
Broods spill more bodies than the enemy can kill, and acid eats the shields and armour that stop everyone else.
The weakness is a focused enemy who kills faster than flesh can knit and refuses to spread fire.

Admirals
Matriarch of Nine Throats (Spawning surge): the brood admiral; broods release extra swarms and all swarms strike again, harder.
Razormaw (Blood frenzy): the frenzy admiral; every ship charges its nearest target and deals more damage the closer it gets.
Mending Throng (Regrowth tide): the regrowth admiral; every ship regrows 25 percent of its hull, damaged sections included.
Voice of the Deep (Spitting volley): the volley admiral; every ship fires a free volley at once, from every throat.

Decisions
Question 13. Who hunts the Chorus as its Nemesis?
A. An Eclipse Archivist who commands the siphon defence and remembers the Fleshwrights.
You face the enemy the swarm was grown to eat, across nine hundred years of study.
B. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, the makers' kin.
You face family, who apologise while they kill you.
C. A Hegemony line admiral, many and neat and all the same taste.
You face the most orderly fleet in the war.
My pick: A. The Chorus exists for one war, and its Nemesis should be that war's other side.

Question 14. Are Chorus admirals the same growths every run, or regrown?
A. The same throats persist, regrown from kept flesh, so levels and scars carry naturally.
The Matriarch you level is the Matriarch you keep.
B. Each run grows new admirals, with inherited growths only.
New faces of the swarm each run, levels carried as growths.
C. The Matriarch persists, and the others are regrown.
My pick: A. A weapon that eats and keeps what it eats should keep its commanders too.

Question 15. May the swarm eat a core mid campaign, as a story event?
A. Yes, once, in sector 3: eat a core you choose not to sync with, gain a permanent growth, lose that core's protocols.
One real, costly choice about what the swarm is for.
B. No, cores are eaten only in the finale.
The campaign stays clean and the hunger peaks at the end.
C. Yes, but only Crucible grows, with no mechanical effect.
My pick: A. A hunger that never gets to choose is only a description; this makes it a decision.

## Eclipse

Who they are
Eclipse is the Starwrights who uploaded: billions of separate minds in machine bodies, each backed up in one shared Archive.
They have lived a thousand years in the dark around the black hole they made of their own sun.
Now they siphon energy from neighbouring stars to build a new sun, knowing what it costs worlds like Vesper, and doing it anyway.

How they look
Beings: tall machine bodies of layered dark violet black plates, a narrow visor of white gold light, and no faces (ruling).
Some carry a small core of trapped light in the chest, but that is one machine pattern only; forms vary by admiral (ruling).
Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind, eclipse rings around the engines, no flaming engine bells.
Colours: dark violet black plates, white gold eclipse corona, violet white inner glow, and no green anywhere.

How they play
Permanence at a price: stronger, more expensive hulls, heavy armour, and no shields at all.
The first ship lost in each battle is rebuilt from its crew's Archive backup, so one death does not count.
There is no carrier; a battleship class hull takes that place in the line (ruling).

Admirals
Nadir Aurex, Ninth Frame (Release the repair swarms): the enduring flagship admiral; the next ship destroyed is rebuilt, and every ship regains hull.
Umbra Vessane, Third-Housed (Noon volley): the array warden; every ship fires its Null weapons in one coordinated volley.
Ostrel of the Long Copy (Out of the shadow): the fold admiral; the whole fleet folds through obstacles, and ships landing beside an enemy fire at once.
Seveth, Twice-Restored (Sunshade wall): the restorer; the fleet raises its sunshade plates as one wall and takes far less damage.

Decisions
Question 16. Who hunts Eclipse as its Nemesis?
A. A Sunward Crusade High Admiral who has sworn to burn the siphon arrays.
You face a duellist who challenges your flagship, never flees, and chalks your ships' names on its hull.
B. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, your siblings.
You face family precision at range.
C. A Chorus Elder Throat, which still wants you.
You face the weapon grown to eat you.
My pick: A. The Crusade is the bill for the Siphoning, presented in person.

Question 17. May restored admirals' portraits change between runs?
A. Yes, each restoration changes the frame slightly: plates, ring pieces, visor.
A levelled admiral visibly differs from a fresh one.
B. No, portraits stay fixed per admiral.
C. Only Aurex and the Nemesis change frames.
My pick: A. Restoration is this faction's levelling made visible; the portraits should show it.

Question 18. Does lighting the new sun change the final battle board?
A. Yes, the run 5 finale is fought in growing light, the board brightening sector by sector as the sun catches.
The win is something you watch happen while you fight.
B. No, the lighting happens only in the ending scene.
The board stays dark and the dawn is a cutscene.
C. Yes, but only the skybox, not the board lighting.
My pick: A. A thousand years of dark should end on the board, not after it.

## Not checked

I worked only from pack.md and packages_all.md. I did not check shipped data, code, the lore bible, or any other leg.
Conflict found: the cross faction appendix still describes the Glasswright look fix under R66 as "faceless", which the Nov 1 ruling overturns. The Glasswrights page above follows the ruling: real faces, normal speech, colour as an emotion tell only (ruling).
Conflict found: the appendix separates Glasswrights from Eclipse by "a trapped-light chest core" as if it were general. The ruling makes the core one machine pattern only, and the Eclipse page above follows the ruling (ruling).
The packages print their own chair picks beside every open question. I treated those as one input, not as rulings; my picks agree with most and stand on the reasons given.
One thing I could not reconcile: the drafted Nemesis set is one directional. Eclipse supplies the hunter for two factions, the Crusade and the Chorus, while no faction's draft names a Chorus hunter except the Glasswrights page. That may be intended, but the packages do not say.
Settled rulings were not offered as questions: no carrier for Eclipse, admiral levelling as the spine, near impossible run 1, equal cores, and per faction learning idioms all stand as written.
