Six faction briefings for Embers of the Hollow Sun

The Glasswrights

Who they are
The Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's own optical engineers, who tried to stop the Upload, failed, and watched their machine kill their sun. They live three to four hundred years, so only two or three generations separate them from the people who built the light. They carry two guilts — the sun, and the Fleshwrights, their splinter, whose weapon ate them — and they fly A-4 Astra, caged and throttled, to hunt the other five cores and shut them all down, Astra last.

How they look
People: tall, slender, pale half-translucent flesh with glass grown and woven into the body — organic beings who evolved to shape glass, not glass people, not statues, not constructs. (ruling) Faces are rooted and recognisably Starwright: grown lens layers over the eyes, crystal at brow and cheek, heavier in the old than the young. They speak normally; colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell only, never a language. (ruling) They are markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse. (ruling)
Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, a stored light glowing deep inside.
Colours: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, stored light.

How they play
Precision under fragility: fastest ships on the board, deep shields, fragile hull beneath, and no brace.
Every battle is range control: strike first, strike exactly, never be where the return fire lands.
Bad at: one bad round inside enemy range with shields down.

Admirals
Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (Starling flight): the wing admiral; wings launch and strike as one flock, choosing targets after they move.
Saro of Long Focus (Burning glass): the lance admiral; extreme range, first strikes, kills before the enemy closes.
Vell of the Third Mirror (Scatter the light): the duellist; scatter, evade, win the one-on-one before the lines meet.
Brin of the Fourth Lens (Prism ward): the ward admiral; layered mirror veils, a fleet that refuses to be hit cleanly.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies the Glasswrights' Nemesis? (Drafted: Chorus Elder Throat.)
(a) Chorus Elder Throat — the makers' weapon, grows tougher from every ship you lose. Pick.
(b) Eclipse Archivist — your uploaded siblings.
(c) Hullbreaker Warlord — they shatter well.
Why: the Chorus is the strongest story tie — the weapon your splinter made, now eating your dead.

2. Which visual direction leads the portrait rework? Package key phrase: "how the glass sits in the body."
(a) Threaded — fine crystal threads under the skin like fibre optics. Pick.
(b) Inlaid — large crystal plates set into flesh like jewellery.
(c) Bloomed — crystal clusters growing outward at joints and spine.
(Any pick can be mixed per admiral for variety, under the portrait ruling.)
Why: Threaded reads most clearly as living flesh with glass in it, which is the owner's rework target. (ruling)

3. May Astra be unthrottled in the finale only?
(a) Yes, one scripted unthrottled shot in the run 5 finale, never in normal play. Pick.
(b) No, the cage never opens — that is the point of the faction.
(c) Yes, as a once-per-campaign player choice with a stated cost.
Why: one scripted shot gives the ending its payoff without breaking the "caged and throttled" identity.

The Ashfall Hullbreakers

Who they are
Alien void hunters from a cold, heavy-gravity belt where only the strongest packs ate: huge, hot-blooded predators who hunt in space the way they hunted at home, by trail and by sound. They do not want riches; they want to be the strongest thing in the dark, and they came because the war around the Hollow Sun is the biggest fight in known space. They strip every wreck, bolt the best pieces into their ships and harness armour, and hunt R-5 Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable.

How they look
People: huge and hunched, with a big shoulder hump, thick forearms, heavy claws, coarse mane over hump and shoulders, tough dark hide elsewhere. (ruling) The species base is the Knuckle Stalker design: low and wide, on all fours at rest. Posture varies by character — Grax upright in challenge, Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies — and the Warlord is always upright. (ruling)
Face: bare, short, blunt, armoured in overlapping horn plates, jaw split down the middle opening sideways, four small eyes across the brow, heat pits beside them. No fur on the face, no snout, no nose. Trophies are machines and hull plates, never skulls or bodies. Not bears with gear, not the film Predator — no dreadlocks, no skull necklaces, no mandible mask. (ruling)
Ships: matte soot black and rust, few windows, dim red running lights, big listening dishes and wake-sniffer scoops, boarding claws, chains between hull sections. Lopsided, with plates torn from every faction bolted on.
Colours: soot, rust, dried blood red, trophy colours, red running lights.

How they play
The close and the prize: thin shields, scrap guns with wild extra shots and too many rockets, no lances.
Close from flanks and behind, ram and board, choose the prize before the fight starts.
Bad at: range — that is where you die.
(ruling) The package says "your ships are fast", but the ruling is that they move about twice as slowly as other factions in fiction terms, and their ships move one at a time in pack order (R64); enemy ships otherwise keep the player's pace (R67). The ruling wins.

Admirals
Grax Ironmaw (Full burn): the Warlord; everything closes at once, rams set up boards, win or break in three rounds.
Sable the Smiling Knife (Knife in the dark): the lone hunter; one silent approach, one ship opened from behind.
Old Mott (Release the swarm): the swarm keeper; cheap hulls, endless young, losses as a cost already paid.
Vesh the Collector (Patch and weld): the collector; fight for the wreck field, strip everything, leave richer than the winner.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies the Hullbreakers' Nemesis? (Drafted: Glasswright Lens-keeper.)
(a) Glasswright Lens-keeper — a duelist who keeps range, four elite ships, quality against quality. Pick.
(b) Eclipse Archivist — dead metal that gets back up.
(c) Hegemony line admiral — their ships hum.
Why: the Lens-keeper's quality-over-quantity duel is the clean opposite of the Hullbreaker swarm, and the apology motif gives the pack a mirror.

2. Which visual direction leads the portrait work? Package key phrase: "read as animals first and captains second."
(a) Knuckle Stalker — low and wide, knuckles down, the species base. Pick.
(b) Den-alpha Upright — towering and narrow, challenge ritual leading.
(c) Long Skulker — lean, long, low, hunting by sound leading.
Why: the Knuckle Stalker is the ruled species base and reads as beast-first, which is the owner's correction target. (ruling)

3. Should trophy plates show on Hullbreaker ships in battle?
(a) Yes, ships gain visible plates from factions they have beaten this run. Pick.
(b) No, trophies show only on portraits and harnesses.
(c) Yes, but only on the flagship.
Why: visible plates make the pack's "leave heavier than you arrived" identity legible on the board.

The Sunward Crusade

Who they are
The Vesper Reach made into a fleet: forty-odd frontier systems the Hegemony wrote off when their stars began to dim, now bound by one oath to stop the Siphoning before their worlds freeze. They fly black-iron ships crewed by unaugmented people who chalk every name on the hull, because the Registry deleted theirs. They fight in line, they hold, and they pay for the dawn in names.

How they look
People: weathered, unaugmented humans with frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, grey stubble, hard tired eyes. High-collared dark void-coats, heavy shoulder plates, chalk dust on the gloves.
Ships: black iron hulls with frost-white markings, ember-orange windows and engines, tall heat-radiator spires glowing ember orange. Heavy broadside decks, big engine bells, old manual gun decks, symmetric, no rust.
Colours: black iron, frost white, bright ember.

How they play
Commit early and hold the line: advance two or more hexes to power broadsides, or brace and refuse the ground.
Torpedoes set targets burning, carriers are precious and few, and the fleet gets better the longer a straight fight lasts.
Bad at: rigidity — a flanked line turns slowly.

Admirals
Roderic Vex (Fire for effect): the line anchor; mass guns, name one target, keep the lane and the target.
Malek Draven (Hunter's salvo): the hunter; torpedo first, set the board burning, call the debt on one marked ship.
Sera Ashgrove (Scramble all wings): the wing admiral; overwhelm with wings, keep the Hearth alive, spend squadrons like the fleet can grow more.
Oryn Thale (Iron wall): the bulwark; screens up, damage refused, a slow advance the enemy can only delay.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies the Crusade's Nemesis? (Drafted: Eclipse Archivist commanding the siphon defence.)
(a) Eclipse Archivist — the Siphoning is their war; never retreats, returns once after death. Pick.
(b) Hegemony line admiral — the secession wound.
(c) Hullbreaker Warlord — the funeral scavenger.
Why: the Eclipse rival ties the Nemesis directly to the Crusade's war aim, and its "ninth body" voice is the strongest in the set.

2. May the Crusade field captured hulls with their links ripped out?
(a) Yes, captured Hegemony and Eclipse hulls appear in the Cinder Line yards from run 3, at Crusade prices; strip and ransom rules unchanged for anything not kept. Pick.
(b) No, prizes are stripped for salvage and credits only.
(c) Yes, but only as one flagship variant.
Why: captured hulls turn the Crusade's grudge into a fleet-building choice, and the "links ripped out" detail keeps the lore clean.

3. Should the Roll of Vesper be a readable campaign screen?
(a) Yes, a screen listing lost admirals, ships, and learned Nemesis weaknesses. Pick.
(b) Story text only.
(c) A screen with names only, intel kept on the debrief.
Why: the Roll is the Crusade's memory idiom made visible, and a readable screen rewards the "pay in names" theme.

The Hegemony Navy

Who they are
The fleet of the Registry: a vast intelligence at Meridian that links every citizen, ship, and officer, plans their careers, predicts their losses, and keeps those losses within acceptable limits. Its people are safe, comfortable, proudly cybernetic, and counted, and its warships are clean wedges with identical turrets and drone escorts, fighting as one formation around the flagship. The horror under the polish is Vesper: proof that when the numbers say so, the Registry will archive a whole frontier and call it acceptable.

How they look
People: composed humans with tasteful implants — a cortical-link port at the temple with a faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Pale grey high-collared uniforms and gloves, calm to the point of eerie.
Ships: light grey wedge hulls, steel-blue trim, blue-white windows, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the fleet, small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.
Colours: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

How they play
The network: fire syncs near the flagship, so the battle is a geometry problem.
Keep formation tight, strip shields with ion, launch fighter screens from carriers, use cheap retro movement to correct mistakes.
Bad at: the flagship itself — when the anchor is threatened, the formation's reason to exist is threatened.

Admirals
Ilsa Kassar (Synchronized broadside): the formation purist; maximum sync, minimum improvisation.
Dace Vorn (Interdiction strike): the interdictor; cut off stragglers, punish anything that leaves formation.
Corin Reyes (Launch the strike group): the carrier marshal; fighter screens, layered strikes, patience.
Oskar Maren (Shield wall order): the shield wall; deflectors layered, losses priced at zero, ground given one hex at a time.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies the Hegemony's Nemesis? (Drafted: Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader.)
(a) Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader — a raider who steals one ship, flees a losing fight, returns stronger. Pick.
(b) Eclipse commander — no oversight.
(c) Sunward Crusade admiral — the secession wound.
Why: the raider's hit-and-run greed is the perfect foil to the Hegemony's formation geometry, and it keeps the "your ships hum" insult in play.

2. Does the Registry speak to the player directly as a character?
(a) Yes, one calm channel voice that prices battles as they happen, in Hegemony runs and in Hegemony Nemesis material. Pick.
(b) No, it speaks only through written Registry Orders between battles.
(c) Only in the Hegemony campaign, never when the Hegemony is the enemy.
Why: a calm voice pricing your battle as it happens is the faction's horror made audible, and it carries into Nemesis material.

3. Should the Registry's projection appear as a UI layer during Hegemony runs?
(a) Yes, a quiet overlay that states the projected result and updates when the battle leaves the projection. Pick.
(b) No, keep projections in dialogue and debrief only.
Why: the overlay makes "acceptable losses" a thing you watch happen, not just read.

The Chorus

Who they are
Not a people: a living weapon the Fleshwrights grew to destroy Eclipse, and it worked too well — merged, multiplied, and ate its makers. Inside the one swarm, two throats argue: Hunger, which is the swarm itself, and Memory, which is what is left of the Fleshwrights it consumed. It is coming to the Hollow Sun to eat the other five cores and finish the war it was grown for.

How they look
Beings: grown organisms with no face, eyes, or limbs to recognise — glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, and bile, nothing on the surface that recalls a person. (ruling) The four admirals are unique creatures, each a different aspect of the Chorus as a hero admiral — not variations on one brood pattern. (ruling)
Ships: grown, not built — ridges, pores, veins, glowing sacs, no panels or pipes, tendrils and brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue.
Colours: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light.

How they play
Appetite and numbers: no shields at all, cheap ships, win by regrowth and mass.
Flesh knits back every round, broods spill more bodies than the enemy can kill, acid eats shields and armour.
Bad at: a focused enemy who kills faster than flesh can knit and refuses to spread fire.

Admirals
Matriarch of Nine Throats (Spawning surge): the brood admiral; spawn volume, swarm strikes, pressure everywhere at once.
Razormaw (Blood frenzy): the frenzy admiral; close fast, chew through one ship, let the panic spread.
Mending Throng (Regrowth tide): the regrowth admiral; outlast everything, make damage feel wasted.
Voice of the Deep (Spitting volley): the volley admiral; the swarm as artillery, spitting in one breath from every throat.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies the Chorus's Nemesis? (Drafted: Eclipse Archivist who remembers the Fleshwrights.)
(a) Eclipse Archivist — the war it was grown for; returns once after death. Pick.
(b) Glasswright Lens-keeper — the makers' kin.
(c) Hegemony line admiral — many and neat and all the same taste.
Why: the Eclipse rival closes the loop on the Chorus's origin — the weapon facing the thing it was made to kill.

2. Are Chorus admirals the same growths every run, or regrown?
(a) The same throats persist, regrown from kept flesh, so levels and scars carry naturally. Pick.
(b) Each run grows new admirals with inherited growths only.
(c) The Matriarch persists and the others are regrown.
Why: persistence matches the digestion idiom — what the swarm eats, it keeps, including its commanders.

3. How present should Memory be in normal play?
(a) Story and debrief only, as the bible rules. Pick.
(b) Occasional single lines during battles, clearly in a maker's voice.
(c) A full second speaker in every Chorus scene.
Why: keeping Memory to story and debrief preserves the "two throats" tension without letting the makers' voice dilute the swarm's voice in battle.

Eclipse

Who they are
The Starwrights who uploaded: billions of separate minds in machine bodies, each backed up in one shared Archive, around the black hole they made of their own sun. They have lived in that dark for a thousand years and are no longer sure the Upload was worth it, so they are building a new sun from stellar energy siphoned from neighbouring stars, whatever it costs those stars. They know what the Siphoning does to worlds like Vesper. They do it anyway, because the alternative is forever in the dark.

How they look
Beings: tall machine bodies of layered dark violet-black plates, a narrow visor of white-gold light, and in each chest a small core of trapped light. (ruling) No faces stands — no eyes, mouth, or features on the visor plate. The trapped-light chest core is one machine pattern, not a universal: machine forms vary by admiral. They are sentient machines, not humans in armour. (ruling)
Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind them, eclipse rings around the engines, no flaming engine bells. Classes: mote, flare, zenith, a battleship-class hull, and the flagship The Unsetting. No carrier. (ruling)
Colours: dark violet-black plates, white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow.

How they play
Permanence and price: stronger and more expensive ships, heavy armour, no shields at all.
The first ship you lose in each battle is rebuilt by repair swarms from its crew's Archive backup — one death that does not count.
Bad at: concentration — few hulls, each costly, and an enemy who kills the same ship twice in one battle is spending your permanence.

Admirals
Nadir Aurex, Ninth Frame (Release the repair swarms): the enduring flagship; armour, restore, outlast the battle.
Umbra Vessane, Third-Housed (Noon volley): the array warden; hold the siphon ground, Null fire at range, make the enemy cross a killing field.
Ostrel of the Long Copy (Out of the shadow): the fold admiral; movement through everything, strikes from inside the enemy's formation.
Seveth, Twice-Restored (Sunshade wall): the restorer; sunshade walls of layered plate, repair drones, death as a logistics problem.

Three biggest decisions

1. Which faction supplies Eclipse's Nemesis? (Drafted: Sunward Crusade High Admiral.)
(a) Sunward Crusade High Admiral — they are the bill for the Siphoning; never flees, challenges your flagship. Pick.
(b) Glasswright Lens-keeper — our siblings.
(c) Chorus Elder Throat — it still wants us.
Why: the Crusade is the human cost of the Siphoning made into a rival, and the oath-and-chalk voice is the strongest counter to Eclipse's cold counting.

2. May restored admirals' portraits change between runs?
(a) Yes, each restoration changes the frame slightly — plates, ring pieces, visor — so a levelled admiral visibly differs. Pick.
(b) No, portraits stay fixed per admiral.
(c) Only Aurex and the Nemesis change frames.
Why: visible frame changes make "the Ninth Frame" literal and let the player see restoration as progression.

3. Does lighting the new sun change the final battle board?
(a) Yes, the run 5 finale is fought in growing light, the board brightening sector by sector as the sun catches. Pick.
(b) No, the lighting happens only in the ending scene.
(c) Yes, but only the skybox, not the board lighting.
Why: a brightening board makes the finale's question — was it worth it — something the player plays through, not just watches.
