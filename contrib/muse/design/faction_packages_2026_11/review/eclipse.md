# Eclipse

Who they are
The Starwrights who uploaded: billions of separate minds in machine bodies, each backed up in one shared Archive, around the black hole they made of their own sun.
They have lived in that dark for a thousand years and are no longer sure the Upload was worth it, so they are building a new sun from stellar energy siphoned from neighbouring stars, whatever it costs those stars.
They know what the Siphoning does to worlds like Vesper. They do it anyway, because the alternative is forever in the dark.

How they look
Beings: tall machine bodies of layered dark violet black plates, a narrow visor of white gold light, and no faces (ruling).
Some carry a small core of trapped light in the chest, but that is one machine pattern only; machine forms vary by admiral (ruling). They are sentient machines, not humans in armour.
Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind them, eclipse rings around the engines, no flaming engine bells. Classes: mote, flare, zenith, a battleship class hull (designed in Job 8), and the flagship The Unsetting. No carrier (ruling).
Colours: dark violet black plates, white gold eclipse corona, violet white inner glow. No green anywhere near this faction.

How they play
Permanence and price: stronger and more expensive ships, heavy armour, no shields at all.
The first ship you lose in each battle is rebuilt by repair swarms from its crew's Archive backup, so you plan around one death that does not count. The fold drive lets ships pass through rocks, wrecks and enemy ships.
Bad at: concentration. Few hulls, each costly, and an enemy who kills the same ship twice in one battle is spending your permanence.

Admirals
Nadir Aurex, Ninth Frame (Release the repair swarms): the enduring flagship admiral; armour, restore, outlast the battle.
Umbra Vessane, Third-Housed (Noon volley): the array warden; hold the siphon ground, Null fire at range, make the enemy cross a killing field.
Ostrel of the Long Copy (Out of the shadow): the fold admiral; movement through everything, strikes from inside the enemy's formation.
Seveth, Twice-Restored (Sunshade wall): the restorer; sunshade walls of layered plate, repair drones, death as a logistics problem.

Decisions

Question 16. Who hunts Eclipse as its Nemesis? (Drafted in the package: the Crusade.)
A. A Sunward Crusade High Admiral who has sworn to burn the siphon arrays. Never flees, challenges your flagship to a duel, chalks the names of every Eclipse ship it destroys on its hull.
B. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, your siblings. Family precision at range.
C. A Chorus Elder Throat, which still wants you. The weapon grown to eat you.
Chair pick: A. The Crusade is the bill for the Siphoning, presented in person: a rival that never flees and chalks your names is the perfect answer to a faction that restores its dead from backups.

Question 17. May restored admirals' portraits change between runs?
A. Yes, each restoration changes the frame slightly: plates, ring pieces, visor. A levelled admiral visibly differs from a fresh one.
B. No, portraits stay fixed per admiral.
C. Only Aurex and the Nemesis change frames.
Chair pick: A. Restoration is this faction's levelling made visible; if the Ninth Frame looks different from the Third, progression shows without a menu. (One leg claimed this question was already settled by a ruling in favour of B. There is no such ruling; the question is live.)

Question 18. Does lighting the new sun change the final battle board?
A. Yes, the run 5 finale is fought in growing light, the board brightening sector by sector as the sun catches. The win is something you watch happen while you fight.
B. No, the lighting happens only in the ending scene. The board stays dark and the dawn is a cutscene.
C. Yes, but only the skybox, not the board lighting.
Chair pick: A. A thousand years of dark should end on the board, not after it.
