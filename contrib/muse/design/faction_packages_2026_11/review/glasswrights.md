# The Glasswrights

Who they are
The Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's own optical engineers, who tried to stop the Upload, failed, and watched their machine kill their sun.
They live three to four hundred years, so only two or three generations separate them from the people who built the light.
They carry two guilts, the sun and the Fleshwrights, their splinter, whose weapon ate them, and they fly the caged core A-4 Astra to hunt the other five cores and shut them all down, Astra last.

How they look
People: tall, slender, pale half translucent flesh with glass grown and woven into the body. Organic beings who evolved to shape glass, not glass people, not statues.
Faces are real faces on a young to old spectrum: near Starwright faces with craft grown lens eyes in the young, deep lens infusion in the old (ruling).
They speak normally. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell only, never a language (ruling). They are markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse.
Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, stored light glowing deep inside.
Colours: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, pale green white stored light.

How they play
The fastest fleet on the board: deep shields, fragile hulls underneath, and no brace.
Every battle is range control: strike first, strike exactly, never be where the return fire lands.
Bad at: one bad round inside enemy range with shields down.

Admirals
Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (Starling flight): the wing admiral; her wings launch and strike as one flock.
Saro of Long Focus (Burning glass): the lance admiral; extreme range, kills made before the enemy closes.
Vell of the Third Mirror (Scatter the light): the duellist; scatter, evade, win the one on one before the lines meet.
Brin of the Fourth Lens (Prism ward): the ward admiral; layered mirror veils, a fleet that refuses to be hit cleanly.

Decisions

Question 1. Who hunts the Glasswrights as their Nemesis? (Drafted in the package: the Chorus.)
A. A Chorus Elder Throat, the makers' weapon. It grows tougher from every ship you lose, anywhere, all campaign.
B. An Eclipse Archivist, your uploaded siblings. Patient, never retreats, returns once after death.
C. A Hullbreaker Warlord. A close range hunter your fragile hulls must never let in.
Chair pick: A. The weapon your own splinter made is the most personal enemy this faction has.

Question 2. Which look leads the Glasswright portrait rework? The package's phrase: "how the glass sits in the body."
A. Threaded: fine crystal threads woven through the flesh, like fibre optics under skin. The body reads as a person first, light moving under the skin.
B. Inlaid: large crystal plates set into shoulders, chest and hands, like jewellery never removed.
C. Bloomed: crystal clusters growing outward at joints, spine and crown, like frost flowers.
Chair pick: A, with C allowed for one admiral. Threaded keeps them closest to their Starwright roots, which is the ruled direction.

Question 3. May Astra be unthrottled in the run 5 finale only?
A. Yes, one scripted unthrottled shot in the finale battle, never in normal play. You see the caged core at full power exactly once, at the end.
B. No, the cage never opens. The faction's whole point stays pure, and the finale is fought with normal weapons.
C. Yes, as a once per campaign choice the player makes, at a stated cost.
Chair pick: A. One scripted shot gives the ending its payoff without breaking the cage rule in play.
