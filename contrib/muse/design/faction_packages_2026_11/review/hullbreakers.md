# The Ashfall Hullbreakers

Who they are
Alien void hunters from a cold, heavy gravity belt where only the strongest packs ate.
They hunt in space by ion trail and by listening, the way they hunted at home, and their own ships run silent.
They came to the Hollow Sun for the biggest fight in known space, strip every wreck they make, and hunt R-5 Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable.

How they look
People: huge hunting beasts, wolf and bear inspired, big shoulder hump, thick forearms, heavy claws, coarse mane over hump and shoulders only (ruling).
The face is bare plated horn: short, blunt, four small eyes across the brow, heat pits beside them. Not bears with gear, and not the film Predator: no dreadlocks, no skull necklaces, no mask face (ruling).
The species base is the Knuckle Stalker design, low and wide (ruling). Posture is by character: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over trophies; the Warlord is always upright (ruling). One hero trophy per portrait.
Ships: matte soot black and rust, lopsided, few windows, dim red running lights, big listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops, boarding claws, plates torn from every faction bolted on.
Colours: soot, rust, dried blood red, trophy colours, red running lights.

How they play
A close range fleet: thin shields, scrap guns with wild extra shots, far too many rockets, and no lances at all.
They close from the flanks and behind, ram, board, and strip the prize named before the fight. Range is where they die.
In fiction they move about twice as slowly as other factions, and their ships move one at a time in pack order (ruling, R64). Note: the package itself says "your ships are fast", which contradicts the ruling; the ruling wins, and the package should be corrected.

Admirals
Grax Ironmaw (Full burn): the Warlord; everything closes at once, rams set up boards, win or break in three rounds.
Sable the Smiling Knife (Knife in the dark): the lone hunter; one silent approach, one ship opened from behind.
Old Mott (Release the swarm): the swarm keeper; cheap hulls, endless young, losses as a cost already paid.
Vesh the Collector (Patch and weld): the collector; fight for the wreck field, strip everything, leave richer than the winner.

Decisions

Question 4. Who hunts the Hullbreakers as their Nemesis? (Drafted in the package: the Glasswrights.)
A. A Glasswright Lens-keeper, a duellist who keeps range. You must close on a rival who withdraws rather than be swarmed, and returns with the same four ships, upgraded.
B. An Eclipse Archivist, dead metal that gets back up. You must kill the same enemy twice.
C. A Hegemony line admiral, whose ships hum and can be heard a system away. A tight formation that knows you are coming.
Chair pick: A. Range against the close is the cleanest test this faction can be given, and quality against quantity writes every meeting.

Question 5. Should trophy plates show on Hullbreaker ships in battle?
A. Yes, ships gain visible plates from factions they have beaten this run. Your fleet visibly grows heavier and more patchwork as the run goes on.
B. No, trophies show only on portraits and harnesses. Ships stay as built.
C. Yes, but only on the flagship.
Chair pick: A. The bolted on plates are this faction's story told on the hull; "leave heavier than you arrived" should be visible on the board.

Question 6. When the Hullbreakers are the enemy Nemesis, how is their credit raiding shown?
A. A stated credit loss on the debrief, with one line from the raider. Quick, clean, keeps the pace.
B. A playable defence battle. You fight for your supply lines each time.
C. Both: debrief losses early, a defence battle from run 3 onward.
Chair pick: A. The raid is a pack behaviour, not a set piece; one line and a number makes it sting without adding a battle type to every run.
