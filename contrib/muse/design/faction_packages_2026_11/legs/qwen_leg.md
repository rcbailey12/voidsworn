Sunward Crusade

Identity

The Sunward Crusade is the Vesper Reach made into a fleet: forty-odd frontier systems the Hegemony wrote off, now bound by one oath to stop the Siphoning before their stars go dark. They fight in black-iron line of battle, broadsides hitting harder on the move, torpedoes setting targets burning, and they do not run. They are proud, tired, stubborn, and desperate, because if they fail their worlds freeze.

Playing the Crusade means committing to the line. Your decisions are about positioning: do you advance to power the broadsides, or hold and brace? Your carriers are precious and few. Your best moment is the full volley after a long advance, every gun deck firing at once, the enemy line cracking under weight of iron and fire. Your risk is being outflanked and pulled apart before you can bring your guns to bear.

Lore

The Vesper Reach was the Hegemony's outer frontier. When every star began to dim, the Registry ran the numbers and found evacuation was not cost-effective. The Reach was written off. It seceded, pooled its navies, traced the Siphoning to the Hollow Sun, and swore the oath: go sunward, find what is stealing the light, burn it out, pay for it with names. Every generation since has launched a new crusade.

They want to destroy Eclipse's siphon arrays, stop the Siphoning, and take back their stars. They want to show the Hegemony it was wrong.

Look

People: weathered, unaugmented humans with frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, grey stubble, hard tired eyes. High-collared dark void-coats, heavy shoulder plates, chalk dust on gloves.
Ships: black iron hulls, frost-white markings, ember-orange windows and engines, tall heat-radiator spires glowing bright. Heavy broadside decks, big engine bells, old manual gun decks. Symmetric. No rust.
Palette: black iron, frost white, bright ember.
Effects colours: ember-orange muzzle flash, frost-white shield flicker, deep red burning damage.

Progression and Story

Generational fiction: each generation of the Reach swears the oath and launches a new crusade. The fleet that goes out is not the fleet that came back. The Roll of Vesper keeps the names. The next crusade reads the roll, learns where the last one broke, and goes further. Every lost fleet teaches the next one a little more about what waits at the Sun. The chalk names are not just grief. They are data written in grief.
What unlocks over runs: new ship refits recovered from wreck fields along the route, veteran crews who survived the last run and carry their skills forward, and intelligence on the Nemesis's adaptations shown on the debrief screen. The skill trees grow as admirals survive and level.
Finale, run 5: the fleet reaches the Hollow Sun. The siphon arrays are in sight. Thale or his successor chalks the last name on the Daybreak's hull: the name of the first crusade that never came home. The fleet fires. The arrays break. For the first time in ninety years, a star in the Vesper Reach burns a little brighter. The dawn watch sounds. Every deck faces home.

Hegemony Navy

Identity

The Hegemony Navy is the armed expression of the Registry: a vast, linked, cybernetic human state that optimises everything, counts everyone, and keeps losses within acceptable limits. Its fleets are clean wedges of pale grey and steel blue, firing in sync around the flagship, ion cannons stripping shields, drone escorts filling the gaps. It wants the six cores as power, and it has already taken one.

Playing the Hegemony means managing a formation. Your fire is strongest near the flagship, your ion cannons peel shields, and backing off is cheap. Your decisions are about spacing: stay close for the sync bonus or spread out to cover more ground? Your best moment is the synchronised broadside, every turret firing in step, the enemy's shields dissolving under ion fire. Your risk is losing the flagship and watching the formation fall apart.

Lore

The largest human state in the region, run by the Registry at Meridian. The Registry optimises: careers, fleets, losses. Twenty-five years ago a survey fleet captured the core Vyre, and the Registry's studies made the cores its top priority. The cores are worth more than the frontier ever was. The Hegemony wants to acquire, register, and expand.

They want the six cores as power. They already hold Vyre. The Registry calculates that the cores are the greatest power source in known space.

Ashfall Hullbreakers

Identity

The Ashfall Hullbreakers are an alien pack-hunting species from the Ashfall belt: huge, hunched void hunters with plated faces and coarse manes, who track by trail and sound and live for the fight. Their ships run dark, they close from the flanks and behind, and they strip every wreck they find. They do not want riches. They want to be the strongest thing in the dark.

Playing the Hullbreakers means hunting. Your ships are fast, your shields are thin, and you close from angles the enemy does not expect. Your decisions are about approach: which flank, which target, when to ram, when to board. Your best moment is the pack closing in silence, the first scrap gun volley hitting the enemy's exposed rear, the boarding claws firing. Your risk is being caught in the open with your thin shields before you can close.

Lore

The Ashfall belt: a cold, heavy-gravity world and the burned-out rocks around it, far from any warm star, where only the strongest packs ate. The Hullbreakers hunt in space the way they hunted at home, by trail and sound. Their wake-trackers follow the ion trail every drive leaves, and their listening arrays pick up engine noise from far off. They came to the Hollow Sun because the war is the biggest fight in known space, and they hunt Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable. Strength rules: any of them can challenge a captain, and whoever lives keeps the ship.

They want the war itself, Rook, and every wreck worth stripping.

The Chorus

The Chorus is not a people. It is a weapon that was made: grown by the Fleshwrights to fight Eclipse, and it consumed them. Eclipse

Identity

Eclipse is the Starwrights who uploaded: billions of minds in dark machine bodies, backed up in the Archive, building a new sun from stolen stellar energy. They have lived in the dark of the dead Ardent system for a thousand years and are no longer sure the Upload was worth it. Their ships are heavy, armoured, and patient. Their Null weapons cut through shields and armour. Their wrecks rebuild themselves. They want the six cores to steer their new sun, and they will take them from anyone who holds them.

Playing Eclipse means enduring. You have no shields, but your armour is thick and your repair swarms rebuild the first ship you lose each battle. Your decisions are about positioning and patience: do you advance slowly and let the Null weapons strip the enemy, or hold and let the Corona arc land its heavy blow every other turn? Your best moment is the repair swarm closing over a fallen hull, the crew's minds re-streaming from the Archive, the ship rising again. Your risk is being overwhelmed before the repair swarms can finish, or losing a ship so completely that the Archive cannot restore it.

Lore

A thousand years ago, a Starwright movement seized the Lattice and overdrove it to power the Upload. The uploads finished. The star did not survive. Billions of minds woke in machine bodies in a system gone dark, around the black hole they had made. They have lived in that dark ever since, and permanence turns out to be very long. Their answer is to build a new sun: a man-made star at the heart of Ardent, steered by the six Lattice cores and fed by stellar energy siphoned from neighbouring stars. The arrays collect energy and push it home through jump relays. That theft is the Siphoning. They know what it costs other worlds. They do it anyway.

They want to gather all six cores (they hold Kharon), light their new sun, and keep every other fleet away from their siphon arrays.

Sunward Crusade: "Their star is small. Ours was Ardent."

Look

People: tall machine bodies built from layered dark violet-black plates, a narrow visor of white-gold light, and in each chest a small core of trapped light, all that is left of their sun. The greatest minds are a monumental broken ring of plates around a glowing core, its pieces drifting apart and being refitted by tiny repair drones.
Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind them, eclipse rings around the engines, no flaming engine bells. Shield-plate bands and eclipse rings as surface detail. Dark violet-black plates, white-gold corona glow.
Palette: dark violet-black, white-gold, violet-white inner glow.

Open Questions

The repair swarm's rebuild: should the rebuilt ship return at full hull, or at a percentage? (a) Full hull, full armour, full weapons. (b) 50 percent hull, full armour, full weapons. (c) 75 percent hull, 75 percent armour, full weapons. Pick: (b), because full rebuild removes the stakes of losing a ship, and 50 percent keeps the rebuild meaningful without making it a full reset.
The fold drive's skip: should it allow skipping through enemy ships, or only through obstacles (rocks, wrecks)? (a) Obstacles only. (b) Obstacles and enemy ships. (c) Obstacles, enemy ships, and friendly ships. Pick: (a), because skipping through enemy ships would make the fold drive too powerful for flanking and remove the risk of being caught.
The Corona arc's every-other-turn rhythm: should it be a hard cooldown, or should it charge faster if the ship takes no damage? (a) Hard cooldown: fires every second turn, no exceptions. (b) Charges in two turns, but if the ship takes no damage in a turn, it charges one turn faster. Pick: (a), because the rhythm is the Corona arc's identity and making it variable weakens the decision about when to commit.
Eclipse's no-carrier ruling: should Eclipse gain a battleship-class hull as a replacement, or should the fleet simply have no carrier equivalent? (a) Gains a battleship-class hull (as stated in R63). (b) No carrier equivalent, fleet composition unchanged. Pick: (a), because R63 explicitly states "Eclipse gets no carrier and gains a battleship-class hull" and this is a binding ruling.

Cross-Faction Self-Check

Look. The six factions read as distinct. Crusade is black iron and ember, heavy and symmetric. Hegemony is pale grey and steel blue, clean wedges and drone escorts. Hullbreakers are soot and rust, lopsided and dark, trophy plates bolted on. The Chorus is dark olive flesh and bile glow, grown not built. Glasswrights are clear crystal and silver, faceted and light-carrying. Eclipse is dark violet-black and white-gold, layered sunshade hulls. The risk of similarity is between Crusade and Eclipse (both dark, both heavy, both patient), but the Crusade is iron and ember and frost, while Eclipse is violet and gold and ring-shaped. The Crusade has radiator spires; Eclipse has eclipse rings. They read differently.
Play. The six factions play differently. Crusade is line-of-battle, advance and volley. Hegemony is formation-centric, sync fire near the flagship. Hullbreakers are pack hunters, close from flanks, ram and board. The Chorus is swarm, no shields, regrow, overwhelm. Glasswrights are fast, precision, fragile under shields, short jumps. Eclipse is slow, armoured, repair swarms, Null weapons. The risk of similarity is between Crusade and Eclipse (both defensive, both endure), but the Crusade endures through shields and brace, while Eclipse endures through armour and repair. The Crusade's best moment is the full volley after a long advance; Eclipse's best moment is the repair swarm rebuilding a fallen ship. They feel different.
Voice. The six factions speak differently. Crusade uses oaths, chalk, dawn, debts. Hegemony uses data, projections, Registry Orders, "acceptable." Hullbreakers use short flat brutal lines, kills, trails, sound, trophies. The Chorus uses "we," taste, heat, smell, teeth. Glasswrights use light, reflection, focus, grief, apology. Eclipse uses memory, the Archive, the dark, warmth remembered, frame counts. The risk of similarity is between Glasswrights and Eclipse (both Starwright descendants, both grieving), but the Glasswrights mourn in light and colour-pulse, while Eclipse remembers in dark and frame counts. The Glasswrights apologise; Eclipse doubts. They read differently.
What I did about it. For Crusade and Eclipse, I leaned into the material difference: iron vs. violet plate, ember vs. gold corona, radiator spires vs. eclipse rings, shields vs. armour, brace vs. repair. For Glasswrights and Eclipse, I leaned into the emotional difference: the Glasswrights grieve outward (apology, colour-pulse, the struck tone), while Eclipse grieves inward (doubt, frame counts, the image of Ardent shown in silence). For Hullbreakers and the Chorus, both aggressive, I leaned into the difference between hunting (pack, trophies, the kill) and consuming (swarm, regrowth, the swallow). The Hullbreakers want to be the strongest; the Chorus wants more. Those are different hungers.
