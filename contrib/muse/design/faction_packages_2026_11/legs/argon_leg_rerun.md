Part 1: Sunward Crusade

1. Sunward Crusade
1. Identity

The Sunward Crusade is the combined fleet of the Vesper Reach, a frontier of forty-odd systems that the Hegemony wrote off seventy years ago when their stars began to dim. Refusing cortical implants and AI, their unaugmented crews fly heavy black-iron battleships armed with Sear broadsides, Oath lances, and Cinder torpedoes drawn from studying the drifted core S-6 Seren the Last Flame. Bound by a generational oath and chalking every crew name by hand onto their hulls, they march sunward to burn out Eclipse's siphon arrays before their home worlds freeze.

Playing the Sunward Crusade feels like commanding a relentless, moving wall of hot iron. Because your Sear broadsides hit 40 percent harder after a ship moves two or more hexes, every turn forces a choice between bracing in place to weather incoming fire or surging forward into crossfires to unleash boosted rolling volleys. Your risk is getting outflanked or kited by faster fleets while your glowing heat-radiator spires draw fire, and your best moment is pushing a full battle line two hexes forward in unison to smash an enemy centre with boosted broadsides and burning Cinder torpedoes.

2. Lore

Origin: The Vesper Reach, forty-odd frontier systems of mining worlds, farm moons, and stations around the capital star Vesper. Ninety years ago the Siphoning dimmed their stars; seventy years ago, after the Registry calculated that evacuating billions was not cost-effective and archived their records, the Reach seceded, cut out its cortical links, built weapons from the drifted core S-6 Seren the Last Flame, and launched a new crusade every generation from the Cinder Line.

What they want in the Hollow Sun war: Destroy Eclipse's siphon arrays, stop the Siphoning to save their freezing worlds, and prove the Hegemony's arithmetic wrong.

How they see the other five factions:

Hegemony Navy: "They did the arithmetic. We are what was left over."

Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Scavengers at a funeral."

The Chorus: "The dark that eats. Burn it."

The Glasswrights: "Their siblings run the siphons. Sorry does not warm a world."

Eclipse: "They are stealing our sun to light a corpse."

3. Look

People: Weathered, unaugmented humans with frost-scarred skin, surgical scars at the temple where implants were cut out, oath brands, grey stubble, and hard tired eyes. They wear high-collared dark void-coats with heavy shoulder plates and chalk dust on their gloves. Portraits vary in pose and framing across gun-decks, observation blisters, carrier catwalks, and the Roll wall, with scars derived from frostbite, surgery, and propellant burns.

Ships: Symmetric black-iron hulls (Warden, Harrow, Vigil, Hearth carrier, Bastion, flagship Daybreak) with frost-white chalk markings, heavy manual broadside decks, big engine bells, and tall heat-radiator spires glowing bright ember orange to shed reactor heat in vacuum. No rust. In the fleet economy, their hulls sit at a sturdy middle cost, paid for by the Reach's debt of days.

Palette in one line: Black iron, frost white, bright ember orange.

Effects colours: White-hot Sear muzzle flashes cooling to ember orange, bright orange Cinder torpedo plumes, and frost-white screen flickers.

Part 2: Hegemony Navy

2. Hegemony Navy
1. Identity

The Hegemony Navy is the armed fleet of the core human state, directed from Meridian by the Registry, a vast optimization intelligence to which every citizen and officer is cortically linked. They fight in clean wedge formations around their flagship, where linked fire-control networks boost accuracy, bow ion cannons strip shields, carriers deploy fighter screens, and retro thrusters make backing off cheap. Having captured the core V-2 Vyre the Storm Queen twenty-five years ago, the Registry now calculates that acquiring all six cores is the highest-value objective in known space.

Playing the Hegemony Navy feels like running a cold, geometric calculation where formation spacing is everything. Because your ships sync their fire near the flagship and can back off cheaply with retro thrusters, each turn is a spatial puzzle of maintaining your wedge while kiting brawlers into overlapping ion and plasma arcs. Your risk is having your formation split apart or your flagship isolated by rams or fold-jumps, and your best moment is stripping an enemy dreadnought's shields with linked ion prongs and deleting its hull with a synchronized pulse-laser broadside while sliding cleanly out of range.

2. Lore

Origin: Meridian and the core human worlds, where every citizen wears a temple cortical link connected to the Registry. Seventy years ago the Registry wrote off the Vesper Reach as statistically unviable; twenty-five years ago a survey fleet captured V-2 Vyre the Storm Queen, reverse-engineered its shield and ion tech, and made capturing all six cores its top priority.

What they want in the Hollow Sun war: Acquire, register, and integrate all six Lattice cores as the ultimate power source, while observing Eclipse as an instructive machine civilization.

How they see the other five factions:

Sunward Crusade: "Secessionists. Unlinked. Statistically doomed."

Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Category: violence, ongoing."

The Chorus: "Biological incident, category one."

The Glasswrights: "Insufficient data. Possibly grieving."

Eclipse: "A machine civilisation with no oversight. The Registry finds this instructive."

3. Look

People: Composed humans in pale grey high-collared uniforms and gloves, with a faint blue cortical-link port at the temple, blue data moving across ocular implants, and thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Portraits vary across glass flag-bridges, Meridian holographic wells, carrier control towers, and dark unlinked quarters, with scars shown as cracked ocular lenses, cauterized temple ports, or pale ion burns.

Ships: Clean, symmetric wedge hulls (picket, cordon, vanguard, dominion, super-dreadnought flagship The Iron Mandate) prefixed HNS, in pale grey with steel-blue trim, rows of identical turrets, bow ion prongs, blue lights pulsing in time across the fleet, and small drone escorts. Economy is standardized: medium-cost hulls with predictable Meridian upgrade and repair rates.

Palette in one line: Pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

Effects colours: Cyan-blue ion lightning, pale blue plasma lances, crisp blue-white pulse laser bursts, and geometric blue deflector ripples.
