Faction Design Packages, First Drafts
1. Sunward Crusade
Identity

The frontier the Hegemony wrote off, come to stop the Siphoning. Black-iron battleships and unaugmented crews who chalk every name on the hull because the Registry deleted theirs. They fight in line, they hold, and they pay for the dawn with names.

To play them is to commit early and hold the line. You pick a firing lane and a target and you keep both, because your broadsides hit harder after moving and your braces let you eat a round you should not have. The risk is that a line that holds too long is a line that dies in place; the best moment is the round your torpedoes set three enemy ships burning and your Hearth launches a wing nobody budgeted for.

Lore

Origin: the Vesper Reach, forty-odd frontier systems whose stars are dimming. The Registry ran the numbers, found evacuation not cost-effective, and archived them. The Reach seceded, pooled its navies, traced the Siphoning to the Hollow Sun, and has sent a new crusade every generation since.

Want: burn out the siphon arrays, stop the Siphoning, take back their stars, and show the Hegemony the arithmetic was wrong.

How they see the others:

Hegemony Navy: "They did the arithmetic. We are what was left over."

Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Scavengers at a funeral."

The Chorus: "The dark that eats. Burn it."

The Glasswrights: "Their siblings run the siphons. Sorry does not warm a world."

Eclipse: "They are stealing our sun to light a corpse."

Look

People: weathered unaugmented humans, frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, chalk dust on gloves, high-collared dark void-coats with heavy shoulder plates.

Ships: black iron hulls, frost-white markings, ember-orange windows and engines, tall heat-radiator spires glowing bright ember orange, heavy broadside decks, big engine bells, old manual gun decks. No rust. Symmetric.

Palette: black iron, frost white, bright ember.

Effects colours: ember orange for Sear and Cinder fire, frost white for Oath lance beams, dull iron grey for flak.

Admirals

Admiral Roderic Vex. A line admiral who came up through the gun decks and still walks them before a battle, chalking the new names himself. He believes a crusade is a debt paid in order, not in fury. Play style: mass your line, move two hexes, and fire everything in one round.

Command: Fire for effect (every ship fires a free volley at its best target now).

Skill tree (idiom: chalk, watch, line):

Branch 1, The Chalk Line: T1 "Name the Target" (mark one enemy ship; your ships prefer it as a target this round), T2 "Read the Roll" (at the start of each round, reveal one enemy ship's next intended action), T3 "The Long Watch" (once per battle, cancel one enemy reinforcement or summon).

Branch 2, Hold the Line: T1 "Brace in Place" (one ship gains a brace that also reduces incoming ion and boarding effects), T2 "Pass the Word" (one ship's brace also grants an adjacent ship a half brace), T3 "The Line Holds" (if three or more of your ships are adjacent at end of round, all gain a small brace until your next turn).

Branch 3, Ember Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% Sear battery damage against ships that moved this round," T2 "+15% Cinder torpedo burn duration," T3 "+15% broadside damage when firing after moving two or more hexes."

Spotlight: Vex chalks the name of a dead world on the Daybreak's bow while the fleet watches, then orders the line forward without a speech.

Oath-Captain Malek Draven. A defector who cut out his own implants and was watched for a decade before he was trusted with a squadron. He fights torpedo-first and treats every launch as a promise. Play style: set the whole enemy line burning early, then let the fires do the work.

Command: Hunter's salvo (torpedoes reload at once, +30% damage this round).

Skill tree (idiom: oath, debt, burn):

Branch 1, Sworn Volley: T1 "Mark the Debt" (one enemy ship takes +15% torpedo damage until end of round), T2 "Two Promises" (one ship may fire torpedoes at two different targets), T3 "Debt Called" (once per battle, all torpedoes this round ignore shields).

Branch 2, Cold Watch: T1 "Read the Wake" (reveal one hidden or cloaked enemy ship), T2 "Stand to" (your ships gain +1 hex of torpedo range this round), T3 "The Watch Relieved" (swap the positions of two of your ships once per battle).

Branch 3, Cinder Reckoning (numerics only): T1 "+15% torpedo damage against burning ships," T2 "+15% burn duration on Cinder torpedoes," T3 "+15% torpedo reload speed."

Spotlight: Draven launches a salvo at a ship he cannot hit, and the miss is the point: the burning wreck drifts into the enemy's firing lane.

Commodore Sera Ashgrove. A carrier commander who was deckborn and has never set foot on a world. She treats squadrons as the real fleet and hulls as the escorts. Play style: overwhelm with wings, keep the Hearth alive, and strike twice.

Command: Scramble all wings (carriers launch an extra wing past the hangar limit, all squadrons strike again at +50%).

Skill tree (idiom: watch, wing, dawn):

Branch 1, Launch Window: T1 "Holding Pattern" (one squadron may delay its strike to the end of round), T2 "Two Dawns" (one squadron strikes twice at reduced damage), T3 "The Whole Sky" (once per battle, launch one squadron from a non-carrier ship).

Branch 2, Deck Discipline: T1 "Recover Under Fire" (one squadron may rearm without returning to the carrier), T2 "Screen the Hearth" (one fighter squadron intercepts one enemy strike this round), T3 "The Deck Holds" (if the Hearth is damaged, its next launch is free).

Branch 3, Wing Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% squadron damage," T2 "+15% squadron strike range," T3 "+15% squadron hit chance against ships that moved this round."

Spotlight: Ashgrove launches a wing from a burning Hearth, and the wing strikes anyway, past the hangar limit, on the round the Hearth dies.

High Admiral Oryn Thale. The oldest admiral in the fleet, a survivor of three failed crusades who keeps the Roll of Vesper in his head. He believes the crusade is a relay, not a battle: someone must survive to hand the names on. Play style: soak the first rounds, keep ships alive, then convert the brace into a counter.

Command: Iron wall (shields refill, 40% less hull damage this round).

Skill tree (idiom: roll, relay, hold):

Branch 1, The Roll: T1 "Carry the Names" (when one of your ships is destroyed, one other ship gains a small brace), T2 "The Relay" (when your flagship survives a round, one damaged ship repairs a small amount), T3 "The Roll Continues" (once per battle, prevent one ship's destruction at 1 hull).

Branch 2, The Wall: T1 "Step Back" (one ship may move one hex without provoking), T2 "Shield the Weak" (one ship grants its brace to a lower-hull ally), T3 "The Wall Bends" (convert one ship's remaining brace into a free volley).

Branch 3, Iron Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% brace effectiveness," T2 "+15% hull repair from the relay," T3 "+15% shield refill on Iron wall rounds."

Spotlight: Thale reads the Roll of Vesper aloud to a bridge crew that has never heard the older names, then orders the fleet to retreat in order, not rout.

Nemesis

The Crusade's rival is an Eclipse Archivist who commands the siphon defense. Meeting rhythm: the Archivist appears in sector 2 with a small escort, withdraws if its flagship drops below half hull, and returns in sector 3 with a repaired fleet and one new adaptation. It stands when the siphon array it guards is threatened. It adapts by hardening the array's escort screen, never by directly countering a weakness you have already shown it. Voice: cold, exact, grieving, counts of years, bodies, and the memory of noon.

Sample lines:

Meeting: "This is my ninth body. I have watched your crusade in eight of them. You are still paying in names."

Mid-battle: "Your line is beautiful. It will not be enough. It was not enough at Vesper."

When its array is threatened: "You would put out the last light to save a small one. I understand. I remember noon."

Victory: "Your names are chalk. Mine are in the Archive. Both are kept."

Defeat: "Go home. Chalk this too. It will not warm anyone."

Progression and Story

The Crusade's fiction is settled: a new crusade each generation, each learning from the last. Run unlocks: the Roll of Vesper grows with each failed run, and each name chalked adds a small permanent option (a new starting ship pattern, a new torpedo variant, a new brace pattern). Between runs the player picks which lesson from the lost crusade to carry forward, one only. The finale: run 5's victory is the fleet reaching the array with a ship from every previous crusade in the line, and the last beat is Thale chalking the current run's names beside the old ones.

2. Hegemony Navy
Identity

The Registry's navy: linked crews, wedge hulls, drone escorts. Every officer is a node, every loss is a projection, and every projection is "acceptable." They are the largest human state in the region and the most quietly terrifying.

To play them is to build a formation and keep it, because fire syncs near the flagship and backing off is cheap. The risk is that a formation is a target; the best moment is the round the ion cannons strip three shields and the fighter screen arrives on schedule, exactly on schedule.

Lore

Origin: Meridian and the core worlds, run by the Registry, a vast intelligence that plans careers, designs fleets, and keeps losses within acceptable limits.

Want: acquire the six cores as power, register them, expand. They already hold Vyre.

How they see the others:

Sunward Crusade: "Secessionists. Unlinked. Statistically doomed."

Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Category: violence, ongoing."

The Chorus: "Biological incident, category one."

The Glasswrights: "Insufficient data. Possibly grieving."

Eclipse: "A machine civilisation with no oversight. The Registry finds this instructive."

Look

People: composed humans with tasteful implants, a cortical-link port at the temple with faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Pale grey high-collared uniforms, gloves, calm to the point of eerie.

Ships: light grey hulls, steel-blue trim, blue-white windows, clean wedges, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the fleet, small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.

Palette: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

Effects colours: light blue for plasma, deeper blue for ion, blue-white for pulse lasers.

Admirals

First Admiral Ilsa Kassar. The Registry's favoured commander, a woman who has never lost a fleet and has never been allowed to forget it. She believes the formation is a moral object: a fleet that holds together is a fleet that deserves to. Play style: keep the wedge tight, sync fire every round, and let the ion do the stripping.

Command: Synchronized broadside (all ships fire at one target, damage shared).

Skill tree (idiom: link, projection, acceptable):

Branch 1, The Link: T1 "Designate Node" (one ship becomes the sync anchor; ships adjacent to it fire together), T2 "Reroute" (move the sync anchor to another ship once per round), T3 "Full Sync" (once per battle, all ships fire as if adjacent to the anchor).

Branch 2, Projection: T1 "Forecast" (reveal the next enemy reinforcement wave), T2 "Acceptable Loss" (convert one ship's destruction into a repair on two others), T3 "Recalculate" (once per battle, undo one enemy critical hit).

Branch 3, Registry Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% ion shield strip," T2 "+15% sync fire damage," T3 "+15% drone escort durability."

Spotlight: Kassar's link goes down mid-battle and she fights the rest of the round on voice and hand signals, and the formation holds anyway.

Commodore Dace Vorn. A mid-rank officer who has been passed over three times for promotion because the Registry projects he will refuse an order one day. He has not refused one yet, and the not-yet is the whole of him. Play style: interdiction, delay, and let the enemy come apart on his terms.

Command: Interdiction strike (target ship cannot move or fire next round).

Skill tree (idiom: order, timestamp, projection):

Branch 1, Interdiction: T1 "Pin" (one ship cannot move this round), T2 "Crossfire" (two ships pin one target between them), T3 "Lockdown" (once per battle, one enemy ship cannot act for two rounds).

Branch 2, Delay: T1 "Back Off" (one ship retreats one hex without provoking), T2 "Screen" (one ship intercepts one enemy strike), T3 "The Order Stands" (once per battle, cancel one enemy order).

Branch 3, Timestamp Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% interdiction duration," T2 "+15% damage against pinned ships," T3 "+15% retreat distance."

Spotlight: Vorn receives an order he knows is wrong, follows it, and logs the timestamp before the fleet dies, and the log is what saves the next fleet.

Fleet Marshal Corin Reyes. A carrier marshal who came up through the fighter screen and still flies a desk that looks like a cockpit. Reyes believes the strike group is the navy's real answer to Eclipse: machines versus machines, but with a pilot in the loop. Play style: launch early, strike deep, and keep the fighters cycling.

Command: Launch the strike group (all carriers launch a full wing, all squadrons strike at +25%).

Skill tree (idiom: cycle, screen, loop):

Branch 1, Strike Cycle: T1 "Ready Five" (one squadron is ready at the start of round), T2 "Double Cycle" (one squadron strikes twice at reduced damage), T3 "The Whole Group" (once per battle, all squadrons strike in the same round).

Branch 2, Screen: T1 "Intercept" (one fighter squadron intercepts one enemy strike), T2 "Escort" (one squadron escorts a bomber group, reducing losses), T3 "The Screen Holds" (if three or more squadrons are active, all gain a small evasion).

Branch 3, Loop Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% squadron damage," T2 "+15% squadron cycle speed," T3 "+15% squadron evasion against Null."

Spotlight: Reyes flies a fighter sim against his own strike group the night before a battle, and the sim is the only honest projection he trusts.

Line Admiral Oskar Maren. A defensive specialist who was assigned to the Shield wall order because the Registry projected he would never waste it. He has spent his career not wasting it, and the not-wasting has become a kind of faith. Play style: hold the wall, absorb the first strike, then convert the shields into a counter-volley.

Command: Shield wall order (all ships gain shields this round, shield effects doubled).

Skill tree (idiom: wall, order, absorb):

Branch 1, The Wall: T1 "Raise Shields" (one ship gains a full shield), T2 "Share the Wall" (one ship shares its shield with an adjacent ally), T3 "The Wall Advances" (once per battle, all ships gain a shield and may move one hex).

Branch 2, Absorb: T1 "Take the Hit" (one ship redirects one incoming hit to itself), T2 "Convert" (one absorbed hit becomes a free ion shot), T3 "The Wall Answers" (once per battle, all shields convert to damage on one enemy ship).

Branch 3, Shield Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% shield strength," T2 "+15% shield regen," T3 "+15% damage conversion from shields."

Spotlight: Maren holds the wall through a round the Registry projected would kill the fleet, and the projection is wrong for the first time in his career, and he does not smile.

Nemesis

The Hegemony's rival is an Ashfall Hullbreaker raider captain, a pack leader who has been raiding Registry supply lines for a decade. Meeting rhythm: appears in sector 1 with a small pack, flees a losing fight, and returns in sector 2 and 3 with more stolen Registry scrap bolted on. It adapts by stealing a Registry ship between meetings (which returns to you when it dies). Voice: short, flat, brutal, counting kills and trails.

Sample lines:

Meeting: "We heard your engines three jumps out. Your wake still stinks of fear."

Mid-battle: "Your ships hum. We can hear them a system away."

When it steals a ship: "Strongest keeps it. You can have it back when I am dead."

Victory: "Good trophies. Your Registry will not log this one."

Defeat: "You fight hard. I will remember your wake."

Progression and Story

Hegemony fiction: the Registry learns by projection, not by loss. Each failed run is logged, and the projection model improves, and the improvement is cold and administrative. Run unlocks: the projection model adds a small permanent option each run (a new starting formation, a new ion variant, a new fighter pattern). The finale: run 5's victory is the Registry projecting the win before the battle starts and the fleet winning anyway, and the last beat is the Registry logging the win as "acceptable" and moving on.

3. Ashfall Hullbreakers
Identity

Plated alien pack hunters who track by heat and sound, in ships that run dark, stripping every wreck. They want the biggest fight in known space and every piece of scrap worth taking. They are the Glasswrights' opposite in every way: many where few, short-lived where long, brutal where grieving.

To play them is to commit to the flank and never stop moving. You close from behind, you ram, you board, and your scrap guns roll wild extra shots. The risk is that a pack that slows down is a pack that dies; the best moment is the round the boarding claws fire and the enemy flagship's shields are still up and you do not care.

Lore

Origin: the Ashfall belt, a cold heavy-gravity world and burned rocks, where only the strongest packs ate. Rattlehold is their biggest den.

Want: the war itself, Rook, and every wreck worth stripping.

How they see the others:

Sunward Crusade: "They fight hard and do not run. Good trophies."

Hegemony Navy: "Their ships hum. You can hear them a system away."

The Chorus: "Wet, and hungry. The only thing out here that fights like we do."

The Glasswrights: "Pretty little ships. They shatter well."

Eclipse: "No smell, no blood. Dead metal that gets back up. Kill it twice."

Look

People: the void hunters. Bear-heavy mass, big shoulder hump, thick forearms, heavy claws, coarse mane only over the hump and shoulders in grey, black or rust-brown, tough dark hide elsewhere. The face is bare, short, blunt, armoured in overlapping dull horn plates like a pangolin, with dark leathery creases. The jaw splits down the middle of the chin and opens sideways, lined with teeth. Four small eyes across the brow, heat pits beside them, ridged plates down the back and shoulders. Scrap from other factions strapped over them in harnesses. No quill dreadlocks, no mouth tusks, no helmet look, no mesh, no humanoid bodybuilder torso.

Ships: matte soot black and rust, few windows, dim red running lights, red-orange engine glow, big listening dishes and wake-sniffer scoops at the bow, lopsided hull plates torn from every faction bolted on in their old colours, boarding claws at the bow, chains between hull sections.

Palette: soot, rust, dried blood, trophy colours, red running lights.

Effects colours: red-orange for rockets and scrap, dull yellow for boarding claws, rust-brown for chaff.

Visual Directions (three)

The shoulder-heavy silhouette. A hullbreaker standing three-quarters on, hunched so the shoulder hump is the highest point of the body. Coarse rust-brown mane only over the hump, neck and shoulders, thick forearms hanging, heavy claws almost touching the deck. The face is bare and plated in dull overlapping horn, the sideways jaw slightly open, four small eyes glinting across the brow, heat pits as dark pores beside them. Ridged back plates show above the mane. A Hegemony panel is strapped over one shoulder, a Glasswright crystal shard over the other. Lighting: dim red from below, cold blue from behind. Background: a wreck field, soot and drifting chains. The one thing: the hump is the body, not a backpack.

The listening pose. A hullbreaker crouched low, one hand flat on the deck of its own ship, head tilted, the four eyes closed, the heat pits flaring. The mane is short and black, the hide dark grey, the face plates duller than usual. A wake-sniffer scoop is visible through a bulkhead behind it, the dish angled. Lighting: red running lights along the deck, one cold white work lamp. Background: the inside of a running-dark ship, cables and chains, a trophy plate from a Crusade battleship on the wall. The one thing: the pose is listening, not looking.

The challenge. A hullbreaker standing upright in a den, mane bristling, sideways jaw wide, claws spread, one foot on a stripped Hegemony turret. The hide is rust-brown, the horn plates chipped and scarred, one eye clouded. A trophy harness carries three faction plates and a Glasswright lens. Lighting: firelight from a scrap brazier, deep shadows. Background: Rattlehold, welded scrap walls, chains, a circle of other hullbreakers watching. The one thing: the challenge is social, not monstrous; the pack is watching, and it is a ritual.

Admirals

Warlord Grax Ironmaw. A warlord who killed his predecessor in a challenge and has held the den for eleven years, longer than any warlord in living memory. He believes the pack is the only honest government. Play style: full burn, close from the flank, and ram what is left.

Command: Full burn (all ships move +2 hexes this round, rams deal +50%).

Skill tree (idiom: pack, trail, challenge):

Branch 1, The Trail: T1 "Sniff the Wake" (reveal one enemy ship's position and heading), T2 "Cut the Trail" (one enemy ship leaves no wake this round, but loses one hex of movement), T3 "The Whole Pack Smells Blood" (once per battle, all ships gain +1 hex of movement toward one target).

Branch 2, The Challenge: T1 "Call Out" (one enemy ship must target your flagship this round), T2 "Answer the Call" (your flagship gains a free ram if challenged), T3 "The Challenge Stands" (once per battle, force one enemy ship to duel your flagship for one round).

Branch 3, Iron Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% ram damage," T2 "+15% rocket damage," T3 "+15% boarding success chance."

Spotlight: Ironmaw calls out the enemy flagship in front of both fleets, and the enemy accepts, and the duel is the battle.

Sable the Smiling Knife. A raider who earned her epithet by boarding a Hegemony cruiser alone and coming back with the captain's coat. She believes the dark is a knife and the pack is the hand. Play style: knife in the dark, one ship, one kill, and vanish.

Command: Knife in the dark (one ship cloaks, next attack deals double damage).

Skill tree (idiom: dark, knife, quiet):

Branch 1, The Dark: T1 "Go Quiet" (one ship cloaks until it fires), T2 "Two Knives" (one cloaked ship may fire twice at reduced damage), T3 "The Dark Answers" (once per battle, all ships cloak for one round).

Branch 2, The Knife: T1 "Mark the Throat" (one enemy ship takes +15% damage from your next attack), T2 "Twist" (one attack ignores shields), T3 "The Knife Turns" (once per battle, one attack deals double damage and cannot be braced).

Branch 3, Quiet Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% cloak duration," T2 "+15% damage from cloak," T3 "+15% evasion while cloaked."

Spotlight: Sable boards a Hegemony ship alone, and the ship's log records her as a maintenance fault.

Old Mott. A raider who has outlived forty captains, mostly by killing them, and who now commands by not being challenged. He believes a pack that stops challenging is a pack that stops being a pack. Play style: release the swarm, overwhelm with numbers, and let the young ones die first.

Command: Release the swarm (all ships launch boarding pods, all pods strike at +50%).

Skill tree (idiom: swarm, young, old):

Branch 1, The Swarm: T1 "Send the Young" (one ship launches two boarding pods), T2 "The Swarm Grows" (each boarding pod that survives spawns a second pod), T3 "The Whole Den" (once per battle, all ships launch boarding pods).

Branch 2, The Old: T1 "Watch the Young" (reveal one enemy boarding attempt), T2 "Teach the Young" (one ship gains a free boarding attempt after a successful one), T3 "The Old Survives" (once per battle, one ship survives a boarding attempt at 1 hull).

Branch 3, Swarm Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% boarding pod damage," T2 "+15% boarding pod durability," T3 "+15% pod spawn chance."

Spotlight: Mott sends the young ones first, and the young ones die, and he counts them, and the count is the point.

Vesh the Collector. A raider who keeps a ledger of every wreck stripped and every trophy taken, and who has never lost a challenge because he never accepts one he cannot win. He believes the pack is a business. Play style: patch and weld, strip the dead, and come back stronger.

Command: Patch and weld (all ships repair 25% hull, one destroyed section restored).

Skill tree (idiom: scrap, trophy, weld):

Branch 1, The Scrap: T1 "Strip the Dead" (when an enemy ship is destroyed, one of your ships repairs a small amount), T2 "Bolt It On" (one ship gains a captured weapon for the rest of the battle), T3 "The Whole Wreck" (once per battle, strip an enemy ship without destroying it, disabling one system).

Branch 2, The Trophy: T1 "Take the Plate" (one ship gains a small armor plate from a destroyed enemy), T2 "Take the Gun" (one ship gains a captured weapon), T3 "The Trophy Wall" (once per battle, all ships gain a captured plate).

Branch 3, Weld Arithmetic (numerics only): T1 "+15% repair from stripping," T2 "+15% captured weapon damage," T3 "+15% hull restored from patch and weld."

Spotlight: Vesh strips a dying Crusade battleship while its crew is still aboard, and the crew watches, and he waves.

Nemesis

The Hullbreakers' rival is a Glasswright Lens-keeper, a duelist who keeps range and withdraws, and who returns with the same four-ship elite fleet, upgraded. Meeting rhythm: appears in sector 2, withdraws if its flagship drops below half hull, returns in sector 3 with the same four ships, upgraded. It adapts by upgrading its fleet, never by countering a shown weakness. Voice: quiet, precise, mournful, light words, apologises often and means it.

Sample lines:

Meeting: "You break things because breaking is easier than grief. I am sorry. I will still stop you."

Mid-battle: "Your pack is beautiful. I am sorry about the light."

When it withdraws: "I will return. I am sorry. I will return with better ships."

Victory: "I am sorry. Your dead are returned to the light."

Defeat: "I am sorry. I was not enough. I will be enough next time."

Progression and Story

Hullbreaker fiction: the pack learns by challenge, not by loss. Each failed run is a challenge lost, and the pack that comes back is the pack that survived it, with the dead stripped and the scrap bolted on. Run unlocks: each run adds a new trophy to the pack's harness, a permanent option (a stolen weapon variant, a captured plate pattern, a new boarding pod). The finale: run 5's victory is the pack stripping the Hollow Sun's wreck field and finding Rook, and the last beat is the warlord chalking the run's dead on the trophy wall, and the pack howling.

4. The Chorus
Identity

A living weapon that ate its makers. No shields at all, but their flesh knits back together every round. They speak as "we," because there are many voices in the swarm and one of them remembers being the Fleshwrights.

To play them is to commit to the swarm and never stop growing. You have no shields and you do not need them, because your hull regrows every round and your boarding tendrils eat through armor. The risk is that a swarm that spreads too thin is a swarm that dies in pieces; the best moment is the round your brood sacs release a wave nobody budgeted for and the enemy's shields are already gone.

Lore

Origin: the Fleshwrights' war vats in the dark after the collapse; since then, the dark between stars. They grew the swarm to fight Eclipse, and it consumed them.

Want: eat the other five cores, reach the sun Memory still remembers, and finish the war it was grown for.

How they see the others:

Sunward Crusade: "Hot. Bitter. Loud."

Hegemony Navy: "Many. Neat. All taste the same."

Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Fur. Rust. Hard to chew."

The Glasswrights: "The makers' kin. Memory knows them. Hunger does not care."

Eclipse: "Memory knows them. Cold. Wrong. Made to eat them."

Look

People: grown organisms with no face, eyes or limbs, glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, bile. Nothing on the surface recalls a person. Admirals: the Matriarch of Nine Throats, Razormaw, Voice of the Deep, the Mending Throng.

Ships: grown, not built, ridges, pores, veins, glowing sacs, no panels or pipes. Dark olive-brown flesh, ochre chitin trim, bile yellow-green glow. Symmetric where the body allows. Tendrils, brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue.

Palette: dark olive, ochre bone, bile yellow-green light.

Effects colours: bile yellow-green for acid, dark olive for spores, ochre for spines.

Admirals

The Matriarch of Nine Throats. The oldest throat in the swarm, a body that has grown nine separate mouths and speaks in nine voices at once. She believes the swarm is a single appetite with many mouths and that Memory is a toothache. Play style: spawn, overwhelm, and strike twice.

Command: Spawning surge (broods release extra swarms, all swarms strike again at +50%).

Skill tree (idiom: hunger, mouth, grow):

5. The Glasswrights
Identity

Few, long-lived masters of light and crystal. Blindingly fast, heavily shielded, fragile once the shields fall. They are the Starwrights who stayed flesh and worked in glass, and they are grieving.

To play them is to strike first and never get hit. You are faster than anything on the board, your shields are heavy, and your precision beams find the crit. The risk is that once the shields fall you are fragile and you cannot brace; the best moment is the round your Prism lance splits a ship in half and the rainbow fringe is still fading when the next shot fires.

Lore

Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, a stored light glowing deep inside. Slender and graceful. Symmetric.

Palette: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, stored light.

Cross-Faction Self-Check

Look overlap. The Crusade and the Hegemony are both human navies with grey/blue palettes. I pushed the Crusade to black iron and ember, and the Hegemony to pale grey and implant blue, and gave the Crusade radiator spires and manual gun decks while the Hegemony gets clean wedges and drone escorts. The Hullbreakers and the Chorus are both "alien," so I kept the Hullbreakers as plated mammal-derived hunters with trophies and the Chorus as faceless grown flesh with sacs and no trophies. The Glasswrights and Eclipse are both "made" peoples, so I kept the Glasswrights as organic crystal-laced flesh with grief and the Eclipse as machine bodies with memory.

Play overlap. The Crusade and the Hegemony both want formation and sync. I made the Crusade about holding a line and the Hegemony about a formation that can be recalculated. The Hullbreakers and the Chorus both want to close and board. I made the Hullbreakers about the trail and the challenge and the Chorus about the swarm and the regrowth. The Glasswrights and Eclipse both want precision and shields (or armor). I made the Glasswrights about speed and crits and Eclipse about armor and rebuild.

Voice overlap. The main risk is Eclipse and the Glasswrights both grieving. I kept Eclipse on memory, the Archive and the lost sun, and the Glasswrights on light, glass, reflection and grief, and I kept them apart in every line. The Hegemony and Eclipse both sound cold. I kept the Hegemony on data, projections, the Registry and "acceptable," and Eclipse on memory, doubt and counts of bodies. The Chorus and the Hullbreakers both sound short. I kept the Chorus on "we" and taste, heat, smell, teeth, and the Hullbreakers on trails, sound, trophies and the pack. No faction sings. No faction uses another's motif.

Skill tree overlap. I checked each tree against the six rules. Branch 1 and Branch 2 are decision-changing in every tree. Branch 3 is numerics only, at 15 percent, and no two admirals in the same faction share a numeric branch. No skill restates an admiral's Command at lower power. No skill rewards losing, dying or retreating; the closest are the Crusade's "Carry the Names" and the Eclipse's "Back Up," both of which convert a catastrophe rather than reward it.
