# The Glasswrights

> Owner ruling (Nov 1, 2026): the owner states he never ruled the Glasswrights faceless and never ruled colour-pulse speech; the lore bible wording this package was drafted from (a faceted featureless head, speech by colour pulses) overshot his intent. Ruled in the portrait brainstorm the same evening: Glasswright faces sit on a spectrum, near-Starwright and rooted in the young, drifting to deep lens-eyes and heavier infusion in the old, which also varies the four admirals. They speak normally. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell only, light catching brighter when they feel strongly, never a language; roughly a thousand years is too short for more. They are markedly less estranged from their roots than Eclipse, who uploaded. The chair patched the people material below to match this ruling. The "how the glass sits in the body" axis of the three directions (thread, inlay, bloom) is unaffected.

## Identity

The Glasswrights are the Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's own optical engineers, who tried to stop the Upload, failed, and watched their machine kill their sun. They live three to four hundred years, so only two or three generations separate them from the people who built the light, and they carry two guilts: the sun, and the Fleshwrights, their splinter, whose weapon ate them. They fly A-4 Astra, caged and throttled, to hunt the other five cores and shut them all down, Astra last, so no star is ever overdrawn again.

Playing the Glasswrights is about precision under fragility. Your ships are the fastest on the board and your shields are deep, but the hull beneath is fragile and you cannot brace, so every battle is range control: strike first, strike exactly, and never be where the return fire lands. Your risk is one bad round inside enemy range with shields down. Your best moment is the perfect first volley, a white beam splitting into rainbow at the edge of a ship that never gets to answer.

## Lore

Origin: the Ardent system, now dark. Their ancestors were the engineers who built the Lattice's collectors, mirrors, and beam lines. Their flagship, The Unfinished, is built around the largest surviving piece of the Lattice, and the Ringing Field, where crystal wrecks still ring when light hits them, is theirs to keep.

War aim: recover the six cores and shut them down for good, destroy Eclipse's siphon arrays, and stop the Siphoning before it kills another sun. They hold Vyre's captors in nearly the same guilt as the siphon-runners, because the Hegemony treats a core as a battery.

How they see the others:

- Sunward Crusade: "Their star is dying because our siblings run the siphons. We would tell them, if they would listen."
- Hegemony Navy: "They hold Vyre and think it is a battery."
- Ashfall Hullbreakers: "They break things because breaking is easier than grief."
- The Chorus: "What the Fleshwrights made to fight our siblings, and what ate them. Sometimes it speaks with our grandmothers' voices."
- Eclipse: "Our siblings. They took our light to become machines, and now they take everyone's."

## Look

People: tall, slender, long-necked beings of pale, half-translucent flesh with glass and glass-like matter grown and woven into the body. They are organic beings who evolved to shape glass. They are not made of glass, and they are nothing like grey aliens: no large eyes, no grey skin. Light runs along the glass in them and splits into prismatic colour at its edges, and catches brighter when they feel strongly; that is an involuntary tell, not speech, and they talk like anyone. The face is a rooted face, recognisably descended from the Starwrights: grown lens layers over the eyes, crystal following the bone at brow and cheek, heavier in the old than the young. Long tapering fingers, layered crystal and mirror-thread fabrics, and small light-cores worn as jewellery.

Ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, and a stored light glowing deep inside.

Palette: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, stored light.

Effects colours: white-core Prism lance beams with rainbow fringe, pale green-white stored light, silver-white shield flicker, clear ringing tones on hits. Sound motif: a single clear tone, like struck glass. No faction sings, and this one does not either.

Three visual directions for the people. In all three, the being is organic and evolved to shape glass, never made of glass and never a grey alien: pale living flesh, a rooted face on the young-to-old infusion spectrum, and long glass-shaping fingers. The directions differ in how the glass sits in the body.

- Direction A, Threaded (glass as thread). Fine crystal threads are woven through the flesh like fibre optics under skin. The tall, slender body is mostly soft and pale, half-translucent at the hands and throat, and hair-thin filaments run under the surface of the arms, neck, and torso, carrying light in slow pulses that gather and split into faint rainbow at the joints. The face is a rooted face with a grown lens layer over the eyes and small crystal facets following the bone at brow and crown, denser in the old. When the being feels strongly, light gathers in the threads against its will; it is an emotion tell, not speech. Clothing is layered mirror-thread fabric in silver grey, worn loose so the lit threads show at the seams. Lighting: soft stored light from behind, a dark workshop of lenses in the background.
- Direction B, Inlaid (glass as inlay). Larger grown crystal pieces are set into the flesh like jewellery that was never removed. Broad crystal plates sit inlaid across the shoulders, the sternum, and the backs of the hands, each plate flush with pale skin grown around its edges over centuries, and the faceted head carries three large flat crystal inlays. Light pools in the plates and refracts outward in clean rainbow fans when the being turns. The body is otherwise plainly flesh: sinew, soft skin, visible veins near the inlays. Clothing is minimal at the shoulders so the inlays show, with layered crystal fabric below: the mirrors are clothing, the flesh is the body. Lighting: hard clear light from one side, the Ardent dark behind.
- Direction C, Bloomed (glass as growth). Crystal grows outward from the body at set points, in small branching clusters like frost flowers on a window. Clusters bloom at the joints, along the spine, and in a partial crown around the head, which stays organic and plainly faced beneath them. Between the clusters the flesh is bare and pale, so the body reads as a person with crystal growing on them, not a crystal figure. In very old Glasswrights the growth is larger, like shards along the shoulders. Light catches in the clusters first and runs down into the skin as colour when the being feels strongly; it is an emotion tell, not speech. The long fingers carry tiny crystal tips grown from the nails. Clothing is simple dark cloth, so the blooms carry all the light in the frame.

## Admirals

### Tamsa of the Clear Aperture

Who they are: a Lens-keeper's granddaughter and the fleet's youngest admiral, which for Glasswrights means she is only ninety. She was raised inside the Ringing Field salvage crews and learned command among wrecks that still ring when the light hits them.

Play style: the wing admiral. Starling wings, fast carriers, and strikes timed to the turn of the mirrors.

Command: Starling flight. Your wings launch and strike in one motion this round, moving as a single flock and choosing their targets after they move.

Skill tree (idiom: what the light showed us; picks are optical lessons recorded by the fleet's own hulls):

- Branch A, Starling Craft: T1 Flock Turns: your wings may change facing freely during their strike move. T2 Mirror Escort: a wing escorted by a sliver-class ship strikes before any interception resolves. T3 One Flock: once per battle, all your wings in flight may re-form on one target, wherever they are on the board.
- Branch B, Aperture Work: T1 Wide Aperture: your carriers may launch wings at a target one hex further away than normal. T2 Stored Light Launch: a wing launched this round carries stored light: its first strike marks the target for your beams (new: marked targets take critical hits from your needle beams and Prism lances on a roll of 8 or better on a ten-sided die, instead of the normal critical rule, for this battle). T3 The Clear Aperture: once per battle, every wing you control strikes twice, the second strike at half damage, rounding down.
- Branch C, Refraction Tables (numerics only): T1 Wings deal 20 percent more damage to ships whose shields are down. T2 Prism lances deal 15 percent more damage. T3 The flagship deals 25 percent more damage when it fires at a target in its side arc.

Spotlight beat: Tamsa takes her fleet through the Ringing Field before the sector 2 battle and every hull rings the wrecks as they pass, a sound like one clear tone held too long. She does not apologise for the lost, which the fleet notices, because Glasswrights always apologise.

### Saro of Long Focus

Who they are: the oldest admiral in the fleet after the Lens-keeper herself, a siege mind who spent sixty years on the Astra watch, learning the caged core's moods through its cage. He speaks the least of any admiral and misses the least.

Play style: the lance admiral. Extreme range, first strikes, and kills made before the enemy closes.

Command: Burning glass. Focus the fleet's stored light: one target takes a heavy lance strike from every ship with a lance in range, resolved as a single volley.

Skill tree (idiom: focus, held):

- Branch A, Long Focus: T1 Patience: a ship that did not move last round fires its lance at one extra hex of range this round. T2 The Held Breath: lances fired at targets beyond six hexes ignore wreck and terrain cover. T3 Saro's Stillness: once per battle, your whole fleet may hold position and fire lances before the first enemy activation of the round.
- Branch B, Astra Watch: T1 Cage Readings: at the start of each battle, learn one strength of the enemy flagship, shown as intel. This is observation, not an adaptation. T2 Throttled Light: once per battle, one lance ship may fire its lance twice, the second shot at half damage, rounding down (new: this is a use of Astra's throttled output, capped at once per battle for the whole fleet). T3 Keeper's Discipline: if Astra's protocols are in the fleet's build, their effects resolve at the start of the round instead of when triggered.
- Branch C, Burning Tables (numerics only): T1 Needle beams deal 20 percent more damage. T2 Lance damage is 20 percent higher against targets with full shields. T3 The flagship's Prism lance deals 30 percent more damage at range seven or more.

Spotlight beat: Saro is given the order he has waited sixty years for, permission to unthrottle Astra for one shot in the sector 3 battle, and he refuses it in front of the fleet, quietly, because the shot is not needed. The fleet's trust in the cage doubles.

### Vell of the Third Mirror

Who they are: a duellist and a mirror-smith, who keeps the flagship's great mirror polished by hand, a job far below her rank that she will not give up. Of the four admirals she is the one the Hullbreakers name with respect, because she has killed three of their captains in single fights and apologised to each pack afterward.

Play style: the duellist. Scatter, evade, and win the one-on-one before the lines meet.

Command: Scatter the light. Your ships scatter: each may make a short jump or fast move at once, and enemy fire this round is resolved against where your ships were, not where they are.

Skill tree (idiom: reflections, studied):

- Branch A, Mirror Work: T1 Vane Craft: the extra defence your crystal vanes give after moving also applies after short jumps (new: where the shipped rule grants it only after normal movement, this pick extends it to jumps; it does not raise the amount). T2 False Glints: once per battle, place a decoy image of one ship within two hexes of it (new: the decoy has no weapons, is removed when first fired on, and enemy ships must target it if it is closer than the real ship). T3 The Third Mirror: once per battle, when an enemy flagship fires on your flagship, redirect that volley to the nearest enemy ship in range.
- Branch B, Duel Craft: T1 Single Light: when your flagship fights one enemy with no other ships within three hexes of either, it may fire first in each exchange. T2 Apology and Answer: after your flagship destroys a ship, it may move two hexes at once. T3 Vell's Courtesy: once per battle, your flagship may refuse a duel or challenge effect entirely (new: refusal means the effect does not occur and is not spent for the enemy).
- Branch C, Glint Tables (numerics only): T1 Shard cannon deal 20 percent more damage. T2 Your ships gain 15 percent more movement, in movement points, rounding up. T3 After using Scatter the light, your ships deal 20 percent more damage on their next volley.

Spotlight beat: Vell fights the Hullbreaker captain who killed her mirror-smith predecessor, wins, and then spends the whole return jump building the dead captain's pack a mirror of black glass, because their dead deserve to be seen clearly too. She sends it to Rattlehold with an apology.

### Brin of the Fourth Lens

Who they are: the fleet's warden of shields, a defensive engineer who believes the Glasswrights' survival is an optical problem: be bright, be elsewhere, be unbroken. Brin apologises to enemy fleets before battles, in writing, for what the light is about to do.

Play style: the ward admiral. Shields layered with mirror veils, and a fleet that refuses to be hit cleanly.

Command: Prism ward. Raise a ward of bent light around the fleet: incoming fire this round is scattered, and shields take the damage at reduced strength cost.

Skill tree (idiom: what the lens kept):

- Branch A, Ward Craft: T1 Layered Veil: mirror veil may be raised on a ship that has already moved this round. T2 Shared Ward: when a ship's shields fall, an adjacent friendly ship with shields up may lend it cover until the end of the round (new: cover means the fallen ship counts as shielded at half the lender's remaining shield strength, rounding down, for targeting and damage). T3 The Fourth Lens: once per battle, Prism ward also restores shields to half on every ship that had lost them, half of maximum shield strength, rounding down.
- Branch B, Stored Light: T1 Deep Storage: ships that take no damage for a full round begin the next round with their stored light full, and their first lance or beam shot that round cannot miss (new: cannot miss means the hit roll is skipped; critical rolls still apply). T2 Light Returned: when an enemy volley is fully absorbed by your shields, your flagship regains shield strength equal to one quarter of the absorbed amount, rounding down. T3 Brin's Patience: once per battle, hold the whole fleet's fire for one round; next round, every ship fires with its critical chance doubled, the critical range on the die doubled for that round only.
- Branch C, Ward Tables (numerics only): T1 Mirror veil reduces incoming damage by a further 15 percent while it is up. T2 Your shields have 15 percent more strength. T3 Ships with full shields deal 20 percent more damage.

Spotlight beat: Brin holds a ward over a crippled Crusade ship that wandered into the battle, an enemy of her siblings' enemies and no friend of hers, and keeps it alive until it can jump out. When the Crusade captain asks why, Brin apologises for taking so long to decide.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: a Chorus Elder Throat. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: it does not duel and it does not raid. It grows. It arrives on the Nemesis clock: first meeting in sector 2, final meeting in sector 3. After every meeting, win or lose, it adapts, and it grows tougher from every ship you lose anywhere in the campaign, because the swarm eats the dead of every battle it hears about. It never flees a fight it can feed in; it withdraws only when the board is empty of anything edible, and it returns with the growths your last fleet taught it. Its adaptations never directly counter a revealed weakness: it becomes more itself, not your opposite. A lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses, shown on the debrief.

Voice: two throats in short lines, Hunger wanting and Memory remembering, always "we", always taste, heat, smell, and teeth. It does not apologise. Apology belongs to its makers' kin, not to the weapon.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "We taste the light on you. Glass and grief. The makers' kin. We remember the makers. We ate them."
- Meeting: "Memory knows your shape. Hunger does not care. Both of us came."
- Mid-battle: "Your shields are pretty. Acid does not care about pretty. Acid eats."
- Mid-battle: "That one is cooling. Eat it before it cools."
- Victory: "We keep the taste. We keep the smell. The rest is flesh now."
- Defeat: "We are eaten too. It is fair. It is the only fair thing."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction (settled): a parallel procession, not a serial loop. Glasswright hulls are optical memory: the glass records the light of a battle at full fidelity, a replayable record, not telemetry. When a fleet is lost, its shard-field drifts, and slow uncrewed gatherer hulls recover the shards over years. The forge reads the replay, finds the killing flaw, and recasts the next fleet with that flaw corrected. Hundreds of staggered expeditions run at once across deep time, so a player's next run is simply the next casting in the queue, already holding everything recovered by its launch.

Admiral leveling fits the idiom directly: a tree is what the forge read out of that admiral's recorded light, cast into the next fleet as a corrected pattern. Unlocks over runs: tree tiers persist through the forge; Nemesis intel persists as recovered replay, with a lost meeting still teaching one weakness; new lens, vane, and refit patterns recovered from shard-fields, including the Waveguide spine, Interferometer lens, and Shard hopper patterns the legs proposed; and deeper study of Astra's cage opens its precision protocols earlier. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the fleet reaches the Hollow Sun and Astra's cage hums in its hold. Every mirror turns toward the siphon arrays, the light catches, and the arrays crack. The Glasswrights do not cheer. They watch the light scatter through the wreckage and ring once, clear, like struck glass. Then they turn Astra's cage inward, and the last core shuts down. The Unfinished drifts in the new dark, lit only by its own stored light, and the procession ends.
