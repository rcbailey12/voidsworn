# Cross-faction check

## Do the six feel distinct?

### Look

Yes, with one pair to watch. No two packages share a palette: the Crusade is black iron, frost white, and bright ember; the Hegemony is pale grey, steel blue, and implant blue; the Hullbreakers are soot, rust, and red running lights; the Chorus is dark olive, ochre, and bile yellow-green; the Glasswrights are clear crystal, silver, and rainbow edge; Eclipse is dark violet-black and white-gold.

Riskiest pair: the Crusade and the Hegemony. Both are human navies with high collars, and two legs initially let them drift toward each other in surface and tone. The assembly separates them by material and light: the Crusade is matte black iron with chalk, scars, brands, and manual gun decks; the Hegemony is clean pale wedges with data moving in the eyes, gloves, and drone escorts. If playtest art ever blurs them, the fix is surface first: chalk and frost against polish and pulse.

Second pair: the Glasswrights and Eclipse. Both are Starwright descendants with faceted, light-carrying silhouettes. They are separated by substance: Glasswrights are living flesh with glass grown into it, warm stored light, and rooted faces; Eclipse is layered machine plate with a trapped-light chest core, a visor with no face, and cold white repair swarms. The Eclipse package carries an explicit guard: no green glow anywhere near the faction.

Third pair: the Hullbreakers and the Chorus. Both are alien and aggressive. They are separated by construction: Hullbreakers are built ships and plated bodies with trophies bolted on; the Chorus is grown flesh with sacs, pores, and no trophies, no panels, and no faces at all.

### Play

Yes. The six play loops do not collapse into each other:

- Crusade: hold a line, advance to power broadsides, brace, and pay in names.
- Hegemony: solve a formation geometry problem around the flagship, strip shields with ion, and reallocate on schedule.
- Hullbreakers: close from flanks and behind, ram, board, strip, and leave heavier.
- Chorus: spend cheap hulls, regrow, swarm, and eat shields and armour with acid.
- Glasswrights: control range, strike first with precision, and never be caught with shields down, because there is no brace.
- Eclipse: pay more per hull, endure in armour, fold through obstacles, and plan around one death the Archive refunds.

Riskiest pair: the Crusade, the Hegemony, and Eclipse can all read as "the tough fleet that outlasts you" at a glance. The packages separate what the toughness is made of: brace and line, formation and deflectors, armour and restore. The second risk is the Hullbreakers and the Glasswrights, both fast. They are split by direction: Glasswrights strike from range and leave; Hullbreakers close, board, and stay. The Chorus cannot blur with anyone: it is the only faction whose plan is numbers and regrowth, and, with Eclipse, one of only two with no shields, for opposite reasons (grown flesh against heavy armour).

### Voice

Yes, and this is the strongest separation in the set. The Crusade is the only faction allowed call and response. The Hegemony is the only faction allowed to sound like a form being filled in. The Hullbreakers count kills and talk in trails and sound. The Chorus wants, in short "we" lines about taste, heat, smell, and teeth. The Glasswrights apologise and talk about light and what was built. Eclipse counts years and bodies and stops halfway through doubts.

Riskiest pair: the Glasswrights and Eclipse, siblings who both grieve. The rule the assembly held is that the Glasswrights mourn and Eclipse regret: same loss, different grammar. If a line could be swapped between them without noticing, it was rewritten or cut.

## Motif ownership audit (bible 1.3)

The binding ownership is: song, choir, hymn, and chant retired for everyone; light, glass, reflection, and grief to the Glasswrights; short hungry sensory lines to the Chorus; memory, the Archive, and the lost sun to Eclipse, with the one bible exception that the Chorus's second throat is called Memory, in story and dialogue only; data, projections, the Registry, and "acceptable" to the Hegemony; oath, chalk names, dawn and dusk, burning, and cold to the Crusade; violence, strength, trophies, scrap, fear, and the pack to the Hullbreakers.

Residual slips found in the legs, and how the assembly handled them:

1. Crusade names treated as data. One leg's Crusade progression text called the chalk names data written in grief. That hands the Hegemony's motif and Eclipse's framing to the faction whose whole point is that names are not data. Cut. The assembled Crusade progression keeps names as chalk, debt, and obligation only.
2. Bookkeeping language in Crusade material, and too much of it in Hegemony material. The older content packs put ledger and account language in the Crusade's voice, and one assembled tree draft carried a bookkeeping skill name for a Hegemony admiral. The assembly removed all such language from Crusade material. In Hegemony material it was lightened rather than kept: the skill was renamed to Most Costly Target, and Hegemony bookkeeping now appears only as Registry files, projections, and stated confidence, never as a voice the Crusade shares.
3. Chorus lines in apology grammar. One leg gave its Chorus Nemesis lines apologies ("We are sorry") in Glasswright defeats and meetings. Apology belongs to the Glasswrights. The assembled Chorus Nemesis lines carry no apology. The Glasswrights package keeps one deliberate exception on the Eclipse side: Seveth uses the word sorry once, flagged in its own spotlight as a borrowed word, which is a character beat and not a motif transfer.
4. Chorus material in first person singular and in human persona registers. The older packs rolled generic cunning, cruel, and patient personas for the Chorus. All assembled Chorus material, including every sample line and both throats, is collective "we" throughout. The two throats argue, but neither speaks as "I".
5. Song framing. No assembled package sings, hymns, or chants. The Chorus name is kept for the bible's own reason: many consumed voices speaking as one "we". The Glasswright sound motif is a single struck tone, and the Crusade sound motif is a horn and spoken oaths.
6. Eclipse memory leaking outward. One leg's Hegemony progression let the Registry remember in Eclipse's grammar. The assembly rewrote it: the Registry logs, projects, and revises. It does not remember, and it does not grieve.
7. Glasswright grief leaking into Eclipse lines. Two legs let Eclipse admirals mourn in light language. The assembly keeps Eclipse on memory, the Archive, frames, and the lost sun, and keeps light and grief words for the Glasswrights, with the bible's shared-grief exception handled by grammar: Eclipse regret, Glasswrights mourn.

No residual slip from this list survives in the assembled files. Where a leg's line was otherwise strong, the motif word was changed and the line kept; where the line was the slip, the line was cut.

## Clashes with the owner's rulings found in the legs

In the three complete legs:

1. Eclipse rival withdrawal. One leg gave the Crusade's Eclipse rival a withdrawal at half hull. The bible anchor is that an Eclipse Nemesis is patient, never retreats, and returns once after death in sector 3 only. The assembly corrected the behaviour in both packages where an Eclipse rival appears (Crusade and Chorus) and kept the leg's lines, which were compliant.
2. Hullbreaker rival that never flees. One leg made the Crusade's Hullbreaker Warlord stand until broken. The bible anchor is that the Hullbreaker Nemesis raids credits, flees a losing fight, returns stronger, and a stolen ship returns when it dies. That pairing was not the drafted assignment, and where Hullbreaker behaviour is drafted as a rival (the Hegemony package) it follows the anchor exactly.
3. Chorus rival growth misassigned. One leg gave the Chorus's Glasswright rival growth from the player's losses. Growing tougher from every ship the player loses anywhere belongs to a Chorus rival. The assembly placed that behaviour in the Glasswrights package, where the Chorus is the Nemesis, and gave the Chorus package an Eclipse rival with the Eclipse behaviour set.
4. Settled rulings re-asked as open. One leg asked whether Eclipse should gain a battleship-class hull instead of a carrier, which R63 already settles: no carrier, battleship-class hull. That question was dropped, and the Eclipse package states the ruling. The same leg asked whether the Chorus boss hull, The Ninefold Maw, should be playable or an NPC boss. The shipped data lists it as the Chorus boss hull, so the assembly does not present it as an open fork.
5. Archive restore scope. Several leg skills extended the Archive restore toward flagships or toward a full reset. The shipped rule is that the restore is partial and excludes flagships. Every assembled Eclipse skill states the exclusion where it touches the restore.
6. Rams and hit rolls, sync and movement, repair and suspension. Leg skill drafts occasionally described effects that imply rams roll to hit, that formation sync is cancelled by moving, or that repair suspends a ship's actions. None of those mechanics exist in the shipped rules. The assembly removed the implications: rams are described by damage, run-up, and recoil only; Hegemony sync is described as a formation state around the flagship; repair effects never suspend firing unless the pick says so as a labelled new mechanic with amounts.
7. Shield effects for Eclipse. One leg's Eclipse trees used shield language for Seveth's wall. The assembly rewrote Sunshade wall and its whole tree as plate, shadow, and damage reduction, and states the baseline in the package: Eclipse has no shields.
8. Towing as a universal verb. Leg drafts used towing and a salvage step name loosely across factions. Towing and the strip step are Hullbreaker-only in the shipped campaign. The assembly confines them to the Hullbreaker package and uses strip and ransom language everywhere else.
9. Unlabelled new mechanics and unexplained shorthand. Leg skills with new effects were kept only where the assembly could label them new and state amounts, caps, and durations in plain words. Shorthand such as aim, evasion, crit, and hex-face was written out as chance, unit, or action wherever it survived.

In the Argon leg (partial, screened out, full record in the legs folder):

1. It replaced the shipped Eclipse admirals Ostrel of the Long Copy and Seveth, Twice-Restored with two invented admirals, and invented a House and Conclave system and flagships to go with them.
2. It gave shieldless Eclipse shield regeneration, cloak states, and shroud mechanics.
3. It wrote real-time seconds and metre ranges into a turn-based hex game.
4. Its Nemesis was an invented Solar Mandate plus an internal Conclave crisis, not a faction from the pack.
5. Its progression was an invented three-phase system and its open questions asked for balance clarifications instead of owner forks.
6. Its text contained em dashes as generated and one ending line truncated mid-word.
7. Its extraction failed structurally: factions 1 to 5 and the first Eclipse reply could not be recovered verbatim from the chat view. Nothing from this leg was used in the assembly.

## The three biggest risks, ranked

1. Nemesis assignment is still an owner decision wearing a draft's clothes. The bible anchors behaviour by the rival's faction, but it does not settle which faction each player faction faces, and the three complete legs produced three different round-robins. The chair drafted the best-supported set and put every alternative in the open questions, but if the owner prefers a fixed rotation, a mirror set, or story-driven pairings, six Nemesis sections change at once. This should be ruled first, before any Nemesis art or lines are produced.
2. The Glasswright look may still not land. The ruling that sent this job out says the current Glasswright look is not landing, and R66 narrows the fix: organic, glass grown or woven into living tissue, faceless, nothing like grey aliens. The three assembled directions are genuinely different in how the glass sits in the body, but all three are still text descriptions of a hard target. Until the owner picks a direction and sees it painted, every Glasswright portrait, and the Chorus's makers' kinship with them, stays provisional. Owner correction (Nov 1, 2026, after drafting): the owner states the "faceless" premise itself was never his ruling and that colour-pulse speech overshoots his intent; see the correction note at the head of the_glasswrights.md. Ruled the same evening (Nov 1, 2026): faces on a young-to-old infusion spectrum, normal speech, colour as an involuntary emotion tell only; the chair patched the package's people material to match. What remains of this risk is the painting itself.
3. Skill tree outlines are one validation pass away from the older packs' failure mode. The assembled trees obey the six rules on paper: decision-changing picks, numerics only on the third branch at a 15 percent floor or better, faction learning idioms, no duplication of the twelve doctrine effects, nothing that rewards losing, and no tier 3 that restates a Command at lower power. But the lint that found 179 affected lines in the older packs was run against shipped code, line by line. These outlines have not had that pass. The labelled new mechanics, about one per admiral, are the exposure: each one needs a code check before it is treated as buildable, and the tree set should go through the same lint before the progression phase builds from it.
