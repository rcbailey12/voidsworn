# Eclipse

Note on the name: Eclipse takes no "the". It is a name the way Hegemony is a name.

> Owner ruling (Nov 1, 2026): no faces stands for Eclipse, per the owner's earlier ruling. The trapped-light chest core described below is one machine pattern, not a universal: Eclipse machine forms and approaches vary by admiral, and portraits and designs should vary them rather than forcing every Eclipse being through the chest core.

## Identity

Eclipse is the Starwrights who uploaded: billions of separate minds in machine bodies, each backed up in one shared Archive, around the black hole they made of their own sun. They have lived in that dark for a thousand years and are no longer sure the Upload was worth it, so they are building a new sun from stellar energy siphoned from neighbouring stars, whatever it costs those stars. They know what the Siphoning does to worlds like Vesper. They do it anyway, because the alternative is forever in the dark.

Playing Eclipse is about permanence and price. Your ships are stronger and more expensive, the ruling's own words, with heavy armour and no shields at all, and the first ship you lose in each battle is rebuilt by repair swarms from its crew's Archive backup, so you plan around one death that does not count. There is no carrier; a battleship-class hull takes that place in the line, as ruled. Your risk is concentration: few hulls, each one costly, and an enemy who kills the same ship twice in one battle is spending your permanence. Your best moment is the fold, when a ship skips through the rock and the wrecks as if they were not there and the Corona arc lands its one heavy blow, and the enemy realises your fleet does not need to hurry.

## Lore

Origin: the Ardent system, dark for a thousand years. The Archive holds a backup of every uploaded mind. Each mind is its own; the Archive holds copies, not a merged self. Eclipse hold K-1 Kharon, the Unbent, which never left the dead system, and they fight the way it taught them: enduring. Their run word is the Homecoming.

War aim: all six cores, their keys, to steer the new sun when it lights, and every other fleet kept away from the siphon arrays until it does.

How they see the others:

- Sunward Crusade: "Their star is small. Ours was Ardent."
- Hegemony Navy: "Their Registry thinks. It does not yet regret."
- Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Scavengers in our ruins."
- The Chorus: "The Fleshwrights' weapon. It wears their voices, and it still wants us."
- The Glasswrights: "Our siblings. They chose to stay small and warm. We chose forever. Neither of us kept the sun."

## Look

Beings: tall machine bodies of layered dark violet-black plates, a narrow visor of white-gold light, and in each chest a small core of trapped light, all that is left of their sun. They are sentient machines, not humans in armour, and they have no faces: no eyes, mouth, or features on the visor plate. Bone is a metaphor in their text only, never a look. The greatest minds wear a monumental broken ring of plates around a glowing core, its pieces drifting apart and refitted by tiny repair drones. Portraits vary in background, pose, and framing, as ruled for every faction.

Ships: hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind them, eclipse rings around the engines, and no flaming engine bells. Classes: mote, flare, zenith, a battleship-class hull, and the flagship The Unsetting. No carrier.

Palette: dark violet-black plates, white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow.

Effects colours: white-gold corona light, violet-white Null fire, cold white repair swarms. No green glow anywhere near this faction: green belongs to the Chorus's bile light and to nobody's Eclipse plate.

## Admirals

### Nadir Aurex, Ninth Frame

Who they are: the oldest continuously restored admiral in Eclipse, on his ninth body, able to describe the first eight funerals, which were also his. He commands The Unsetting, speaks for the Homecoming in council, and has begun, in private, to leave gaps in his backups.

Play style: the enduring flagship. Armour, restore, and a fleet built to outlast the battle.

Command: Release the repair swarms. Repair swarms rebuild the next friendly ship destroyed this round as if it were the battle's first loss, and every ship regains some hull at once. The Archive restore never applies to the flagship, as shipped.

Skill tree (idiom: what the Archive kept; picks are memories restored with commentary):

- Branch A, Archive Craft: T1 Deeper Backups: the first-loss rebuild also applies to the second ship lost in battles in sector 3 (new: sector 3 only, and never to the flagship, matching the shipped rule). T2 Warm Restoration: a rebuilt ship returns with its crew's last orders already set, so it may act in the round it returns. T3 The Ninth Frame's Method: once per battle, when the Archive restore is used, the restored ship returns at half hull rather than the standard restored amount, and keeps any fitted refits it had (new: stated as a trade, more of the ship kept, less of it whole).
- Branch B, Fold Craft: T1 Longer Skips: fold movement may pass through one additional blocked hex per move (new: blocked means terrain or wrecks, not ships). T2 Out of the Dark: a ship that ends its move after folding may fire before any reaction fire resolves. T3 Aurex's Shortcut: once per battle, the flagship folds directly to any hex within six, ignoring everything between.
- Branch C, Endurance Tables (numerics only): T1 Your armour counts as 20 percent stronger against non-Null weapons. T2 The Corona arc deals 20 percent more damage on the turns it fires. T3 The flagship takes 20 percent less damage from critical hits (new: damage reduction only; critical effects still apply).

Spotlight beat: Aurex, before the sector 3 battle, deletes one memory from his own backup, a small one, a morning on Ardent a thousand years old, and tells no one what it was. The Archive logs the deletion as the first thing he has ever chosen to forget.

### Umbra Vessane, Third-Housed

Who they are: a siege admiral restored three times, each time into a taller frame, who commands the siphon array screens and has watched Vesper's star dim on instruments for ninety years. She keeps a private count of the Reach's remaining light and has never once shared it.

Play style: the array warden. Hold the siphon ground, Null fire at range, and make the enemy cross a killing field.

Command: Noon volley. Every ship fires its Null weapons at once in a single volley: one heavy, coordinated strike, remembering noon.

Skill tree (idiom: counts kept):

- Branch A, Array Craft: T1 Screen of Shades: your sunshade ships count as cover for friendly ships behind them against long-range fire. Cover here is hull and plate, never a shield: Eclipse has no shields. T2 Relay Discipline: while your fleet holds the same sector of the board for a full round, your Null weapons gain one hex of range next round. T3 Vessane's Perimeter: once per battle, declare the flagship's hex and all adjacent hexes as array ground: enemy ships entering it take damage from the corona defences (new: damage equal to one Corona arc hit at half strength, rounding down, once per ship per battle).
- Branch B, Null Craft: T1 Deeper Null: Null weapons ignore a further quarter of armour, on top of the half they already ignore (new: total ignored is five eighths, stated plainly so the stacking is not hidden). T2 Cold Fire: a target struck by Null fire cannot benefit from repair or regrowth until the end of the next round. T3 Third-Housed Patience: once per battle, hold a volley: ships that do not fire this round fire at double damage next round (new: double damage on that volley only; the Corona arc is excluded, because it fires every other turn by rule).
- Branch C, Siege Tables (numerics only): T1 Null weapons deal 20 percent more damage. T2 Your ships deal 15 percent more damage to targets at range six or more. T3 The flagship deals 25 percent more damage against the ship class it has destroyed most often this campaign (new: counted across the campaign; ties go to the most recent).

Spotlight beat: Vessane is ordered to increase the siphon draw on Vesper itself during the sector 2 battle, and she complies a full minute late, every time, all battle, a delay too regular to be error and too small to be charged. Her count of the Reach's light is the reason, and the player is shown the count.

### Ostrel of the Long Copy

Who they are: a scout mind copied so often for deep missions that the copies began being restored from copies, and Ostrel is the one who came back with the fold routes nobody else remembers taking. Even other Eclipse minds find Ostrel quiet.

Play style: the fold admiral. Movement through everything, strikes from inside the enemy's formation, and no respect for terrain.

Command: Out of the shadow. The whole fleet folds at once, each ship skipping a short distance through any obstacle, and ships that land beside an enemy may fire immediately.

Skill tree (idiom: copies of copies):

- Branch A, Fold Mastery: T1 Charted Dark: fold range increases by one hex. T2 Through the Wreck: ships may end a fold inside a wreck hex, treating it as open for one round. T3 The Long Copy's Route: once per battle, one ship folds to any hex on the board (new: any hex, including beside an enemy; it may not land on another ship).
- Branch B, Arrival Craft: T1 First Words: a ship that fires in the same activation it folded counts the target's armour as one step lower. T2 Inside the Formation: your ships take no reaction fire on the round they arrive by fold. T3 Ostrel's Habit: once per battle, after folding, the flagship may fold again at the end of the round, alone.
- Branch C, Shadow Tables (numerics only): T1 Your ships deal 20 percent more damage on the first volley they fire in each battle. T2 Null weapons deal 20 percent more damage. T3 The flagship deals 30 percent more damage when it fires after folding in the same activation.

Spotlight beat: Ostrel folds the flagship somewhere the charts say is inside the Hollow Sun's disc and comes back with a recording of Ardent as it was, pulled from a Lattice relay that has been looping it for a thousand years. Ostrel watches it once, then files it in the Archive where the others will find it later, or not.

### Seveth, Twice-Restored

Who they are: restored twice, both times after deaths Seveth describes as educational, and now the fleet's wall of shadow: the admiral sent when the order is to bring everyone home, in whatever frame remains. Seveth keeps the fleet's restore lists personally and reads them before battles, all of them, every time.

Play style: the restorer. Sunshade walls of layered plate, repair drones, and a fleet that treats death as a logistics problem.

Command: Sunshade wall. The fleet raises its sunshade plates as one wall: heavy damage reduction this round, strongest on the ships nearest the flagship. This is plate and shadow, not shields. Eclipse has no shields, and nothing in this tree gives it any.

Skill tree (idiom: the restore list, read and kept):

- Branch A, Restore Craft: T1 Gentle Swarms: repair drones may also repair one adjacent friendly ship when they repair their own. T2 Second Ignition Discipline: Second Ignition, where fitted, may be assigned after seeing which ship is destroyed, instead of before (new: assignment timing only; its effect and once-only limit do not change). T3 Twice-Restored's Promise: once per battle, a rebuilt ship returns with full movement for its next activation.
- Branch B, Shade Craft: T1 Layered Fans: sunshade plating also reduces lance and beam damage, not only direct fire. T2 Corona Edging: when Sunshade wall is used, enemy ships firing into the wall take return damage from the corona edging (new: return damage equal to 10 percent of the damage they dealt, rounding down, minimum zero). T3 Seveth's Screen: once per battle, extend the wall to cover one ship outside its normal area, chosen when the wall is raised.
- Branch C, Keeping Tables (numerics only): T1 Repair effects restore 20 percent more hull. T2 Ships under the Sunshade wall take a further 15 percent less damage. T3 The flagship restores 15 percent of its hull whenever another friendly ship is rebuilt by any effect.

Spotlight beat: Seveth restores a mind that went too long without a backup and should have gone dark, from a partial copy recovered off a wreck, and then sits with the restored mind while it learns which parts of itself are missing. It is the only scene in the Eclipse package where anyone uses the word sorry, a word that belongs to their siblings, and Seveth uses it anyway.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: a Sunward Crusade High Admiral who has sworn to burn the siphon arrays. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: it never flees, ever. It arrives on the Nemesis clock: first meeting in sector 2, final confrontation before the Hollow Sun. Once per meeting it challenges your flagship to a duel, moving to engage it directly and ignoring other targets until one flagship falls or the battle ends, exactly as the bible describes the Crusade's Nemesis. Between meetings it chalks the names of every Eclipse ship it has destroyed on its hull and brings one additional Bastion escort each time. Its adaptations tighten its line, never directly countering a revealed weakness. A lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses, shown on the debrief.

Voice: spoken oaths, short imperatives, call and response, dawn and dusk, burning and cold, and chalked names. Names here are chalk and debt, never data.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "Who carries the dawn? We do. Who pays for it? You will."
- Mid-battle: "Your metal gets back up. Good. We will put it down again. And again. The oath does not count rounds."
- Mid-battle: "This is my fourth crusade. I have chalked more names than you have frames."
- Victory: "You fought hard. You did not run. We will chalk your names. The dawn watch will say them."
- Defeat: "You broke this fleet. The next one is already sworn. The oath does not die. We do."
- Final meeting: "Last fight. Your arrays or our sun. The Roll of Vesper is watching."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction: the Archive itself. A lost Homecoming fleet is not lost the way other fleets are: its minds restore from their last backups, and they remember everything up to the backup, including the war they are about to fight again. Eclipse does not learn like a student. It learns like a witness who cannot stop testifying.

Admiral leveling is therefore literal: a tree is what the restored admiral carries out of the restoration chamber, lessons they died holding and now hold permanently, and the iteration epithet is the level counter in fiction: the Ninth Frame has learned eight deaths' worth of lessons. Unlocks over runs: tree tiers persist through restoration; Nemesis intel persists as Archive records, and a lost meeting still teaches one weakness, recorded by the ships that did not come back; new frames, Null variants, and fold patterns developed in the dark Ardent system, including the Sunshade plating, Long-memory sight, and Second Ignition refits the legs proposed; and deeper sync with Kharon opens its armour protocols earlier. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the fleet reaches the Hollow Sun and the six cores come into range. Aurex, or whoever holds the Ninth Frame, shows the last recorded image of Ardent to the fleet. The light fills every chamber. No one speaks. The image fades, the fleet fires, the siphon arrays hold against the Crusade's last line, and then the cores align and the new sun ignites. For the first time in a thousand years the Ardent system is not dark. Aurex says: "We remember noon." Whether it was worth it is the question the package leaves open on purpose, and the open questions below ask how far the owner wants that doubt to go.
