# The Chorus

> Owner ruling (Nov 1, 2026): the four Chorus admirals are unique creatures, not variations on one brood pattern. Each is a different aspect of the Chorus played out as a hero admiral in its own right. Where the material below tells them apart by shared brood features, read that as emphasis only; the finished designs are four distinct beings.

## Identity

The Chorus is not a people: it is a living weapon the Fleshwrights grew to destroy Eclipse, and it worked too well, merged, multiplied, and ate its makers. Inside the one swarm, two throats argue: Hunger, which is the swarm itself, and Memory, which is what is left of the Fleshwrights it consumed. It is coming to the Hollow Sun to eat the other five cores and finish the war it was grown for.

Playing the Chorus is about appetite and numbers. You have no shields at all and your ships are cheap, the opposite end of the owner's economy ruling from Eclipse, so you win by regrowth and mass: flesh knits back together every round, broods spill more bodies than the enemy can kill, and acid eats the shields and armour that stop everyone else. Your risk is a focused enemy who kills faster than flesh can knit and refuses to spread fire. Your best moment is the turn the enemy's perfect volley lands, and the fleet they just crippled stands back up wet and hungry.

## Lore

Origin: the Fleshwrights' war vats in the dark after the collapse. The Fleshwrights were the Glasswrights' splinter, Starwrights who worked in living tissue instead of glass and swore to destroy Eclipse for killing their sun. They powered their vats with H-3 Crucible, "the Hunger Below", a Lattice core they took in the collapse. The swarm grew around Crucible and feeds on it still. Since then it has spread through the dark between stars, living on starlight and eating anything.

War aim: eat the other five cores, reach the sun Memory still remembers, and finish the war against Eclipse.

How it sees the others, in its own words. The Chorus always speaks as "we":

- Sunward Crusade: "Hot. Bitter. Loud."
- Hegemony Navy: "Many. Neat. All taste the same."
- Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Fur. Rust. Hard to chew."
- The Glasswrights: "The makers' kin. Memory knows them. Hunger does not care."
- Eclipse: "Memory knows them. Cold. Wrong. Made to eat them."

## Look

Beings: grown organisms with no face, eyes, or limbs to recognise: glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, and bile, with nothing on the surface that recalls a person. Admirals are commanding growths, not officers: throat masses, bladeback weapons, mending tides, and far-travelling voices.

Ships: grown, not built: ridges, pores, veins, and glowing sacs, no panels or pipes, tendrils and brood sacs, regrowing scar tissue.

Palette: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light.

Effects colours: bile yellow-green glow, acid green-white burns, dark olive spore clouds, wet red boarding tendrils.

## Admirals

### The Matriarch of Nine Throats

Who they are: the swarm's oldest commanding growth, nine throats grown into one mass around a shard of the original vats. When the Matriarch speaks, Hunger and Memory speak in the same breath more often than anywhere else in the swarm, and the broods obey before the sound ends.

Play style: the brood admiral. Spawn volume, swarm strikes, and pressure everywhere at once.

Command: Spawning surge. Broods release extra swarms, and all swarms strike again at plus 50 percent.

Skill tree (idiom: what the swarm has eaten and kept; picks are growths now carried in every brood):

- Branch A, More Mouths: T1 Fast Brooding: brood-sacs release their first swarm one round earlier than normal. T2 Split Broods: when a brood-sac releases a swarm, it may release it as two half-strength swarms in adjacent hexes instead (new: half strength means half the swarm's ships, rounding down, minimum one). T3 The Ninth Throat: once per battle, every brood-sac releases a swarm at once, whether it was ready or not.
- Branch B, Feeding: T1 Tendrils First: boarding tendrils attach before the target fires its next volley, not after. T2 Eat the Screens: acid damage that strips the last of a target's shields also deals half its remaining strength to the hull beneath (new: half of the acid hit's shield damage, rounding down). T3 Swallow the Guns: a ship fully boarded by tendrils cannot fire while your boarding growth lives.
- Branch C, Thick Flesh (numerics only): T1 Drone-spawn cost 20 percent less to field, fielding cost only, in line with the ruling that Chorus stays cheap. T2 Bile spray deals 20 percent more damage. T3 The flagship regrows 15 percent more hull from its normal regrowth each round.

Spotlight beat: Memory speaks through the Matriarch in a dead Fleshwright's voice and gives the swarm a name it used to have, a maker's name for the vats. Hunger eats the name, and the Matriarch never says it again.

### Razormaw

Who they are: a bladeback growth shaped almost entirely into a weapon, the part of the swarm that enjoys the first bite. Razormaw does not command so much as arrive, and the rest of the swarm follows the smell of what it opens.

Play style: the frenzy admiral. Close fast, chew through one ship, and let the panic spread.

Command: Blood frenzy. Every ship charges its nearest target this round and deals more damage the closer it gets, biting and boarding at full strength after moving.

Skill tree (idiom: the taste that taught it):

- Branch A, First Bite: T1 Open It: the first time each of your ships damages a new target each round, that hit also strips one layer of armour (new: one layer means the armour value is reduced by one step for this battle). T2 Worry It: your ships may change targets freely after destroying a target, keeping any frenzy movement they have left. T3 Shake It Apart: once per battle, one ship may make all its attacks against a single already damaged target in one activation.
- Branch B, The Smell of It: T1 Hot Blood: your ships move one hex further when their move ends closer to a damaged enemy than they started. T2 Fear Is a Smell: enemy ships adjacent to two or more of your ships fall back one hex at the start of their activation (new: this is a forced move of one hex directly away, once per ship per battle). T3 The Pack of Us: your ships gain boarding strength from every other friendly ship adjacent to their target.
- Branch C, Sharper Teeth (numerics only): T1 Spine volleys deal 20 percent more damage at close range, within two hexes. T2 Acid sacs deal 20 percent more damage to armour. T3 The flagship's attacks deal 25 percent more damage to targets below half hull.

Spotlight beat: Razormaw reaches a Hegemony picket first and stops, tasting the air, because the crew inside is the first it has ever met that is not afraid. It lets the picket go, and Memory argues about it for a sector.

### The Mending Throng

Who they are: a vast, slow growth of repair flesh that moves through the swarm like a tide through a reef, knitting, feeding, and sealing. Where the Throng passes, ships the enemy counted as kills open their eyespots again.

Play style: the regrowth admiral. Outlast everything, win the long battle, and make damage feel wasted.

Command: Regrowth tide. Every ship regrows 25 percent of its hull, and damaged sections are healed, including destroyed ones.

Skill tree (idiom: what the flesh remembers being):

- Branch A, Knitting: T1 Quick Flesh: regrowth at the start of the round happens before the first volley of the round resolves. T2 Seamless: a section healed by regrowth returns at full function, not reduced function (new: where the game restores sections at reduced function, this pick removes the reduction for this fleet). T3 Tide Timing: once per battle, Regrowth tide may be held and released as a reaction the moment a friendly ship would be destroyed, healing it before the destruction is applied.
- Branch B, Feeding the Hurt: T1 Spare Flesh: a ship at full hull adjacent to a damaged friendly ship passes half its regrowth to that ship, rounding down. T2 Grafts: once per battle, move one intact section's function from a destroyed friendly ship to a damaged one of the same class (new: the donor ship is removed; this converts a wreck, it does not reward its loss, and it cannot take the flagship's sections). T3 The Throng Provides: brood-sacs may release a mending swarm instead of an attack swarm, which attaches to a friendly ship and regrows it for two rounds (new: the mending swarm has the swarm's normal hull and can be shot down).
- Branch C, Deep Flesh (numerics only): T1 Your ships regrow 15 percent more hull each round, as a percentage of the normal regrowth amount. T2 Acid damage your ships deal is 20 percent stronger against shields. T3 The flagship regrows one destroyed section at the start of each round, before any other regrowth.

Spotlight beat: the Throng regrows a captured Hegemony cordon back to wholeness with its crew still inside, and Memory, speaking through the new flesh, asks the crew what the Registry told them the Chorus was. Their answer changes nothing, and the Throng keeps them anyway, as listeners.

### Voice of the Deep

Who they are: the swarm's far-travelling throat, grown in the dark between stars where the only food was old wrecks and older signals. The Voice speaks first in every meeting the swarm has, because it is the part of the swarm that learned how the makers argued.

Play style: the volley admiral. The swarm as artillery, spitting in one breath from every throat at once.

Command: Spitting volley. Every ship fires a free volley now. Note: the shipped Command name in the data is Spitting volley. An older Muse note called it Discordant volley. The data wins, and no rename is proposed.

Skill tree (idiom: what the dark taught it):

- Branch A, One Breath: T1 Aimed Throats: when Spitting volley is used, choose one target per three ships instead of each ship choosing freely (new: rounding down, minimum one chosen target). T2 Second Breath: once per battle, a single ship may fire a second free volley immediately after Spitting volley resolves. T3 The Deep Speaks: Spitting volley may be used while your ships are boarded or grappled, which normally prevents firing.
- Branch B, Spore Weather: T1 Drifting Spores: spore drifts you release last one extra round. T2 Spore Cover: friendly ships inside your spore drifts cannot be targeted at long range. T3 Weather of Us: once per battle, fill a three-hex area with a spore drift at once, anywhere within range of one of your ships.
- Branch C, Far Taste (numerics only): T1 Spine volleys deal 20 percent more damage at long range, five hexes or more. T2 Bile spray range increases by one hex. T3 The flagship deals 25 percent more damage with the first volley it fires each battle.

Spotlight beat: the Voice answers a Glasswright hail in the makers' old greeting, word-perfect, in a Fleshwright voice, then says in its own: "We kept the words. We ate the rest." The Glasswright fleet holds fire for one full round.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: an Eclipse Archivist who commands the siphon defence and remembers the Fleshwrights. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: patient and implacable, and it never retreats, because its dead are a restore queue. It arrives on the Nemesis clock: first meeting in sector 2, final meeting in sector 3. It studies the swarm between meetings and hardens its escort screen, and its adaptations never directly counter a weakness you have already shown it. When it is killed, it returns once after death, in sector 3 only. A lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses, shown on the debrief. Note the mirror: this rival does not grow from your losses. Growing tougher from every ship the player loses anywhere is what a Chorus rival does, and that behaviour is drafted in the Glasswrights package, where the Chorus is the Nemesis.

Voice: cold, exact, quietly doubting, counting years and bodies. Memory and the Archive belong to Eclipse, and this rival uses both, including about the makers.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "The Fleshwrights made you to fight us. They are inside you now. I remember their voices. Do you?"
- Mid-battle: "You eat everything. You ate them too. I remember them. You wear them."
- Mid-battle: "This is my ninth body. Your acid is new to it. I will remember."
- Victory: "The swarm is quiet. I remember when it was loud."
- Defeat: "You will eat the sun. You will eat the memory too."
- Final meeting: "I have studied swarms for nine hundred years. Today I stop one."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction: digestion. What the swarm eats, it keeps. Lost fleets are eaten by the swarm that follows, and their growths, their scars, and the tastes of what killed them pass into the next broods grown around Crucible. Broods grown with the last generation's adaptations are the Chorus form of learning, in flesh idiom, as ruled. Memory is the second channel: the makers' knowledge surfaces a little more each run, and the debrief after a lost run is written as the two throats arguing over what was learned.

Admiral leveling is framed as the swarm growing a better throat: a tree pick is a growth the commanding growth now carries in every battle, grown from something the swarm ate and understood. Unlocks over runs: tree tiers persist because the growths persist; Nemesis intel persists as taste, the swarm remembering how a rival smells; new growth forms developed from consumed enemies, new swarm types, and new acid variants; and deeper feeding on Crucible opens its wing, boarding, and feeding protocols earlier. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the swarm reaches the Hollow Sun and Crucible's signal is deafening. Hunger wants Eclipse. Memory wants the sun it remembers. The swarm hits the siphon arrays, the arrays crack, and the swarm eats the wreckage. For one moment Memory speaks clearly, in story only: "We were the Fleshwrights. We built this to end them. We are ending them." Then Hunger swallows the words. The swarm moves on. More. Always more.
