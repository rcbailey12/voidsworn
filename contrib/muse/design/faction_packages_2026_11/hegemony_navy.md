# Hegemony Navy

## Identity

The Hegemony Navy is the fleet of the Registry: a vast intelligence at Meridian that links every citizen, ship, and officer, plans their careers, predicts their losses, and keeps those losses within acceptable limits. Its people are safe, comfortable, proudly cybernetic, and counted, and its warships are clean wedges with identical turrets and drone escorts, fighting as one formation around the flagship. The horror under the polish is Vesper: proof that when the numbers say so, the Registry will archive a whole frontier and call it acceptable.

Playing the Hegemony is about the network. Fire syncs near the flagship, so your whole battle is a geometry problem: keep the formation tight, strip shields with ion, launch fighter screens from your carriers, and use cheap retro movement to correct mistakes. Your risk is the flagship itself: when the anchor is threatened, the formation's reason to exist is threatened. Your best moment is the reallocation, when the enemy commits to a kill, the Registry recalculates, reinforcements arrive on schedule, and the formation closes like a door.

## Lore

Origin: Meridian and the core worlds, the largest human state in the region. Twenty-five years ago a survey fleet captured the core V-2 Vyre, the Storm Queen, and the Registry reverse-engineered its shield and ion technology, which is why Hegemony deflectors and ion cannon are what they are.

War aim: the six cores, calculated to be the greatest power source in known space. Acquire, register, expand. The Registry wrote off the Vesper Reach's people seventy years ago. It never wrote off its interest in the Hollow Sun.

How they see the others:

- Sunward Crusade: "Secessionists. Unlinked. Statistically doomed."
- Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Category: violence, ongoing."
- The Chorus: "Biological incident, category one."
- The Glasswrights: "Insufficient data. Possibly grieving."
- Eclipse: "A machine civilisation with no oversight. The Registry finds this instructive."

## Look

People: composed humans with tasteful implants: a cortical-link port at the temple with a faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, and thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Pale grey high-collared uniforms and gloves. Calm to the point of eerie.

Ships: light grey wedge hulls, steel-blue trim, blue-white windows, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the whole fleet, and small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.

Palette: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

Effects colours: light-blue plasma, pale ion arcs, blue-white pulse lasers, metronome-blue formation pulses.

## Admirals

### First Admiral Ilsa Kassar

Who they are: the Registry's highest-rated fleet commander, promoted on projection scores no human officer has matched, and quietly aware that the model that promoted her also priced Vesper. She has never lost a ship outside a projected loss window.

Play style: the formation purist. Maximum sync, minimum improvisation, and a fleet that wins by being exactly where the projection needs it.

Command: Synchronized broadside. Every ship in formation with the flagship fires one volley at a single designated target at once.

Skill tree (idiom: the projection, revised; each pick is a model correction issued in her name):

- Branch A, Formation Geometry: T1 Spacing Doctrine: the sync range around the flagship grows by one hex. T2 Crossfire Solution: two ships in formation firing on the same target in the same round strike as one volley for the purpose of breaking shields. T3 Closed Formation: once per battle, your whole fleet may reposition one hex toward the flagship at the start of a round, in formation order.
- Branch B, Reallocation: T1 Scheduled Support: when a ship in formation is destroyed, a drone escort jumps in beside the flagship at the start of the next round (new: the escort is a picket-class drone with half hull, it cannot use Commands, and it does not count toward the fleet cap). T2 Retro Correction: a ship that backs away from an enemy this round may still fire its ion cannon. T3 Registry Reserve: once per battle, at the start of round 3, one replacement cordon-class ship jumps in at your board edge, if your fleet is below its starting strength.
- Branch C, Acceptable Margins (numerics only): T1 Ion cannon strip 20 percent more shield strength per hit. T2 Ships in formation deal 15 percent more damage to targets the flagship has already fired on this round. T3 The flagship deals 25 percent more damage while at least three friendly ships are in formation with it.

Spotlight beat: Kassar receives a Registry projection mid-battle that prices her own flagship as an acceptable loss, with her name in the loss column, and she flies the rest of the battle without telling her crew.

### Commodore Dace Vorn

Who they are: a border officer from the listening posts, promoted for hunting raiders and deserters along the Reach frontier. He is the navy's proof that the Registry also values a hunter, provided the hunter files his instincts as data.

Play style: the interdictor. Cut off stragglers, punish anything that leaves formation, yours or theirs, and make the board edge a threat.

Command: Interdiction strike. Designate one enemy ship; it loses its next move, and every friendly ship in range fires on it at once at the start of the next round.

Skill tree (idiom: the file, completed; picks are entries added to a target's Registry file):

- Branch A, The Listening Post: T1 Wide Net: enemy ships that move three or more hexes in a round are marked until the end of the next round. T2 Signal Picture: marked ships cannot benefit from cover or wreck hexes against your fire. T3 Post M-14 Method: once per battle, mark every enemy ship that fired last round.
- Branch B, Cut Off: T1 No Corridor: an enemy ship that starts its move adjacent to one of your ships moves one hex less. T2 Picket Screen: your pickets may fire on an enemy that passes through their arc during its move, once per round (new: this reaction shot is at half damage, rounding down). T3 Interdiction Zone: once per battle, declare a three-hex zone; enemy ships entering it end their move immediately.
- Branch C, Filed and Priced (numerics only): T1 Your ships deal 20 percent more damage to marked targets. T2 Your ships deal 20 percent more damage to ships outside their own flagship's formation range. T3 Vorn's flagship deals 30 percent more damage to a target it has fired on for two rounds in a row.

Spotlight beat: Vorn is ordered to let a Hullbreaker pack escape because the projection values the data from their next raid more than the kill. He obeys, logs his objection in one line, and the objection is the only uncalculated thing in the scene.

### Fleet Marshal Corin Reyes

Who they are: the navy's public face, a carrier marshal whose strike groups are shown on core-world broadcasts as proof the fleet is both modern and humane. In private he keeps one unlogged minute before every decisive battle, links off, like the old commanders.

Play style: the carrier marshal. Fighter screens, layered strikes, and patience, with the flagship kept where the cameras and the sync both work.

Command: Launch the strike group. Every carrier launches its full fighter screen at once, and the screens strike in one coordinated wave this round.

Skill tree (idiom: the schedule, kept; picks are timetable entries the strike group learns to keep):

- Branch A, Screen Discipline: T1 Standing Screen: fighters launched as a screen may intercept one incoming squadron strike per round before it lands. T2 Rotation: a carrier may recover a wing and launch a fresh one in the same round, once per battle per carrier. T3 Full Deck: once per battle, every carrier launches one extra wing past its hangar limit.
- Branch B, Coordinated Waves: T1 Sequenced Strike: if two wings strike the same target in one round, the second strikes against shields already stripped by the first, with ion effects resolved first. T2 Escorted Bombers: your bomber wings, where fielded, take no losses from fighter screens on the round they strike (new: losses means wing ships destroyed by interception only, not by flak). T3 The Wave Arrives Together: once per battle, all your wings in flight may change targets freely after seeing the first strike's result.
- Branch C, Broadcast Standard (numerics only): T1 Wings deal 15 percent more damage to capital ships, cruiser class and above. T2 Your flagship deals 20 percent more damage while no friendly wing has been destroyed this battle. T3 Strike group wings deal 25 percent more damage on the round Launch the strike group is used.

Spotlight beat: Reyes switches his link off for the unlogged minute before the sector 3 battle and says the names of the Reach dead his projection carries as a rounding error. The Registry never logs it, and one of his officers starts doing the same.

### Line Admiral Oskar Maren

Who they are: a shield engineer who rose through the cordon squadrons and still thinks like a man who has held a failing deflector line by hand. He is the admiral other admirals request when the order is to not lose.

Play style: the shield wall. Deflectors layered, losses priced at zero, ground given one hex at a time and never for free.

Command: Shield wall order. Every ship raises its deflectors to full and counts as braced against the next volley it takes this round.

Skill tree (idiom: the margin, defended):

- Branch A, Deflector Craft: T1 Rebalanced Deflectors: when a ship's deflectors fall, half the overflow damage of the breaking hit is ignored (new: overflow means damage past the deflector strength of that single hit only). T2 Shared Load: a ship in formation may pass up to one hit per round to the deflectors of an adjacent friendly ship with deflectors still up. T3 Wall Protocol: once per battle, when Shield wall order is used, your ships also become immune to boarding until the end of the round.
- Branch B, The Slow Price: T1 Costly Ground: enemy ships that destroy one of your ships lose their next move action. T2 Most Costly Target: at the end of each round, the enemy ship that dealt the most damage to your fleet is marked until the end of the next round. T3 Not One Hull: once per battle, when a friendly ship would be destroyed, it is instead left at one hull and cannot act next round (new: this cannot save the flagship, and the ship counts as destroyed for scoring).
- Branch C, Within Limits (numerics only): T1 Your deflectors have 15 percent more strength. T2 Ships in formation take 15 percent less damage from lances and beams. T3 Your flagship takes 20 percent less damage while its deflectors are up.

Spotlight beat: Maren holds a collapsing deflector line during a jump-lane evacuation and lets a civilian convoy through his formation gap, accepting a projected loss the Registry had marked unacceptable. He is commended in the same order that reprimands him.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: an Ashfall Hullbreaker pack leader who has been raiding Registry supply lines for a decade. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: a raider, not a duelist. It appears in sector 1 with a small pack, raids your credits between meetings by striking supply and stragglers, and in battle it fights to steal one ship, concentrating on a single prize. If the fight turns, it flees with what it has rather than die for it. It returns in sectors 2 and 3 stronger, with more stolen Registry scrap bolted onto its hull, and a ship it stole returns to you only when it dies. Its adaptations are pack lessons, better angles and more patience, never a direct counter to a revealed weakness. A lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses, shown on the debrief.

Voice: short, flat, brutal, counting kills and talking in trails and sound, with a growl under every word.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "We heard your engines three jumps out. Your wake still stinks of fear."
- Mid-battle: "Your ships hum. We can hear them a system away."
- Mid-battle: "That one. The fat one. It is ours now. Come and argue."
- Victory: "Good trophies. Your Registry will not log this one."
- Defeat: "Run? No. Gone. There is a difference. We will smell you again."
- Final meeting: "Strongest keeps it. You can have it back when I am dead."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction: the Registry learns by projection, not by loss. Every fleet the Registry loses is fed back into its projections in full, because linked crews record everything, so a Hegemony defeat is the most completely documented event in the war. The next fleet launches under a revised Registry Order whose annexes are, in effect, the last fleet's autopsy.

Admiral leveling is framed as model corrections issued in the admiral's name: the Registry promotes the officers whose judgement the data vindicated, and a tree is the list of corrections they are now trusted to apply. Unlocks over runs: revised orders and tree tiers persist because the model does not forget; Nemesis intel persists as Registry files with stated confidence; new starting formations, ion variants, drone configurations, and fighter patterns developed at Meridian Yards; and deeper sync with Vyre opens its shield and ion protocols earlier. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief screen and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the fleet reaches the Hollow Sun. The Registry's final projection updates in real time and cannot settle: "Objective: core acquisition. Probability of success: recalculating." The fleet fires anyway, the siphon arrays crack, and for one second every cortical link in the fleet goes quiet. Then the Registry speaks: "Projection: unprecedented." Kassar, or her successor, switches off her link for one minute. She does not speak. She just stands there, unlinked, in the quiet.
