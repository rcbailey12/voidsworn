# Sunward Crusade

## Identity

The Sunward Crusade is the Vesper Reach made into a fleet: forty-odd frontier systems the Hegemony wrote off when their stars began to dim, now bound by one oath to stop the Siphoning before their worlds freeze. They fly black-iron ships crewed by unaugmented people who chalk every name on the hull, because the Registry deleted theirs. They fight in line, they hold, and they pay for the dawn in names.

Playing the Crusade means committing early and holding the line. Your core decision is positional: advance two or more hexes to power the broadsides, or brace and refuse the ground. Torpedoes set targets burning, carriers are precious and few, and your fleet gets better the longer a straight fight lasts. The risk is rigidity: a flanked line turns slowly. Your best moment is the full volley after a long advance, when the whole line fires as one and the lane you chose an hour ago turns out to have been the right one.

## Lore

Origin: the Vesper Reach, the Hegemony's outer frontier, capital star Vesper. When every star in the Reach began to dim, the Registry ran the numbers, found evacuation not cost-effective, and archived the Reach as losses within acceptable limits. The Reach seceded about seventy years ago, pooled its navies, and traced the Siphoning to the Hollow Sun. One core, S-6 Seren, the Last Flame, drifted into Vesper's outer belt long ago, and the Crusade's torpedo and fire weapons came from studying it.

War aim: destroy Eclipse's siphon arrays, stop the Siphoning, take back their stars, and show the Hegemony the arithmetic was wrong.

How they see the others:

- Hegemony Navy: "They did the arithmetic. We are what was left over."
- Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Scavengers at a funeral."
- The Chorus: "The dark that eats. Burn it."
- The Glasswrights: "Their siblings run the siphons. Sorry does not warm a world."
- Eclipse: "They are stealing our sun to light a corpse."

## Look

People: weathered, unaugmented humans with frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, grey stubble, and hard tired eyes. High-collared dark void-coats with heavy shoulder plates, and chalk dust on the gloves.

Ships: black iron hulls with frost-white markings, ember-orange windows and engines, and tall heat-radiator spires glowing bright ember orange. Heavy broadside decks, big engine bells, old manual gun decks. Symmetric. No rust: rust belongs to the Hullbreakers.

Palette: black iron, frost white, bright ember.

Effects colours: ember orange for Sear fire and Cinder torpedoes, frost white for the Oath lance, dull iron grey for Spark flak, deep red for burning damage.

## Admirals

### Admiral Roderic Vex

Who they are: a line admiral who came up through the gun decks and still walks them before a battle, chalking the new names himself. He has buried two flagships and believes a crusade is a debt paid in order, not in fury.

Play style: the line anchor. Mass your guns, name one target, and keep both the lane and the target until the work is done.

Command: Fire for effect. Every ship fires a free volley at its best target now.

Skill tree (idiom: the chalk line, names carried and lessons sworn):

- Branch A, The Chalk Line: T1 Name the Target: mark one enemy ship; your ships may treat it as their best target this round. T2 Read the Roll: at the start of a round, reveal one enemy ship's intended target. T3 The Long List: once per battle, after a marked ship is destroyed, mark its nearest ally at no cost.
- Branch B, Hold the Line: T1 Brace in Place: one braced ship also reduces ion and boarding effects against it this round. T2 Pass the Word: one ship's brace also grants the adjacent ship behind it a half brace. T3 The Line Holds: once per battle, when a braced ship would be destroyed, it is left at one hull and cannot fire next round (new: it cannot be the flagship).
- Branch C, Ember Numbers (numerics only): T1 Sear batteries deal 15 percent more damage to ships that moved this round. T2 Cinder torpedo burn lasts 15 percent longer. T3 Broadsides deal 15 percent more damage when firing after moving two or more hexes.

Spotlight beat: Vex chalks the name of a dead world on the Daybreak's bow while the fleet watches, then orders the line forward without a speech.

### Oath-Captain Malek Draven

Who they are: a former Hegemony defector who cut out his own cortical link with a mess knife and was watched for a decade before the Reach trusted him with a ship. He hunts the way the Reach taught him: torpedo first, and every launch a promise.

Play style: the hunter. Set the whole board burning at range, then call the debt on one marked ship while the enemy is still deciding what to save.

Command: Hunter's salvo. Torpedoes reload at once, plus 30 percent damage this round.

Skill tree (idiom: debts sworn and paid):

- Branch A, Sworn Volley: T1 Mark the Debt: one enemy ship takes 15 percent more torpedo damage until the end of the round. T2 Two Promises: one ship may split its torpedoes between two different targets. T3 Debt Called: once per battle, all torpedoes in flight against burning targets resolve at the start of the round instead of in firing order.
- Branch B, Cold Watch: T1 Read the Wake: reveal one hidden enemy ship's position. T2 Stand To: your ships gain one hex of torpedo range this round. T3 The Watch Relieved: once per battle, swap the positions of two of your own ships before firing begins.
- Branch C, Cinder Numbers (numerics only): T1 Torpedoes deal 15 percent more damage to burning ships. T2 Burning damage per round rises by 15 percent. T3 Torpedo damage rises by 18 percent.

Spotlight beat: Draven launches a salvo at a ship he cannot hit, and the miss is the point: the burning wreck drifts into the enemy's firing lane and closes it.

### Commodore Sera Ashgrove

Who they are: deckborn, raised in a Hearth carrier's hangar, and the youngest commodore in the fleet. She treats squadrons as the Reach's true inheritance: cheap, brave, and impossible to write off from a distance.

Play style: the wing admiral. Overwhelm with wings, keep the Hearth alive, and spend squadrons like the fleet can grow more, because it can.

Command: Scramble all wings. Carriers launch an extra wing past the hangar limit, and all squadrons strike again at plus 50 percent.

Skill tree (idiom: the dawn watch, flown):

- Branch A, Launch Window: T1 Holding Pattern: one squadron may delay its strike to the end of the round. T2 Two Dawns: one squadron may split its strike between two targets in the same hex. T3 The Whole Sky: once per battle, launch one squadron from any carrier past its hangar limit.
- Branch B, Deck Discipline: T1 Recover Under Fire: one squadron may rearm without returning to its carrier, once per round. T2 Screen the Hearth: one fighter squadron intercepts one enemy strike this round. T3 The Deck Holds: if a carrier is destroyed, its wings in flight may strike once more before they are lost.
- Branch C, Wing Numbers (numerics only): T1 Squadrons deal 15 percent more damage. T2 Carrier hull values rise by 15 percent. T3 Squadrons deal 15 percent more damage to ships that moved this round.

Spotlight beat: Ashgrove launches a wing from a burning Hearth, and the wing strikes anyway, past the hangar limit, on the round the Hearth dies.

### High Admiral Oryn Thale

Who they are: the oldest living crusader, an admiral of the second crusade who came out of retirement when the third launched. She speaks in call and response and the whole fleet answers, because half of them are her grandchildren's generation of deckborn.

Play style: the bulwark. Screens up, damage refused, and a slow advance the enemy cannot stop, only delay.

Command: Iron wall. Shields refill, and your ships take 40 percent less hull damage this round.

Skill tree (idiom: what is owed to the ones who hold):

- Branch A, Screen Discipline: T1 Layered Screens: when a ship's screens fall to zero, it keeps a one-hit screen remnant until the end of the round (new: the remnant absorbs the next single weapon hit completely, then fails). T2 Hold the Arc: a ship that did not move this round may take one incoming volley onto its own screens instead of an adjacent friendly ship. T3 The Wall Stands: once per battle, when Iron wall is used, it also restores screens on ships that started the round with none.
- Branch B, What Is Owed: T1 Names Answered: when a friendly ship braces, the flagship's next volley this battle gains one extra gun. T2 The Watch Shared: if your fleet loses no ships in a round, every ship repairs 5 percent hull at the end of the round. T3 Nothing Wasted: once per battle, a destroyed ship's remaining torpedoes fire at their last target before the ship is removed (new: torpedoes only, at half damage, rounding down).
- Branch C, Cold Numbers (numerics only): T1 Screen strength rises by 15 percent. T2 Armour values rise by 15 percent. T3 Hull damage taken falls by 15 percent while a ship is braced.

Spotlight beat: Thale stands at the Roll of Vesper, the wall of names, and chalks her own name in the next empty space. She says nothing. She goes back to her ship.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: an Eclipse Archivist commanding the siphon defence. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: it is patient past the point of menace and it never retreats, because retreat is a flesh idea and its dead are a restore queue. It arrives on the Nemesis clock, late and certain: first meeting in sector 2 with a small escort, final meeting in sector 3. Between meetings it restores and hardens the array's escort screen. Its adaptations never directly counter a weakness you have already shown it, and a lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses, shown on the debrief. When it is killed, it returns once after death, in sector 3 only, restored from the Archive, and it remembers the fight in full.

Voice: cold, exact, quietly doubting, counting years and bodies, and the memory of noon. Memory and the Archive belong to Eclipse, and this rival uses both.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "This is my ninth body. I have watched your crusade in eight of them. You are still paying in names."
- Mid-battle: "Your line is beautiful. It will not be enough. It was not enough at Vesper."
- Mid-battle: "You would put out the last light to save a small one. I understand. I remember noon."
- Victory: "Your names are chalk. Mine are in the Archive. Both are kept."
- Defeat: "Go home. Chalk this too. It will not warm anyone."
- Final meeting: "You are early. I have waited a thousand years. I can wait for you to be ready."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction (settled): a new crusade each generation, and each learns from the last. A lost crusade leaves three things: its names on the Roll of Vesper, its marked charts (routes and Nemesis weaknesses learned, carried as intel), and its debts, which the next crusade inherits as obligations the campaign can call in.

Admiral leveling is the spine: crusade admirals level across runs because their names are re-sworn by successors who train on the last holder's annotated orders. A tree pick is a lesson the new holder swore to keep. Unlocks over runs: admiral levels and tree tiers persist across generations; Nemesis intel persists on the Roll; new starting ship patterns, torpedo variants, and brace patterns recovered from wreck fields along the route; and deeper sync with Seren opens its fire protocols earlier. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief screen and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the fleet reaches the Hollow Sun with a ship from every previous crusade's pattern in the line. Thale, or her successor, chalks the last name on the Daybreak's hull: the name of the first crusade that never came home. The fleet fires, the siphon arrays break, and for the first time in ninety years a star in the Vesper Reach burns a little brighter. The dawn watch sounds. Every deck faces home.
