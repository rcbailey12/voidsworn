# Ashfall Hullbreakers

> Owner ruling (Nov 1, 2026): portrait posture is by character, not by rank alone, with the Warlord always upright: Grax upright in challenge, Old Mott low and wide, Sable low and skulking, Vesh upright over his trophies. One hero trophy per portrait; harness clutter beyond that is costume. The chair patched Direction 1's harness line below to match.

## Identity

The Ashfall Hullbreakers are alien void hunters from a cold, heavy-gravity belt where only the strongest packs ate: huge, hot-blooded predators who hunt in space the way they hunted at home, by trail and by sound. They do not want riches. They want to be the strongest thing in the dark, and they came to the Hollow Sun because the war around it is the biggest fight in known space. They strip every wreck, bolt the best pieces into their ships and their harness armour, and hunt R-5 Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable.

Playing the Hullbreakers is about the close and the prize. Your ships are fast, your shields are thin, and your guns include scrap weapons with wild extra shots and far too many rockets, but no lances, so range is where you die. Your decisions are angles: close from the flanks and behind, ram and board, and choose which ship is the prize before the fight starts. Your best moment is the board: claws in, a ship taken whole, and its plates on your hull by the next battle.

## Lore

Origin: the Ashfall belt, with Rattlehold as their biggest den. Their wake-trackers follow the ion trail every drive leaves behind, and their listening arrays pick up engine noise and signal chatter from far off, while their own ships run silent. Any of them can challenge a captain, and whoever lives keeps the ship. Old Mott has outlived forty captains, mostly by killing them.

War aim: the war itself, Rook, and every wreck worth stripping.

How they see the others:

- Sunward Crusade: "They fight hard and don't run. Good trophies."
- Hegemony Navy: "Their ships hum. You can hear them a system away."
- The Chorus: "Wet, and hungry. The only thing out here that fights like we do."
- The Glasswrights: "Pretty little ships. They shatter well."
- Eclipse: "No smell, no blood. Dead metal that gets back up. Kill it twice."

## Look

People: huge and hunched, with the heavy mass of a great hunting beast: a big shoulder hump, thick forearms, and heavy claws. Fur only as a coarse mane over the hump, neck, and shoulders, in grey, black, or rust-brown; everywhere else, tough dark hide. The face is bare, short, and blunt, armoured in overlapping plates of dull horn grown from the hide, with dark leathery skin in the creases: no fur on the face, no snout, no nose. The jaw splits down the middle of the chin into two halves that open sideways, lined with teeth. Four small eyes run across the brow and shine in the dark, with heat-sensing pits beside them. Ridged plates run down the back and shoulders. Scrap from other factions' ships is strapped over them in armour harnesses. Trophies are machines and hull plates, never skulls or bodies.

Ships: they run dark. Matte soot black and rust, few windows, dim red running lights, a red-orange glow from the engines, big listening dishes and wake-sniffer scoops at the bow, boarding claws, and chains between hull sections. Lopsided, with hull plates torn from every faction bolted on in their old colours.

Palette: soot, rust, dried blood red, trophy colours, red running lights.

Effects colours: red-orange engine glow, dull red tracers for autocannon and scrap guns, white-red rocket exhaust, chain-grey boarding effects.

Three visual directions for the people. All three are the same species: alien, not bears, and nothing like the film Predator. No quill dreadlocks, no mouth tusks framing the jaw, no helmet or mask look, no polished metal on the face, and no human bodybuilder torso. The bulk is a beast's bulk in every direction.

- Direction 1, Knuckle Stalker (silhouette: low and wide, on all fours). The hunter moves knuckles down at rest, shoulder hump rising above the head like a loaded spring, thick forearms taking the weight. Surface: scarred horn face plates, one eye clouded, and a heavy trophy harness carrying a single hero trophy plate. Lighting: dim red from below, cold blue from behind. Background: a wreck field, soot and drifting chains. Paint this if the pack should read as animals first and captains second.
- Direction 2, Den-alpha Upright (silhouette: towering and narrow, standing tall). The same species upright and hunched forward, head carried low between massive shoulders, ridged back plates rising in a crest behind the mane, sideways jaw wide in a challenge. Surface: bristling rust-brown mane, chipped plates, fresh scars re-derived from this pose, one foot on a stripped Hegemony turret. Lighting: firelight from a scrap brazier, deep shadows. Background: Rattlehold, welded scrap walls and a circle of watching pack members. Paint this if the challenge ritual should lead the portraits.
- Direction 3, Long Skulker (silhouette: lean, long, and low, mid-stride). A longer build of the species, stretched torso and longer neck, the whole body closer to the ground, one hand flat on the deck and head tilted to listen, four eyes closed and heat pits flared. Surface: sleeker dark grey hide, short black mane, minimal trophies, plates duller than in the other directions. Lighting: red running lights along the deck and one cold white work lamp. Background: the inside of a running-dark ship, a wake-sniffer dish angled behind. Paint this if hunting by sound should lead the portraits.

## Admirals

### Warlord Grax Ironmaw

Who they are: the current Warlord of the biggest pack at Rattlehold, who took the Pyre-Barge by challenge and has kept it through eleven more. He talks in trails and counts, and his idea of a plan is naming the prize out loud.

Play style: the full burn. Everything closes at once, rams set up boards, and the fleet wins or breaks in the first three rounds.

Command: Full burn. Every ship gains full movement this round and rams and boards at full strength after moving, with no penalty for the distance covered.

Skill tree (idiom: kills counted, lessons taken off the dead):

- Branch A, The Trail: T1 Smell the Wake: at the start of a battle, learn which enemy ship has the weakest armour. T2 Close Quiet: your ships take no reaction fire on the round they first close to boarding range. T3 On the Scent: once per battle, after an enemy ship fires, one of your ships may move two hexes toward it at once.
- Branch B, The Prize: T1 Named Prize: name one enemy ship at the start of the battle; your ships board it at greater strength. T2 Strip As You Go: a ship you board drops salvage even if the boarding fails. T3 Ironmaw's Share: once per battle, when you capture a ship, it may act on your side next round at half strength instead of being towed out (new: half strength means half its remaining weapon damage, rounding down; towing and stripping stay Hullbreaker-only, as shipped).
- Branch C, Pack Numbers (numerics only): T1 Rockets deal 15 percent more damage. T2 Ram damage rises by 20 percent. T3 The flagship deals 25 percent more damage to ships larger than itself.

Spotlight beat: Grax fights the challenge for the Pyre-Barge again, because a young Ripper has the right, and wins it in front of the pack with one hand already broken from the morning's boarding action.

### Sable the Smiling Knife

Who they are: a Howler who hunts alone ahead of the pack and is called Smiling for the way her split jaw hangs open when she has found a trail. Nobody has ever heard her ship's engines. The pack is not sure she likes them, and follows her anyway.

Play style: the knife. One silent approach, one ship opened from behind, and the pack arrives at a fight that has already started bleeding.

Command: Knife in the dark. One of your ships moves silently to the rear arc of any enemy ship in range and boards it before it can fire this round.

Skill tree (idiom: what the quiet taught her):

- Branch A, Silent Running: T1 Dark Ship: one ship per round counts as silent for reaction fire while it does not fire. T2 Cold Wake: your ships leave no trail marker for enemy wake effects this battle. T3 The Quiet Round: once per battle, your whole fleet counts as silent until your first shot.
- Branch B, Rear Arc: T1 Behind Them: a ship that ends its move in an enemy's rear arc boards at greater strength. T2 Open the Seam: boarding attacks against a ship already boarded this battle strip one weapon. T3 Sable's Cut: once per battle, one boarding action also disables the target's engines for one round.
- Branch C, Knife Numbers (numerics only): T1 Boarding strength rises by 15 percent. T2 Ships deal 20 percent more damage from the rear arc. T3 The flagship moves 15 percent further on rounds when it does not fire.

Spotlight beat: Sable brings back a listening-array recording of a Glasswright fleet mourning, and plays it once in the den, and the pack is quiet for longer than anyone can remember, and then she deletes it, because it is not a trophy.

### Old Mott

Who they are: the oldest Hullbreaker alive, a scarred mountain who has outlived forty captains, mostly by killing them, and now keeps the hulk-carrier and the pack's young. Mott no longer challenges anyone. Captains challenge around Mott, carefully.

Play style: the swarm keeper. Cheap hulls, endless young, and a carrier that treats losses as a cost the pack has already paid.

Command: Release the swarm. The hulk-carrier launches every swarm craft it carries at once, past its normal launch limit.

Skill tree (idiom: forty captains' worth of scar tissue):

- Branch A, The Young: T1 More Mouths to Feed: swarm craft launch one round earlier than normal. T2 Thick Young: swarm craft that are destroyed in boarding range still complete their boarding attack. T3 Mott's Brood: once per battle, relaunch one destroyed swarm craft at half strength beside the carrier.
- Branch B, Old Scars: T1 Been Hit Before: a ship that survived a volley that destroyed an ally beside it gains armour for the rest of the battle. T2 Scar Tissue: the flagship ignores the first critical effect applied to it each battle (new: the damage still applies; only the extra effect is ignored). T3 Forty Captains: once per battle, when the flagship would be destroyed, it is left at one hull and its swarms relaunch at once.
- Branch C, Swarm Numbers (numerics only): T1 Swarm craft deal 15 percent more damage. T2 Swarm craft have 20 percent more hull. T3 The carrier deals 15 percent more damage for each swarm craft in flight, capped at plus 45 percent.

Spotlight beat: a young Claw asks Mott what the forty-first captain was like, and Mott points at a plate on the harness, and then at the young Claw's own empty harness, and says: "Space for yours."

### Vesh the Collector

Who they are: a Ripper who fights with a quartermaster's eye, famous for finishing a boarding action and asking, politely, where the good plating is kept. Vesh's ships always come home heavier than they left.

Play style: the collector. Fight for the wreck field, not the kill count: strip everything, patch everything, and leave richer than the winner.

Command: Patch and weld. Every damaged ship regains hull from salvage on the field, and one stripped weapon may be refitted at once.

Skill tree (idiom: what the wrecks taught him):

- Branch A, Stripping Craft: T1 Good Plates First: salvage from wrecks you strip is worth 15 percent more credits. T2 Strip Under Fire: your ships may strip a wreck while enemies are in range, without ending their activation. T3 Vesh's Eye: once per battle, name one wreck; stripping it also recovers one fitted weapon from it, at half strength.
- Branch B, Weld Craft: T1 Field Patch: a ship repaired by Patch and weld may still fire this round. T2 Trophy Armour: a ship repaired from a specific faction's wreck gains extra armour against that faction for the rest of the battle. T3 Heavier Home: once per battle, a ship at one hull may be fully patched from two wrecks at once (new: both wrecks are consumed, and the ship cannot act next round).
- Branch C, Collector Numbers (numerics only): T1 Repair from salvage restores 20 percent more hull. T2 Scrap guns deal 15 percent more damage. T3 Captured and stripped ships pay 20 percent more ransom, under the strip and ransom rule.

Spotlight beat: Vesh strips a Chorus wreck that is still moving, and keeps stripping while it tries to eat the boarding party, and comes back with the only clean plate on the field and three fewer fingers on one hand, and calls it a fair trade.

## Nemesis

Drafted assignment: a Glasswright Lens-keeper. The alternatives are in the open questions.

Behaviour: a duelist who keeps range and withdraws rather than be swarmed. It arrives on the Nemesis clock: first meeting in sector 2, final meeting in sector 3. When it withdraws from a losing meeting it returns with the same four-ship elite fleet, upgraded, never enlarged, because a Glasswright rival answers quality with quality. Its adaptations are refinements of aim and angle, shown on the debrief, and never a direct counter to a revealed weakness. It will break off a winning fight if continuing would cost it a ship it cannot replace, because its people are few. A lost meeting still teaches you one of its weaknesses.

Voice: quiet, precise, mournful, light words, and apologies that are meant. Light, glass, reflection, and grief belong to the Glasswrights, and this rival uses all four.

Sample lines:

- Meeting: "Forgive the range. We have learned to begin far apart."
- Meeting: "You break things because breaking is easier than grief. I am sorry. I will still stop you."
- Mid-battle: "Hold the angle. Let them cross the light first."
- Victory: "We are sorry. The mirrors will be turned for your dead, as for ours."
- Defeat: "A clean break. Take the field. We will gather our shards, and we will be exact about what you did."
- Final meeting: "The same four ships. Better light. Begin."

## Progression and story

Generational fiction: the pack learns by challenge and by scar. A lost pack is not gone: its survivors are picked up by other packs, its trophies pass to whoever killed its killers, and its story is told at the den fires until the pups can smell the mistake in it. A new Warlord inherits the old one's harness, plates, and stories, which is why the lessons persist.

Admiral leveling is framed the same way: a tree is the set of lessons an admiral's fights have taught the pack. Unlocks over runs: trophy refits persist between runs once a craft is known to the pack, and ships gain visible plates from factions they have beaten this run; Nemesis intel persists as trail knowledge, the pack remembering how the rival hunts; deeper sync with Rook opens its ram and speed protocols earlier; and by run 4 the chain of captains at Rattlehold is long enough that new admirals start with one of the old one's scars. Nemesis adaptations are shown on the debrief and hidden during the run.

Finale, run 5: the pack reaches the Hollow Sun and Rook's signal is loud. Grax, or whoever holds the title, howls over open comms and the pack answers. They hit the siphon arrays from every flank at once, the arrays crack, and the pack strips the wreckage. A piece of the array is bolted to the Pyre-Barge's bow. It is the biggest trophy there has ever been. Strongest keeps it.
