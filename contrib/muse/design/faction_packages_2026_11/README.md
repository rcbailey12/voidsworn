# Faction design packages, first drafts: chair assembly

## What this folder is

This folder holds the chair assembly for Job 2 of the Nov 1, 2026 commission: first-draft design packages for all six factions of Embers of the Hollow Sun, written for the owner to review before anything is built. Each faction file follows the same order: identity, lore, look, admirals, Nemesis, progression and story, and open questions for the owner. A separate cross-faction check audits the set as a whole.

These are review drafts, not final game text. Shipped names, Command names, and faction baselines come from the evidence pack and win over any leg draft. The owner's rulings R59 to R72 win over the lore bible, and the lore bible wins over everything else.

## Method

Four legs were commissioned with the same pack and the same leg prompt.

Three legs completed in full and carry this assembly:

- DeepSeek: complete, the tightest draft, 9,568 words.
- Qwen: complete, the most mechanically specific draft, 15,291 words. Its first reply truncated mid-sentence in the Glasswrights section and was completed with one continuation under the same verified model header.
- Rey (blind subagent): complete, the longest draft at 16,518 words, and the most careful with the pack's defect list. Its skill tree outlines form the backbone of the assembled trees.

One leg did not complete usefully:

- Argon: partial, and screened out. The run took 66 minutes, stopped twice at the same point in the Eclipse section, and the browser view could not recover the earlier factions verbatim after the fact, because the chat view renders only the last turn on reload and page reads truncate long paragraphs. Only an Eclipse continuation was recovered, and that continuation is non-compliant with the pack: it invents admirals, gives a shieldless faction shields, and writes real-time mechanics into a turn-based game. It is filed in the legs folder with a chair header as the record of the leg. No Argon text was used as drafting material. The full account is in the cross-faction check.

Argon re-run (Nov 1, 2026, later the same evening, at the owner's request): the leg was run again, this time with the reply read back inside the session. The Crusade, Hegemony, and Hullbreaker drafts were recovered verbatim (minus the tail of Vesh the Collector, where generation stopped) and are on-pack at first screen. The Chorus and the Glasswrights were never generated, and the Eclipse continuation repeated the first run's non-compliance almost point for point, including the same two invented admirals. The chair's call: the Eclipse drift is systematic for this model on this faction, and no further Argon attempt was made. The re-run arrived after the assembly was built and changed nothing in it. It is filed as legs/argon_leg_rerun.md with its own chair header and screening note.

## How the chair resolved disagreements

Where the three complete legs agreed, agreement is the draft. Where they disagreed on a genuine fork, the fork was not silently settled: it sits in that faction's open questions with concrete options and a marked chair pick, where the pick is the option best supported by the rulings and by the majority of legs.

The largest disagreement was the Nemesis round-robin: each leg paired the factions differently. The chair drafted each Nemesis section from the assignment the lore bible best supports, using the bible's own behaviour anchors: a Hullbreaker rival raids credits, flees a losing fight, returns stronger, and a stolen ship returns when it dies; a Glasswright rival is a duelist who keeps range, withdraws, and returns with the same four-ship elite fleet upgraded; a Chorus rival grows tougher from every ship the player loses anywhere and adapts after every meeting; an Eclipse rival is patient, never retreats, and returns once after death in sector 3 only; a Crusade rival never flees and challenges the flagship to a duel. The drafted assignments are: Crusade faces Eclipse, Hegemony faces the Hullbreakers, Hullbreakers face the Glasswrights, the Chorus faces Eclipse, the Glasswrights face the Chorus, and Eclipse faces the Crusade. Every alternative pairing the legs proposed is listed in the relevant open questions.

Where a leg drifted from shipped data or from the defect list, the assembly corrected it and the cross-faction check records the correction. Examples: withdrawal behaviour wrongly given to an Eclipse rival was replaced with the bible anchor; apology language wrongly given to Chorus lines was removed; a Crusade line that treated chalk names as data was cut; a Hegemony bookkeeping name in one tree was lightened; and two questions that re-asked settled rulings (the Eclipse no-carrier ruling and the Chorus boss hull) were dropped rather than presented as open.
