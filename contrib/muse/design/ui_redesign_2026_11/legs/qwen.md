# Qwen stress-test leg (content and reading), issue #270 (from the browser run, Nov 8, 2026; gate verified, Qwen3.8-Max before and after; single response, no preference prompt)

Method: one dense moment walked through every candidate. Mid-battle, fleets clustered on adjacent hexes, flagship Judgement Bell at 31 percent hull (HULL_LOW, needs a second channel), Heat one tick from max, a Nemesis fight (the reserved ALERT tag, a clock, an adaptation line), Captain Oath-Captain Malek Draven, a lesson line in the log, 20 ships in about 250 px, bright nebula behind everything, 12 px floor.

Headline results per candidate (the full leg text is in the run record; key judgements preserved here):

- D1 Plotting Table: the ALERT tag has no natural home on the rim; engraved capitals inflate the 26-character captain name past the marker width; a ticker cannot carry an adaptation line the player must absorb; the phase bar forces a floating element that breaks the "UI lives on the rim" rule; projected panels occlude the field. 
- D2 Gun Director: the ALERT tag has the most natural home in the pool (a backlit warning plate on steel); the dropped-forward tab is unambiguous; the aperture eats 60-100 px per side and the roster column math is tight (20 tabs at about 35 px); the Nemesis clock and the enemy phase bar need elements the description does not supply (an inset counter, a strip gauge); the paper-tape log scrolls, so a lesson can be missed, but it is always visible.
- D3 Chart Room: the always-present margin-column log is the single best structural decision in the pool for a text-heavy game; the focus ring is native (registration marks); the ALERT stamp on a quiet chart is the most powerful use of the reserved red; risks are contrast off the chart's backing (nameplates over bright scenery) and no alarm vocabulary for near-max Heat beyond colour and numerals.
- D4 Vector C.I.C.: stepped-segment bars are the best second-channel solution in the pool; the clock is native in segmented digits; but phosphor bloom at 12 px smears prose, the single-line ticker with toggle is the worst log in the pool for a lesson line, the monochrome phosphor rule fights the multi-colour locked palette (the ALERT red needs a second phosphor), and nameplates live outside the bezel's language.
- D5 Salvaged Console: hand-painted labels at 12 px fail the floor in practice; the smudging chalkboard log destroys the one line the player must read; the warm EMBER rim shifts perceived hues of the locked colours; the ALERT tag can drown in the noise; a strong single screenshot, a poor system.
- D6 Glass Bridge: the core rule (almost nothing opaque) is structurally incompatible with the spec: the roster hides itself, the ship panel collapses, the log has no home, readability depends on scene lighting. A cinematic HUD idea, non-functional for this game.
- W1 Admiralty Table: serif and italic at 12 px are the weakest text in the pool; brass markers give each roster entry about 12 px of text height; the war-diary log is the one element worth stealing (for the end card, where reading speed matters less).
- W2 Living Membrane: the proposer's own flag is the verdict; it is structurally closer to the rejected UI (soft edges, glow, flowing type) than to the ordered console; pulsing modulates text brightness, which is an anti-feature for required reading.
- W3 Signal and Semaphore: not a direction, a layer; its ALERT signal lamp is the purest expression of the one reserved red and should be offered to the winner.

Distinctness screening (with quotes): D1 and W1 are the same idea in different metals (both tables, brass, engraved or inlaid marks; the difference is tone, not structure). D2 and D5 are the same console in different states of repair (riveted frame, physical roster metaphors, analog strip logs). D4, D3 and D6 each stand alone. Families: Table, Physical Console, CRT Terminal, Printed Chart, Glass Overlay, Organic, Signal Layer.

Qwen's ranking: 1. D3 Chart Room. 2. D2 Gun Director. 3. D4 Vector C.I.C. 4. D1 Plotting Table. 5. W1 (merged into D1). 6. D5 Salvaged. 7. D6 Glass Bridge. 8. W2 Membrane. W3 unranked (a layer). Qwen's carry-forward recommendation: D3 as the primary system, borrowing D2's keys and gauges for the action bar and Heat, W3's signal lamp for the ALERT tag and phase changes, and W1's diary italic for the end card only.
