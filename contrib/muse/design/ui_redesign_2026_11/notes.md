# #270 concept notes: one note per direction

Each direction has two images (restrained and bold), generated as concept art for the battle screen at 1920x1080. The images were shown to Ryan in chat (image generation cannot travel through the text-only repo push). Caveats that apply to every image: the generator drew naval-flavoured hulls and, in places, a square grid and repeated roster labels; the real build keeps the game's actual 3D hex field and ship models. Judge each image for frame language, type, and material, not for its ships.

Locked law underneath every direction (from contrib/opus/briefs/ui_spec.md): the palette meanings are fixed (GILT is the player's own and the primary action, ALERT red is reserved for the Nemesis, shield cyan and hull amber on bars only, foe lavender family), battle nameplates and the roster never use faction colour (the enemy faction is carried by the crest in the top bar and the faction name in plain BONE), every colour-carried state needs a second channel, the roster is 250 px and must work at 20 ships, text floor 12 px, the #231 scale law and the bone focus ring stay.

## D3 The Chart Room (images d3_chart_room_restrained, d3_chart_room_bold)

Frame language: a printed plotting chart that happens to update. Hairline rules, coordinate ticks, registration corners, and ruled scales instead of panels; the screen reads as one sheet of chart paper laid over the battle. Type: a strict monospace on a fixed column grid, tabular figures, no display face anywhere. Colour use: the locked palette printed flat, no glow; bars are ruled scales with tick marks, so the second channel is the scale itself. Faction accent: the enemy crest printed in the top margin like a compass rose, in BONE line work, which is exactly the spec's crest-only rule. What it hides that today's HUD shows: nothing structural; the margin log column is always present, which the stress legs rated the single best structural decision in the pool for a text-heavy game. Its risk is the opposite: with no metal and no glow it may read as flat print rather than a warship console.

## D2 The Gun Director (images d2_gun_director_restrained, d2_gun_director_bold)

Frame language: a riveted steel fire-control console framing the field as an aperture. The roster hangs as stamped metal tabs down the left rail; the selected tab drops physically forward. Type: stencil and stamped capitals for labels, odometer-style counters for round and clock. Colour use: locked colours arrive as backlight: GILT behind the primary action key, ALERT as a backlit warning plate (the stress legs rated this the most natural home for the Nemesis tag in the whole pool), bars as lit segments under stamped scales. Faction accent: the crest as a stamped brass plate bolted to the top rail. What it hides: the aperture eats roughly 60 to 100 px of field per side, and the phase bar and Nemesis clock are not native to the metaphor; both need invented elements (a strip gauge under the aperture, an inset counter). The bold variation is the same console battle-worn and salvaged: mismatched plates, hand-painted corrections over stamped labels, warm work-light. That variation absorbs the Salvaged Console direction, which both stress legs judged a skin of this console rather than a separate system.

## D1 The Plotting Table (images d1_plotting_table_restrained, d1_plotting_table_bold)

Frame language: the hex field sits in a shallow octagonal holographic well; the interface lives on the etched brass rim rails around it. The roster stands as upright markers along the left rim, lit from below; the log is a ticker along the bottom rim that expands on demand. Type: engraved capitals on the rails, tabular readouts in the well. Colour use: locked colours as light in the well and enamel on the rails; the phase bar wants to live on the rim, which the content stress test flagged as a break in the rim rule. Faction accent: the crest as an enamel inlay in the top rail. What it hides: the ALERT tag has no natural home on a rim, engraved capitals inflate long captain names past the 250 px roster, and a ticker cannot carry a full adaptation line. The Admiralty wildcard (brass, serif, war diary) was screened as the same idea in different metal and is merged here; its one stealable element, the formal war-diary log, is recorded in the synthesis as an end-card borrowing.

## D4 Vector C.I.C. (images d4_vector_cic_restrained, d4_vector_cic_bold)

Frame language: a cathode combat-information-centre terminal in a chamfered bezel casing: phosphor bloom, a slow raster sweep, segmented line glyphs. Type: a terminal face with telemetry codes (TRK//04-CRUISER) giving the data a visible grammar. Colour use: the fight in this direction is that a phosphor terminal wants to be monochrome and the locked palette is not; the concepts cheat toward green-amber phosphor with the locked colours as accents. Its stepped-segment bars are the best second-channel solution in the pool (segments count as well as colour), and the Nemesis clock is native to a terminal. What it hides: bloom smears prose at the 12 px floor, its ticker-plus-toggle log is the worst in the pool for a lesson line, and 4K scanlines moire. Build cost is also real: the CRT look needs a SubViewport shader pass, per the buildability leg.

## D6 The Glass Bridge (images d6_glass_bridge_restrained, d6_glass_bridge_bold)

Frame language: the interface etched onto transparent glass panes standing at the viewport edges, in two layers with parallax; almost nothing is opaque. Etched text and hairlines catch light and reflections from the nebula. Type: fine etched capitals, very light weight. Colour use: locked colours as light through glass, beautiful over dark space. Faction accent: the crest etched and back-lit in the top pane. What it hides: this is the direction whose core rule fights the locked spec. The roster wants to collapse when idle and the spec requires it present; the ship panel and log have no honest home; and legibility depends on the scenery behind the glass, which the player does not control. It is the prettiest direction and the riskiest, and both stress legs ranked it last among the finalists. It stays in the set because Ryan should see the extreme of the diegetic family before ruling it out.

## Wildcard: The Reliquary (images reliquary_restrained, reliquary_bold)

Frame language: a cathedral organ console crossed with a torpedo data computer: dark brushed metal, enamel plaques, engraved niches, restrained gold leaf. The roster is a row of reliquary niches with engraved captain names and enamel bars; the log is kept as formal entries, a war diary on dark enamel cards. Type: engraved capitals and a formal serif for the diary lines. Colour use: locked colours as enamel, so they read as material rather than light; ALERT would be a red enamel plaque, very legible and very solemn. Faction accent: the crest as an enamel plaque with gold leaf edging, the most ceremonial crest treatment in the set, and a natural fit for the Crusade's oath and dawn motifs. What it hides: ornament density is the enemy; the bold variation already crowds the field, and a whole game in this register risks kitsch. As a full system it is a wildcard; as an accent language for Crusade-facing screens it is the strongest flavour in the pool.

## Wildcard: The Living Membrane (images membrane_restrained, membrane_bold)

Frame language: the interface grown on an organic ship membrane: dark translucent biofilm panels, visible veins, data pulsing through them in bioluminescent gold. The roster is a column of membrane bubbles; the round counter is a heartbeat ring; actions are firm membrane nodes. Type: plain bone capitals on the dark membrane, deliberately unstyled so the data stays readable against the organic frame. Colour use: locked colours as bioluminescence, which works for GILT and the bars but makes the reserved ALERT red just another glow. Faction accent: the crest grown into the membrane at the top, lit from inside. What it hides: both stress legs killed this as a system. It conflicts with the hard-edged brief, its pulsing animation fights the reading of numbers, and it is the rejected dashboard philosophy in a new skin: decoration carrying no information. It is in the set because R137 asked for wildcards that are genuinely outside the family, and this is the honest extreme. If any part of it survives, it should be as a Chorus or Glasswright faction accent, never as the shared style.
