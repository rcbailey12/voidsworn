# Issue #270 reference study: ship-bridge and tactical-console interfaces

Eight shipped works with strong console or bridge interfaces. For each: what it does well that fits a turn-based hex fleet game, and one screenshot reference link (store pages carry screenshot galleries; no art is copied, these are look references only).

1. **NEBULOUS: Fleet Command** (game, Eremite Games). A tactical plot, not a dashboard: contacts are vector tracks with classification codes, missile telemetry reads as formatted text, and the map is a radar-style scope where information has a grammar (track number, course vector, confidence). What fits Voidsworn: the Nemesis clock and adaptation lines are exactly this kind of telemetry; a fleet game can let codes and tracks carry story instead of prose. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/887570/NEBULOUS_Fleet_Command/

2. **HighFleet** (game, Konstantin Koshutin). Skeuomorphic analog war: stamped faceplates, physical dials, lamps and switches that are the UI, drawn as if machined. What fits: it proves a whole game can live inside one instrument material without reading as a joke, and its gauges show how Heat and hull can be instruments instead of bars. The caution it carries is also useful: skeuomorphism spends screen area, and 1080p is unforgiving. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1434950/HighFleet/

3. **Homeworld Remastered Collection** (games, Gearbox). The Sensors Manager: the battle dissolves into a holographic projection of tracks, rings and vectors over darkness, and the player commands the projection, not the ships. What fits: a turn-based game can afford a plotting layer this abstract because nothing is real-time; the projection look is the strongest reference for any table or holo direction. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/244160/Homeworld_Remastered_Collection/

4. **Battlestar Galactica Deadlock** (game, Black Lab Games). Its tactical screen is framed as the Daidalos CIC: a DRADIS-style plot with Colonial military chrome, subsystem readouts and a war-room campaign layer around it. What fits: it is the closest shipped relative (turn-based capital-ship tactics) and it shows a licensed military console carrying a full campaign's worth of screens, not just one battle view. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/544610/Battlestar_Galactica_Deadlock/

5. **Aliens** (film, 1986, James Cameron). The APC and operations-room screens: blocky telemetry, abbreviated columns, personnel readouts as flat data, everything low-bandwidth and military. What fits: it is the root reference for the cold terminal family, and its screens stay readable at a glance in a dark room full of noise, which is the actual viewing condition of a tactics game. Screenshot reference (university film-stills archive): https://sites.pitt.edu/~goscilo/Sci-Fi/FilmStills/Aliens.html

6. **Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2** (game, Tindalos Interactive). A gothic admiralty in space: brass, parchment tones and cathedral framing wrapped around a real-time tactics HUD. What fits: it is the shipped proof that an archaic naval material can carry a space fleet game and sell the setting's religion-and-empire tone; also a warning, since its ornament density is a constant fight with readability. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/573100/Battlefleet_Gothic_Armada_2/

7. **Hardspace: Shipbreaker** (game, Blackbird Interactive). A salvaged industrial cockpit: the HUD is a working tool's interface, worn labels, utilitarian frames, diegetic readouts on the suit and the cutter. What fits: the best reference for the salvaged direction and for how wear should be distributed (edges and touch points, not uniform grime), plus a lesson in keeping diegetic UI honest about what the character could actually see. Screenshot reference: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1161580/Hardspace_Shipbreaker/

8. **Dead Space** (games, Visceral/EA). The RIG: health as a spinal readout, inventory and maps projected in-world as holograms the character looks at, almost no non-diegetic chrome. What fits: the extreme end of the diegetic spectrum; it shows what can be pushed into the world (bars on the ship, plates as physical objects) and what still needs a screen, which is the exact trade the glass and table directions have to price. Reference article with screenshots: https://www.thegamer.com/dead-space-horror-ui/

## What the study says, in one paragraph

The references split into three material families: projected light (Homeworld, Dead Space, NEBULOUS's scope), machined hardware (HighFleet, Hardspace, Deadlock's CIC chrome), and printed or engraved matter (Aliens' telemetry, Armada 2's admiralty). Every candidate direction in the pool belongs to one of these families, and the families, not the individual skins, are the real choice: light is cheap in screen area and expensive in legibility discipline; hardware is the most immersive and the most expensive in pixels; print is the most legible and the least cinematic. The two films and the oldest game here (Aliens, and Deadlock as its direct descendant) also share one trait the pool should steal regardless of winner: data formats with a visible grammar (codes, columns, tracks), so the UI reads as an instrument of a navy that exists, not as a skin over a spreadsheet.
