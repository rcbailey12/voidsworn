The Glasswrights (ithar): Species Design

1. The substance system: vitreous flesh

A Glasswright is built from one material, and that material is the design. Call it vitreous flesh: a living, warm, pale, translucent tissue that is simultaneously flesh and glass, because it grows glass within itself along the paths where it is stressed and where it is used. There is no separate skeleton, no separate lens, no separate sail material, and no glass "added" to a flesh body. There is one tissue with a continuous gradient of density, and every organ is a shaping of that gradient.

The gradient runs through three visible states of the same substance:

• Soft vitreous flesh — the pale, half-translucent, vascular base. Warm, flexible, living. This is what most of the body is.

• Dense vitreous glass — the same tissue with its optical cells packed tight along load paths and wear paths. This is the skeleton, the brow, the joint surfaces, the working surfaces of the hands. It is grown in place, not attached.

• Clear optical crystal — the same tissue clarified to true transparency where the body does precision light-work: the eye lenses, the sail vanes, the spinneret nozzles. Still living, still fed by the blood, still able to cloud and clear.

Light is the second circulatory system. The blood is pale, clear, and faintly luminous; it carries both nutrient and light. Light is gathered at the eyes and sails, conducted through clear channels in the flesh, spent by the glass-secreting cells, and stored as glowing inclusions deep in the thorax and abdomen. A Glasswright glows from inside because it is full of stored light. That stored light is also its record: Glasswright glass holds light at full fidelity, so what the body stores is what the body has seen. The same fact at body scale that makes a Glasswright hull a replayable record of a battle makes a Glasswright's deep glow a replayable record of a life.

Colour in the glass is an involuntary optical response of the light-conducting tissue to physiological state. It shifts with emotion the way a face flushes. It is never a language, never a signal, never deliberate.

The derivation rule. Every part of this design is the same tissue under a different regimen of stress and light-use. Cover any single part — the face, the hands, the sails — and the same story explains it: flesh that has grown glass where the body works, and light moving through both. Nothing on this body is a different material story from anything else.

2. Anatomy

2.1 Body plan and carriage

The body is built around a segmented thorax — a broad, low, forward-leaning trunk divided into overlapping plates of denser vitreous glass within the flesh. This trunk is the "little spider" in the carriage: the segmentation, the low wide set, the planted readiness. It is not a spider's limb count. The base species has six limbs: four arms and two stout support limbs. That is deliberate. The eight-point full weaver is an elder form, not the admiral base, and keeping the base at six is what keeps a Glasswright an admiral at a glance rather than a monster.

The two support limbs plant wide at the sides of the thorax, short, thick, glass-boned, fleshy, ending in broad splayed pads. With the low abdomen as a third contact behind, the stance is a wide, stable tripod: the low wide weaver stance. A working Glasswright does not stand upright like a person. It settles: support limbs planted, thorax low and forward, abdomen counterweighting, four arms free above the work. The whole silhouette is a bench with a mind in it.

Behind the thorax, a rounded segmented abdomen carries the deepest stored light — a slow amber-green glow that is the brightest thing about a resting Glasswright. The head is carried forward and low at the front of the thorax on a short, thick, flexible neck-stalk that lets it turn and drop toward the work. The head is a rounded, softly faceted fleshy mass, pale and translucent, with the faceting internal and subtle, following the glass beneath the skin. It is not a cut crystal block. It is a face.

2.2 The face

Two forward-grown lens eyes sit side by side, flush in the flesh. They do not bulge; they are clear grown lenses set into the face like windows, and the deep green is the light-conducting tissue behind them. The lenses themselves are clear; the green is the life behind the glass. Fleshy lids close over them, and above them a heavy segmented brow — a ridge of denser vitreous glass in the flesh, three or four segments — carries expression. Brow angle, lid closure, and the shift of green behind the lens are the whole expressive register, and it is a real one.

Below the eyes, low in the face, sits the reed grille: a short oval of fine vertical glass reeds set flush into the flesh. It does not protrude, does not protrude as a tube, and is nearly invisible when the being is silent. Speech passes through the reeds in a normal voice. The grille is the mouth and only the mouth; the work light does not come from it.

The face reads alien because none of its parts are human parts doing human jobs: flush grown lenses rather than eyeballs in sockets, a segmented glass brow rather than a brow of skin and hair, a reed organ rather than a lipped mouth, on a rounded faceted head on a short thick neck. It reads as a face because it has eyes, a brow, a mouth, and a full expressive range.

2.3 The four arms and the spinnerets

Four arms rise from the upper thorax in two functional pairs:

Two working arms, lower and heavier, with broad four-digit hands — three thick fingers and an opposed thumb — built for holding, turning, and steadying a workpiece.

Two precision arms, upper and slimmer, with six slender digits, built for fine weaving and placement.

Every wrist carries a spinneret rosette on its inner face: a cluster of small grown nozzles, the glass-secreting cells of the body concentrated at the wrist. The spinnerets draw vitreous thread — the same substance, molten at the nozzle and cooling clear — and lay it onto the workpiece. The working arms hold and turn; the precision arms weave. The largest thing a Glasswright weaves is a ship's sail vane, and the same rosette that lays a lens filigree at the bench can lay a vane's leading edge at the yard.

2.4 Stored light and the interior

The thorax and abdomen are the reservoir. Light gathered at the eyes and sails is pumped in the luminous blood and banked as glowing inclusions in the deep glass of the trunk. A calm Glasswright glows steady and slow. Fear or grief pulls the glow deep and dim; joy or concentration brightens it and pushes it toward the surface. The glow is the body's record and the body's mood at once, and it is never speech.

3. The sails

The sails are grown crystal vanes rising in a paired fan from the shoulder blades — three per side, six in all, the middle vane longest. They are modest: the largest is roughly the length of a forearm, and a working Glasswright keeps them furled most of the day. They echo the faction's ships exactly — clear crystal, silvered leading edges, rainbow fringes at the thin margins, stored light glowing deep at the root — because a vane and a hull vane are the same grown crystal in the same material culture.

They are jointed with tendon and cartilage at the root and along each vane, grown, no hardware. They furl at the bench: the fan folds back into a compact stack along the spine, out of the way of the arms, and the middle vane's leading edge stays aimed at the workpiece. That leading edge is a lens edge, and it focuses light onto the work — the work light, a clean bright spot on the bench, never from the mouth.

The sails are for two things, in the being's own terms. They are how a Glasswright eats light: the vanes gather it, the body banks it, the glow stores it. And they are how a Glasswright aims light: the lens edge is the precision instrument of the craft, the thing that lets a weaver set a lens or fuse a vane edge without a tool. At the bench they are furled and working; away from the bench they open partway to drink light and to sense it, because the vanes are also light-sense organs. A Glasswright in open space stands with its fan half-open, reading the light around it.

And they are how a Glasswright grieves. The species watched its own machine kill its sun. The sails still open toward where the sun was, by habit older than the loss, and the gesture is the faction's whole grief in one motion: the hand held out to the light that should be there. That is on-motif and it is not decoration — it is what the organ is for, at the deepest layer of the culture.

4. Variation inside one species

Variation lives in the sail plan and the depth of glass infusion, never in different anatomy. Every Glasswright is six-limbed, segmented-thoraxed, flush-lensed, reed-grilled, and glowing from the trunk. What changes is the fan and where the glass runs deepest.

Workers at the bench. Compact, furled fan, dense glass in the working arms and the brow, deep stored light in the abdomen. The working posture is the species' default: planted, low, four arms busy, one edge-beam on the work.

The age spectrum. A Glasswright lives three to four hundred years, and age shows as glass.

Young: flesh paler and more translucent, glass sparse and fine, fan small and simply jointed, stored light shallow and bright, eyes a clear bright green. They are mostly soft.

Mature: glass densifies along stress and wear paths, the fan grows and gains joints, the brow ridge thickens, stored light deepens and slows. They are half glass.

Old: the glass blooms. Crystal growth extends past the stress paths into bloom edges along the vanes — ornamental in look, functional in fact, more lens edge and more light-gather. The flesh thins and clarifies, the fan is large and bloom-edged and visibly worn (chipped margins, clouded tips, silver worn to soft grey), and the stored light is very deep and slow, like a coal bed. They are more glass than flesh and still wholly organic. This is where the old end of the species is allowed to look like the Bloomed direction: it is the same substance, further along.

The admirals. The four named admirals are the same species and the same substance, and each reads as one organism at a glance because the anatomy and the material story never change. They differ in the fan and the glass depth:

Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (wing admiral): the broadest, cleanest fan, wide clear aperture edges built for fleet-wide light signaling and formation work; deep glass in the working arms; the largest sails of the four.

Saro of Long Focus (extreme-range lance admiral): long, narrow, lance-like vanes with a single long-focus edge — a light-focusing instrument for extreme range; lean body, long precision arms, unusually clear lenses.

Vell of the Third Mirror (duellist): small, tight paired vanes with one silvered mirror edge; high glass density in the precision arms; built for close work and light-deception at short range.

Brin of the Fourth Lens (ward admiral): broad, layered, overlapping vanes like stacked lenses, heavy and dense, built to gather and throw light as a shield; the thickest glass of the four.

None of these is a different species. Each is a sail plan and a glass depth on the same body.

The ancient elder. The full weaver belongs here and nowhere else. At the far end of a long life, the thorax and abdomen elongate, additional support-limb pairs grow in, and the being settles into an eight-point stance — the full spider carriage that is too monstrous for the base and exactly right for an elder. The fan becomes huge, bloom-edged, and worn; the flesh clarifies to near-pure glass; the stored light is a small slow sun in the trunk. The elder is not a monster but a living bench and a living record — revered, slow, mostly glass, the far end of the same gradient. The base species never reaches this form; it is the age spectrum's last room.

5. Life and craft

Work. Glasswrights are optical engineers and glasswrights: they weave glass thread, focus light, and record light. The spinnerets draw the thread; the sails gather and aim the light; the glass banks the record. At the bench a Glasswright is weaving a lens, a vane, a hull plate, or a record of light, and the same four hands and one edge-beam do all of it. Their hulls record battle light as a replayable record and the forge reads the replay; at body scale the deep glow is the same fact, a life held in glass.

Clothing. Minimal, because the body is the uniform and clothing would cover the glass. At the bench they wear a work-apron of their own woven glass thread, and tool sashes of silver and crystal. For ceremony, a formal sash of silver and clear crystal. The fan is always free. The glass is what a Glasswright wears.

Movement and rest. They move low, wide, and deliberate, on the support limbs with the four arms held ready, the fan half-open in open space and furled indoors. At the bench they plant and go still. They rest by furling the fan, dimming the stored light, and clouding the glass slightly — a resting Glasswright goes quiet and dark, support limbs locked, and sleeps planted. They eat food and they drink light; they breathe; they bleed a pale luminous blood. They are warm, fleshy, organic beings, and the glass is grown in them, not worn on them.

6. Design bans and how this design meets them

No human, grey alien, or elf base. The body is six-limbed, segmented-thoraxed, tripod-stanced, with flush grown lenses and a reed grille. The anatomy itself is alien.

No vertical eye or lens stacks, bead-eye rings, brow magnifiers, or secondary eye arcs. There are exactly two lens eyes, one segmented brow, one flush reed grille.

No steampunk or hardware. Every joint is tendon and cartilage, grown. No aperture rims, brass, brackets, or loupes anywhere.

No grille as a held tube. The grille is flush reeds set into the face.

No literal naval sails or masts. The sails are grown body vanes and working light rigs, furled at the bench, never rigging.

No crystal construct bodies. The being is warm, fleshy, and organic; the glass is grown within living tissue.

No colour-as-language. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell, never speech.

No borrowed motifs. The design stays inside light, glass, and grief: no archive or uploaded-being imagery, no song or choir language, no data or Registry language, no oath or dawn imagery, no trophy or scrap imagery. The sails, the glow, and the craft are Glasswright facts at body scale and at fleet scale, one material culture from the bench to the hull.
