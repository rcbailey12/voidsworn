Ideation only — candidate substance systems, challenges, and risks

Candidate substance systems (one-system first)

These treat glass, flesh, and light as a single continuous biology rather than adjacent materials.

Living optical continuum (primary candidate)
Flesh is a semi-translucent, living silica-gel matrix that continuously secretes, reabsorbs, and refracts glass. Glass is not "grown on" or "woven into" the body; it is the densified, ordered phase of the same tissue. Light is both energy source and structural memory: photons trapped and replayed inside the matrix stiffen or soften regions on demand. Everywhere the same gradient exists — soft, veined, light-storing gel → denser crystalline facets → pure focusing edges. Face lenses, spinneret threads, sail vanes, and internal "bones" are simply different crystallisation densities of one substance.
Photonic lattice flesh
The body is a living photonic crystal. Organic cells arrange themselves into periodic refractive lattices that trap and guide light the way a fibre-optic cable does. "Glass" is the ordered lattice state; "flesh" is the disordered, vascularised state of the same cells. Emotion colours the lattice by changing packing density (involuntary). Spinnerets extrude lattice filaments that the body can later reabsorb or lock. Sails are large, low-density lattice sheets that can be collapsed by flooding the lattice with disordered cells.
Secretory silicate biology
Every surface continuously secretes a thin silicate film that polymerises under light into optical glass. The film is living: it carries nutrients, nerves, and light-memory. Thickened films become lenses, vanes, or structural plates; thin films remain translucent skin. Joints stay flexible because the film is kept deliberately under-polymerised there. Wrist spinnerets are specialised secretory glands that can spin the film into continuous thread before it hardens. The entire organism is one continuous secretion surface.
Gradient refractor
From core to surface the body is a single refractive-index gradient, like a living GRIN lens. Soft, light-storing interior → intermediate translucent flesh → outer crystalline epidermis. Eyes, sail edges, and spinneret tips are simply local maxima of the gradient. No separate "glass organs"; the body itself is the optical system. Age deepens the gradient (more extreme index difference), producing larger, more faceted sails and denser internal glow.
Challenges to the pack's assumptions and carry-forwards
"Pale, half-translucent flesh with glass grown and woven into the body" still risks reading as two materials (flesh + glass additions). The designer must collapse that language so a reader never sees "flesh, then glass." Any residual "grown into" phrasing will recreate the bolted-on problem the verdict rejected.
Four-arm / spinneret / "little spider in the carriage" is useful craft logic but carries strong arachnid silhouette risk. The pack already notes full spider body is monster territory. Even a modest low-wide stance + segmented thorax can tip into "spider person" if the support limbs are too leg-like or the thorax too distinct. The substance system must make the segmentation feel like optical segmentation (faceted bands of denser lattice) rather than arthropod plates.
Sails as "grown crystal vanes that echo the ships" is strong, but the practical/modest/furling requirement fights the visual drama that made Prism Sails attractive in the first place. If the sails are too modest they may disappear in silhouette; if they retain any manta/cloak drama they risk looking like display wings again. The substance system needs a clear mechanical reason the vanes exist (light-focusing work surfaces that can be collapsed by changing lattice order) so they never feel optional decoration.
Face rules are extremely tight (exactly two forward deep-green lens eyes, heavy segmented brow, flush reed grille). This is good for consistency, but the "segmented brow" and "reed grille" both risk reading as hardware or insect mouthparts unless the substance system makes segmentation and reeds feel like natural crystallisation fronts and respiratory/optical channels of the same matrix.
Age and admiral variety living only in sail plan + glass depth is clean, but three-to-four-hundred-year lives imply cumulative optical scarring, internal light-record density, and possible partial resorption of older vanes. The pack under-specifies how age actually changes the continuum beyond "larger, bloom-edged, worn." Without that, elders risk becoming the same body with bigger sails.
"Working glasswrights shown at their craft" is correct, but the design must still read as a species, not as a job costume. If every image shows them at the bench with spinnerets active, the resting/moving form may be under-designed.
Risks the designer should actively avoid
Reintroducing any second material story (crystal head on flesh body, membrane sails, metallic joints, aperture rims). Even subtle "glass inlaid into flesh" language will fail the holistic test.
Letting the low-wide weaver stance + support limbs + four arms produce a silhouette that reads as "spider-centaur" or "arachnid artisan." The carriage must feel like an optical organism that happens to work low to the bench, not a spider that happens to have a glasswright face.
Making the reed grille look like teeth, a pipe, a moustache, or a held tube. It must be flush continuous surface of the same matrix, with the reeds as natural channels.
Over-emphasising the sails so they dominate the silhouette or look like wings/cloaks. They are work tools that furl; when furled they should read as folded crystalline ridges or collapsed lattice, not stowed fabric.
Allowing any steampunk, jeweller, or camera visual language to creep back through "precision" cues on the secondary arms or brow.
Treating colour-in-glass as communicative in any way, even subtly. It is only an involuntary physiological tell.
Designing the ancient-elder fuller spider form first and then "toning it down." The base species must stand alone; the elder is an extreme expression of the same continuum, not the source of the design.
Leaving the internal light-memory (optical recording) purely decorative. If the substance system includes trapped light as structural or metabolic, that should visibly affect how the body glows, stiffens, or records under stress — otherwise it becomes another bolted-on faction motif.

Strongest path appears to be a single living optical continuum (candidate 1 or 2) in which every part is a different density or order state of the same light-responsive silicate matrix. That forces face, arms, spinnerets, thorax, and sails to be derived rather than assembled.
