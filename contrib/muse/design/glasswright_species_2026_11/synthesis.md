# Chair synthesis: Glasswright species design (Voidsworn issue #48)

Chair: Rey. Legs: blind Rey (written from the pack only, before any other leg was read), DeepSeek (writer), Qwen (writer), Flash (ideation), Grok (ideation). Pack: PACK.md in this folder. The final design is design.md in this folder.

## Verdict

GO. The five legs converge on one substance system with unusual strength, and every hard constraint in the pack is met by the chaired design. The three-substances problem Ryan ruled on is solved the same way by all three writer legs independently: one living silicate tissue whose local state, not a second material, explains flesh, glass, and light everywhere on the body. The remaining disagreements are anatomical details, settled below with reasons.

## The substance system (chair ruling)

Adopted: the tissue is **lumen-flesh** (name from the blind Rey leg), and its state at any point is set by three axes (mechanics from the Qwen leg): crystallinity (how ordered the lattice is), hydration (how much living water the tissue holds), and light-saturation (how much stored light it carries). DeepSeek's "vitreous flesh" and Grok's "living optical continuum" describe the same system in other words; the chair treats them as corroboration, not rivals. Flash's three candidate systems are compressed variants of the same gradient and were used as ideation only, per its seat.

Why this wins: it is the only formulation that derives every required feature without residue. Lens eyes are the tissue at maximum crystallinity. Joints are the tissue at intermediate crystallinity and high hydration. Sails are the tissue thinnest and most ordered. The silver on body vanes and ship vanes is fibre that has fused, died back from the living matrix, and turned opaque silver-white (blind Rey leg), which explains the fleet palette with no second material. Stored light is the tissue's metabolic reserve and its memory at once, which keeps the faction's optical-memory lore true at body scale.

## Conflict dispositions

1. **Support limbs: four, not two.** Qwen and the blind Rey leg independently give four support limbs (two forward, two aft) under a segmented thorax. DeepSeek gives two support limbs plus the abdomen as a tripod. Chair rules four: it delivers the pack's "little spider in the carriage" honestly (eight limbs total, four arms and four supports), and two of three writer legs landed there without seeing each other.
2. **Arm order: working pair upper, precision pair lower.** Qwen and the blind Rey leg agree. DeepSeek reverses them. Chair rules upper working, lower precision: the precision hands work closest to the bench surface where the fine placement happens.
3. **Hands and spinnerets.** Working hands: four fingers and an opposed thumb, glass-capped fingertips (all three writers agree in substance). Precision hands: five slender digits (Qwen, blind Rey). Spinnerets sit at all four wrists, largest at the precision wrists (DeepSeek's every-wrist spread merged with the other legs' precision-wrist focus). Mechanism, answering the Flash challenge: stored light released as metabolic heat softens the tissue inside the wrist channel, the strand is drawn and cools in air within a second (Qwen). No external heat, no tool.
4. **Sails: four to six vanes, furl back, one focusing vane stays out.** Count and fan arrangement from Qwen (two primaries, two to four secondaries, layered). Furl direction: folded back along the thorax (Qwen, DeepSeek). At the bench, everything furls except the leading vane on the dominant side, angled forward and down, its focusing edge throwing the light line onto the work (Qwen). The blind Rey leg's full forward canopy over the bench is rejected for work on the Flash collision challenge (vanes sweeping over the head crowd the arms and the workpiece), but its sleep canopy is adopted: at rest the sails fold forward over the settled body. Away from the bench the vanes gather light and vent heat (Qwen).
5. **Voice.** The vocal cavity shapes speech and the flush reed grille is its outlet, with a faint harmonic from the reeds (Qwen, blind Rey). This answers the Flash challenge directly: the reeds do not generate the voice, so the voice stays a normal speaking voice.
6. **Age ceiling: the pack governs.** Lifespan is three to four hundred years. Bands from Qwen: youth to about sixty, working maturity to about two hundred and fifty, elders to four hundred, with bloom-edged, worn sails and deepening glow. The ancient spider form lives at the far end of the elder band, past roughly three hundred and fifty years (blind Rey): elongated rear segments, the full eight-point stance, huge bloom-edged sails. Qwen's ancient elders at five to six hundred years are rejected: its own age section and the pack both cap the lifespan at four hundred, and the chair does not extend a ruled lifespan on one leg's say-so.
7. **Admirals.** Sail-plan identities from Qwen, the richest of the three, checked against the faction package. Tamsa: six clean vanes, maximum clarity, the youngest admiral at ninety (package). Saro: four vanes, the two primaries long and narrow with the finest focusing edge, deepest green lenses. Vell: five vanes, asymmetric, port side heavier; the chair merges the blind Rey leg's silver mirror panel on her left primary vane with Qwen's combat-fractured scatter. Brin: six broad, cupped vanes with concave geometry that spreads light instead of focusing it, the most silver in the lattice. Genders follow the faction package where it speaks: Tamsa she, Saro he, Vell she. The package never gives Brin a pronoun; Qwen's "his" for Brin is noted but not adopted, and the design writes Brin by name. Flagged for Ryan as a one-word ruling whenever he wants it.
8. **Admiral presence.** Flash challenged the low carriage against a commanding portrait silhouette. Chair ruling, from Qwen: there is no upright stance. A Glasswright standing on two limbs is unstable and rare. Admirals command settled, and presence reads through sail plan, glow, and brow. This is a feature of the species, not a gap, and the concept sheet is drawn to it.

## Ideation dispositions

Grok (ideation, adopted in part): segmentation must read as density bands of the one tissue, never arthropod plates; the chair design states this outright. Age is cumulative record density and scarring, not just bigger sails; adopted (bloom as centuries of fusing, micro-fracturing, and re-fusing, from the blind Rey leg). The sails needed a mechanical reason to exist; the design gives three (focus, gather, vent). Grok's warning against designing the elder first and toning it down was honored: the base was chaired first, the elder derived last.

Flash (ideation only, per its seat since Nov 6): its three challenges (voice versus grille, spinneret mechanism, admiral silhouette) were all put to the writer legs' texts and are answered in rulings 3, 5, and 8. Two of its framings are flagged as not in the pack: it describes the spinnerets as weaving "onto wooden frames" (the pack names no frame material) and presents "furling forward over the head" as a pack claim (the pack says only that sails furl at the bench; the forward fold came from the blind Rey leg and is ruled on above). Its "quartz-chitin" system name is rejected: chitin is an arthropod signal and works against the silhouette ruling. Nothing from Flash is treated as evidence, per the standing rule for its seat.
