## 2. The owner's verdict, in full

The verdict text in the issue block above is the ruling. The companion brief written the same day (glasswright_inspiration_for_opus.md, section 6 below) states the core point this way:

"In the concepts, the head is cut crystal block, the body is veined translucent flesh, and the sails are webbed membrane: three substances bolted together. In the real design, decide the one substance system first (how glass, flesh, and light relate everywhere on the body) and derive the face, limbs, and sails from it, so an admiral reads as one organism at a glance."

Two execution lessons from the same day's record, both binding on this pass:

- Glasswright visuals must read alien in the anatomy and body plan itself, not a human or grey alien wearing glass effects. Alien anatomy plus a real face is the target: not faceless, and not human-with-decoration.
- Recurring failure to design against: the voice grille kept rendering as a glass tube held in the mouth. It must sit flush in the face.

## 3. What the Nov 2 concept run tried (record, compressed)

All images are in ~/workspace/voidsworn-review/concepts/ (paths in section 6).

1. Direction portraits v1 (Threaded, Inlaid, Bloomed: same admiral, only the glass placement varies). REJECTED by Ryan: "They're all basically human with a weird head dress. That's the problem with all of these designs. They're all human. Let's make it actually alien." Failed on anatomy, not on glass placement.
2. Direction portraits v2 (same three directions, alien anatomy drivers: elongated skull, long neck, concentric lens ring eyes, faceted bone under translucent skin). REJECTED: still read as a little green or grey guy made elfish or human. Ryan directed Rey to think much different and more ambitious.
3. Radical body plans v3: Bellwright (living glass bell, spiral lens ring, aperture), Armillary (grown glass rings around an organic core, no head), Prism Sails (cathedral glass sail fans grown from the back, low wide head, horizontal lens band, twin glass throat reeds). Ryan took the Prism Sails framing, noting it aligns with the faction ships' crystal vanes.
4. Prism Sails five-option sheet: 1 Cathedral Twin Sails, 2 Schooner Rig, 3 Manta Sailcloak, 4 Lattice Rig, 5 Bloom Sail Elder. Reads at the time: Schooner Rig too literal naval (cloth sails on a mast), Lattice Rig too construct and Eclipse, Cathedral vanes slightly ear-like. Ryan picked option 3, Manta Sailcloak, with conditions: the head is not good; the sails should be slightly less prominent and more practical; these are working glasswrights, so the sails are working light-focusing rigs that furl at the bench, not display wings. The face of option 4 was maybe closest but still needed rework.
5. Head tests (Keel Crest, Lens Turret) were overtaken and sit unused. Face reworks on the option 3 body: R1 Lens Stack (reads as jeweller's loupes, grille as a moustache), R2 Aperture Brow (reads as steampunk camera goggles). Ryan rejected the vertical eye and lens stack motif outright. Simplification refinements: A (two eyes plus a brow magnifier lens) cut, because the brow lens reads as a monocle and the mouth tube as a pipe; B (Two Eyes Only: faceted crystal head, deep green lens pair, stern segmented brow) was the closest face yet. Open fixes on B at the time: even out the eye pair, shrink the grille so it sits flush, move the work light beam off the mouth to the sail edge so the mouth is only for speaking.
6. Spider evolutions off B, at Ryan's ask to evolve it more alien, almost spider-like: S1 Spinneret Craftsman (B's face kept, four arms, wrist spinnerets weaving glass thread onto the workpiece), S2 Spider Brow (secondary eye arc rendered as black rivets, cut; mouthparts dangled like claws), S3 Weaver Stance (full spider body in an eight-point stance over the bench, sails folded down the back; striking but crosses from admiral to monster, ancient-elder material only). Ryan ruled S1 is the closest, with a little of S3 folded in.
7. Composite C1, generated on that ruling: S1's face, four arms and spinneret weaving, plus S3's low wide weaver stance, segmented thorax, planted support limbs and sails folded back. It rendered strongly; the grille finally sits flush, though it reads slightly like teeth. A stronger elder-dose variant (composite C2) could not be generated; only its generation record survives (see section 6).
8. Final verdict on the whole run: INSPIRATION ONLY, not a locked species base (section 2 above). The earlier question of a portrait base (Threaded, with Bloomed of interest for the old end) stays open on those terms and is superseded as a framing by this holistic pass.

## 4. Standing lore constraints (binding)

Faction identity:
- The Glasswrights, data id ithar. They are the Starwrights who stayed flesh: the Lattice's own optical engineers, who tried to stop the Upload, failed, and watched their machine kill their sun. They live three to four hundred years, so only two or three generations separate them from the people who built the light. They carry two guilts, the sun and the Fleshwrights, their splinter. They are markedly less estranged from their Starwright roots than Eclipse, who uploaded.
- They are organic beings who evolved to shape glass. Not glass people, not statues, not constructs. Pale, half-translucent flesh with glass grown and woven into the body.
- Their glass is optical memory in the fiction: Glasswright glass records light at full fidelity (their hulls record battle light as a replayable record, not telemetry), and the forge reads the replay. Stored light glowing deep inside the body and the ships is the same fact at body scale.
- Speech and expression rulings: Glasswrights speak normally, in a normal voice. Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell only, never a language. They have real faces: an early "faceless" framing was an overshoot and was corrected by the owner; do not design a faceless being.
- The four named admirals the design must accommodate: Tamsa of the Clear Aperture (wing admiral), Saro of Long Focus (extreme-range lance admiral), Vell of the Third Mirror (duellist), Brin of the Fourth Lens (ward admiral). Variety across admirals and ages lives in the sail plan and the depth of glass infusion, not in different species.

Motif ownership (no faction may borrow another's motifs):
- Glasswrights (ithar): light, glass, grief. Glass and reflection belong to them.
- Eclipse: memory, archive, the lost sun. Crystal construct bodies and uploaded-being imagery are Eclipse drift; keep out.
- Chorus: taste, heat, smell, teeth. No song or choir words anywhere in the project.
- Hegemony: data, the Registry. Sunward Crusade: oath, dawn, burning. Ashfall Hullbreakers: violence, trophies, scrap.

Ships and the naval rule:
- Glasswright ships: clear crystal and silver hulls, faceted edges splitting light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes, stored light glowing deep inside. Palette: clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, pale green-white stored light. The body's sail vanes echo the ships' crystal and silver vanes; the being and the fleet are one material culture.
- Project fiction rule: these are space ships, never naval. No keels, masts, or funnels on ships, and by the same ruling no literal naval sails on a mast on the body. The sails in this design are grown body vanes and working light rigs, not rigging.

## 5. Hard constraints checklist (from the verdict and the drop list)

- One substance system, decided first, stated explicitly, and visibly governing every body part. No part may be a different material story from the rest.
- Face: two forward grown lens eyes, set side by side, deep green, flush in the flesh. Lids and a heavy segmented brow carry expression. A short reed grille set flush low in the face is the speaking mouth. Speech is a normal voice.
- No vertical eye or lens stacks, no rings of identical bead eyes, no extra brow magnifier lenses, no secondary eye arcs.
- No human, grey alien, or elf base, however dressed up. The anatomy and body plan themselves read alien.
- No steampunk or hardware anywhere: no camera aperture rims, brass fittings, brackets, jeweller's loupes. Joints are tendon and cartilage, grown.
- Four arms: two working, two precision. Wrist spinnerets draw and weave glass thread onto the workpiece. A ship's sail vane is the biggest thing they weave.
- Carriage: a little spider in it. Segmented thorax, low wide weaver stance, support limbs. Full spider body is too monstrous for the base and is reserved as a possible ancient elder form only.
- Sails: grown crystal vanes, practical and modest, jointed, furling at the bench; one edge focuses light onto the work. Working rigs, not display.
- Colour in the glass is an involuntary emotion tell, never speech.

## 7. Leg prompt

You are designing a species for a space opera game. Using only the evidence in this pack, write the complete Glasswright species design as a single written design document. Follow these instructions exactly:

1. Decide the ONE substance system first, and open the document with it: how glass, flesh, and light relate everywhere on this body, as one material and one biology. Every later section must be derived from that system, and the document must show the derivation, not just assert it. If a reader covers any single part (face, hands, sails), the same substance story must explain it.
2. Anatomy, derived from the system: the whole body plan and how it reads alien in itself; the carriage (segmented thorax, low wide weaver stance, support limbs, a little spider in it, without crossing into monster); the face (two forward grown lens eyes set side by side, deep green, flush in the flesh; lids and a heavy segmented brow carrying expression; a short reed grille set flush low in the face as the speaking mouth; normal voice; colour in the glass as an involuntary emotion tell); the four arms (two working, two precision) and the wrist spinnerets that draw and weave glass thread onto the workpiece.
3. The sails: grown crystal vanes echoing the faction ships' clear crystal and silver vanes, derived from the same substance system. Practical and modest, jointed with tendon and cartilage, furling at the bench, with one edge that focuses light onto the work. Explain what they are for, at the bench and away from it, in the being's own terms.
4. Variation inside one species: workers at the bench; the age spectrum across a three-to-four-hundred-year life (what deepens, blooms, or wears with age, and where variety is allowed to live: the sail plan, larger, bloom-edged, and worn for elders); admirals, who must read as one organism at a glance and as individuals (address how the four named admirals in this pack could differ without becoming different species); and the ancient elder, including whether and how the fuller spider body belongs there and nowhere else.
5. Life and craft: how the substance system shapes their work as optical engineers and glasswrights (weaving, focusing, recording light), their clothing or lack of it, and how they move and rest. Keep every detail inside the motif ownership and rulings in this pack.
6. Hard bans, no exceptions: no human, grey alien, or elf base; no vertical eye or lens stacks; no rings of identical bead eyes; no brow magnifier lenses; no secondary eye arcs; no steampunk or hardware of any kind; no grille as a held tube; no literal naval sails or masts; no crystal construct bodies; no colour-as-language; no borrowed motifs from other factions.

Write it as a design document another artist or writer could build from without asking questions: concrete, specific, committed. Do not present a menu of alternatives for the core design; make the calls. Do not refer to this pack, the issue, or the process. Produce the complete design as your reply, with no preamble or plan.
