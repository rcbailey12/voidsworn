## Part 1: the nebula look

### The pick: bake the volume, run the parallax

All four seated legs independently rejected raymarched volumetrics and landed on layered decks below the board. They differed on where the volume comes from. The chair adopts the Qwen leg's answer as the core, because it is the only one that solves this game's actual problem: Voidsworn is turn-based, so the camera is still while the player thinks, and every motion cue (drift, parallax) is silent exactly then. Shading has to carry the volume in a still frame. Qwen's line, adopted as the design's first principle: shading makes the claim, motion confirms it.

The recipe:

1. Offline bake (the volume is real, once). Author a density volume (fBm billow plus flow-warped filaments). Render it straight down with a short raymarch and ONE directional key light at a fixed azimuth, the same azimuth the scene's key light uses. Output a 2048-square two-channel texture per cloud family: R = integrated density (extinction), G = scattered light including self-shadow and the key-side rim. Dust lanes are authored inside the volume as high-density, low-albedo regions, so a lane comes out of the bake dark with a lit edge, with no separate multiply deck and no sorting hazards (Gemini and DeepSeek both wanted a dedicated dust deck; the bake gives the same read for free and removes a blend layer). Three shared atlases serve all biomes; biomes differ by colour LUT, scale, drift rate and key colour temperature, not by new bakes. Storms reuse the nebula stack with an ember-heavy LUT and faster drift.
2. Runtime decks (the parallax is cheap). Four horizontal decks per cloud field, all below the board except the last: a deep floor, two body decks at different depths, and (Ultra preset only) one wisp deck just above the board at alpha 0.06 or less. Deck depths are set as fractions of camera height, so the parallax ratio survives zooming instead of flattening at full zoom-out (Qwen). Drift directions stay within about 25 degrees of one flow axis per field, at different speeds per deck, so the field reads as one body slipping against itself, not as sliding wallpaper (DeepSeek's named fear, answered by the shared flow axis and by the bake carrying the still read). A second, slower sample of the same texture multiplies into the density so edges slowly shred and reform (boil period 90 to 180 seconds).
3. Composite (the colour is a lookup, never a blend accident). Premultiplied alpha over, colour driven by a 256-step palette LUT indexed by density: extinct near-black slate, slate teal in the mid densities, smoke grey in the lit body, bone at the brightest baked light, ember ONLY on the top sliver of the light channel (the rims). The LUT is where the palette law is enforced: hue windows for teal and ember plus neutrals, and the ember ramp runs rust to bone and skips the 40 to 55 hue band entirely, because that band is where mustard lives (Qwen). This also answers Gemini's biggest risk, mud: because colour comes from a LUT rather than from adding teal and ember light together, overlaps cannot drift through olive or brown in linear space.
4. Contact shadows. One soft multiply quad per ship on the near body deck, alpha about 0.20, radius about 0.6 hex (Qwen; roughly 0.02 ms for twenty ships). This is the cheapest depth cue in the plan and the one that ties the ships to the cloud instead of leaving them floating over a rug (Gemini's phrase for the failure).
5. Edge and outline. The cloud's own edge is a warped falloff fading over 4 to 8 hex widths of world space; there is no hex-shaped edge anywhere in the art. The gameplay outline stays a separate board-plane decal, crisp, constant screen width (about 1.5 px), brighter when a move or target preview touches the patch (Qwen). Crisp geometry inside a soft body is the R247 read, and the two edges are allowed to disagree: the cloud spills, the outline rules. Hard rule from the Qwen leg, adopted verbatim in substance: visual drift never moves the gameplay footprint or the outline, or players will read a moving hazard.

### Cost

Every leg's estimate lands in the same band: about 0.5 to 1.1 ms at 4K on the RTX 5080, against the 12.5 ms frame budget. The cost is blended fill (overdraw), not shader math: four decks is roughly 4x screen overdraw, two texture fetches and one LUT fetch per fragment. The chair sets a hard cap of 2 ms for the whole nebula system (DeepSeek) and adopts DeepSeek's composite as the standard rendering path below Ultra: render the deck stack into a half-resolution SubViewport and composite once under the board, which roughly halves the fill cost and is safe because the cloud is low-frequency and entirely below the board (Qwen agrees; a plain bilinear upsample suffices, no depth-aware upsample). Preset ladder (consolidated): Ultra, four decks full-res with the wisp; High, three decks full-res; Medium, three decks in the half-res buffer; Low, two decks half-res, drift only; Minimal, one pre-composited beauty card with UV drift. Degradation principle (Qwen, adopted): lose motion depth first, never lose the baked shading; the Minimal card still has self-shadow and dust lanes, so it still reads as a cloud in a still screenshot.

### Readability, stated as measurable floors

DeepSeek's objection is adopted as a requirement, not a caveat: this design is not done when it is pretty, it is done when it passes numbers.

- Draw order is the guarantee: sky, cloud decks, board-plane decals (grid, patch outline, contact shadows), ships, the Ultra wisp, then markers, selection and UI as an overlay pass with depth test off (Qwen, Gemini).
- Silhouette floor: peak cloud luminance capped at 0.55 linear under the board while hull key highlights run to 1.0 or more; target at least 3:1 silhouette-to-local-cloud luminance (Qwen).
- Grid: board plane, constant screen-space width, a light core over a slightly wider dark casing (DeepSeek's dual stroke, Qwen's casing in the same shape). The casing is local to the line, not a scrim; the no-scrim ruling (R344) stands.
- Markers and selection: overlay pass, light fill with a near-black keyline, immune to the backdrop by construction (Qwen, Gemini).
- Acceptance test for the bake (Qwen, adopted): freeze all drift, take a 4K still over a nebula patch, and ask five people "cloud or backdrop?" Four of five say cloud, or the bake is redone. Layer count is never the fix for a weak bake.
- Acceptance test for the wisp deck: five players call out ship class through it at gameplay zoom. It ships only on a pass, Ultra only, and it never enters the half-res buffer.

### What the pictures should show (for Codex, per R451)

One backdrop study per cloud family (nebula, storm), drawn as a game screenshot at default zoom: a fleet of mixed ship classes over a cloud field that exits the frame on at least two sides, dust lanes crossing under the fleet, one lane's lit edge visible, the hex grid and a nebula patch outline drawn over the cloud, contact shadows under the ships. A second study at full zoom-out to check the overdraw worst case and the whole-field read. No purple, no mustard, fewer stars than feels natural.

## Part 2: biome ambience plan

### The shared architecture (all six beds)

The council converged hard here: every leg chose the nebula bed first, every leg put beds on their own bus under the mix being built in issue 2034, and every leg ducked the bed under combat instead of letting it compete. The chair consolidates the strongest version of each piece:

- Bus shape (Qwen, adopted): Ambience splits at a 150 Hz crossover into Bed_Low and Bed_Mid, plus a separate Character sub-bus for sparse one-shots. Bed_Mid carries the combat duck (a compressor keyed from the combat bus); Bed_Low is ducked lightly or not at all, so the weight stays while the masking band clears. Character sounds are suppressible outright, not just ducked, and a capital death takes their voice. This fits inside issue 2034's channel work as one more bus family; it does not change the stem plan.
- Duck numbers (DeepSeek's global curve as the base, Gemini's per-biome deltas folded in): Bed_Mid drops about 6 dB under sustained combat (attack about 150 ms, release about 2 s), Character drops 12 dB or mutes, and objective urgency thins the bed a further 3 dB and suppresses character sounds, so the music's urgency feed (issue 2034) carries tension while the bed gets quieter, never busier. A capital death ducks the whole bed about 10 dB with a hold of about 1.5 s and a slow release, joining the near-silence beat the sound playbook already specifies for capital deaths.
- The anti-rain contract (Qwen, adopted as the acceptance spec for every bed): whole-bed spectral centroid at or below 400 Hz; no sustained energy above 3 kHz; no layer with grain density above 3 events per second; every character sound has an attack of at least 300 ms. "Sounds like rain" is now four numbers a bed can be measured against in an analyser instead of argued about, which is exactly the test the rejected nebula bed would have failed.
- A shared floor (Qwen): a very low "your own ship is alive" hum sits under all six biomes at the same level. Biome identity is what changes on top of it.
- What the chair rejected: Gemini's urgency pitch shifts (station turbine up 1.5 semitones, dead star pulse tightening). Gemini itself named this its least-sure decision: unless the bed and the battle music share a tonal root, a shifting bed rubs against the score. Tension in this plan comes from thinning and ducking only.

### The six beds

Each bed: one-line description, layers, and its duck character. All follow the shared architecture above; only the differences are noted.

- Nebula. Line: a cathedral-sized breath of warm gas, a low pressure hum that leans in and out (Qwen). Layers: base of two low tones (about 44 and 66 Hz) plus band-limited noise lowpassed at 220 Hz; a swell layer at 60 to 140 Hz breathing on a slow LFO, rounded, never peaking; a character "gas fold" every 50 to 100 seconds, several seconds long, mostly sub with one faint bowed partial. Loops of mismatched length (about 89 and 97 seconds) so the combined pattern does not repeat inside a session. Build this bed FIRST (unanimous): it is the approval gate, since the owner has already rejected one nebula bed, and it is median on every anti-rain axis, so the other five derive from it.
- Asteroid field. Line: enormous rocks turning slowly in the dark, deep stone groans and one hollow low knock that arrives from far away (Qwen). Layers: base tones about 38 and 57 Hz with a bandpassed noise band at 90 to 160 Hz reading as mass and cavity; a bowed stone-grind swell every 40 to 75 seconds; one sub-thud every 35 to 70 seconds with a slow onset and a long dark decay, never two within 20 seconds of each other (gravel is patter, Qwen). The blind leg rated its own rock knocks its least-sure piece; the spacing and onset rules above are the discipline that keeps them, and the fallback is cutting them for pure quiet.
- Dead star. Line: the silence after something enormous died, one vast low tone that barely moves, with a cold metallic ring far above it that never resolves (Qwen). Layers: the emptiest bed, a 28 to 32 Hz base with a faint 90 Hz partial; a collapse-breath swell every 70 to 120 seconds, a slow two-semitone glide down and back; one bowed inharmonic metal bar every 90 to 150 seconds, bowed never struck, because a struck bell reads as a UI cue (Qwen, and the owner's electronic-cue dislike backs it). At urgency the bed tightens spectrally (the 90 Hz partial rises slightly) instead of ducking further. This is the finale biome, so it gets the most composed bed and the deepest death duck (about 12 dB).
- Planet orbit. Line: a world turning underneath you, a broad warm low drone with one slow harmonic shift as you cross the terminator (Qwen). Layers: base of root, fifth and octave tones (about 50, 75, 100 Hz), gently chorused, lowpassed at 500 Hz; the terminator crossfade, 30 to 45 seconds between two harmonic states every 60 to 90 seconds, the only bed with harmonic movement; one limb tone every 60 to 110 seconds, a bowed air-column sound with no transient. Explicitly no filtered-noise sweeps: this is the highest weather-risk bed (Gemini and Qwen agree), and ionosphere wind is banned by the anti-rain contract anyway. It masks the most of any bed, so it takes the deepest combat duck on Bed_Mid (about 8 dB).
- Station. Line: being inside something built, a low structural hum from machinery you cannot see, and now and then the far-off weight of the thing settling on its frame (Qwen). Layers: base structural drone about 45 and 90 Hz from bowed metal and vent resonance, lowpassed at 400 Hz, with a slow load-cycle amplitude movement; a load-cycle swell every 45 to 80 seconds; one structural settle (a low metal groan, half-second onset, slight downward bend) every 40 to 80 seconds. No clang, no servo, no beep: this is the highest "sounds electronic" risk bed (Qwen), and Gemini's docking thumps are adopted only in their suppressed form, character sounds on the station bed mute entirely while the Effects bus is hot, so a thump is never mistaken for incoming fire (Gemini).
- Deep space. Line: almost nothing, a wide cold very low floor tone with so little movement that you notice it only when it stops (Qwen). Layers: base tones about 30 and 45 Hz, quieter than the nebula base; a swell that is effectively none (the absence of movement is the identity, the audio mirror of the fewer-stars ruling); a character partial every 120 to 200 seconds, or nothing. At urgency the bed does not duck; its lowpass corner rises slightly to make room for the music's low end. On a capital death it ducks about 8 dB and the floor returning is the beat.

### Ambience acceptance tests (adopted)

- Blind identification (Qwen): at final mix level, ducked under combat, not soloed, a listener identifies at least 4 of 6 biomes from 10-second clips. If it fails, the fix is character events or movement-rate contrast, never level. This answers DeepSeek's and the blind leg's shared biggest risk (six beds collapsing into one hum) with a test instead of a hope.
- Loop fatigue (blind leg): beds are auditioned in a 30-minute soak, not a 30-second preview. A bed a listener can hum by minute ten goes back for a longer loop or a slower swell, before any new sound is added.
- Small-speaker audition (from Qwen's objection, see picks): every bed is heard once on a mono laptop speaker before approval, whatever Ryan rules on the body layer below.

## Picks for Ryan

1. The small-speaker question (Qwen's objection, the strongest in the council). Five of six beds live below 70 Hz at the fundamental, and laptop and TV speakers roll off hard below about 120 Hz, so on that hardware this plan is close to silence, and silence reads as "too quiet" with no level fix available. The chair recommends ruling BEFORE production: every bed carries a deliberate harmonic body layer in the 150 to 450 Hz band (the 2nd, 3rd and 4th harmonics, so the ear infers the missing fundamental), explicitly permitted as neither hiss nor electronic. The alternative is accepting ambience as a headphones feature and cutting biome variety spend. Either way the laptop-speaker audition above stays in the acceptance tests.
2. The wisp deck (gas above the board, Ultra only, alpha 0.06): ships only if it passes the ship-class callout test in Part 1. The chair's recommendation is test-gated, not assumed; Gemini's stronger veil (0.08 to 0.14, depth-faded) is the rejected upper bound, recorded so the Board can see the range the council considered.
3. The still-frame test as the bake's acceptance gate (four of five say "cloud" with drift frozen). The chair recommends adopting it, because it is the only proposed check that can fail the bake rather than the layer count.
