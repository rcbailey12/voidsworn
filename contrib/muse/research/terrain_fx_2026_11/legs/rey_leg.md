# Terrain effects research: Rey leg for board issue 81

Scope note. This leg covers only terrain that sits on the board: nebula hexes (id neb), ion storm hexes (id storm), debris hexes (id debris), and the dead star gravity well as an in-board board shape. Rock, hulk, station and planet blockers are out of scope except where they catch light. The sky, the sky nebula bake, the backdrop planet and moons, and the backdrop station are settled backdrop scope from the earlier job. Where this report touches them it gives one hand-off line only. All facts about our game come from the evidence pack. Where the pack is silent, this report says so.

Headline diagnosis, from the pack. The owner complaint is that nebulae look like just smoke and the other non-asteroid effects look much worse (pack section 1, playtest 2026-11-02c). The screenshots back a specific failure, not a general lack of effort. In ryan_c07 the nebula patch is flat light grey puffs on the hexes with no violet, no internal light, no colour gradient, stars fully hidden inside the patch, and puff edges following the hex shapes. In ryan_c06, ryan_c03, ryan_c04 and ryan_c05 the gas at close and mid range is a uniform mauve, brown or grey wash with no layers and no light inside it (pack section 5). The living system in the shipping Godot build already does several right things on paper: one continuous world-space noise field, a footprint mask built from the patch hexes so there is no hex outline and no spill (godot/world/world_living.gd:4-7, _gas at line 227, _mask at line 298), and for storms a real lightning system with lights that catch ships (world_living.gd:344 and 447-465). So the problem is not the mask or the structure. The problem is the shading model inside the mask. That is fixable in shader and colour work, without touching rules, generation, or the mask system.

Why the current shading reads as smoke, in pack terms. First, colour: nebula colour is the enemy faction sky colour pulled about 50 percent toward violet (0.62, 0.42, 1.0), and the dark side, lit side and glow core are the same base colour times 0.18, times 0.72 and times 0.62 (world_living.gd:273-282). Scalar multiples of one hue cannot produce a colour gradient across a cloud. They produce one grey-violet at three brightnesses, and over a near-black sky the additive-ish blend at render_priority -5 (world_living.gd:285) lifts the dark side toward grey. Grey plus soft edges plus one hue is the definition of smoke. Second, occlusion: an additive style blend can only add light. It cannot darken stars in a controlled way, carve dark dust lanes, or leave bright stars shining through thin edges while dimming them in the core. The pack records the result in ryan_c07 as stars fully hidden inside the patch, which is a tint and fill artefact, not a density gradient. Third, scale and frequency: a puff is 24 to 34 units, which at the verified camera (170 units distance, godot/render/camera_rig.gd:9-10) is about 370 to 525 px wide at 4K (pack section 3). A single low-frequency noise sample drifted at TIME times 0.012, 0.004, 0.007 (GAS_SHADER, world_living.gd:31) across a 400 px blob has almost no visible structure and almost no visible motion. At 4K the eye sees a soft blob, not gas. Fourth, internal light: the nebula path has none. Only the storm path has lights, from its bolts. A nebula with no embedded bright cores, no lit rim, and no dark lanes has nothing for the eye to read as depth. Every shipped game below that does nebulae well solves at least three of these four problems. None of them solves them with more puffs of the same kind.

## 1. Per-game findings

Labelling. Documented means a citable source below states it. INFERRED means the published record is thin and the point is reasoned from how the game looks in play or from general technique. Inferred points are never presented as a developer claim.

### 1.1 Homeworld 3 (Blackbird Interactive, Unreal Engine 4)

Documented. This is the strongest documented case in this leg, and the closest match to our problem.

- The team started from Unreal Engine ray-marched clouds, found them pretty but not controllable enough to mirror NASA nebula photography, and built their own nebula system instead. Source: developer update co-authored by Technical Art Director Demetrius Apostolopoulos, Art Director Karl Gryc and Audio Director Dave Renn, republished at GamingTrend, https://gamingtrend.com/news/massive-homeworld-3-dev-diary-showcases-huge-audio-and-visual-upgrades/
- How their ray marching works, in their own description: a ray is marched through the gas volume in steps, density is measured at each depth and accumulated, and lighting and colour are derived from that accumulated density. It is computed for every pixel of nebula visible on screen at each frame, and they call it powerful and resource-intensive in the same paragraph. Same source.
- Density drives the look in two directions at once. Where density is low they blend in wisp-like shapes with more movement. Where density is high, toward the core, they use a fractal volume density effect to create interest and shape. Same source. This is a documented two-regime model: thin edge equals wisps and motion, thick core equals fractal structure. It is the direct opposite of one noise field at one frequency.
- Gameplay coupling is by density and depth. The resulting clouds react to different light sources and can hide ships based on the density of the gas and the depth of the ship within the cloud. Same source. PC Gamer reports the same system from an interview: when you go into a nebula, the ship understands how much coverage it has, which affects the fog of war concept, per Apostolopoulos. Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/homeworld-3-is-blackbirds-ambitious-vision-for-a-cinematic-sci-fi-rts-that-was-utterly-impossible-to-make-20-years-ago/
- Ships are lit by the environment, not flat-lit. The same update describes physically based rendering with dynamic lighting, real-time reflections, shadows and global illumination, so hull materials react to the lighting scenario of the level, including low orbit cloud seas and nebula light. Earlier Homeworld ships were flatly lit and felt disconnected. Same GamingTrend source. The lesson for us is not the engine feature list. It is that gas colour must land on the ships inside it, or the gas and the ships read as two separate pictures.
- Audio reinforces concealment. Crews comment on sensor and comms interference inside dense nebulae and on the fact that an enemy can hide in the same soup. Same source. Readability is not visual only in their design.
- Layered emissive gas, star occlusion, colour gradients. The dev update does not publish a layer count, an occlusion formula, or a colour ramp. It documents density accumulation plus a low-density wisp regime and a high-density fractal regime, and reaction to light sources. Anything beyond that about their layer stack is INFERRED.

INFERRED for Homeworld 3. From the documented model and the shipped look: colour variation across one cloud most likely comes from the density accumulation itself (thin parts transmit the light source colour, thick parts saturate toward the gas colour) plus per-volume art direction in the editor clip the update shows for adjusting a nebula. Star occlusion is therefore graded by density, not binary. Parallax is true volumetric parallax, which is the one part that does not transfer to our board (see section 1.6). Performance: full per-pixel ray marching at 4K on a hex board game with our puff counts is not a sensible target for us, and the developers themselves flag the cost. We should steal the two-regime density idea and the density-based hiding, not the renderer.

### 1.2 Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (Ironclad Games, Iron Engine)

Documented. Sins 2 is the useful counterexample: its board effects are mostly not in-well gas volumes at all. They are whole gravity well conditions, read at system scale, and the game is honest that several of them have little visible in-well drama. That is a warning as much as a model.

- New non-colonizable gravity well types were added in the November 2024 Ruins of War update, version 1.30: Plasma Storm prevents strikecraft launches, Magnetic Cloud reduces linear acceleration by 50 percent, Radiation Storm disables passive regeneration of hull, armor and shields, Particle Fountain increases antimatter regeneration by 1 per second. The same update added main view icons for the new gravity well types. Source: Stardock official franchise wiki, Ruins of War update page, https://stardock.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SSEFW/pages/2151546881/November+2024+-+Ruins+of+War+Update
- The series pattern is one well, one rule, stated in the well tooltip and icon. In the wider series documentation, Plasma Storm disables strike craft, Magnetic Cloud disables special abilities and starbase deployment, and an asteroid belt well applies decreased accuracy for all units in the well. Source: Planet Types listing, https://sinsofasolarempire.fandom.com/wiki/Planet_Types (community wiki, treated here as gameplay documentation, not as an art source).
- Presentation controls that matter for us are documented in the official player manual: dynamic lights let elements such as weapons fire light objects as shots pass, with a dynamic light quality setting capping simultaneous light sources; an effect counts setting caps drawn particle effects; and there is a Use Only Dark Skybox option that removes nebula ambient from the background entirely. Source: Stardock official franchise wiki, Welcome to Sins of a Solar Empire II, https://stardock.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SSEFW/pages/1900150822/Welcome+to+Sins+of+a+Solar+Empire+II
- Players themselves report the weak point. In Sins forum discussion, players say they have seen Plasma Storms and Magnetic Clouds in game and paid them no attention because they had no noticeable effect on fleets transiting the area, and effects are discovered by zooming out and clicking the system icon. Source: Sins forums, https://forums.sinsofasolarempire.com/424064/solar-storms-magnetic-clouds-strategy and https://forums.sinsofasolarempire2.com/177513/violent-space-weather (player reports, not developer claims).

INFERRED for Sins 2. No citable developer diary, GDC talk or art document was found that describes how Sins 2 renders the inside of a Plasma Storm or Magnetic Cloud well: no layer count, no volumetric method, no particle budget for the wells. On the visual evidence of play, INFERRED: the wells carry a tinted ambient and particle wash over the whole well plus the icon and tooltip do the real readability work. That is exactly the failure mode our owner is complaining about when it is applied at hex scale: condition is legible in UI, invisible in the world. What transfers to us is the discipline, not the look: one terrain kind, one rule, one icon and tint language used consistently (our TERRAIN_INFO table at battle.js:8-15 and ZONE_TINT at godot/render/board.gd:325 already give us that spine), plus explicit quality scaling for dynamic lights and particle counts. What does not transfer is well-scale ambience. Our effects are 5 to 14 hex patches inside a shared board, so they must be readable in the world at a glance without a click.

### 1.3 Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2 (Tindalos Interactive, Unreal Engine 4)

Stated choice: this section is about Armada 2 (2019). Armada 1 (2016) is mentioned only where a source is clearly about the first game.

- Documented gameplay. Gas clouds hide ships. In Armada 2, a ship inside a gas nebula enters stealth, and stealth interacts with two vision ranges: a wider detection range that reveals a signal, and a fixed scanning range (4500, same for all ships) that breaks stealth and reveals the ship. Marking skills and upgrades can strip stealth for 45 seconds. A ship inside a gas cloud is also immune to solar wind, and a gas cloud combined with a plasma explosion environmental factor is more dangerous. Sources: Armada 2 map object and vision system summary, https://en.namu.wiki/w/Battlefleet%20Gothic:%20Armada%202 (community wiki summarising the game manual and in-game rules; no developer art source was found, see INFERRED below).
- Documented gameplay, asteroid comparison. An asteroid field in Armada 2 deals 6 damage per second to a moving ship, no damage to a stationary or turning-in-place ship, puts a stationary ship in stealth, reduces macro weapon hit rate against ships inside by 20 percent regardless of movement, does not affect carrier craft or torpedoes, and drains morale while moving. Same source. The design lesson is that two concealing terrains are separated by a cost verb: the gas cloud is free concealment, the asteroid field is concealment that hurts if you move. Our nebula and debris pair has the same shape (15 points harder to hit in nebula at battle.js:649, 10 points in debris at battle.js:650, neither deals damage), so our visual separation must do the work that their damage verb does: nebula must look deep and soft, debris must look thin and physical.
- Documented look, at review level only. Reviewers describe nebulae and clouds of cosmic dust making the map beautiful despite the limiting grid, with ships, stars, particle effects and lights competing at maximum zoom. Source: Gamereactor preview, https://www.gamereactor.eu/previews/688543/Battlefleet+Gothic+Armada+2/ . For Armada 1, a review states the core terrain contract plainly: the scenery is interactive, asteroid fields damage ships, gas clouds conceal them. Source: Bell of Lost Souls, https://www.belloflostsouls.net/?p=119441
- Readability method, documented by the rules rather than by art. Concealment in Armada is communicated mainly by state, not by making the ship literally invisible to its owner: stealth ships are handled through detection and scanning ranges, signals, and marking. The enemy loses the target, the owner keeps control. That split is directly usable on our board.

INFERRED for Armada 2. No developer diary, GDC talk or official art or modding document was found that describes the gas cloud renderer: layer count, billboard versus volume, colour ramp, star occlusion, particle counts and LOD are all undocumented in the sources found. INFERRED from play footage and screenshots: the clouds are soft billboarded volumes in faction-neutral blue, green and rust tones with darker cores, slow internal drift, and ships fading by stealth state rather than by true volumetric depth. Treat that sentence as inference, not as a pipeline fact. The transferable, documented part is the state language: hidden from the enemy, visible and selectable to the owner, breakable by a scan or a mark. Our hex game already resolves concealment as a hit penalty, not as invisibility, so we need even less literal hiding than Armada: our gas should suggest depth without ever swallowing the ship model or its UI.

### 1.4 Stellaris (Paradox Development Studio, Clausewitz)

Documented. Stellaris works at system scale, one system per screen, which makes its methods more transferable to a hex patch than its scale suggests: a system effect in Stellaris is roughly the screen area of one of our larger storm blobs.

- Method, from the VFX artist directly. Stellaris VFX artist Erik Forsstrom states in Dev Diary 203 that his main tool is particles, described as flat planes whose direction, size and colour he changes over time, and that the big system effects, explicitly including the nebulae and space storms added the previous year, are made differently: because of their sheer size they need more complex shapes than particles alone give, so a system effect is built from 3D meshes with animated shaders on them that create the movement, plus particles on top, and that combination is the complete effect. Source: Stellaris Dev Diary 203, VFX in Nemesis, https://devtrackers.gg/stellaris/p/1dd0f3f4-stellaris-dev-diary-203-vfx-in-nemesis
- That is a documented three-part stack: shaped mesh body, animated shader for internal motion, particle layer on top. It is the cheapest documented stack in this leg and the closest to what our billboard puff system can grow into without a renderer change.
- Storm identity is by type, colour and effect together. The Cosmic Storms expansion (September 10, 2024) replaced the old space storms with eight storm types: Electric, Particle, Gravity, Magnetic, Radiant, Stardust, Shroud and Nexus. All storms share reduced chance of emergency FTL jumps and monthly devastation, and each type adds its own effects, with Shroud and Nexus as special, more dangerous cases. Storms spawn, travel the galaxy at various speeds, and grow and shrink over time. Sources: Stellaris Dev Diary 350, Storms and their Aftermath, https://devtrackers.gg/stellaris/p/cc570f45-stellaris-dev-diary-350-storms-and-their-aftermath and Paradox press release, https://news.cision.com/paradox-interactive-ab/r/cosmic-storms--the-new-mechanical-expansion-for-stellaris--is-now-available-on-pc,c4034862
- Storm look, at review level. A review describes each storm as a swirling particle effect of colours that lights up entire systems, with chaotic flashes of lightning and other cosmic explosions through the storm as it circles an area, and notes a weather map overlay and forecast for tracking storms. Source: Destructoid, https://www.destructoid.com/stellaris-cosmic-storms-makes-the-galaxy-even-more-beautiful/
- Readability separation. Documented: type is carried by name, colour family, a map mode (Weather Forecast Map Mode), and a distinct effect list. The tactical view and the strategic view use different representations of the same storm. Our equivalent split already exists: living look for the world, and the Z key tactical view with ZONE_TINT fills (godot/render/board.gd:325). Stellaris validates keeping both and making them agree in colour.

INFERRED for Stellaris. No source found publishes particle counts, mesh counts per system effect, shader layer counts, LOD rules, or a star occlusion method for nebulae or storms. INFERRED from the dev diary method and the shipped look: occlusion is faked by density of the particle and mesh layers rather than computed, nebula systems read as scenery and sensor flavour rather than as tactical concealment volumes, and the colour gradient across a storm comes from the animated shader scrolling two or more colour bands over the mesh, not from lighting. The transferable facts are the documented ones: mesh plus animated shader plus particles, and one colour family per storm type, held constant between world view and map view.

### 1.5 EVE Online (CCP Games)

Documented. EVE is two separate lessons. Its famous nebulae are background, which in our terms is backdrop scope, and this leg cites them only for colour and occlusion principles. Its in-play-area gas clouds and wormhole phenomena are the tactical part.

- Background nebulae, colour and occlusion principles. CCP dev blog, Introducing New Nebulae into EVE (November 3, 2011): the nebulae are computer generated and painstakingly rendered, inspired by real imagery. The redesign replaced 30 constellation-random backgrounds with 68 unique regional backdrops, so a phenomenon grows larger and more detailed as you travel toward it and eventually surrounds you. Nebulae occupy only parts of the sky, with more dark patches, especially in low security and null security space, where the palette is cooler and more stars are visible because it is darker. High security space is warmer and more saturated. The dominant phenomenon of each empire matches the colour palette of that empire ships, for distinguishability. Ships reflect the nebula of the area they are in, so ships look different in different areas. Source: https://www.eveonline.com/fr/article/introducing-new-nebulae-into-eve
- Independent confirmation of the geography idea: the Crucible nebulae are described as real three-dimensional objects that you move through system by system, redrawn per system into the skybox, with few but highly distinctive nebulae positioned over specific regions: Gallente green and gold, Caldari silver and blue with streaks of black, Minmatar deep red, Amarr brown with a golden glow. Source: Engadget, EVE Evolved, Touring a galaxy reborn, https://www.engadget.com/2011-12-11-eve-evolved-touring-a-galaxy-reborn.html
- Principles that transfer: first, darkness is part of the design. A nebula that fills the whole view with mid-tone colour destroys star contrast everywhere; EVE deliberately leaves dark sky so stars stay a reference. Second, black streaks inside a bright nebula (the Caldari example) are a named feature, not an accident: dark lanes are what make the bright parts read as gas rather than fog. Third, one strong colour identity per place, repeated on the ships through reflections, beats a technically varied palette that means nothing.
- In-area gas clouds, documented gameplay. Harvestable gas clouds sit inside sites in known space and wormhole space: mykoserocin nebulae for synth boosters, cytoserocin nebulae for stronger boosters, and fullerite reservoirs in wormhole space. Cytoserocin clouds are unstable: each harvester cycle completion carries a chance the cloud explodes and damages the harvesting ship. The harvesting visual itself is documented as subdued, with no pretty visual effect in older documentation and a harvester effect you must zoom close to see. Sources: EVE University Wiki, Gas cloud harvesting, https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/index.php?title=Gas_cloud_harvesting&oldid=172548 (community wiki documenting in-game behaviour).
- System-wide phenomena, documented gameplay. Wormhole space systems carry a phenomenon (Pulsar, Wolf Rayet, Red Giant, Magnetar, Black Hole, Cataclysmic Variable in the wider documentation) whose stat effects scale by system class 1 to 6. Example documented values for Pulsar: shield capacity plus 25 percent at class 1 rising to plus 100 percent at class 6, with armor resists, capacitor recharge, targeting range and signature radius moved by matching scaled amounts. Source: EVE University Wiki, Wormholes, wormhole phenomena tables, https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/index.php?title=Wormholes&diff=next&oldid=41279 . The point for us is not the stats. It is that EVE players learn a phenomenon by its sky: the background object in the system is the hazard, and its identity is visible before its tooltip is read.

INFERRED for EVE Online. No citable CCP source was found that documents the in-site gas cloud renderer, its layer count, its particle budget, or any tactical-camera occlusion rule. INFERRED from play: in-site clouds are local billboard and particle volumes in a site-specific colour, kept dim enough that the overview list, brackets and target UI carry selection and readability, while the 3D cloud carries only place and flavour. The documented EVE lesson is therefore about restraint: the game that is famous for nebulae keeps its tactical gas quiet and lets UI do the precise work, while putting all the richness into colour identity, dark lanes, and ship reflections. For our board the balance point is different, because our gas is the terrain and must be read without a click, but the restraint principle still applies to ship readability inside the gas.

### 1.6 Cross-game synthesis: what transfers to a hex board, and what does not

Transfers, documented in at least one game above:

1. Two density regimes in one cloud (Homeworld 3, documented). Thin edge: wispy, more movement, more transparent, stars show through. Thick core: structured, fractal or filament detail, saturated colour, stars dimmed. One noise at one frequency cannot do this. Two noise scales with a density threshold between them can, inside our existing shader billboards.
2. Mesh or shaped body plus animated shader plus a top layer (Stellaris, documented). Our equivalent is: footprint-masked body puffs (already have), an animated internal shader pass with a scrolling second colour band (do not have), and a sparse top layer of small bright particles or filament puffs (do not have).
3. Dark lanes and dark surroundings (EVE, documented). A dark absorptive component is what separates gas from smoke. Our current gas has no dark component at all except the storm body, which is why the storm, for all its faults, reads less like smoke than the nebula does.
4. Gas colour lands on ships (Homeworld 3 PBR, documented; EVE reflections, documented). Our storm bolts already light ships through OmniLight3D (world_living.gd:344). The nebula has no equivalent. A cheap fake, a soft coloured light or a hull tint by hex, would join gas and ships into one picture.
5. One kind, one colour, one rule, everywhere (Sins 2 icons and tooltips, documented; Stellaris storm types and map mode, documented; EVE regional palettes, documented). Our spine exists: TERRAIN_INFO legend colours at battle.js:8-15 (neb violet #b07cff, debris tan #d4bf86, storm yellow #ffe866) and ZONE_TINT at board.gd:325. The failure in ryan_c07 is that the world colour does not match the legend colour. Fixing that agreement is the single highest value change in this report.
6. Concealment as state for the enemy, clarity for the owner (Armada 2, documented rules). Our hit penalty model (battle.js:649-651) already implies this. The gas should never hide a ship from its owner or bury its UI.

Does not transfer:

- True volumetric ray marching per pixel (Homeworld 3). Documented as resource-intensive by its own developers, built for free 3D space where the camera flies inside clouds. Our camera is fixed at 170 units looking down 34 degrees (pack section 3), our clouds are flat patches 5 to 14 hexes across sitting low at y minus 4 to 5.5 (world_living.gd:236-237). We need the look of depth from a fixed elevated camera, which is a layering and shading problem, not a volume integration problem.
- Whole-system ambience as the effect (Sins 2, Stellaris system scale, EVE phenomena). Our patches share a board with clear space, blockers and other terrain kinds. A patch must end cleanly at its footprint without tinting the whole board.
- Background richness techniques (EVE skyboxes, our own sky bake). Backdrop scope, settled. Hand-off line only: if the board gas is fixed and still fights the sky, the sky bake palette per faction is the backdrop job to align, not this one.
- Literal invisibility in gas (Armada stealth as a visual). Our rules give a hit penalty, not invisibility. Ships stay drawn.


## 2. Per-kind look spec for our kinds

Shared rules for all four kinds, from the pack. Board is 30 by 22 hexes, hex size 10 world units, 17.32 units flat to flat (battle.js:2 and battle.js:86). Default camera is 170 units, looking down 34 degrees, about 15.4 px per world unit at 4K. Ships and their UI stay readable at 4K at that camera. Each effect reads as its gameplay effect at a glance. Footprints stay honest to the hexes covered, using the existing footprint mask system (world_living.gd:298), without drawing hex-shaped blobs: the mask clips, the noise shapes. No rules or generation change: TERRAIN_INFO at battle.js:8-15, hit effects at battle.js:649-651, storm tick at battle.js:398, genTerrain at battle.js:171-254 and its Godot parity copy at godot/rules/rules.gd stay untouched. All work below is presentation in the Godot build only. The browser build is frozen and stays as is (pack section 4).

Shared layer language. Every gas kind gets named layers so tasks and screenshots can refer to them: L0 shadow body (dark, absorptive, dims what is behind), L1 main body (the colour the legend promises), L2 filament and core glow (thin, bright, high frequency), L3 top wisps (small, sparse, above ship feet, never above ship UI in screen terms), plus where relevant L4 active events (lightning, sparks, lensing). Today the living gas is effectively L1 only, with L4 present for storm only. That single fact explains most of the screenshot evidence.

### 2.1 Nebula (neb). Gameplay: ships inside are 15 points harder to hit (battle.js:649). It is the deepest concealment on the board. Legend colour violet #b07cff.

Layer stack.
- L0 shadow body: one masked puff per hex, large (30 to 40 units), low at y minus 4 to 0, near-black violet (base colour times about 0.05 to 0.08, not the current 0.18), normal blend, not additive, alpha driven by the high-density part of the noise only. Its job is star dimming and dark lanes. It sits under ships.
- L1 main body: keep the current puff counts as the starting point (5 puffs per hex, 3 at low quality, sized 24 to 34 units, plus about 1 tying puff per 3 hexes sized 36 to 46 units, world_living.gd:231-246), most at y minus 4 to 5.5 with a thin veil above (world_living.gd:236-237). Rework the shader, not the counts (see colour behaviour).
- L2 filament and core glow: a second sample of the same world noise at 3 to 4 times the frequency, thresholded hard so it appears only as thin veins and 2 to 4 bright cores per patch, additive, small total screen area (target under 10 percent of the patch area at the default camera). This is the Homeworld 3 high-density regime and the Stellaris top layer, done in shader.
- L3 top wisps: keep the existing thin veil, but make it the low-density regime: faster drift, lower alpha, broken edge. No new puffs needed if the veil can take a separate drift speed in shader.
- Embedded star points: 1 to 3 tiny bright points per patch, placed inside L2 cores, drawn as small additive sprites with a soft halo. They are the internal light story: young stars inside the gas. The pack is silent on any existing embedded star feature in board gas, so this is new presentation work, flagged as such. If it proves costly, L2 cores alone carry the effect and this layer is cut first.

Colour behaviour.
- Base must read violet at the default camera in the cine look. The legend colour #b07cff is the anchor: L1 mid-tone should sit at or near that hue, not near the mauve-brown in ryan_c03 to ryan_c05.
- Gradient across one patch, by density and by position: edge is deep indigo violet (dark, blue shifted), mid body is the legend violet, cores shift toward magenta pink on one side and toward teal blue on the other side of the patch, driven by a slow world-space gradient so a patch has a lit side and a shadow side. Hue must change, not only brightness. The current scalar multiples (times 0.18, 0.72, 0.62 at world_living.gd:273-282) are replaced by a three-stop ramp: shadow stop, mid stop, glow stop, each with its own hue.
- Faction tint becomes a secondary influence only: keep pulling from the enemy faction sky colours (godot/fx/faction_look.gd:15 and :47, key nebula) but cap the pull at about 25 percent and apply it mainly to the teal or magenta end of the ramp, so two battles can feel different while every nebula still reads violet and matches its legend and its tactical tint (board.gd:325, neb violet alpha 0.16). Today the roughly 50 percent pull plus scalar shading is a direct cause of the brown and mauve reads when the faction palette is warm.
- Saturation guard: in the cine look, L1 should never be lighter than the sky behind it at its edges, and never grey. If a test screenshot histogram of a patch shows the mid-tone within 10 percent lightness of neutral grey, the ramp has failed. That is a checkable rule, written for the screenshot pass in section 3.

Internal light.
- L2 cores are emissive in the shader only, plus one soft OmniLight3D per patch (not per hex), violet, low energy (estimate 0.6 to 1.0, to be tuned in playtest), placed at the largest core, so ship hulls at the patch edge catch violet on one side. Basis for the light count: the storm system already budgets lights from bolts (first 4 bolts carry lights, world_living.gd:344) so a small fixed light budget per patch is consistent with existing practice. The pack is silent on a per-scene light cap, so the task must measure and report the worst case (storm map plus sector nebula condition can stack patches: genTerrain storm map has 2 nebula blobs, and a nebula sector condition adds 3 blobs to every battle, battle.js:234 and battle.js:239).
- Weapons fire and explosions inside the patch lighting the gas: desirable, documented in spirit by Sins 2 dynamic lights, but the pack is silent on whether our gas shader can receive dynamic light. Treat as a stretch item in the tasks, not as part of the core spec. Do not promise it.

Motion.
- Three speeds, one per regime: L0 almost still (drift at or below the current TIME times 0.012 rate), L1 slow churn (current rate, plus a slow internal rotation of the second noise sample so cores appear to boil over 20 to 40 seconds), L3 wisps 2 to 3 times faster than L1. Motion must be visible in a 5 second screen recording at 4K and invisible as texture swimming at the hex edges: the mask stays fixed in world space, only the interior moves.
- No pulsing of whole-patch alpha. Pulsing reads as a gameplay state change and nebula has no state change.

## 5. Owner questions

Q1. Nebula colour identity: fixed violet for every battle, or faction-tinted per battle as today?
Trade-off: fixed violet makes nebula instantly learnable and matches the legend #b07cff and tactical tint everywhere, but loses per-battle variety the faction sky colours (faction_look.gd:15 and :47) were giving. Faction tint keeps variety and keeps causing warm nebulae that read brown and break the legend agreement, as in ryan_c03 to ryan_c05.
Chair pick: fixed violet family as the identity, faction tint capped at about 25 percent on the ramp ends only (the Task 1 spec). Variety survives, identity wins.

Q2. How far should nebula gas dim the ships inside it?
Trade-off: a slight hull dim sells depth and the concealment fantasy, and Homeworld 3 hides by density and depth, but our game resolves concealment as a hit penalty (battle.js:649), not invisibility, and the owner judges and plays at 4K where a dim ship is a ship he cannot assess. Full dim risks selection and state-reading errors.
Chair pick: ships stay crisp. Gas dims stars and sky only, plus the violet patch light on hulls from Task 4. No alpha over ships, no hull fade. Depth comes from layers round the ship, not over it.

Q3. Storm flash comfort: keep the current bolt cadence, or make storms more violent?
Trade-off: current cadence is a flash every 2.2 to 6.5 seconds with a roughly one-third second envelope (world_living.gd:430 and 447-465). More violence (shorter intervals, brighter sheet glow, branching) makes the only damaging terrain feel as dangerous as its shield tick (battle.js:398), but raises flash comfort and photosensitivity concerns over long planning phases, and the owner reads the board in stills where violence does not show anyway. The still test is fixed by filaments, not by flash rate.
Chair pick: keep cadence and envelope exactly as they are. Add filaments, sheet glow and the tick flare. No full-screen flash, no rate increase. Revisit only if a playtest says the storm still feels calm in motion.

Q4. Debris dust: visible tan dust as part of the identity, or near-invisible dust and let rock and wreck alone carry the kind?
