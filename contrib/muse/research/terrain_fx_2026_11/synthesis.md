## Verdict

## 1. Diagnosis: why the gas reads as smoke

Owner complaint (Ryan, Nov 2, 2026): "Nebulas look like just smoke? We need to rethink the other non-asteroid effects on the map, they look much worse." The three usable legs agree on the cause, and the chair adopts their shared diagnosis in full. It has four parts, all in the shading model inside the footprint mask, none in the mask or the structure.

1. One hue at flat multipliers. Nebula colour is the enemy faction sky colour pulled about 50 percent toward violet (0.62, 0.42, 1.0), and the dark side, lit side and glow core are that same base colour times 0.18, times 0.72 and times 0.62 (godot/world/world_living.gd:273-282). Scalar multiples of one hue cannot produce a colour gradient across a cloud. They produce one grey-violet at three brightnesses. When the faction palette is warm, the same shading produces the mauve and brown washes in ryan_c03 to ryan_c05.
2. A blend that can only add light. The gas is drawn additive-ish over the sky at render_priority -5 (world_living.gd:285). An additive style blend cannot darken stars in a controlled way, cannot carve dark lanes, and cannot grade occlusion by density. The recorded result in ryan_c07 is stars fully hidden inside the patch: a tint and fill artefact, not a density gradient.
3. No internal light. The nebula path has no light sources at all. Only the storm path has lights, from its bolts (world_living.gd:344). A nebula with no embedded bright cores, no lit rim and no dark lanes gives the eye nothing to read as depth.
4. One noise at one frequency at 4K scale. A puff is 24 to 34 units, which at the verified camera (170 units, godot/render/camera_rig.gd:9-10) spans about 370 to 525 px at 4K. A single low-frequency noise sample drifted at TIME times 0.012, 0.004, 0.007 (GAS_SHADER, world_living.gd:31) across a 400 px blob has almost no visible structure and almost no visible motion. At the owner's camera the eye sees a soft blob, not gas.

The screenshot pattern follows from these four causes: wherever gas appears in the Nov 2 playtest set, near or far, it reads as flat smoke in grey, mauve or brown, whatever colour the data says it should be (pack section 5). The living system already does the structural things right: one continuous world-space noise field, and a footprint mask built from the patch's own hexes so a patch reads as one body with no hex outline and no spill (world_living.gd:4-7, func _mask at world_living.gd:298). The fix therefore stays inside func _gas and its shader.

Scope of the spec: the gas and field kinds (neb, storm), debris, and the dead star well as an in-board board shape. Rock is the asteroid kind and is out of scope except where it interacts. Hulk, station and planet hex set pieces were covered by Job 10 and are not redesigned here. All look work is presentation only: the Godot rules parity copy (godot/rules/rules.gd) and the browser rules (battle.js) stay untouched, and the browser build stays frozen as it is.

## 2. Settled look spec

Shared readability rules for every kind below, from the pack: ships and their UI stay readable at 4K at the default camera (170 units, about 15.4 px per world unit at 4K); each effect reads as its gameplay effect at a glance (nebula conceals, storm threatens and is visibly active, debris is light physical cover, the well is a hazard centre); footprints stay honest to the hexes covered, using the existing footprint mask, without drawing hex-shaped blobs. There are no texture files for terrain gas; everything below is shaders, generated geometry, or engine lights, consistent with the current pipeline.

### 2.1 Nebula

The nebula fix is shader work on the existing _gas path (world_living.gd:227). Puff counts, sizes, heights and the footprint mask stay as they are: 5 puffs per hex (3 at low quality) sized 24 to 34 units, plus about 1 broad tying puff per 3 hexes sized 36 to 46 units (world_living.gd:231-246). What changes is the shading.

Layer structure, by height: a ground haze band (y -4 to 2) that grounds the ships; the main body (y 1 to 8) carrying most of the colour and density; wisps and tendrils (y 6 to 14), thinner and faster; and a high tying veil (y 12 to 18) of one or two very broad, very faint puffs per patch that softens the top silhouette.

Colour: a four-stop density-to-colour ramp replaces the scalar multiples at world_living.gd:273-282. The stops are: near-black at low density; dark violet at density 0.3; the legend violet #b07cff, blended 50 percent toward the enemy faction sky nebula colour (godot/fx/faction_look.gd:15 and :47, key "nebula"), at density 0.6; and bright violet-white at density 0.9 and above. Colour is driven by noise density, not by position, so one cloud carries dark edge, violet body and bright core at once. The faction pull at the ramp ends is the subject of owner question Q1; the chair pick caps it at about 25 percent there, and the build should treat that weight as a single tunable constant, not as structure.

Filament cores: a second sample of the same world noise at 3 to 4 times the frequency, thresholded hard so it appears only as thin veins and a small number of bright cores per patch, additive, with a target of under 10 percent of the patch screen area at the default camera. This is the high-density regime the Homeworld 3 legs describe (section 3.1) and the top layer in the Stellaris stack (section 3.4), done inside our existing shader billboards.

Glow cores: the current c_glow flat multiply (times 0.62 at world_living.gd:282) becomes an emissive add at density peaks, ramping from dim violet at medium density to bright violet-white at peak density. The glow is in the shader; it is not a light source.

Patch light: at most one low-energy OmniLight3D per nebula patch, placed at the largest glow core, energy tuned in the screenshot and playtest pass (the Rey leg's working estimate is 0.6 to 1.0). This is the DeepSeek middle position, adopted: emissive cores alone (the Qwen position) leave gas colour off the hulls, and a light per patch as core spec (the Rey position) spends the light budget before it is measured. The light is optional and judged in playtest. The build task must report the worst-case light count: a storm map carries 2 nebula blobs and a nebula sector condition adds 3 more to every battle (battle.js:234, battle.js:239), on top of the storm bolt lights.

Star occlusion: the gas gains an absorptive component so stars dim by density instead of vanishing at a boundary or shining through untouched. Working targets: stars read at about 30 percent brightness through the nebula body and about 60 percent through the wisps. The sky nebula bake stays faintly visible as a colour influence through the thinnest gas. This is a blend change at world_living.gd:285, and its risk is flagged in build task 3.

Motion: the existing TIME offset rates (0.012, 0.004, 0.007, GAS_SHADER at world_living.gd:31) become per-layer multipliers: ground haze at 0.6 times, body at 1 times, wisps at 1.6 times, tying veil at 0.4 times. The body also gets a slow breathing oscillation of the density threshold (period about 8 seconds, small amplitude) so the cloud appears to churn rather than slide. The mask stays fixed in world space; only the interior moves. No whole-patch alpha pulsing: pulsing reads as a state change, and the nebula has none.

### 2.2 Ships inside gas

Ships inside a nebula stay crisp and selectable. The gas alpha at ship height (y 0 to 3) stays below 0.4, and the nebula body alpha starts at 0.40 at patch centre, tunable upward after the first playtest if hulls still read well. The ceiling reference is the 0.7 densest-point cap the Qwen leg reports for EVE (adopted as a design rule with the citation caveat in section 3.5): our working numbers stay well under it. Selection rings, health bars, icons and all UI render on top, unchanged. Concealment is carried by the hit penalty (battle.js:649), not by hiding or dimming hulls. Depth is sold by the layers and the patch light around the ship, never by gas drawn over the ship. This adopts the Rey leg's owner pick over the DeepSeek leg's rim-lit silhouette framing; the dissent is recorded in section 6.

### 2.3 Ion storm

The storm keeps its dark body exactly as the step-2 fix set it: c_dark 0.003, 0.003, 0.006 and c_lit 0.035, 0.028, 0.06, density 1.0, glow_from 0.5 (world_living.gd:262-271). The step-2 comment at world_living.gd:265-267 is primary evidence and the chair treats it as binding: a dark violet still read pale and lavender over near-black skies because it was lighter than them. No leg's proposal may lighten the body.

The legend yellow #ffe866 enters through the energy, not the body. Glow cores stop being pure violet (0.3, 0.17, 0.62) and become a density ramp: deep violet at medium density, yellow-white at peaks. Violet is demoted to the deep shadow accent. On top of that ramp, the storm gains four activity elements:

1. Electric filaments: thin bright veins in the yellow-white family, thresholded from the same high-frequency noise sample as the nebula cores, concentrated toward the patch centre, crawling slowly (the vein pattern advects over 3 to 6 seconds). These are the always-on activity signal between bolts. Without them, a still screenshot of a storm is a black patch with violet cores: a colour cousin of the nebula, and stills are how the owner judges.
2. Sheet glow: a brief brightness lift of the storm body within one to two hexes of a bolt, on the bolt's existing flash envelope, so a bolt lights its own cloud and not only ships and rocks.
3. Flash response: during a bolt's flash frame, the shader emissive term of storm gas within about 12 units of the bolt is boosted (the Qwen leg's figure is 0.3), so the whole local volume pulses with each strike.
4. Shield-tick flare: a short filament flare across storm patches that contain ships, timed to the existing per-turn shield drain (battle.js:398), so the storm's gameplay beat lands as a visible event. The rule itself is untouched; this is presentation reading the same event in the Godot presentation layer, and the build task must identify that presenter rather than invent one (build task 4).

Between bolts, a low-frequency pulse in the glow cores (period 1.5 to 2.5 seconds, small amplitude) keeps the storm from ever reading as static.

Bolts are unchanged. Count stays at about 1 bolt per 2 storm hexes, at most 6; cadence stays at one flash every 2.2 to 6.5 seconds on the roughly one-third second envelope (world_living.gd:344, world_living.gd:430, _process at world_living.gd:447-465); the first 4 bolts keep their OmniLight3D at light_energy up to 3.2. Lightning stays local to the patch: no board-wide flash (DeepSeek pick, adopted). Storm star occlusion is heavier than the nebula's: stars read at about 10 to 15 percent through the storm body. Storm churn is faster than nebula churn and its veil twists against the body drift. The still test governs: a single still with no bolt visible must still read as storm, not nebula, to a viewer who has not been told.

This settlement is the main dissent in the pack and all three leg positions are recorded in section 6: the Rey leg wanted yellow to lead the storm's light outright, the DeepSeek leg wanted yellow kept out of the body and confined to tint, UI and bolt-lit highlights, and the Qwen leg proposed the violet-dominant ramp the chair adopts as the mechanism, with the Rey leg's filaments, sheet glow and tick flare added on top.

### 2.4 Debris

Debris is physical cover, not gas, and its counts are already settled by Job 10: 15 to 22 rocks plus 3 to 5 wreck pieces per hex on the living path (func _debris at world_living.gd:469; the four procedural wreck types at world_living.gd:549-556). Those counts do not change. What changes is value, hierarchy and dust:

1. Size hierarchy: at least 20 percent of rocks per hex are large (3 to 5 units), and at least one wreck piece per 3 hexes is clearly larger than the rocks (6 to 9 units) as a focal anchor.
2. Sub-clumping: pieces cluster into 2 to 3 sub-clumps per hex with clear gaps, instead of an even scatter, so a hex reads as a few cover objects a ship can sit behind, not as gravel. The pack is silent on current intra-hex distribution; this is a target to verify in the first screenshot pass, not a claim about current code.
3. Specular glint: a sharp specular term on the rock and wreck materials, keyed to the system sun direction, so the field sparkles as pieces tumble and reads as solid metal and stone. The pack is silent on where the sun direction is set; the implementer checks world_bake.gd or the scene lighting (build task 6).
4. Hot torn edges: kept, and capped at 2 to 3 emissive points per wreck piece (DeepSeek and Qwen agree), so they read as detail, not as a light source.
5. Dust haze: visible but capped. The dust is tan, in the legend family #d4bf86 at low saturation, alpha 0.06 to 0.10, darker at the bottom of the field and thinner at the top, masked tight to the footprint and invisible one hex outside it. If it reads as smoke in the first screenshot round, its alpha is halved before it is cut (Rey pick, adopted). Dust must never be grey.
6. Motion: tumble periods vary by size: small rocks 20 to 40 seconds, large rocks 60 to 90 seconds, wreck pieces 80 to 120 seconds. Dust drifts at about 20 percent of the nebula gas rate, near still.
7. Ship clear zone: debris placement keeps a clear zone of radius 4 units around each ship hex centre so a hull is never buried in rocks. Height discipline: most pieces stay below ship midline; one hero wreck piece per patch may rise higher. Debris does not block movement or line of fire (it is not in the blocking set at battle.js:7), and its height must not pretend otherwise.

Stars show through the gaps; occlusion is by geometry only. No violet, no yellow: tan and rust only, so debris can never be confused with either gas kind in a still.

### 2.5 Dead star well

The well is stated in structure and subtle in brightness. That framing is where the Rey leg (stated structure, subtle brightness) and the Qwen leg (the stronger treatment) converge once their items are separated: the chair adopts the Qwen leg's structural items and the Rey leg's brightness discipline.

Keep: the WELL_SHADER lensing ring and its slow twist (world_living.gd:154; one full rotation of the distortion pattern in about 30 seconds), and the inward ripples described in the header at world_living.gd:13-14.

Add:
1. Timed ripples: a new ripple spawns at the outer edge every 3 to 4 seconds and travels inward over 2 seconds, fading toward the centre; ripple alpha about 0.12 with a faint warm tint.
2. A thin bright inner rim on the lensing ring (white-blue, low alpha) so the boundary is findable at the default camera without the tactical view.
3. Subtle chromatic aberration at the ring edge (red and blue channels shifted 1 to 2 px in opposite directions), a shader-only change.
4. Infalling dust: a sparse field of tiny particles, 1 to 2 per hex inside the influence radius, size 0.5 to 1 unit, alpha 0.15, drifting inward at about 1 unit per second at the outer edge and accelerating to about 3 units per second near the centre, with a slight spiral (tangential component about 20 percent of radial speed).
5. A faint warm accretion hint: a flat ring at y -2 to 1, deep red at the outer edge warming toward orange-white near the centre, alpha 0.10 to 0.20, rotating very slowly (one revolution in about 45 seconds). This is a hint of matter being pulled in on the play surface. The dead star itself as a sky object is backdrop scope (section 4).
6. Ring debris alignment: the debris ring the deadstar shape already generates (battle.js:248) follows the debris spec in section 2.4, with pieces aligned tangentially and tumbling in a shared orbital direction, so the ring reads as orbiting the well.

Colour structure is radial and cold-to-warm at low brightness: near-black centre with a very faint warm glow (alpha about 0.08), deep red-orange at mid range, cold blue-grey at the outer edge fading to transparent. The well borrows no field kind identity: no legend violet, no storm yellow, no debris tan, and its rim brightness stays below storm filament brightness in a shared screenshot. No dark disc stronger than a nebula shadow body. Lensing applies to the background only, never to ship models or hex UI; whether the current shader already respects that scope is a verify item in build task 7, because the pack is silent on the shader's draw scope.

No part of this spec implies a pull mechanic. The dead star is background only as a mechanic under the standing ruling; the well's gameplay is the existing board shape (VOID.deadstar set in genTerrain at battle.js:190-191, well position carried on the battle state at battle.js:325), and the debris ring is its board furniture.

### 2.6 Mask feather and footprint

The footprint mask (func _mask at world_living.gd:298) gains an edge feather: 2 units on the nebula, 1.5 units on the storm (the tighter storm feather is deliberate: storms should feel more defined and aggressive). The silhouette stays inside the hex footprint, the noise and puff placement keep breaking up any straight hex edges, and the tactical tint remains the honest footprint statement in the Z view. No hex-shaped blobs, in either view.

### 2.7 Tactical tint

ZONE_TINT (godot/render/board.gd:325) alpha comes down so the gas itself carries the terrain read, while a faint tint stays for gameplay zoning. Current values: nebula violet alpha 0.16, debris tan alpha 0.12, storm yellow alpha 0.13. The exact new values are not fixed in this document; they are a screenshot-judged tuning step inside build task 8, taken against the landed gas, because fixing them before the ramps exist would be guessing. The TERRAIN_INFO legend colours (battle.js:8-15) are the reference and are not edited by this work: if a world ramp and a legend colour cannot be reconciled, that goes to the owner (it is part of Q1), not to a silent legend edit. This is also the terrain half of R70: the board-game-on-a-background read is partly the tint doing the terrain's job, and the settlement moves that job into the gas.

### 2.8 Old clouds path (today and holo looks)

The old clouds (godot/world/world.gd, CLOUD_SHADER at line 1063, func cloud_hexes at line 1141) show only in the today and holo looks; look C (cine) is the default (godot/world/board_look.gd:12 and :21) and the living system is what the owner plays. The old path gets a minimal port of the colour ramp only: the same density-to-colour idea in CLOUD_SHADER, the storm internal flash (world.gd:1088-1096) tinted into the yellow family, and no layer separation, no parallax, no fake occlusion (the path is additive with depth_draw_never, so true occlusion is not available there and grey must not be used to fake it). It is the last task in the build order and is deferrable without blocking the living path. It is owner question Q5.

### 2.9 Quality ladder

Quality scaling reduces presence last. The ladder reduces puff counts and bolt light usage first (the pack already records 5 puffs per hex at full quality and 3 at low quality on the gas path, and 4 old-cloud billboards per hex dropping to 2), and it never cuts the colour ramp or the glow cores at any quality level, because those carry the read. The mindset is the one the DeepSeek leg draws from EVE's documented low-quality half-resolution textures: gas can drop resolution before it drops presence. Frame cost is measured and reported, never assumed; the pack states no frame-time budget or target GPU, so build task 10 records measured frame time on the storm and graveyard maps at 1080p and 4K rather than asserting a target.

## 3. What the shipped games actually tell us

Sourcing honesty governs this section. Where a leg's account is documented, the synthesis says so. Where two legs conflict, the conflict is stated and left unresolved rather than averaged. Where a claim rests on an unverified citation, the caveat travels with the claim.

### 3.1 Homeworld 3: mechanism contested, behaviours agreed

The two research legs conflict on how Homeworld 3 renders its nebulae, and the chair does not settle it.

The DeepSeek leg documents a custom volumetric system: Blackbird started from Unreal's ray-marched clouds, found them too limited for NASA-like nebulae, and built its own system in which a ray steps through the volume per pixel, accumulates density at different depths, and derives lighting and colour from that accumulated density. Low-density regions blend wisp-like shapes with more movement; high-density regions toward the core use a fractal volume density effect. The same leg documents the gameplay coupling: a density threshold (default 0.5) defines when a ship counts as inside, and the standard nebula status effect applies Primary Sensors Range -0.5 and Visual Range -0.5, with damage multiplier and status effect settable per volume. The Rey leg independently reports the same ray-marching account from the developer update (republished at GamingTrend) and the PC Gamer interview, including the two-regime density model and ships hidden by density and depth.

The Qwen leg documents the opposite mechanism: layered billboard planes, explicitly not true ray-marched volumes, in three functional layers (a dense inner body, a mid-density colour layer, an outer wisp layer catching rim light), citing a 2023 developer diary and a GDC 2024 art talk. Its layer counts beyond the three functional layers are labelled INFERRED in its own text.

Chair position: the mechanism is CONTESTED between legs and is not presented as settled anywhere in this synthesis. It does not block the build, for one reason: the transferable behaviours both accounts support are the same, and they are what section 2 uses. Density-graded colour across one cloud. Density-graded occlusion, with stars dimmed rather than switched off. Embedded emissive cores as the internal light. Layers or regimes drifting at different speeds for parallax. Ships kept legible and selectable inside gas, with concealment carried by rules, not by hiding hulls. Our spec implements those behaviours on our existing masked billboard path, which is the one point all three legs' mappings agree our code can do.

### 3.2 Sins of a Solar Empire 2: no terrain technique on record

The chair position is that Sins 2 contributes no terrain rendering technique to this job.

The DeepSeek leg is explicit: the published record does not document board-level nebula or ion storm gas in Sins 2. Its public identity is a large-scale strategy map with orbiting bodies and skybox nebulae; the documented modding surface covers skyboxes, and the one named in-engine effect it found, the console variable main_view.render_gravity_well_prop_clouds, toggles a small cloud around gravity wells as a backdrop or prop effect. Its lesson is compositional (a strong sky carries the "we are in a nebula" read while the board stays clean), which for us is a backdrop hand-off, not a terrain change.

The Rey leg documents the gameplay side instead: gravity well conditions such as Plasma Storm and Magnetic Cloud (Ruins of War update, version 1.30), one well, one rule, carried by icon and tooltip, plus player reports that the wells go unnoticed in the world. Its transferable lessons are discipline, not technique: one kind, one colour, one rule, stated consistently in world, icon and tint; and the warning that a condition legible only in UI is the failure mode this issue exists to fix.

The Qwen leg's Sins 2 claims (modding documentation describing layered particle clouds with emissive tinting, a gas shader sampling two noise layers, a storm intensity parameter) are not verified against a citable source in this synthesis and are not relied on. They are recorded here so the file shows they were made and set aside, not silently dropped.

### 3.3 Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2: two readability lessons

No developer source documents how Armada 2 renders its in-play gas clouds: the DeepSeek leg states this outright after finding gameplay descriptions and the Tindalos procedural environment dev diary (which documents backdrop and environment dressing, not the cloud renderer), and the Rey leg likewise found no developer diary, GDC talk or art document for the gas renderer. The Qwen leg additionally cites a Digital Foundry review describing volumetric-looking fog planes; that is review-level description, not a pipeline source, and the chair treats the technique as undocumented.

What is used, because it is documented at the rules and design level:

1. Gas conceals as a zone state with a readable boundary. A ship inside a gas cloud enters a stealth or hiding state and can ambush from it, and is immune to solar winds inside; asteroid fields instead damage ships over time. Concealment is communicated as a state the enemy loses, while the owner keeps control and selection. Our hit-penalty model (battle.js:649-651) needs even less literal hiding than Armada: the gas suggests depth and never swallows the ship or its UI.
2. Legend colour coding is carried in the gas, not only in the UI (Qwen leg, from the developer diaries' colour storytelling account): concealing nebulae deep blue-violet, hazardous storms sickly green-yellow, decorative dust warm orange-brown, as a deliberate at-a-glance readability choice. Our TERRAIN_INFO already assigns the legend colours; the failure in ryan_c07 is that the world colour does not match them. Section 2 closes that gap for all three field kinds.

A supporting point from the Rey leg: Armada separates its two concealing terrains by a cost verb (gas is free concealment, asteroids hurt if you move). Our nebula and debris pair has no damage difference (15 points at battle.js:649, 10 points at battle.js:650, neither deals damage), so the visual separation must do that work: nebula deep and soft, debris thin and physical.

### 3.4 Stellaris: shape plus shader plus particles, and the cautionary floor

Two documented points and one cautionary inference.

Documented, from VFX artist Erik Forsstrom in Dev Diary 203 (cited by both the DeepSeek and Rey legs): system-scale effects, explicitly including nebulae and space storms, are built from 3D meshes with animated shaders that create the movement in the textures, plus particles on top, because particles alone cannot give shapes at that size. That three-part stack is the cheapest documented stack in the legs and the closest to what our masked billboard system can grow into: shaped body, animated internal shader, sparse bright top layer. Section 2's filament cores are that top layer, done in shader.

Documented, from the Rey leg's Cosmic Storms sourcing (Dev Diary 350 and the expansion material): storm identity is carried by type, colour family and effect list together, and the same colour family is held constant between the world view and the map view. Our equivalent split already exists (living look, and the Z key tactical view with ZONE_TINT fills), and build task 8 makes the two agree.

The cautionary floor is the Qwen leg's Stellaris account, labelled INFERRED in its own text: at the fixed top-down system view, its gas reads as simple flat-colour billboard particles with minimal internal structure, close to what our old clouds path produces. The chair records it as the leg's inference from footage, not as documented fact, and uses it only for the negative lesson the leg itself draws: flat particle gas at a fixed top-down camera is exactly the smoke problem, and it is the baseline this job exists to move past.

### 3.5 EVE Online: heavy volumetrics not adopted, two design rules adopted

EVE's sourcing in these legs is mixed, and the synthesis separates what is documented from what is adopted.

Documented (DeepSeek leg): CCP's newer volumetric clouds are authored in EmberGen, exported as VDB volumetric data, and consumed by an engine shader, with in-house compression (re-digitizing, run-length encoding, zlib) taking 300 to 400 megabytes of DDS data per cloud down by roughly 10x, and half-resolution textures shipped for low quality settings. Also documented (both the DeepSeek and Rey legs): the 2011 nebula update replaced 30 reused backgrounds with 68 unique region-level backdrops, with deliberate dark patches and cooler palettes in low security space, one strong colour identity per region, and ships reflecting the local nebula. The Rey leg adds the named detail that black streaks inside a bright nebula are a designed feature: dark lanes are what make the bright parts read as gas rather than fog. That principle is adopted in section 2 as the nebula's absorptive component and dark rim.

Not adopted: the VDB volumetric pipeline itself. It is too heavy for a procedural, texture-free terrain system with no hand-painted terrain textures, and nothing in our pipeline could consume it. It is recorded here so the rejection is visible, not silent.

Adopted as design rules, with a citation caveat: the density-to-colour ramp, and the densest-point alpha cap of about 0.7. Both come from the Qwen leg's EVE account, which rests partly on a 2019 GDC talk whose exact title the leg itself flags as unverified in its own Not checked section. The chair adopts both anyway, for two reasons that do not depend on that citation: the ramp is independently supported by the Stellaris and Homeworld 3 accounts and by the Rey leg's EVE colour principles, and the cap is used only as a ceiling reference that our working alpha numbers (0.40 at centre, below 0.4 at ship height) stay well under. If the citation is later shown to be wrong, no number in section 2 changes.

## 4. Scope boundary and coordination

Backdrop boundary, restated and not reopened. The sky and its GPU-baked nebula (godot/world/world_bake.gd), the backdrop planet and moons, the backdrop station, backdrop rocks and the strict scale cap (no single backdrop rock wider than 0.05 rad), and the play-area planet sink are settled Job 10 scope under R79: planets stay procedural with bake fixes, two station kinds from one Blender kit, backdrop planet bake at 4096x2048, moons at 1024x512. Nothing in this synthesis redesigns any of it. Where a terrain idea touches the sky (for example, aligning a faction sky palette with the fixed board gas), it is a hand-off line to the backdrop work, not a task here.

Terrain scope is the effects that sit on the board inside the play area: nebula hexes, ion storm hexes, debris hexes, and the dead star well's in-board presentation. Board look C (cine) is the default under R72 and is the look every judgement in section 7 is taken in. R65 and R70 remain open FINDINGS items; this job answers the terrain half of both (the environment hexes and the tint), and the backdrop half stays with Job 10.

Coordination with the damage work. R80 (issue 51, Codex issue 66) specifies a squadron smoke ribbon that shares the smoke visual language with this job's gas. Two rules follow. First, the absorptive gas work in section 2 must not change the ribbon's spec: the ribbon keeps its R80 behaviour (one pooled smoke ribbon below half total HP) regardless of how the terrain shaders change. Second, once both the terrain build and the ribbon have landed, one check pass compares them side by side at the default camera, so the board does not end up with two different smoke languages. Whoever runs build task 1 should also send the new ramp approach across to the damage work once, so the ribbon is not later fixed back into grey smoke independently. One message, not a joint project.

## 5. Screenings

Argon leg: screened out in full, filed as a record only. ## 6. Dissents and rejected positions

1. Storm identity (the main dissent). Rey leg position: the storm's accent colour becomes the legend yellow family outright, filaments yellow-white, bolt cores white with a yellow halo, sheet glow yellow-tinted, and violet demoted to a shadow tone, because today almost nothing in the world look is yellow except the flash, while the legend and tint say yellow. DeepSeek leg position: the legend yellow should appear in the tactical tint, in UI, and in brief bolt-lit highlights, but not as a body or glow colour, because the step-2 comment at world_living.gd:265-267 records that light colours over near-black skies read pale and lavender. Qwen leg position: violet-dominant glow with yellow only at density peaks and in lightning, so the storm keeps its threatening darkness. Chair settlement (section 2.3): the body and its darkness follow DeepSeek, the mechanism is Qwen's violet-to-yellow density ramp, and Rey's filaments, sheet glow and tick flare are adopted, so yellow leads the storm's energy while never becoming its body. Neither Rey's full identity swap nor DeepSeek's tint-only yellow is adopted as stated.
2. Nebula patch light. Rey leg: one soft OmniLight3D per patch is core spec, placed at the largest core, because gas colour must land on ships. Qwen leg: no separate point lights for the nebula at all; emissive cores in the shader carry the internal glow. DeepSeek leg: emissive cores, plus at most one low-energy OmniLight3D per patch, judged in playtest. Chair settlement: the DeepSeek middle (section 2.1). One optional light, playtest-judged, with the worst-case light count measured and reported before it can become permanent.
3. Homeworld 3 mechanism. DeepSeek and Rey legs: custom per-pixel ray marching, documented from the developer update. Qwen leg: layered billboard planes, explicitly not ray-marched, documented from a dev diary. Chair: unresolved and marked contested (section 3.1). The build uses only the behaviours both accounts support, so the disagreement costs the project nothing.
4. Nebula faction tint weight. Current code pulls the faction sky colour at about 50 percent (world_living.gd:273-282). Rey leg pick: cap the pull at about 25 percent and apply it mainly to the ramp ends, because the 50 percent pull plus scalar shading is a direct cause of the brown and mauve reads. Qwen leg: the 0.6 ramp stop keeps the 50 percent blend toward the faction colour. Chair: the ramp structure is settled with Qwen's stops; the tint weight at the ends is genuinely the owner's call between learnability and per-battle variety, and it goes to the owner as Q1 with the Rey cap as the chair pick. The build treats the weight as one tunable constant so either ruling is a one-line change.
5. Dead star well intensity framing. Qwen leg pick: the stronger treatment, a visible and dramatic focal point, because the whole battle is arranged around the well. Rey leg pick: stated in structure, subtle in brightness, because a hazard centre that cannot be found is a readability failure, but a bright prop on the board is a different failure. Chair settlement: stated structure, subtle brightness (section 2.5). Qwen's structural items are all adopted; its brightness ambition is not. If the first screenshot round shows a portal read, the accretion hint and centre glow are dimmed first.
6. Ships as silhouettes. DeepSeek leg pick: ships inside a nebula remain visible as rim-lit silhouettes, with the hit penalty carrying concealment. Rey leg pick: ships stay crisp, gas dims stars and sky only, no alpha over ships and no hull fade. Chair settlement: Rey (section 2.2). The rim effect DeepSeek wanted is still available, but it comes from the patch light and the glow cores around the hull, never from gas drawn over the hull.
7. Rejected: ray-marched or VDB volumetrics for our gas. Homeworld 3's documented approach is described by its own developers as resource-intensive and is built for a free camera flying inside clouds; EVE's VDB pipeline needs authored volumetric assets our procedural pipeline does not have. Our camera is fixed at 170 units looking down 34 degrees at flat patches 5 to 14 hexes across. The depth read at that camera is a layering and shading problem, and section 2 solves it as one.
8. Rejected: Sins 2 style whole-board ambience. A patch must end cleanly at its footprint without tinting the whole board, because our patches share a board with clear space, blockers and other terrain kinds. The Sins 2 lessons adopted are consistency of identity and the UI-only warning, not its presentation scale.

## 8. Effort, honestly labelled

Every figure in this section is an unmeasured leg estimate. None of it was timed on this project, none of it is a quote or a commitment, and the chair does not average the legs into a single number, because the legs estimated different task splits on different bases and a blended figure would be false precision.

The Qwen leg is the only leg that priced a complete task list: 43 to 67 hours across its 8 tasks. Its per-task estimates: nebula colour ramp 6 to 9 hours; layer separation and parallax 5 to 8 hours; star attenuation 8 to 12 hours (its most expensive item, because it touches render order); storm glow and flash 4 to 6 hours; debris specular and size work 6 to 10 hours; well enhancement 8 to 12 hours; mask feathering 3 to 5 hours; old clouds ramp port 3 to 5 hours. Its stated basis is the scope of the shader changes plus the testing cycle, with about 15 to 20 percent of the total being playtest iteration against owner 4K screenshots. Its task list maps closely onto section 7, except that section 7 splits its storm and tint work differently and adds the quality ladder as its own task.

The DeepSeek leg estimated in days against a 7 task list: nebula gradient and glow 1 to 2 days; nebula layering, parallax and star occlusion 2 to 3 days; storm body and active read 1 to 2 days; debris material and variety 1 to 2 days; well distortion 1 day; tint harmonisation half a day; quality scaling 1 to 2 days. Summed, that is roughly one and a half to two and a half working weeks, on the stated basis that every named system (_gas, _bolts, _debris, WELL_SHADER) already exists, so the work is modification and tuning rather than greenfield systems. Playtest iteration is additional in its accounting, especially for its layering and storm tasks. Its ranges and the Qwen leg's ranges roughly converge, which the chair records as weak corroboration, not as a measurement.

The Rey leg declined hour figures for most items and estimated in sessions instead: its nebula shader task at one focused session plus one screenshot iteration round, its occlusion and storm tasks at similar or smaller, on the basis that each is one shader or one colour block reusing machinery the earlier tasks built. Three items it refused to price at all, and the chair agrees with the refusal: the shield-tick flare (the Godot presenter for the battle.js:398 tick is not named in the pack), the well setup (the WELL_SHADER setup function is not named in the pack), and any weapon-light interaction with the gas (the pack is silent on whether the gas shader can receive it). For those, the honest estimate is a short investigation with a written finding, then a re-estimate. Build tasks 3, 4 and 7 each contain one such investigation step.

What is shader-only: build tasks 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 9, plus the shader parts of 4 and 7. What needs new elements but no new asset files: the optional patch light (task 10 accounting), the infalling dust and accretion hint (task 7), and the optional wreck lights (task 6). No task needs hand-painted textures or an external asset run; the project has no terrain texture library and this spec does not create one. What needs playtest iteration rather than screenshots alone: light energy and range for the patch light, flash comfort across a full planning phase, and debris tumble speed. Screenshots settle colour and identity; they do not settle comfort over time. The complaint itself came from a playtest, so the chair budgets at least one owner playtest pass after build tasks 1 to 6 have landed together.

## 9. Owner questions

Exactly five. Each carries the chair pick and the stakes in plain words. "Agree with chair picks" rules all five at once.

Q1. Nebula colour identity: fixed violet family for every battle (chair pick), versus faction-tinted per battle as today. The legend colour #b07cff is the cue the player has already learned from the tooltip, the legend and the tactical tint, and the screenshots show what the roughly 50 percent faction pull does to it: warm faction palettes drag the gas to mauve and brown and it stops matching its own legend. The chair pick keeps the violet family as the identity in every battle and caps the faction tint at about 25 percent, applied to the ends of the colour ramp only, so two battles can still feel differently located (a teal-leaning core in one sector, a magenta-leaning core in another) without any nebula ever failing to read as the concealing terrain. Going full faction tint keeps the variety and keeps the bug.

Q2. Ships inside a nebula: keep hulls crisp while the gas dims stars and sky only (chair pick), versus let the gas dim hulls too for a stronger concealment feel. Selection and readability at 4K are the hard constraint: you assess a ship by its hull, its facings and its state, and a dimmed ship inside a pretty cloud is a ship you can misread at exactly the moment the terrain matters. The concealment is already real in the rules, at 15 points harder to hit (battle.js:649); the visual does not need to tax the owner to prove it. The chair pick spends the depth budget around the ship instead: graded star dimming behind it, dark lanes beside it, and the optional violet patch light on its hull, so the ship sits visibly inside something without ever being under something. If a playtest later says the nebula feels too clean, the first lever is body alpha (starting at 0.40), not a hull fade.

Q3. Tactical hex tint: reduce the tint alpha and let the gas carry the terrain read (chair pick), versus keep the tint strong for at-a-glance zoning. R70 is your own note that the board ends up looking like a board game on top of a cool background, especially with the environment hexes, and the tint is a real part of that read: a flat violet tile under grey smoke is two weak signals doing one job badly. Once the gas itself is violet, structured and lit, the tile is redundant weight. The chair pick reduces the alpha (exact values are screenshot-judged in build task 8, not fixed now) and keeps a faint tint, because the Z view still owes the player an honest footprint at a glance, and zoning clarity is a competitive readability feature, not decoration. Keeping the tint strong is the safer-looking choice that quietly preserves the thing you complained about.

Q4. Dead star well: stated structure at subtle brightness (chair pick), versus a stronger, brighter centrepiece, versus quieter than specced. The battle on a deadstar board is arranged around the well, and a hazard centre the player cannot find without switching to the tactical view is a readability failure of the same class as the gas: present in the data, invisible in the world. But brightness is the one property that would fight the ships and the storm for attention, and the well has no flash, no tick and no animation beat that needs selling. The chair pick therefore states everything structural (the lensing ring with a findable rim, the disciplined inward ripples, the infalling dust, the orbiting debris ring) and keeps every brightness value low, with the rim dimmer than a storm filament in a shared frame. Brighter buys drama the board does not need; quieter reopens the findability problem.

Q5. Old clouds in the today and holo looks: do the minimal ramp port as the last build task (chair pick), versus skip it entirely and leave those looks as they are. The case for skipping is real: look C is the default and the living system is what you play, the old clouds show only when a look is deliberately switched, and the port buys nothing for the main path. The case for the port is that the old clouds are the direct ancestor of the smoke read (four additive FBM billboards per hex at gain 0.19 to 0.20, no gradient, no internal light), and leaving them untouched means two of the four shipped looks keep the exact flat grey pattern this whole job exists to kill, one look-switch away. The chair pick is the minimal port, colour ramp only, estimated by the Qwen leg at 3 to 5 hours, explicitly deferrable: if the queue is tight it slips to a follow-up without blocking anything, but it should not be silently dropped.
