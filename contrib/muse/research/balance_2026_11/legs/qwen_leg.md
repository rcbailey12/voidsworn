Headline. The 46-point spread from 34 percent (Sunward Crusade) to 80 percent (the Chorus) is mostly three things: the Crusade loses 22 of its 33 failed runs in sector 1 before its broadside identity can come online, the Chorus regenerates hull every round for free so attrition cannot bring them down, and Eclipse reanimate plus high armour gives effective durability far beyond the printed hull points. A secondary contributor is the veteran brace fix (AI-1), which the pack measures as costing the Crusade row about 8 points in the faction matrix by making enemies survive longer without helping the Crusade's own attack pattern.
Why each faction lands where it does.
Sunward Crusade (34 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion from the data: sector-1 deaths dominate. The pack's death split is 22 / 8 / 3, so two-thirds of Crusade losses happen in the opening sector against Hullbreaker and Hegemony enemies (SECTOR_POOL). The harrow carries the torpedo, and the W table gives the torp evMult 2, meaning a Hullbreaker raider's evasion of 28 counts as 56 against torp fire. Draven's doctrine (torpedoes reload a turn faster, rear-arc hits +50 percent) amplifies a weapon that is already nearly useless against the fastest sector-1 hulls. The starting fleets are modest at CP 6 or 7, and the penitent's evasion of 5 makes it a large slow target.
Second cause, strong conclusion: the veteran brace fix (port_notes step 13, AI-1) costs the Crusade about 8 points. The pack shows the Crusade row dropped from 55.8 to 47.5 with the fix applied. The Crusade's strength is the 40 percent broadside bonus after moving 2 or more hexes (MOVE_RULES); bracing does not help the Crusade attack, but it makes enemies tankier against it.
Third cause, plausible but unproven at fine grain: the 75-point admiral spread (Thale 83.3, Ashgrove 8.3) is the widest in the game and suggests fleet composition matters more than faction stats. Thale starts with a cathedral (hp 220, sh 30) and gets +15 percent hull. Ashgrove starts with a reliquary carrier (sp 2, ev 0) and needs time to build squadrons that never arrive. The pack warns per-admiral numbers at n = 12 or 13 are unstable smoke, so the exact split is uncertain, but the direction is clear.
As an enemy, the Crusade appears mainly as the Daybreak boss. Step 14 raised the Daybreak's speed from 1 to 2 (HULLS.ascendant.sp), taking the boss fight from 81 to 67 percent. The Crusade does not appear in regular sector pools, so it is mostly a boss or mirror threat.
Hegemony Navy (54 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion: ion weapons lose their edge in later sectors. Ion deals 2.5 times damage to shields and nothing special to hull (W table, ion entry). Sectors 2 and 3 include the Chorus and Eclipse, both shieldless. The h_interdictor and h_dominion carry ion weapons, so their primary stripping tool is wasted against half the enemy pool. Against sector-1 enemies (Hullbreakers and Crusade, both shielded), ion is strong, which explains why the Hegemony is stable early but does not scale.
Second cause, moderate conclusion: the Hegemony has no faction price multiplier (FAC_PRICE all 1.0), so they pay standard costs. This is neutral, but it means they lack the cheap-swarm advantage of the Chorus or Hullbreakers. Their starting fleets at CP 5 to 7 are reasonable but not exceptional.
Third cause, plausible: the Hegemony movement rule (backing off one hex costs 1 movement instead of 2) is defensive utility that preserves ships but adds no damage. Under the "baseline" bot policy, this may not be fully exploited.
As an enemy in sectors 1 and 2, the Hegemony's ion weapons punish shielded player fleets. The h_vanguard with hangar 1 adds squadron pressure. They are a moderate threat consistent with their middle-of-table rate.
Ashfall Hullbreakers (62 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion: cheap fast ships. FAC_PRICE gives Frigate .8 and Destroyer .85, so a raider costs 56 and a reaver costs 110. The c_raider at sp 6 and ev 28 is the fastest, most evasive hull in the game. This lets the Hullbreakers field more ships and dodge torpedo fire.
Second cause, moderate conclusion: the boss Grax sits at 49 percent after step 14, just below the 50 percent floor noted in port_notes. This is a tuning note, not a player-faction problem, but it signals the boss is appropriately placed.
Third cause, plausible: Vesh at 83.3 percent (patch 5 percent hull per round, +10 percent armour) is the strongest Hullbreaker admiral; Sable at 46.2 percent is the weakest. Sustained healing appears to beat burst crits in the current meta, but per-admiral numbers are unstable smoke.
As an enemy in sector 1, Hullbreaker raiders and reavers are the primary threat to Crusade runs. Their speed and evasion make them hard for torpedo-dependent fleets to kill.
The Chorus (80 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion: free hull regeneration. The Chorus has no shields; regen is hull regrown per round (b_drone regen 4, b_spitter regen 5, b_razor regen 7). A five-ship fleet regrows roughly 25 to 35 hull per round for free. Repair between battles is irrelevant because they heal during them. Their weakness is focus fire and no shield buffer against burst, but the regen offsets this over the long fights the bot produces (average 12.50 battles, the highest).
Second cause, strong conclusion: very cheap ships. Frigate multiplier .65 makes a drone cost 45 credits. Destroyer .75 makes a spitter cost 100. Mending starts at CP 4 (b_razor, b_drone), the cheapest starting fleet in the game, and still wins 83.3 percent of runs.
Third cause, moderate conclusion: the movement rule (first turn each round costs no movement) gives every Chorus ship a free reposition each round, compounding their ability to control range and avoid focus fire.
Fourth cause, plausible: all four Chorus admirals sit above 66 percent in this execution, and Razormaw at 92.3 is the highest single admiral rate in the game. The pack's per-admiral data is unstable, but the pattern of a high floor across all four admirals is consistent with a faction-level advantage rather than an admiral-specific one.
As an enemy in sectors 2 and 3, the Chorus is a grind opponent. Their regen forces the player to burst them down before they heal back. The tendril weapon (range 1, pierce .5) punishes players who close to melee range.
The Glasswrights (64 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion: extreme evasion and shields compensate for the lowest hull in the game (30 to 140 hp). The i_wisp has ev 28, the i_blade ev 24. The movement rule gives enemies minus 18 to hit after the Glasswright moves 2 or more hexes; other factions need 3 hexes for minus 5. This is a massive defensive bonus.
Second cause, strong conclusion: the noBrace gate (FAC_GATES, data.js:541: ithar noBrace) means the Glasswrights cannot brace. Their defence is entirely shields, evasion, and movement. This is a real weakness against burst that connects, but in practice the evasion is so high that most attacks miss.
Third cause, moderate conclusion: expensive ships. Frigate and Destroyer multipliers of 1.3 make a wisp cost 90 and a blade cost 170, limiting fleet size. The shard weapon (vsSq 1.6) gives strong anti-squadron capability, which is valuable against carrier-heavy enemies.
As an enemy in sectors 2 and 3, the Glasswrights' high evasion frustrates torpedo-dependent fleets. Only 1 sector-3 death suggests they scale well into the late game.
Eclipse (72 percent).
First cause, strong conclusion: reanimate. The Archive restores a fallen ship once per battle at 35 percent hull, flagships excluded (battle.js:480 and 518). This gives the Eclipse fleet roughly an extra ship's worth of durability every battle. Combined with armour .20 to .35, the effective hull pool is much larger than printed hp.
Second cause, strong conclusion: gauss weapons with pierce .6 and kind Null ignore a large fraction of enemy defences. The doom weapon (1x40, pierce .6, cd 2) is a heavy hitter. Eclipse ships trade efficiently because their shots go through shields and armour.
Third cause, moderate conclusion: the movement rule (passes through rocks, wrecks, and enemy ships at no extra cost) gives Eclipse superior positioning and ignores terrain that other factions must route around.
Fourth cause, strong conclusion: Eclipse is the most expensive faction (Frigate 1.45, Destroyer 1.5, Cruiser 1.55, Battleship 1.6). A scythe costs 100, a jackal 195, a harvester 390. This is by design per R63. The question is whether 72 percent is too high for a faction this expensive. R63 says Eclipse should sit near the top; the 8-point gap between Eclipse at 72 and the Chorus at 80 is small.
As an enemy in sector 3, the Eclipse is the hardest fight. Reanimate means the player must kill ships twice. High armour reduces the value of low-pierce weapons.

Out-of-scope or rejected levers.
RAMP curve (battle.js:141). The sector budget scaling may be the real driver of Crusade sector-1 deaths. If the sector-1 enemy budget is too high relative to the Crusade's starting fleet, no data.js hull tweak fully fixes it. RAMP is in battle.js and out of scope.
Reanimate restore percentage (battle.js:480, 518). If Eclipse at 72 percent stays too high after price increases, the 35 percent hull restore is the next lever, but it requires a code change.
Sector pool composition (SECTOR_POOL). Swapping sector-1 enemies would change the Crusade's opening matchups, but that is a structural design choice, not a number tweak.
Glasswright noBrace gate (FAC_GATES, data.js:541). Removing it would help the Glasswrights but is a boolean gate, not a tunable number, and defines the faction's vulnerability profile.
Eclipse carrier. R63 rules that Eclipse has no carrier. Adding one fights the ruling.
Chorus regen removal. Removing regen entirely would flatten the Chorus into a generic swarm and fight R63. The evidence supports reducing regen, not removing it.
Crusade starting fleet recomposition. Ashgrove's 8.3 percent rate suggests her fleet (reliquary, harrow, warden) is mismatched for sector 1. Changing the admiral's start list is a data.js edit but a structural change to the fleet roster, not a number adjustment. Flagging for the owner.
Admiral doctrine numbers. In scope as data.js numbers, but the per-admiral data is unstable smoke at n = 12 or 13. The pack's correction warns splits can move 10 to 20 points between executions. Proposing doctrine changes from that data would be guessing.
