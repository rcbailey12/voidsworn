1. Headline

The 46-point spread is mostly two stacking sustain mechanics at the top and one faction with no sustain story at the bottom. The Chorus (80 percent) regrows hull every round with the cheapest hulls in the game, and Eclipse (72 percent) gets armour, pierce and a free 35 percent resurrection per battle, while the Sunward Crusade (34 percent) has the weakest admiral set, a torpedo tax against the evasive factions that fill sector 2, and an identity built on moving broadsides while the veteran brace fix rewards standing still. The middle three factions sit within 10 points of each other already; the work is at the two ends.

2. Why each faction lands where it does

### Sunward Crusade: 34 percent

Strong conclusions first. The deaths are concentrated in sector 2 (21 of 33 losses), which is exactly where the Chorus and the Glasswrights enter the enemy pool (SECTOR_POOL, section 4.6), and the pack gives two measured reasons the Crusade suffers there. Torpedoes carry evMult 2, so target evasion counts double against them, and the Glasswright roster runs evasion 16 to 28; the Crusade's harrow is a torpedo hull and Draven's whole start list and doctrine lean on torpedoes. Separately, the veteran brace fix (AI-1) is measured to cost the Crusade row about 8 points in the faction matrix, because braced enemies survive longer while the Crusade's own strength, the 40 percent broadside bonus after moving 2 or more hexes, gains nothing from bracing. Second strong conclusion: the admiral spread is the widest in the game (Thale 64, Draven 28, Vex 20, Ashgrove 16, small samples but a 48-point gap). Thale is the only Crusade admiral whose doctrine adds durability (plus 15 percent hull, plus 10 percent shields); Vex gets plus 15 percent broadside damage and Ashgrove gets squadron damage on the joint most expensive carrier in the game (reliquary at FAC_PRICE Carrier 1.25). Plausible but unproven: the carrier price is why Ashgrove trails, and the broadside rule itself is fine but is taxed by longer fights rather than being weak on its own numbers. As an enemy the Crusade barely matters: it appears only through the boss rotation, so almost everything above is player-side.

### Hegemony Navy: 54 percent

The Hegemony is the control faction: no price multiplier in either direction, mid-range hull stats, and it lands mid-table, which is itself evidence that the extremes are mechanic-driven rather than noise. Strong conclusion: ion is a wasted edge in half the late game. Ion deals 2.5 times damage to shields and nothing special to hull, and two of the three sector 3 enemy factions (Chorus, Eclipse) have no shields at all; the interdictor and the vanguard both spend weapon slots on ion. Plausible but unproven: the admiral spread (Reyes 92, Maren 54, Kassar 46, Vorn 25, on samples of 12 or 13) is doing real work. Reyes combines squadrons with cruisers plus 1 wing, and hangar wings cost 0 CP, so his fleet is effectively larger than its CP says. Kassar's doctrine is the only one in the game with a built-in drawback (batteries plus 1 shot, all guns 5 percent lighter damage). Vorn's start list is four small hulls at CP 6, the frailest fleet shape in the pack. As an enemy it appears in sectors 1 and 2 against everyone, so its mid strength is a shared condition.

### Ashfall Hullbreakers: 62 percent

Strong conclusions: the player-side economy is generous where it matters. Frigates at 0.8 and destroyers at 0.85 (FAC_PRICE) make the raider and reaver cheap to buy and cheap to repair (repair is half the missing hull times price), and the raider pairs speed 6 with evasion 28, the joint highest in the game. Their movement rule (sideslips cost 1) and high speeds mean they reach rear arcs, where Sable's and the faction's crit and rear-arc bonuses live. Plausible but unproven: the torpedo tax should hurt them (the reaver and marauder both carry torps), but their main guns are autocannon and scatter, so the tax lands on a secondary weapon, unlike the Crusade where it lands on a hull's purpose. Admiral results are the flattest of the top factions (Sable 75, Grax 73, Vesh 55, Mott 46), suggesting hull and price strength rather than one broken doctrine. As an enemy they are sector 1 for everyone, and the boss matrix puts Grax's boss fight at 49 percent, the hardest measured boss, so they tax other factions' early runs as well.

### The Chorus: 80 percent

Strong conclusion: regeneration that scales with fleet size is the single biggest driver in the pack. Every Chorus hull regrows hull per round (drone 4, spitter 5, razor 7, brood 8, boss 20), a five-ship fleet regrows roughly 25 to 35 hull per round for free, and unlike every other faction the Chorus does not need the repair economy at all, because it heals during the fight rather than between fights. Attrition across roughly nine battles is what a full run is, so a faction immune to attrition wins runs. The prices compound it: frigate 0.65 and destroyer 0.75 give the cheapest fleet in the game (drone 45 credits), so losses are also cheap to replace. The Mending admiral is the cleanest proof: her start fleet is only CP 4, the smallest in the pack, yet she scores 77 percent, because her doctrine adds another 4 percent hull regrown per round on top of hull regen. Deaths are spread evenly (2, 4, 4); there is no wall anywhere. Plausible but unproven: having no shields should expose them to burst and focus fire, the weakness the pack names, but the bot data says burst rarely gets the chance across a whole run. As an enemy in sectors 2 and 3 the same regen taxes everyone else, which slightly compresses the gap rather than widening it.

### The Glasswrights: 64 percent

Strong conclusion: their paper weakness (the lowest hull points in the game, 30 to 140, and no brace per FAC_GATES) is protected by two stacked defences that are both in the data. Evasion is 16 to 28 across the roster, and their movement rule gives enemies minus 18 to hit after the ship moves only 2 hexes, where other factions get minus 5 after 3. Behind that sit the deepest shields relative to hull (wisp shield 12 on hull 30) with regen 6 to 10. The result is a faction whose hull points are rarely reached. Their weapons support it: shard at accuracy 80 and pulsar with pierce 0.6 do not need to close. Plausible but unproven: they do this despite the highest frigate and destroyer prices (1.3), so their strength is defensive mechanics, not economy; Saro at 82 percent on a small sample may be aim and damage doctrines amplifying already-accurate weapons. As an enemy in sectors 2 and 3, their evasion is the other half of the torpedo problem that kills Crusade runs, so trimming Glasswright evasion helps two factions at once.

### Eclipse: 72 percent

Strong conclusion: effective hull is far above printed hull. Reanimate returns a destroyed non-flagship at 35 percent hull once per battle, armour runs 0.20 to 0.35, every hull also regrows 3 to 4 per round, and Null weapons (gauss, pierce 0.6) ignore most of the defences other factions rely on. A fleet that gets one ship back per battle, every battle, has a built-in attrition advantage second only to the Chorus. Also strong: this is partly the owner's design. R63 says Eclipse ships should be stronger and more expensive, and they are (FAC_PRICE 1.45 to 1.6, the dearest in the game), so the question is the size of the gap, not its existence. Deaths are spread evenly (3, 5, 6) with the best sector 3 survival of any faction, consistent with armour and reanimate mattering most in the hardest sector. Plausible but unproven: the thin roster (three line hulls, no carrier, ruled under R63) should cost flexibility and squadron presence, and Seveth (plus 20 percent hull doctrine) and Ostrel both at 82 percent on small samples may flatter the faction. As an enemy in sector 3 they are everyone's wall, so trimming their hull numbers helps every other faction's late run a little too.

3. Ranked changes

Target window: 55 to 65 percent, centre 60 percent. The centre sits one point below the current overall bot rate of 61 percent, which respects R62: the bot is far stronger than a first-time human, so a bot centre around 60 keeps a human first run very hard, and the package must not push the overall rate up. All changes are data.js numbers only. Expected sizes are rough and stacking is not additive; the chair's simulator runs are the real test. I flag where a stack may fall short rather than pretend precision.

1. Chorus regen trim (HULLS table). b_drone regen 4 to 3; b_spitter regen 5 to 4; b_razor regen 7 to 6; b_brood regen 8 to 7. Expected: Chorus down roughly 5 to 9 points, since this taxes every ship in every battle of the run. Risk: enemy Chorus in sectors 2 and 3 also regrows less, which lifts all other factions slightly, including a little of the Crusade gain counted below. Chorus still has the best regeneration in the game by a wide margin, so identity holds. Owner's call: no.
2. Torpedo evasion tax softened (W table). torp evMult 2 to 1.5. Expected: Crusade up roughly 3 to 5 points (its sector 2 wall is evasive fleets), Hullbreakers up roughly 1 to 2. Risk: enemy torpedoes also land more often, including sector 1 Hullbreaker fleets; on balance the factions that build around torpedoes gain more than they lose. Owner's call: no.
3. Crusade weak-admiral doctrines (ADMIRALS table, admiral-targeted). Vex doctrine broadside damage plus 15 percent to plus 25 percent. Ashgrove doctrine squadron damage plus 40 percent to plus 50 percent. Expected: Crusade up roughly 3 to 6 points, by lifting the two admirals at 20 and 16 percent toward Thale rather than touching Thale. Risk: small; both stay inside their existing identities. Owner's call: no.
4. Eclipse regen trim (HULLS table). d_scythe regen 3 to 2; d_jackal regen 3 to 2; d_harvester regen 4 to 3. Expected: Eclipse down roughly 3 to 5 points; reanimate and armour, the core of the identity, are untouched. Risk: enemy Eclipse in sector 3 also regrows less, lifting everyone slightly. Owner's call: no.
5. Crusade hull durability (HULLS table). warden hp 45 to 50; harrow hp 78 to 85; penitent sh 20 to 22. Expected: Crusade up roughly 2 to 4 points. Risk: two-sided in principle, but the Crusade is almost never an enemy outside the boss rotation and the boss hull (ascendant) is unchanged, so this is close to player-only in practice. Owner's call: no.
6. Chorus top-end hull trim (HULLS and ADMIRALS tables). b_razor hp 150 to 135; b_brood hp 200 to 180; Mending doctrine regrow 4 percent to 3 percent per round (admiral-targeted). Expected: Chorus down roughly 3 to 5 points; the razor and brood are the hulls that live long enough for regen to compound. Risk: low; drone and spitter swarms are untouched, so the cheap swarm identity stays. Owner's call: no.
7. Eclipse prices up (FAC_PRICE table). Eclipse Frigate 1.45 to 1.55; Destroyer 1.5 to 1.6; Cruiser 1.55 to 1.65. Expected: Eclipse down roughly 2 to 4 points through slower reinforcement and dearer repairs, since repair scales with price. Risk: low; this moves with R63 (stronger but more expensive), not against it. Owner's call: no.
8. Seveth doctrine trim (ADMIRALS table, admiral-targeted). Seveth all ships plus 20 percent hull to plus 15 percent hull. Expected: Eclipse down roughly 1 to 2 points, aimed at one of its two 82 percent admirals. Risk: low. Owner's call: no.
9. Glasswright evasion trim (HULLS table). i_wisp ev 28 to 25; i_blade ev 24 to 21. Expected: Glasswrights down roughly 2 to 4 points, and torpedo and gun fleets landing a little more against them in sectors 2 and 3. Risk: they remain the most evasive faction in the game; the song and carrier are untouched. Owner's call: no.
10. Glasswright movement rule trim (MOVE_RULES table). Glasswright post-move penalty to enemy accuracy, minus 18 to minus 14 (still far above the generic minus 5). Expected: Glasswrights down roughly 1 to 3 points. Risk: this is the faction's named defensive rule, so the chair may prefer to hold it and take the whole Glasswright adjustment from entries 9 and price instead. Owner's call: yes, because it edits a faction-defining rule rather than a stat line, even though the number itself is small.
11. Kassar drawback removal (ADMIRALS table, admiral-targeted). Kassar doctrine, all guns 5 percent lighter damage to 0 (the batteries plus 1 shot stays). Expected: Hegemony up roughly 1 to 3 points via its 46 percent admiral. Risk: the drawback reads as a deliberate trade for the extra shot, which is why this is flagged. Owner's call: yes, because removing a designed trade-off is a design decision, not a tuning trim.
12. Vorn doctrine lift (ADMIRALS table, admiral-targeted). Vorn aim plus 10 to plus 15; crit plus 10 percent to plus 15 percent. Expected: Hegemony up roughly 1 to 2 points via its weakest admiral (25 percent). Risk: low. Owner's call: no.
13. Crusade carrier price (FAC_PRICE table). Crusade Carrier 1.25 to 1.10. Expected: Crusade up roughly 1 to 3 points, mostly through Ashgrove's reliquary and cheaper carrier repairs. Risk: low; the Crusade keeps no other price break, so its poor-frontier economy still reads. Owner's call: no.
14. Chorus prices up (FAC_PRICE table). Chorus Frigate 0.65 to 0.75; Destroyer 0.75 to 0.85. Expected: Chorus down roughly 2 to 4 points through slower swarm replacement and dearer repairs. Risk: this leans against R63's principle that the Chorus is the cheap faction, though it remains the cheapest in the game after the change. Owner's call: yes, for exactly that reason; take entries 1 and 6 first and use this only if the measured gap is still open.
15. Hullbreaker frigate price offset (FAC_PRICE table). Hullbreakers Frigate 0.8 to 0.85. Expected: Hullbreakers down roughly 1 to 2 points, offsetting their gain from entry 2 so they stay inside the window's top half. Risk: low. Owner's call: no.

Honest summary of the package: the middle four factions should land inside 55 to 65 on these numbers. The Crusade stack (entries 2, 3, 5, 13) is worth roughly plus 9 to 18, which may still leave it a few points under the window, and the Chorus stack (entries 1, 6, 14) is worth roughly minus 10 to 18, which may leave it a few points over. Those two ends are where the chair's measured deltas matter most, and if either falls short, the remaining levers are mostly in section 4, not in bigger versions of these numbers.

4. Out-of-scope or rejected levers

- The RAMP budget curve and the veteran brace fix (battle.js). The brace interaction alone is measured at about 8 Crusade points, but both are code, and the brief puts battle.js out of scope.
- Reanimate's 35 percent and once-per-battle cap (battle.js:480, 518). The strongest single Eclipse lever, but it is code behaviour, not a data.js number.
- Cutting Eclipse prices or raising them for anyone else as the main tool. Making Eclipse cheap fights R63 directly.
- Core and protocol power. R62 rules every core equal, so cores are not a faction-balance lever.
- Removing the Glasswright noBrace gate (FAC_GATES). It is a rule flag, not a number, and bracing Glasswrights would flatten the evasion identity the faction is built on.
- Boss hull tuning (Grax at 49 percent in the boss matrix, the ascendant's speed change). Boss hulls are shared enemy content; moving them shifts every faction's run at once and does not address a player-side spread.
- Squadron weapon multipliers (shard vsSq, flak and spore numbers). Several factions share these tables and the pack gives no faction-specific split to isolate them.
- CP cap or starting credit changes (core.js, campaign_run.js). Economy structure, not data.js tuning, and it would move all six factions together.

5. Not checked

The pack does not let me verify: how the headline faction rate aggregates the four admirals (I have assumed the 50 runs are spread across them, matching the per-admiral samples of 12 or 13); any admiral-level result beyond smoke, since samples of 12 or 13 cannot carry strong conclusions; whether the enemy ever fields admiral doctrines, which decides how two-sided the hull changes really are; the sector-by-sector death splits per admiral, which would sharpen the Crusade diagnosis; what the startorp's evasion multiplier is, so entry 2 may not cover the Glasswrights' own torpedo; repair and yard price interactions beyond the one formula quoted; and any human play at all, since every number here is one bot under one policy, with earlier measurement showing the absolute rate swings widely by policy even when the ordering holds.
