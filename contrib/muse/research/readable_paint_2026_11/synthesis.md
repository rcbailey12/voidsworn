## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS. The standard in section 2 is ready to bind round 6 (#187) and every later hull, subject to the five owner picks in section 6. The one-line lesson the standard encodes: round 5 matched the reference's colour totals and still read as clutter, because readability lives in patch sizes, value steps and shapes, not in how much of each colour a crop contains.

## 1. Why the round 5 paint read as cluttered

All three writer legs converge on the same four mechanisms, and the Argon review confirms each is backed by the pack. In plain words:

1. Value structure: many darks, no dark ground. The recipe assigned different soot shades to many neighbouring surface regions, so the hull carried dozens of small value steps instead of a few big ones. The crop measured darker than the reference (V1 94.76% dark against 85.41%) yet its median luma ran brighter (41.63 and 44.26 against the reference's 30.07): the dark was spread thin and lifted, so nothing formed a stable ground for accents to sit on. The eye reads many small edges as patchwork, not armour.
2. Zone size below the camera. Small bounded regions became trim plates, and rust broke up coats inside plates. On a generated hull those regions are numerous and small. At the battle camera (about 170 units out, about 15 px per unit at 4K, about 7 to 8 px per unit at 1080p) the 41.6-unit barge spans about 620 px at 4K and about 310 px at 1080p, so a half-unit region is roughly 4 to 8 px across: too big to vanish, too small to read as a plate. That dead band, visible but uninterpretable, is what "speckle" means here, and round 5 filled the hull with it.
3. Noise frequency versus the camera. A photo grain (PaintedMetal004 scratches, displacement and roughness) supplied a second, finer variation layer on top of the patchwork. At battle distance its features sit at a few px each, where they cannot resolve into scratches or wear and instead read as shimmer across every surface. On V2 the grain followed a ragged generated surface, so the noise also traced the geometry's own defects. Two noise layers, zone patchwork at mid frequency and grain at high frequency, left no calm area anywhere on the hull. The owner's words map onto this exactly: a cluttered messy mix, too busy, no detail readable.
4. Melted geometry underneath. Both hulls scored 0 of 9 clean construction cells again (fused cylinders, torn panels, floating blocks, blended roots). Every mask the recipe uses (region assignment, curvature, AO, edge wear) is derived from the surface, so on a melted surface the masks are melted too: AO pools where there is no recess, wear follows edges that are not panel edges, and region boundaries cut across forms. Even a disciplined recipe then puts boundaries in the wrong places, and wrongly placed boundaries describe a form that is not there. Round 4 is the control case: the hand-built V3, seven of nine cells clean, read at battle distance under the same recipe family.

And the meta-lesson for every future round: the colour classifier passed both hulls (all bins within 10 percentage points, brightness within 11 to 14 levels) while the owner rejected the paint on sight. Colour totals are a calibration check, not an approval. The round 5 report said so itself: colour bins passing is not visual approval.

## 2. The paint readability standard

Judged on a battle crop at r=170 units, 1:1 pixels. Camera anchors (pack, derived): about 15 px per unit at 4K, about 7.5 px per unit at 1080p; the 41.6-unit Pyre-Barge spans about 620 px at 4K, about 310 px at 1080p. Luma steps are on the 0 to 255 scale throughout.

S1. Tone count. The base coat uses at most three tones, plus one recess tone darker than all three. Trim colour and glow are accents under S8 and never count as tones. Because: R103 specifies few big plates and two or three tones, and round 5 showed many neighbouring shades reading as patchwork even inside one colour bin (all legs).

S2. Plate floor (derived). No zone carrying its own tone may be smaller than 2 units across: 30 px at 4K, 15 px at 1080p. A region smaller than the floor takes its largest neighbour's tone, or becomes trim under S8 if it qualifies. Because: 2 units is twice the test blur radius in S-test (1 unit), so a floor-size zone survives the blur as a shape while anything at 1 unit is erased by it; round 5's sub-floor regions read as speckle (DeepSeek and Qwen both land on 2 units; the blind leg's smaller 1.3-unit floor is not adopted, because its own test then needs a smaller blur than the camera math supports).

S3. Value steps (INFERRED thresholds, chosen by the chair). Neighbouring plates differ by at least 12 luma levels; at major form breaks and at the silhouette the step is at least 25 levels, or the break is carried by a recess shadow under S9. Below the step, zones merge into one. Because: DeepSeek's 12 is the smallest step that reads as deliberate after blur and the blind and Qwen legs both put 25 at the breaks that must survive; the two-tier form takes both observations instead of averaging them. Argon is right that neither number is a pack measurement; they are the standard's chosen thresholds, stated openly.

S4. Zone discipline. Plate boundaries follow a construction edge, a trim line or a deliberate faction seam, and nothing else: never a UV seam, a texture tile edge, or a region border that exists only because the mesh exposed it. Because: round 5 followed mesh-exposed regions and manufactured its clutter there (DeepSeek clause 4, blind mechanism 1).

S5. Wear placement. Wear sits only on real edges and corners, driven by the geometry-baked masks of the settled stack (curvature, AO, edge wear, per R96). No wear on flat plate interiors, no wear following a texture pattern, and no painted dark patches faking recesses where no recess exists. Hand painting stays where R96 put it: hero touch-up on flagships and problem panels only. Because: wear on flat interiors is a new value event at zone scale, which is clutter by mechanism 1, and fake shadow describes form that is not there (blind clauses 5 and 9; Argon cut DeepSeek's "hand-placed wear" line, which contradicted the settled stack).

S6. Rust and break-up. Rust lives inside plates and along real edges, in patches at or above the S2 floor, never scattered across plate boundaries. For the Hullbreakers, the reference faction, total rust in the battle crop stays near the reference's 13.62%: working band 5 to 15% (INFERRED band, chair judgment, anchored on the reference at the top and on round 5 V1's 4.67%, which visibly failed to break up its ground, at the bottom). Rust may form full plates on Hullbreaker hulls, as the shipped look pack's palette itself lists rust red-brown plate, but plates pay out of the same total share; there is no second rust budget. Round 4 V2 is the warning: overwhelmingly rust red failed the soot-and-rust balance outright. Other factions use their own break-up vocabulary (section 4) under the same placement rules.

S7. Grain, the dead band rule. At the battle camera, grain is either invisible or structural. Invisible: local contrast at or under 8 luma levels (INFERRED cap, from the blind leg; below the S3 minimum plate step, so grain can never pose as a plate step), any feature size. Structural: features at least 1 unit across, treated as zones under S2 and S4. Anything visible between those two, contrast above 8 levels at sub-unit feature size, is banned: that is the dead band that produced round 5's fizz. Full-strength grain lives in the close band only and fades with distance (S11). Because: R103 says grain cut at battle distance; the three legs each grabbed one end of this rule (blind the invisible end, DeepSeek the structural end, Qwen a 4 px floor at 15 levels that sits squarely in the dead band and is not adopted).

S8. Trim and light discipline. The faction's trim accent covers at most 2% of the battle crop, with about 1% as the working target (derived: the reference measures 0.97% red trim and 0.00% bright red; round 5 measured 0.53% and 0.52% and its trim still registered). The accent never appears as a base tone, a broad wash or a rust substitute. For Hullbreakers red specifically, R97 and R103 bind without exception: trim plates, markings and light only. Only real lamps, engine throats and faction glow features emit light, as round 5 already enforced; a glow element smaller than the S2 floor at 1080p is removed or merged into a strip, because lights are the highest-contrast marks on the hull and each one reads as information.

S9. Recesses. Geometry AO supplies recess darkening inside real recesses only (panel gaps, undercuts, between stacked forms). A recess sits at least 12 luma levels below its surrounding plate (same step as S3). There is no maximum drop: Qwen's 40-level cap is rejected, because R103 specifies dark recesses and a cap would forbid them exactly on the bright hulls that need them most (Argon's catch, adopted). The recess tone is reserved for depth and is never used as a base tone on open faces.

S10. Value mass, judged per faction. A candidate's battle-crop median luma is compared with its own faction's reference, not with a universal window: target within 5 levels, fail outside 10 (INFERRED tolerances, chair judgment). For the Pyre-Barge reference the anchor is 30.07, so round 5 V1 (41.63) and V2 (44.26) both fail, as they should. DeepSeek's universal 25 to 35 window is rejected: it would fail the Glasswrights' shipped crystal white hull on sight (Argon's catch, adopted). A faction without a measured reference art crop gets one before its first standard judgment, the same way the Pyre-Barge reference anchors Hullbreakers.

S11. Two distance bands. The battle band is judged by S1 to S10 and the test in section 3. The close band is judged separately on material crops, where richer grain and stronger weathering are wanted: round 4 recorded close crops as too subdued on V1 and V3 while the battle view was too busy, and one recipe strength serving both bands is how round 5 happened. Grain and wear strength must therefore fade between the bands, by texture falloff or an explicit distance fade in the material. Whether the close band gets its own numbered clauses is owner question Q4; the separation itself is not optional.

S12. Construction and sign-off. Paint may be applied and tested on any hull, and round 6 does exactly that on V1 by ruling (R103). But a hull's paint is signed off as its finished look only when its construction reaches the bar round 4 set: V3's seven of nine clean cells is the only observed pass point, and no paint has rescued a 0 of 9 hull in five rounds. Below the bar, a blur-test failure is recorded as a geometry failure first and a paint failure second. Whether 7 of 9 becomes the formal sign-off threshold is owner question Q5. (This clause replaces both the blind leg's painting ban, which contradicts R103, and DeepSeek's precondition as a settled rule; Argon caught the contradiction, the chair keeps the sign-off distinction both legs were reaching for.)

## 3. The test: blurred greyscale battle crop next to the reference

Purpose: judge value structure and zone size the way the eye receives them at battle distance, with colour removed so hue cannot rescue a broken value layout.

Procedure:
1. Capture the candidate at battle r=170, 1:1 pixels, same framing as the faction reference art crop, at 1920x1080 (the binding capture, matching the round 5 pipeline) and at 3840x2160 (kept as the 4K record).
2. Convert candidate and reference crops to greyscale (Rec. 709 weights).
3. Blur both with a Gaussian radius of 1 unit at the capture resolution: about 7.5 px at 1080p, 15 px at 4K (derived: half the S2 plate floor, so the blur erases exactly what the standard forbids tone zones to be, and preserves what it allows).
4. Set the blurred candidate beside the blurred reference at the same size. On the unblurred colour crop, also run the round 5 classifier measures: dark share, break-up share, trim share, median luma against S10.

Pass, all required:
- The blurred hull shows its two or three base tones plus recess as distinct large areas, and every surviving area is at or above the S2 floor (15 px across at 1080p).
- No surviving speckle: after a blur that should have erased sub-floor contrast, the open faces are calm.
- The big forms can be named from the blurred crop alone: where the front section ends, where the midsection steps, where the rear fittings sit.
- The trim accent can be found without searching, and no accent blob survives the blur out of proportion to its unblurred size.
- Median luma inside the S10 band for the faction.

Fail, any one:
- The blurred crop still looks speckled or patchy (noise is at zone scale, not grain scale).
- The blurred crop collapses to one flat tone with no form breaks (steps too small, S3).
- A form break visible unblurred cannot be found blurred.
- Median luma outside the S10 fail line.

Examples from evidence:
- Fail: round 5 V1. Colour bins passed within 10 points, yet median luma 41.63 fails S10 outright, its rust at 4.67% sits below the S6 band, and its region-assigned soot shades are the patchwork of mechanism 1: under this test the blur cannot clean it, because the noise lives at zone scale. V2 fails the same way (median 44.26, grain tracing a ragged surface).
- Near-pass: round 4 V3. Hand-built, seven of nine cells clean, palette recorded as reading at battle distance under the same recipe family. Its large plates and dark recesses should survive the blur as a few large value areas with findable form breaks, which is the pass signature. It is near-pass, not pass, on today's evidence: no blurred crop of V3 exists yet, and its close band was judged too subdued, which is an S11 question this test does not cover.
- Pass anchor: the reference art itself, by construction of the comparison, the same anchor the round 5 colour measurement used.

Running the test is a one-time script build (capture, greyscale, blur, side-by-side sheet, classifier measures), then a per-hull unattended run; the owner's part is looking at one sheet per hull. Round 6 (#187) is the first job that runs it, and LOOP.md already holds the round 6 PR for exactly this sheet plus the blurred greyscale readability check.

## 4. Per-faction notes

The standard does not bend on S2 (plate floor), S3 (steps) or the test. It bends on which values the tones take, what break-up is made of, and where wear lives. Palettes are the shipped ones; the chair corrected DeepSeek's Chorus palette (it printed Eclipse-flavoured colours) and its invented Hegemony gold trim against this list before merging.

Sunward Crusade (black iron, frost white, bright ember). Black iron is the base, frost white is a full tone, never a decoration: white plates obey S2 and S3, which they pass easily against black iron, and white used in small pieces turns signal into speckle. Ember is trim and light under S8 only. Recess barely separates from black iron, so form breaks are carried by the white plates and the ember, not by shadow (blind leg). Wear vocabulary: scorch and edge brightening, since soot on black iron is invisible (Qwen).

Hegemony (pale grey, steel blue, implant blue). The pale-on-pale case, and the faction the blur test matters most for, because the hue gap between pale grey and steel blue collapses in greyscale and only the value step survives. Steel blue must work as a full plate tone at S2 size, at the top of the S3 step range. Implant blue is trim and glow under S8. Wear is scuffed paint down to bare metal, never rust: rust belongs to the Hullbreakers' motif, not the Registry's (Qwen, motif ownership).

Ashfall Hullbreakers (soot rust #6a2c22, dried blood red #b8402a, hot red #e0402c). The reference faction, the one the standard's numbers are anchored on. Soot black base, rust as plates and break-up inside the S6 total budget, both reds under S8 with no exceptions. Trophy plates in other factions' faded colours (flagship only, per the settled stack) count as trim under S8 and must be faded enough not to read as a second faction's livery (blind leg). This faction may carry the fleet's highest grain budget in the close band; the battle band's S7 rule does not bend for it.

The Chorus (dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light). Grown, not plated, so plates become zones: a continuous region of one surface kind (a flesh field, a chitin plate grown over it, a bone ridge). S2 and S3 apply unchanged with zone for plate; flesh against chitin gives a natural two-tone structure, with the recess tone in the folds and joins. Chorus wear lives at the joins: scarring, callus, bruising and discoloration where growth meets growth and on leading surfaces, bounded by the growth form, never speckle across open flesh (blind and Qwen converge here). Skin texture is grain under S7 with no exception for being natural; mechanism 3 does not care that the noise is organic. Bile light follows S8: glow only from real glow organs, in strips or clusters big enough to pass S2 at 1080p.

Glasswrights (crystal white #eef3f1, pale gold #d7c88a, mint stored light #9fffd2). The brightest palette, judged against its own reference under S10, never against the Hullbreaker window. A near-white hull cannot use darkness for structure on open faces, so form is carried by facet value steps, internal stored-light shadowing, recess in the joins, and pale gold as trim plates at facet edges. Facets are treated as zones (blind leg): neighbouring facets share tones so the value layout stays coarse even where the geometry stays fine. The test judges the rendered crop, not the method, so value steps earned through facet angle and internal shadow count exactly like steps earned through albedo (Qwen's question 4, chair pick: yes, they count; it is also owner question material only if the owner disagrees). Wear vocabulary: clouding, chips and etching, in zones above the floor.

Eclipse (dark violet-black plates, white-gold corona, violet-white inner glow). The darkest palette, where the failure mode is the flat black slab fail in section 3. Two dark tones alternate on the biggest plates only, stepping by S3's 25 at the breaks that matter (Qwen). The white-gold corona is this faction's frost white: a full value event spent on a few large features, never speckle. Inner glow follows S8 and should run along real structural lines so the glow traces the ship's actual shape instead of decorating it (blind leg). Grain sits at the fleet's low end: the aesthetic is smooth, cold and archival, and heavy grain fights the corona's edge signal.

## 5. How shipped space games keep hulls readable at strategy distance

Screened hard by Argon and the chair. What survives, with its sourcing tier stated:

- Homeworld 3 (sourced: the ship look round 3 synthesis, section 2, from that job's DeepSeek leg): ships carry three UV channels and a packed texture set (base colour, emissive, normal, ARM, and a MASK channel carrying dust/dirt, engine, panel and team colour) plus a dynamic splat damage texture. The large-scale colour layout comes from a small set of masked zones; fine detail lives in channels that cannot fight the zone layout. That is S1, S2 and S11 in production form.
- EVE Online V5++ (sourced: round 3 synthesis, plus the two CCP devblog URLs DeepSeek cited, as narrowed by Argon to what they actually state): packed PBR maps, a four-tone greyscale material map, and a dirt map; dirt accumulates over time and killmarks land as a separate layer on top of the base paint. The transferable point is the layering order: base value structure first, dirt and marks second, never the other way round. DeepSeek's extra detail (dirt pooling in recesses and on lower surfaces) is not what those devblogs say and is cut.
- Homeworld and Homeworld 2 (pattern claim, sourcing cut): both writer legs assert silhouette-first ship identity with bold, simple colour schemes. DeepSeek's GOG forum URL could not be relied on (Argon reports it dead; the chair did not verify and does not cite it), and the blind leg marked its version unsourced itself. Kept as a pattern statement only: silhouette and a few tone masses carry recognition before any detail does.
- Sins of a Solar Empire (pattern claim, sourcing cut): the legs agree that at far zoom the game leans on icons, silhouette and a dominant faction colour with one accent, with surface detail reserved for close zoom. DeepSeek's citations were a Sins 1 modding thread and a forum search URL, neither of which sources the Sins II claim, so no citation is carried. The owner himself rates Sins as this project's presentation benchmark, which is chair knowledge from the owner, not a leg finding.
- Battlefleet Gothic: Armada (pattern claim, sourcing cut): large faction colour fields and per-faction silhouette families carry recognition at fleet distance, with obsessive detail reserved for close inspection. Both legs' citations failed screening (a staging-domain article that does not make the claim, vague press-kit references), so the claim stands as an unsourced pattern note or not at all; the chair keeps it one line long and labelled.

The pattern across all five, and the reason this section exists: shared maps and masks rather than unique projected paint, two or three large value zones, one small accent, and detail scaled to resolve at a closer distance than the strategy camera. Voidsworn's settled stack (R96) already chose the first of those; this standard adds the other three as checkable numbers.

## 6. Owner questions (five, chair picks first)

Q1. Which resolution binds the standard: 1080p (blind pick) or 4K (Qwen pick)? Chair pick: 1080p binds. The plate floor is one physical size either way (2 units is 30 px at 4K and 15 px at 1080p), the round 5 captures and the review sheets are 1920x1080, and a plate that passes at 1080p passes at 4K automatically. Binding at 4K would let plates half the physical size through on the record captures nobody reviews at 4K.

Q2. The value step: two tiers (12 levels between neighbours, 25 at major breaks, S3) or a flat 25 everywhere (blind and Qwen)? Chair pick: two tiers. A flat 25 forces every neighbouring pair into high contrast and pushes mid palettes like Hegemony's out of their own colours; the failure round 5 actually showed was many small steps between many small zones, which S2 and S4 already forbid.

Q3. May rust form full plates on Hullbreaker hulls inside the total rust budget (chair's S6), or is rust break-up only, with the rust tone never a plate (DeepSeek's reading of R97)? Chair pick: plates allowed inside the budget. The shipped look pack's own palette line lists rust red-brown plate, and the budget is what round 4 V2 actually broke. If the owner wants break-up only, S6 tightens with one sentence and nothing else in the standard moves.

Q4. Does the close band get its own numbered clause set now (Qwen pick) or after two hulls pass the battle test (blind pick)? Chair pick: after, sequenced. Round 5 came from one recipe serving both bands; the battle standard plus the test is the gate round 6 needs today, and close-band numbers written before any hull has passed the battle test would be guesses stacked on guesses. The S11 separation itself applies from now.

Q5. Is seven of nine clean construction cells the formal paint sign-off threshold (S12)? Chair pick: yes. It is the only observed pass point in five rounds, V3 read at battle distance and both 0 of 9 hulls failed under every recipe tried. Signing off paint below the bar spends review on geometry failures wearing paint failures' clothes. Round 6 proceeds either way, since R103 already ruled it paints V1 as a test case.
