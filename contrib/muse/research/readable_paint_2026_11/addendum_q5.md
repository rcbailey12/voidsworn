# Addendum: Q5 clarification (Nov 4, 2026, evening)

Filed after delivery, while PR #191 is open, at the owner's request ("let's make sure that's documented"). This addendum records the chair's position on synthesis question 5 after the owner questioned the pick. It does not change the standard's clauses S1 to S12 or the test; it clarifies what the Q5 bar means and where AI mesh sits in the route. Owner rulings on Q1 to Q4 are still open.

## The clarification

1. The 7 of 9 bar is an origin-neutral gate, not an anti-AI rule. Any hull that reaches 7 of 9 clean construction cells under the standard's test can be signed off, however it was built. The bar stands because it is the only observed pass point in five rounds: the hand-built V3 scored 7 of 9 and read at battle distance; both AI hulls scored 0 of 9 under every recipe tried, including a round whose colour totals passed their bins.
2. Hand-built Blender geometry is the authoritative hull. The paint stack (S1 to S12) is geometry-driven: its masks are baked from curvature, ambient occlusion and edge wear, so it can only read as clean as the construction underneath. Melted transitions, fused parts and false curvature in a generated mesh are painted faithfully by those masks, which is why bad paint and bad geometry present as the same failure on the sheets.
3. AI mesh is not layered over a hand-built hull as a skin. Mesh on mesh gives double surfaces, uneven density and break lines that no longer match the construction. The only permitted layering is maps and decals, and AI texturing stays gated on the R96 one-hull unlit PBR proof.
4. AI stays in the toolbox in three places: reference views and proportion studies before a build; a generated mesh as a massing underlay to trace from, never the shipping asset; and the reviewed per-faction part library (R96 Q5), where a part earns its place against a hand part.
5. A different generator is not the lever by itself. A challenger earns adoption only as a per-part test (engine block, gun pair, bridge tower) against hand parts, judged at the battle camera and close zoom, inside the R96 ceiling and this standard's test. No generator gets a whole flagship hull to prove itself; that experiment has run five rounds.
6. Round 6 (#187) is the isolation test for the remaining question: the same simple paint on the hand-built V3 and the repaired AI hull V1. If V1 reads clean, paint was the main culprit and the AI route earns confidence. If V1 improves but lands mid-pack, the next spend is targeted geometry cleanup (part separation, crease seams, normal-wave removal, hero part replacement), not another paint round. If V3 passes and V1 stays near zero, full AI hulls are demoted to massing and parts.
