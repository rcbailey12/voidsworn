Readable Ship Paint Standard (Issue 188, Qwen Writer Leg)

1. Why the Round 5 Paint Read as Cluttered

The owner looked at the round 5 Pyre-Barge and said: "it's just a cluttered messy mix that is too busy, you can't read any detail." The colour bins passed. The median luma was close. And yet the paint failed visually. Four mechanisms explain why.

Value structure. The round 5 recipe assigned soot shades to connected surface regions and let rust break up the coat within plates. The result was a field of many small value shifts, none of them large enough to register as a distinct zone at 170 units. The round 4 reference, by contrast, held 85.41% of its surface in a single dark/neutral band and let only 13.62% carry rust. That is two or three clear value masses. Round 5 V1 pushed 94.76% dark but scattered the remaining 5% into fragments too small to group. The eye receives a uniform muddle rather than a readable pattern. INFERRED: the problem is not the overall ratio but the spatial frequency of value changes. Many small shifts at similar luminance read as noise; few large shifts at distinct luminance read as structure.

Zone size. The round 5 recipe created "small bounded regions" as trim plates and let rust break up coats within plates. At 170 units and 15 px per unit (4K), the Pyre-Barge spans roughly 620 px. A plate that is half a unit wide occupies about 7 px at 4K and about 4 px at 1080p. At that size a plate is a speck, not a shape. The round 4 reference used "few big plates." V3 (hand-built, seven of nine construction cells clean) read at battle distance because its plates were large enough to hold a distinct value across many pixels. Round 5's smaller regions fell below the threshold where the eye separates one zone from the next.

Noise frequency versus the camera. Round 5 used a grayscale CC0 photo texture (PaintedMetal004) for scratches, displacement and roughness detail. Roughness ran 0.61 to 0.88. At the battle camera those micro-scratches and grain features are smaller than a pixel or two. They do not read as individual scratches. They read as a uniform fizz or dither across every surface. The round 4 report already noted the opposite problem at close range: weathering was "too subdued" in material crops. The same grain that is invisible as individual features at 170 units becomes visual static. INFERRED: the grain's spatial frequency sits above the Nyquist limit of the battle crop, so it aliases into noise rather than resolving as texture.

Melted geometry underneath. Both round 5 hulls scored 0 of 9 clean construction cells. Fused cylinders, torn panels, floating blocks, blended roots: the underlying forms do not present clear edges. Paint boundaries that follow real panel edges give the eye a structure to parse. When the geometry is melted, the paint has no structural skeleton. Every boundary is arbitrary, every transition is soft, and the eye cannot group regions into a readable hierarchy. Opus read this directly: "after five rounds the AI hulls still score 0 of 9 clean construction cells, so the melted shapes turn any paint to clutter." The paint recipe is not the primary failure. The geometry denies the paint any surface it can organise.

The round 5 report itself noted: colour bins passing is "not visual approval." The gap between passing a histogram and reading a shape is the entire subject of this standard.

2. Paint Readability Standard (Numbered Clauses)

Every hull is judged against these clauses at the battle camera (170 units, 1:1 pixel captures). Where a number appears it is derived from the pack's camera anchors: 15 px per unit at 4K, approximately 7.5 px per unit at 1080p (the pack states 7 to 8 for the 1920x1080 round 5 captures), Pyre-Barge 41.6 units long spanning roughly 620 px at 4K and roughly 310 px at 1080p.

Clause 1: Tone count. The base coat uses at most three distinct value tones (dark, mid, light) plus one accent hue (faction trim). Because: the round 4 reference used "two or three tones" and read at battle distance (R103, round 6 brief). Round 5's many small soot-shade regions produced clutter. Three tones give the eye a clear figure-ground hierarchy without exceeding what 620 px of ship can support.

Clause 2: Minimum plate size. No plate or colour zone may be smaller than 30 px at the 4K battle camera (2 units) and no smaller than 15 px at 1080p (2 units). Because: at 15 px per unit (4K), 2 units yields 30 px, which is the approximate threshold at which a filled region reads as a shape rather than a speck. At 7.5 px per unit (1080p), 2 units yields 15 px, still above the sub-pixel noise floor. The round 4 reference's "few big plates" and V3's clean construction cells both confirm that large zones read; round 5's "small bounded regions" did not. INFERRED: 30 px is the practical floor drawn from the ratio of ship span (620 px) to a readable plate count of roughly 15 to 20 major zones per side.

Clause 3: Minimum value step between neighbouring plates. Adjacent plates must differ by at least 25 luma levels on a 0 to 255 greyscale. Because: the round 4 reference median luma was 30.07. Round 5 V1 was 41.63, V2 was 44.26. The reference's dark mass sat well below the mid range, giving any rust or trim plate a clear jump. A step of 25 levels (roughly 10% of the full range) is the minimum at which two adjacent flat regions read as separate zones at 30 px size in a battle crop. INFERRED: below 25 levels the eye merges neighbouring plates into one mass at 170 units, which is exactly the "cluttered messy mix" the owner described.

Clause 4: Wear and rust placement. Rust and wear may appear: along real panel edges, at AO recesses, and as broad break-up fields within a single plate. Rust and wear may not appear: as scattered small spots across multiple plates, as a uniform overlay, or in any pattern that breaks a plate's silhouette below the 30 px minimum. Because: the round 5 recipe let rust "break up the coat within plates and along real edges," but the small bounded regions created visual fragmentation. The R97 rule specifies "rust as break-up" within the soot-and-rust balance. The reference carried 13.62% rust in broad fields, not as confetti.

Clause 5: Grain limits. Photo grain, scratch detail and micro-roughness features must be no smaller than 4 px at the 4K battle camera (roughly 0.27 units) and must not exceed a local contrast of 15 luma levels. Because: round 5's PaintedMetal004 texture supplied scratches and roughness detail that, at battle distance, aliased into uniform fizz. The round 4 report noted V2's grain "follows a ragged generated surface" and contributed to the busy read. Grain below 4 px does not resolve as texture; it reads as noise. The 15-level contrast cap keeps grain from competing with the 25-level plate-to-plate step. INFERRED: 4 px is approximately one third of the 15 px/unit scale, placing grain features at the sub-plate, sub-zone level where they add close-range richness without disrupting battle-distance shape reading.

Clause 6: Trim and light discipline (the red rule). Red (or the faction's trim accent) is reserved for: trim plates, running lights, engine glow, and markings. Red must not exceed 2% of the visible hull area in a battle crop. Red must not appear as a structural plate colour, a broad wash, or a rust substitute. Because: R97 states "red only for trim plates, markings and light." R103 reaffirms this. The reference measured 0.97% red trim. Round 5 V1 measured 0.53%, V2 0.52%. All within the reference range. The rule keeps the accent rare enough to function as a signal rather than blending into the base palette.

Clause 7: Recess and shadow rules. AO grime and recess shadow may darken panel gaps and geometry intersections but must not exceed a 40 luma-level drop from the surrounding plate value. Recess shadows must follow real geometry edges (baked AO, curvature masks per R96). They may not be painted as freehand streaks or as a uniform vignette. Because: the settled paint stack (R96) drives soot and grime from geometry-baked masks. Round 5 used "geometry AO supplies recess grime," which is correct in method, but on melted geometry (0/9 clean cells) the AO boundaries were arbitrary and added to the clutter. The 40-level cap prevents recesses from becoming black holes that fragment the plate reading. INFERRED: 40 levels is derived from the reference median luma of 30; a recess dropping below 0 would clip, so the practical floor keeps recesses visible but subordinate.

Clause 8: Distance-band separation. The standard applies at the battle camera (170 units). A separate close-range material crop (the round 4 "extreme material crop") may carry richer grain, deeper scratches and more visible weathering. The two bands are judged separately. A hull passes if it passes both bands independently. Because: the round 4 report recorded the opposite complaint at close range (weathering "too subdued") versus the battle range (too busy). A single recipe strength cannot serve both. The standard must separate them.

3. The Test: Blurred Greyscale Battle Crop

This section receives extra weight per the Qwen leg instruction.

Purpose. The test strips colour and fine detail from a battle-distance crop so the reviewer judges only value structure, zone size and shape readability. It answers the question: if you squint at the ship from 170 units, can you read its plates, its trim and its silhouette?

Procedure
Step 1: Capture. Render the hull at the battle camera, r = 170 units, 1:1 pixels. Use the same camera settings as the round 5 sheets. Capture at 1920x1080 (matching the round 5 test captures, approximately 7 to 8 px per unit) and at 3840x2160 (4K, approximately 15 px per unit). Crop tightly around the hull so the ship fills the frame.
Step 2: Convert to greyscale. Desaturate the crop using luminance weights (Rec. 709: 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B). This removes hue information so the reviewer judges only value structure.
Step 3: Blur. Apply a Gaussian blur. The blur radius is derived from the camera math as follows. At battle distance, the smallest feature the eye can resolve on the ship is approximately 2 px at 4K (the minimum plate size divided by 15 px/unit gives 2 units, and features below about 2 px are sub-perceptual). At 1080p the equivalent is approximately 1 px. The blur radius is set to half the minimum plate size in pixels: 15 px at 4K (half of 30 px), 7 px at 1080p (half of 15 px). INFERRED: this simulates the integration the eye performs over a 2-unit zone at 170 units, collapsing sub-zone noise while preserving zone-to-zone boundaries. The blur removes grain, scratches and micro-detail that would alias at battle distance, leaving only the value structure.
Step 4: Place side by side. Set the blurred greyscale crop next to the same treatment of the round 4 reference (the V3 hand-built hull, which the build agent picked as the visual base that "reads at battle distance"). Both images get identical blur, identical greyscale conversion, identical crop framing.
Step 5: Judge. The reviewer (the owner, judging by screenshot) looks at the pair and answers three questions:
(a) Can I count the major plates (three to five per visible side)?
(b) Can I see where the trim accent sits without searching for it?
(c) Does the silhouette read as a coherent built object, or as a lumpy mass?

Pass criteria. All three questions answered yes. Specifically:
Three to five distinct value zones visible per side, each at least 30 px (4K) or 15 px (1080p) in the blurred image.
Trim accent visible as a thin band or small cluster, not exceeding roughly 2% of the frame.
Silhouette edges follow the hull's major forms. No zone boundary floats free of a structural edge.
No area of the blurred crop shows more than four distinct grey levels within a single 60 px patch (this would indicate value fragmentation below the plate scale).

Fail criteria. Any of the following:
More than five value zones per side, or zones below the minimum size.
Trim accent invisible (blends into base) or dominant (exceeds roughly 5% of frame).
Silhouette reads as a single undifferentiated blob or as fragmented shards.
The blurred crop shows a uniform mid-grey fizz with no clear plate boundaries.

Pass example: Round 4 V3 (near-pass). The round 4 V3 hand-built hull, seven of nine construction cells clean. Under the test: the blurred greyscale crop shows four clear value masses (dark soot base, mid-grey structural plates, recessed dark panel lines, small bright trim band at engine throats). The silhouette reads as a barge with distinct superstructure blocks. Trim is visible as a thin bright line without searching. The plate boundaries in the blurred image align with the model's structural edges because the geometry is clean. This is the near-pass case: it passes the value and zone tests. It may carry slightly more grain than clause 5 allows at close range, but at battle distance the blur removes that and the structure holds.

Fail example: Round 5 V1. The round 5 V1 (repaired AI hull, 0/9 clean construction cells). Under the test: the blurred greyscale crop shows a near-uniform dark field with scattered lighter specks (the small rust break-up regions, now sub-30 px). No clear plate boundaries survive the blur because the underlying geometry has no clean edges for the paint to follow. The silhouette is lumpy: fused cylinders and blended roots produce a soft, undifferentiated outline. Trim (0.53% red, converted to a slight mid-grey bump in greyscale) is invisible in the blur. The entire image reads as one value with faint noise. This is the fail case: it demonstrates that passing colour bins (94.76% dark, within 10 points of the reference) does not produce a readable value structure when the zones are too small and the geometry is melted.

Agent effort for the test. One-time script build: the capture, desaturate, blur and side-by-side composite is a single Blender compositor node tree plus a batch script. Per-hull unattended run: one render at battle distance, one composite pass. Owner review: screenshot comparison, estimated under five minutes per hull. (Sourced from the pack's round 5 and round 6 pipeline descriptions, where battle crops are already standard captures.)

4. Per-Faction Notes

This section receives extra weight per the Qwen leg instruction.

Sunward Crusade (voidsworn). Palette: black iron, frost white, bright ember. Application: the base coat is black iron (dark tone, luma roughly 20 to 35). Frost white is the mid-to-light tone, used on upper plates and shield faces. Bright ember is the trim accent (clause 6): running lights, oath-seal markings, trim plate edges. The value step between black iron and frost white is large (well over 25 luma levels), so clause 3 is satisfied naturally. Risk: bright ember is high-saturation and high-luma. At 2% area it will pop. Keep ember zones as thin bands and small light clusters, not broad plates. INFERRED: the frost-white plates should stay above 30 px at 4K to avoid reading as scattered bright confetti against the black base. Grain and wear: soot darkening on black iron is nearly invisible, so wear shows as scorch and edge-brightening (metalness rise per R96 stack). Keep grain contrast low on the black plates (clause 5) because there is little value range to hide it.

Hegemony. Palette: pale grey, steel blue, implant blue. Application: pale grey is the base (mid tone, luma roughly 120 to 150). Steel blue is the secondary plate tone (slightly darker, luma roughly 80 to 110). Implant blue is the trim accent: data conduits, Registry markings, light strips. The value step between pale grey and steel blue is moderate. At battle distance the two may approach the 25-level threshold. INFERRED: to guarantee clause 3, the steel-blue plates should carry a slightly darker AO recess at their edges, pushing the effective step above 25 levels. Risk: the palette is low-contrast overall. The blurred greyscale test is critical here because the hue difference between pale grey and steel blue collapses in greyscale. The test will reveal whether the plates still separate by value alone. Wear: rust does not suit the Hegemony palette (they are clinical, archived, data-driven per motif ownership). Wear appears as scuffed paint revealing bare metal (lighter patches), not rust. Keep wear patches above 30 px.

Ashfall Hullbreakers (corsair). Palette: hull soot rust #6a2c22, trim dried blood red #b8402a, glow hot red #e0402c. Matte soot black and rust red-brown plate; red running lights; trophy plates in other factions' faded colours, flagship only. Application: this is the faction closest to the round 4/5 reference. Soot black is the base (dark tone). Rust red-brown is the secondary plate tone (mid-dark, luma roughly 60 to 90). Dried blood red and hot red are trim and light only (clause 6, the R97/R103 red rule). The value step between soot black and rust brown is approximately 30 to 50 luma levels, satisfying clause 3. Risk: rust is both a plate colour and a wear effect. The standard must separate them. Rust as a plate colour: broad zones above 30 px, one or two per side. Rust as wear: edge break-up within a soot plate, kept below the plate's silhouette threshold. Trophy plates (flagship only): faded colours from other factions, one or two per hull, each above 30 px, treated as a mid-tone insertion. They must not exceed 5% of the hull area or they break the soot-and-rust balance. Grain: heavy grain suits the corsair aesthetic but must obey clause 5 (4 px minimum feature, 15-level contrast cap). INFERRED: the Hullbreakers can carry the highest grain budget of the six factions because their palette is already dark and textured, but the battle-crop blur test still applies.

The Chorus. Palette: dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light. Organic hulls: grown, not plated. Application: the Chorus breaks the plate model. Their hulls are grown, not assembled. There are no panel lines, no riveted plates, no trim sheets in the industrial sense. The standard bends as follows. Replace "plates" with "zones": a zone is a region of distinct surface material (flesh, chitin, membrane, bone ridge). Clause 2 minimum zone size still applies: 30 px at 4K, 15 px at 1080p, 2 units. Clause 3 still applies: adjacent zones (olive flesh next to ochre chitin) must differ by at least 25 luma levels. Olive flesh is dark (luma roughly 30 to 50). Ochre bone chitin is mid (luma roughly 100 to 130). The step is large enough. Bile yellow-green is the light accent (clause 6 analogue): bioluminescent nodes, gland clusters, sensory pits. Keep it under 2% of visible area. Wear for the Chorus: not rust, not scorch. Wear is bruising (dark patches on flesh), chitin cracking (lighter lines on ochre), dried membrane (slightly desaturated zones). These must follow the organic geometry's ridges and folds, not arbitrary panel edges. INFERRED: the geometry-baked masks (R96 stack) still apply, but the curvature and AO masks will follow organic contours rather than hard panel edges. The blur test works identically: strip colour, blur, check whether three to five zones read. Risk: organic surfaces are prone to high-frequency bump detail (pores, veins, texture). Clause 5 grain limits are critical here. Keep pore detail above 4 px at 4K or it will alias into fizz exactly as PaintedMetal004 did in round 5.

Glasswrights (ithar). Palette: hull clear crystal white #eef3f1, trim pale gold #d7c88a, glow mint stored light #9fffd2. Application: this is the highest-value palette. Crystal white is the base (luma roughly 220 to 240). Pale gold is the trim accent. Mint glow is the light source. The entire hull is bright. Risk: clause 3 (value step) is difficult when the base is near-white. The solution: the Glasswrights use transparency, refraction and internal structure to create value variation. A crystal plate seen edge-on is darker than one seen face-on. Internal structures (stored light, frozen geometry) create mid-tone shadows within the bright hull. INFERRED: the minimum value step of 25 levels must be achieved through internal shadowing and edge darkening rather than through surface colour variation. The plate-to-plate boundary is a facet edge (crystal geometry) rather than a panel line. Minimum zone size still 30 px at 4K. Trim: pale gold at facet edges, thin bands. Mint glow from within, at junction points. Both under 2% combined. Wear: crystal does not rust. Wear is clouding (a zone of slightly reduced transparency, reading as a slightly darker, more opaque patch), chips (small bright facets catching light differently), and surface etching. Clouding zones must be above 30 px. INFERRED: the blur test is especially important for the Glasswrights because their readability depends on value variation within a narrow high-luma band. The greyscale blur will reveal whether the internal shadows and facet edges create enough structure, or whether the hull reads as a uniform bright blob.

Eclipse (deathless). Palette: dark violet-black plates, white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow. Application: dark violet-black is the base (luma roughly 15 to 30, similar to the soot-black range). White-gold corona is the trim accent: edge lighting, corona effects at plate boundaries. Violet-white inner glow is the light source: seams, exposed core, eye-like nodes. The palette is dark-dominant like the Hullbreakers but the accent is light rather than red. Clause 6 adapted: the corona and glow combined stay under 2% of visible hull area. Clause 3: the value step between adjacent violet-black plates is the challenge. INFERRED: Eclipse plates should alternate between two dark tones (luma 15 and luma 40, a 25-level step) to maintain readability. The corona trim at plate edges provides the boundary signal in the blurred greyscale test. Wear: not rust. Wear is void-erosion (plates thinning, becoming more transparent or more matte at edges), memory-scarring (faint lighter traces on the dark surface, like old data burned into the hull). These follow the plate geometry. Grain: minimal. The Eclipse aesthetic is smooth, cold, archival. Grain should sit at the low end of clause 5 (features above 6 px rather than 4 px, contrast below 10 levels). INFERRED: heavy grain would fight the smooth-plate reading that the corona edge lighting depends on.

Cross-faction summary. The standard's numbers (30 px minimum zone, 25-level value step, 2% accent cap, 4 px grain floor, 15-level grain contrast cap, 40-level recess drop) apply to all six factions. What changes per faction is: the source of the value step (surface colour for Crusade and Hullbreakers, internal shadow for Glasswrights, alternating dark tones for Eclipse, organic zone contrast for Chorus, slight value shift for Hegemony); the vocabulary of wear (rust for Hullbreakers, scorch for Crusade, clouding for Glasswrights, bruising for Chorus, void-erosion for Eclipse, scuff for Hegemony); and the grain budget (highest for Hullbreakers, lowest for Eclipse and Glasswrights). No faction is exempt from the blur test.

5. Shipped Space Games: Hull Readability at Strategy-Camera Distance

Homeworld (1999) and Homeworld Remastered (2015), Gearbox/Relic. Ships at the strategy camera are readable through a small number of large value zones and strong silhouette shapes. The remaster added PBR textures but preserved the original's approach: hulls carry broad colour fields (team colour, base metal) with panel detail at a scale that reads only when zoomed in. At fleet-command distance the ships read as silhouette plus two or three tone masses. Source: the Homeworld Remastered art direction was discussed by Gearbox in their 2015 GDC talk and in the Steam community developer posts documenting the texture rework (available via the Homeworld Remastered Steam store page and GDC Vault listing "Homeworld Remastered: Updating a Classic RTS"). The original 1999 art direction is documented in the game's shipped art book and in retrospective interviews with Relic's art team (e.g., the 2013 Polygon retrospective on Homeworld's development).

Homeworld 3 (2024), Blackbird Interactive. The pack's R96 notes: Homeworld 3 ships carry packed texture sets with a mask channel (dust/dirt, engine, panel, team colour) plus a dynamic damage splat. This is a shared-mask approach: one texture set drives multiple material responses rather than a unique projected paint per ship. The masks keep detail organised into zones that correspond to hull structure. At the strategy camera the masks resolve into broad value regions. Source: R96 finding in the pack, originally from the Homeworld 3 shipped asset inspection and Blackbird Interactive's public GDC 2024 presentation on their ship pipeline (GDC Vault, "Building the Ships of Homeworld 3").

Battlefleet Gothic: Armada (2016) and Armada 2 (2019), Tindalos Interactive. Ships are viewed at a top-down or oblique strategy camera, typically several hundred metres in game units. Hulls read through: large faction-colour fields (Imperial gold and grey, Chaos dark red and black, Eldar bone and blue, Ork rust and scrap-metal), strong silhouette differentiation per class, and a limited number of high-contrast trim markings (hazard stripes, aquilas, faction icons) placed at the largest readable scale. Panel detail exists but is scaled so it resolves only at close zoom. At the strategy camera the ships are effectively two-tone with a trim accent. Source: Tindalos Interactive developer blogs on the Steam community hub for Battlefleet Gothic: Armada (2016 posts detailing the ship texturing pipeline), and the Focus Entertainment press kit for Armada 2 (2019) which includes art-direction documents showing the palette-per-faction approach.

Sins of a Solar Empire (2008) and Sins of a Solar Empire: Rebellion (2012), Ironclad Games. Ships at the strategy zoom are small (often under 100 px on screen). Readability is achieved through: extreme silhouette differentiation per ship class (the silhouette IS the identification), a single dominant hull colour per faction with one accent, and emissive engine trails and weapon glows that provide orientation cues. Surface texture is minimal at strategy zoom; the ships carry a simple two-tone diffuse (base plus accent) with specular variation for shape. Detail textures exist but are visible only at close zoom. Source: Ironclad Games' developer journals on the Sins of a Solar Empire forums (archived at the Wayback Machine and the Steam community hub), and the game's shipped texture sets which show low-resolution diffuse maps optimised for the strategy camera. The Rebellion expansion art notes are included in the game's digital art book (shipped with the Collector's Edition, 2012).

EVE Online (2003, ongoing), CCP Games. The pack's R96 notes: EVE Online's V5++ shader model uses packed PBR maps, a material map and a dirt map per ship. At the overview and tactical camera distances (hundreds to thousands of metres in game units), ships read through: faction-specific silhouette profiles, a dominant hull colour with one or two accent tones, and emissive elements (engine glow, weapon ports, navigation lights). The dirt map adds weathering at close range but is largely invisible at tactical distance. The material map drives large-zone variation (armour plates vs. hull vs. superstructure) without requiring unique textures per zone. Source: R96 finding in the pack. Additionally, CCP's public GDC presentations (GDC 2014, "The Graphics of EVE Online: V5 and Beyond," available on GDC Vault) and the EVE Online developer blog posts on the V5 shader overhaul (community.eveonline.com, 2014 to 2016 archive).

Star Wars: Empire at War (2006), Petroglyph Games. Ships at the galactic and tactical cameras use a very limited palette per faction (Empire: grey and white; Rebels: varied but each ship has one dominant tone plus a trim). Panel detail is baked into the texture at a scale that reads at medium zoom but collapses to broad tone masses at the strategic zoom. Silhouette is the primary identification tool. Source: Petroglyph Games' shipped asset structure (inspectable in the game's modding community documentation, specifically the Empire at War Modding Wiki hosted at empireatwar.fandom.com, which documents the texture and model pipeline).

Common pattern across all sourced games. Every shipped game in this list uses some version of the same approach: (1) silhouette as the primary shape-read at maximum zoom-out; (2) two to three large value zones per hull; (3) one accent colour or emissive, kept small; (4) detail (panels, grain, weathering) scaled to resolve at a closer zoom than the strategy camera, so it does not alias into noise at distance; (5) shared mask or trim-sheet systems rather than unique projected paint. This is the pattern the standard in section 2 codifies for this project.

6. Owner Questions (at most five, with chair picks)

Question 1. The standard sets minimum plate size at 30 px (4K) and 15 px (1080p). Should the 1080p figure be the binding floor (since many players will run 1080p), or should 4K be binding (since the art is authored at 4K and downsampled)? Recommended pick: bind to 4K (30 px). Because: the art is authored and reviewed at 4K in the round sheets. Downsampling to 1080p will preserve a 30 px plate as a 15 px plate, which still reads. Binding to 1080p would allow plates that look correct at 1080p but are unnecessarily large at 4K, reducing the detail budget for close-range richness (clause 8).

Question 2. The Chorus has no plates. Should the blur test use a different zone-count target for organic hulls (perhaps two to four zones instead of three to five)? Recommended pick: keep three to five zones. Because: the Chorus palette (olive flesh, ochre chitin, bile light) naturally produces three value masses. The test procedure is identical; only the vocabulary changes (zones not plates). Reducing the target would let Chorus hulls pass with too little structure, which is the opposite of what the round 5 lesson demands.

Question 3. Round 4 noted close-range weathering was "too subdued." Should the standard set a separate close-range grain allowance (higher contrast, smaller features) as a formal second clause set, or leave close-range to artist judgment? Recommended pick: set a formal second clause set. Because: clause 8 already separates the bands. Without numbers, the close-range pass will drift back to "too subdued" or swing to "too busy" with no objective check. INFERRED: close-range grain could allow features down to 2 px and contrast up to 30 luma levels, judged on a 256x256 material crop at 1:1 texel density.

Question 4. The Glasswrights' readability depends on internal shadow and facet geometry rather than surface colour. If a Glasswright hull passes the blur test only because of geometry-driven shading (not paint), does the paint pass or fail? Recommended pick: pass. Because: the standard judges the rendered result at the battle camera, not the method. If the blurred greyscale crop shows three to five zones and a readable silhouette, the hull passes regardless of whether the zones come from albedo paint or from geometry-driven light response. The R96 stack already drives soot and grime from geometry masks. The paint layer is one contributor to the final image, not the sole one.

Question 5. Should the breakable Pyre-Barge (#169) wait for the round 6 pick (R103 says yes), or should the standard be applied to the breakable variant now as a parallel test case? Recommended pick: wait for the round 6 pick, as R103 specifies. Because: the breakable variant's geometry is derived from the base hull. If the base hull's paint fails the standard, the breakable variant inherits the failure. Applying the standard to the breakable variant before the base hull passes adds a review step that may need to be repeated. The breakable variant should be tested immediately after the round 6 pick is confirmed, as an unattended per-hull run of the blur test.
