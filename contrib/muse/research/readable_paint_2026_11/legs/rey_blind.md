# Blind Rey leg (writer): readable ship paint standard (issue 188)

Written from the evidence pack only, before reading any other leg. Mechanisms the pack does not state outright are labelled INFERRED. Shipped-game claims in section 5 are marked with their sourcing status so the chair can screen them.

## 1. Why the round 5 paint read as cluttered

The short version: the colour totals matched the reference and the shapes underneath did not. The paint was measured by how much of each colour appeared in the whole crop, and those totals passed. Readability is not a total. It is about where each tone sits, how big each patch of it is, and whether the patches line up with forms the eye can follow. Round 5 failed on placement, patch size, and the geometry under the paint, even though its colour histogram passed.

Mechanism 1, value structure: the tones were spread across the hull instead of stacked into a few clear steps. Round 5 assigned soot shades to connected surface regions, so many neighbouring regions each carried a slightly different dark shade. On paper the crop was 94.76 percent dark on V1, darker than the reference at 85.41 percent. In the view, though, the dark was not one dark. It was many darks side by side, each boundary a small value step the eye still registers as an edge. INFERRED: a hull reads as one form when large areas share one value and value changes happen at a few deliberate places. When value changes happen at every region boundary, the eye reads patchwork, not armour. The brightness numbers point the same way: round 5 median luma ran 41.63 (V1) and 44.26 (V2) against the reference at 30.07, so the mid darks were lifted into a busier band while the overall dark share also rose. More contrast in more places, in smaller patches, is the definition of busy.

Mechanism 2, zone size: the patches were too small for the camera. Soot went to connected surface regions, and small bounded regions became trim plates. A generated or repaired hull has many small regions, so the paint inherited many small zones. At the battle camera (about 170 units out, about 15 px per unit at 4K, about 7 to 8 px per unit in the 1920 by 1080 test captures), a region half a unit across is about 4 to 8 px. That is below the size at which a patch reads as a plate with a tone of its own. It reads as a speckle. INFERRED: below roughly 10 px across at the capture resolution, a zone stops contributing shape information and starts contributing noise, because the eye cannot group it with anything and cannot ignore it either. Round 5 had many zones in exactly that dead band: too big to vanish, too small to read.

Mechanism 3, noise frequency versus the camera: rust break-up and photo grain added a second, finer layer of variation on top of the patchwork. Rust broke up the coat within plates and along edges, and a greyscale photo texture supplied scratches and roughness detail. At close range that detail is richness; round 4 recorded the opposite complaint in close crops, that weathering and grain looked too subdued. At battle distance the same detail sits at a few px per feature, right at the edge of visibility, where it does not resolve into scratches or wear. It resolves into shimmer and speckle. INFERRED: detail whose features land near the pixel size of the capture is the worst kind, because it is strong enough to see and too fine to interpret. The round 5 report itself says V2's grain follows a ragged generated surface, which means the grain was tracing surface noise instead of sitting quietly inside clean plates. Two noise layers stacked, region patchwork at mid frequency and grain at high frequency, left no calm area for the eye to rest on. The owner's words match this exactly: a cluttered messy mix, too busy, no detail readable. Every scale of detail was present and none of them survived the camera.

Mechanism 4, the melted geometry underneath: both round 5 hulls again scored 0 of 9 clean construction cells, with fused cylinders, torn panels, floating blocks and blended roots. Paint is applied to surfaces, and every mask in the recipe (curvature, AO, edge wear, region assignment) is derived from the surface. On a melted surface the masks are melted too. AO pools in places that are not recesses. Edge wear follows edges that are not panel edges. Region boundaries cut across forms instead of following them. So even a disciplined recipe produces boundaries in the wrong places, and wrongly placed boundaries are read as clutter because they describe a form that is not there. Round 4 showed the contrast: the hand-built V3, with seven of nine cells clean, had a palette that "reads at battle distance" under the same recipe family. Same paint idea, clean shapes, readable result. INFERRED: paint cannot be judged separately from construction on these hulls, and on a 0 of 9 hull no paint standard will pass, because the paint's own guide maps are corrupted at the source. That is why R103 pairs simple paint with the construction question instead of asking paint to fix the hulls alone.

The gap the job names is real and now explained: colour distribution matched, readability did not, because the measurement counted colours and readability lives in sizes, steps and shapes.

## 2. The paint readability standard

Every clause is judged on a battle crop at r=170 units, 1:1 pixels, at 1080p and at 4K. The binding resolution is 1080p unless clause 1 says otherwise, because the round 5 captures were 1920 by 1080 and a plate that only reads at 4K fails most players. Camera math used throughout: about 15 px per unit at 4K, about 7.5 px per unit at 1080p. The 41.6 unit reference hull spans about 620 px at 4K and about 310 px at 1080p.

Clause 1, tone count: the base coat uses at most 3 tones, plus 1 recess tone darker than all of them. Trim colour and glow are accents under clauses 7 and 8 and do not count as tones. Because: R103 specifies few big plates and two or three tones, and round 5 showed that many neighbouring shades read as patchwork even inside one colour bin.

Clause 2, minimum plate size: no tone zone may be smaller than 20 px across at 4K (about 1.3 units) or 10 px across at 1080p (about 1.3 units). A region smaller than that takes its largest neighbour's tone, or becomes trim under clause 7 if it qualifies. Because: below about 10 px at the capture resolution a zone reads as speckle, not plate (section 1, mechanism 2), and zone size was round 5's core failure.

Clause 3, value step: neighbouring plates must differ by at least 25 levels on the 0 to 255 luma scale, or be merged into one zone. The recess tone must sit at least 25 levels below the darkest base tone. The base tones of a dark hull should stay in the lower half of the scale, with the reference's median luma of about 30 as the guide for dark factions. Because: small steps between neighbours still register as edges and multiply boundaries (mechanism 1), and round 5's median luma ran 11 to 14 levels above the reference while reading busier, not clearer.

Clause 4, silhouette step: where two zones meet at the hull's outer silhouette or at a major form break (a turret base, a hull step, a fin root), the step must be at least 40 luma levels or be backed by a recess shadow under clause 9. Because: INFERRED, the silhouette and the big form breaks are what a player reads first at strategy distance, so those boundaries need the strongest signal the palette allows.

Clause 5, wear and rust placement: wear sits only on real edges and corners, bounded by curvature or edge masks, and rust sits only as break-up inside a plate, never crossing a plate boundary and never forming a patch smaller than the clause 2 minimum. Rust share in the battle crop stays between 5 and 15 percent, with the reference's 13.62 percent as the top guide. Wear and rust may not appear on a surface whose construction cell failed (fused, torn, floating or blended), because that surface is rebuilt or left plain base tone. Because: the pack's round 5 recipe already bounded wear to real edges, V2 in round 4 failed by going overwhelmingly rust red, and wear drawn on melted geometry traces the melt (mechanism 4).

Clause 6, grain limits: at battle distance, grain and scratch detail must be either invisible or structural. Concretely, grain features must be no larger than 4 px across at 4K (2 px at 1080p) and no stronger than 8 luma levels of contrast, so at r=170 they merge into surface feel instead of drawing shapes. Richer grain is allowed and wanted in the close band only: full strength in close crops, faded out by the battle distance, whether by texture resolution falloff or an explicit distance fade in the material. Because: round 4 recorded close crops wanting more richness while the battle view wants less noise, so one recipe strength cannot serve both bands, and grain near pixel size is the worst frequency (mechanism 3).

Clause 7, trim discipline: trim colour appears only on deliberate plates, markings and details that pass clause 2 size, and covers at most 2 percent of the battle crop. For Hullbreakers red specifically, R97 and R103 bind: red only for trim plates, markings and light, never as a base tone and never as rust. The round 5 crops measured 0.53 percent red against the reference's 0.97 percent, so the working band is about 0.5 to 2 percent. Because: trim reads as signal only when it is rare and placed; the reference proves about 1 percent is enough to register at battle distance.

Clause 8, lights: only real lamps, engine throats and faction glow features emit light, as round 5 already enforced. Glow colour is the faction's shipped glow colour only, glow features obey clause 2 size at 1080p (a light smaller than 10 px at 1080p is removed or merged into a strip), and no glow may be scattered as decoration. Because: lights are the highest contrast element on the hull, so each one is read as information; stray glow reads as noise with authority.

Clause 9, recess and shadow: geometry AO supplies recess darkening only inside real recesses (panel gaps, undercuts, between stacked forms). The recess tone is the darkest value on the hull and is reserved for depth: it may not be used as a base tone on open faces. Open faces may not carry painted dark patches that fake recesses where no recess exists. Because: fake shadow patches are extra zones with no form behind them, which is clutter by mechanism 1, and on melted hulls AO already pools in wrong places (mechanism 4), so the standard restricts AO to verified recesses.

Clause 10, construction gate: a hull with failed construction cells does not get judged on paint until the failed surfaces are fixed or rebuilt. Paint review applies to hulls at the round 4 V3 level of construction or better. Because: R103 records 0 of 9 clean cells on the AI hulls after five rounds, round 4's clean V3 read well under the same recipe family, and judging paint on melted geometry measures the geometry, not the paint.

## 3. The test: blurred greyscale battle crop against the reference

Purpose: check value structure and zone size the way the eye receives them at battle distance, with colour removed so hue cannot rescue a broken value layout.

Procedure:
1. Capture the candidate hull and the reference (the round 4 reference art crop used in the round 5 colour measurement) at battle r=170, 1:1 pixels, same framing, at 1920 by 1080. Repeat at 4K if the hull is a flagship.
2. Convert both crops to greyscale.
3. Apply a Gaussian blur with radius 5 px at 1080p (10 px at 4K). The radius comes from the camera math: 5 px at 1080p is about 0.7 units, so the blur erases features smaller than about 10 px across at 1080p, which is exactly the clause 2 minimum zone size. Anything the standard allows to carry tone must survive this blur; anything below the minimum is supposed to disappear into its neighbours.
4. Place the blurred candidate and the blurred reference side by side at the same size. Compare three things: the number of distinct value areas on the hull, the share of the hull in the darkest band (judged against the reference crop's balance, which measured 85.41 percent dark or neutral before blurring), and whether the big form breaks of the candidate (bow, midsection, stern, major fittings) can be pointed to without the unblurred image.

Pass criteria, all required:
- The blurred candidate shows at most 4 value areas on the hull (3 base tones plus recess), matching clause 1.
- No surviving speckle: after blur, no isolated value patch smaller than 10 px at 1080p remains visible on open faces.
- The hull's big forms can be named from the blurred crop alone: where the bow ends, where the midsection steps, where the stern fittings sit.
- The dark balance reads close to the reference: the candidate does not look like a light hull or a flat black slab next to it. As a guide, median luma of the unblurred candidate crop stays within 10 levels of the reference's 30.07 (round 5 V1 at 41.63 and V2 at 44.26 would fail this guide).

Fail criteria, any one fails the hull:
- The blurred crop still looks speckled or patchy, meaning sub-minimum zones are carrying contrast.
- The blurred crop collapses to one flat tone with no form breaks, meaning the value steps are too small (clause 3) or the tones merged.
- A form break visible in the unblurred crop cannot be found in the blurred crop.
- Trim or glow blobs survive the blur as large bright or dark spots out of proportion to their unblurred size, meaning accents are oversized or overbright.

Fail example, round 5 V1 (from pack evidence): its colour bins passed within 10 points of the reference, yet the owner rejected it as cluttered, and its median luma of 41.63 sits outside the pass guide. Under this test its region-assigned soot shades and within-plate rust would leave a blurred crop full of small mid-dark patches: many more than 4 value areas, speckle surviving a blur that should have erased it. That is the fail signature: the blur cannot clean it up because the noise is at zone scale, not grain scale.

Near-pass example, round 4 V3 (from pack evidence): the hand-built hull whose palette "reads at battle distance" under the same recipe family, with seven of nine construction cells clean. Under this test its big plates and dark recesses should survive the blur as a few large value areas with findable form breaks, which is the pass signature. It is called near-pass, not pass, on pack evidence alone: the pack gives no blurred crop of V3 and records that its close crops were judged too subdued, which is a close band question this test does not cover. INFERRED: V3's remaining risk under the standard is clause 6's close band richness, not battle distance structure.

Running the test is a one-time script build (capture, greyscale, blur, side-by-side sheet) plus a per-hull unattended run; the owner's part is minutes of looking at the sheet next to the reference. No other effort figures are offered, per R99.

## 4. Per-faction notes

The standard does not bend on zone size, tone count or the test. It bends on which values the tones take and where wear lives, because the six palettes sit at very different places on the scale.

Sunward Crusade (black iron, frost white, bright ember): the dark faction problem in reverse. Black iron is the base, frost white is a full tone, not a trim, so white plates must obey clause 2 size and clause 3 step, which they pass easily against black iron. The risk is white used as decoration in small pieces, which turns signal into speckle. Ember glow follows clause 8 strictly: few, large, only on real fittings. Recess tone barely separates from black iron, so clause 4 form breaks should be carried by the white plates and ember, not by shadow.

Hegemony (pale grey, steel blue, implant blue): the light hull case. Its tones sit in the upper mid range, where 25 luma levels of step are harder to see against space and engine light than on a dark hull. INFERRED: Hegemony hulls should use the top of the allowed step range and let steel blue do the work of a tone, not a tint, meaning blue areas must be full plates at clause 2 size. Implant blue is trim and glow under clauses 7 and 8. The blurred test matters most here, because pale on pale is where value structure quietly collapses.

Ashfall Hullbreakers (soot, rust, dried blood red, hot red glow): this is the faction the standard was built from, via the Pyre-Barge rounds. Rust is a base tone here, not just wear, so rust plates obey clauses 2 and 3 like any tone, and the clause 5 rust share band (5 to 15 percent) applies to rust as wear inside soot plates; rust as a full plate tone counts toward the 3 tone limit instead. Red obeys the R97 rule without exception: trim plates, markings and light only, about 0.5 to 2 percent of the crop. Trophy plates in other factions' faded colours (flagship only, per the settled stack) count as trim under clause 7 and must be faded enough that they do not read as a second faction's livery.

The Chorus (dark olive flesh, ochre bone chitin, bile yellow-green light): organic hulls, grown, not plated, so plates become zones. A zone is a continuous region of one surface kind: a flesh field, a chitin plate grown over it, a bone ridge. Clause 2 applies unchanged with zone for plate: no flesh or chitin zone smaller than 20 px at 4K or 10 px at 1080p carries its own tone. Chitin over flesh gives a natural 2 tone structure, with recess tone in the folds and joins where growth meets, which is where Chorus "wear" lives: wear on an organic hull is scarring, callus and discoloration at joins and leading surfaces, bounded by the growth form, never speckle across open flesh. Grain is the danger: skin texture at battle distance is exactly the mechanism 3 failure, so organic surface texture falls under clause 6 with no exception for being natural. Bile light follows clause 8: glow only from real glow features, in strips or organs big enough to pass clause 2 at 1080p.

Glasswrights (clear crystal white, pale gold trim, mint stored light): the brightest palette in the game, and the standard's hardest test of clause 3. A near-white hull cannot use darkness for structure, so form must be carried by the recess tone in real facets and joins, by pale gold as full trim plates, and by mint glow. INFERRED: Glasswright hulls need their facets treated as plates, meaning facet zones below clause 2 size must share tones with their neighbours even if the geometry keeps the facet edges, so the value layout stays coarse while the geometry stays fine. Transparency and refraction effects, if any, do not exempt a hull from the blurred greyscale test: if the blurred crop has no readable form breaks, the paint fails regardless of how the material behaves up close.

Eclipse (dark violet-black plates, white-gold corona, violet-white inner glow): the darkest palette, where the risk is the flat black slab fail in section 3. Violet-black tones must still step by clause 3's 25 levels, which on a near-black hull means the steps live in a narrow band and must be placed on the biggest plates only. The white-gold corona is this faction's frost white: a full value event, used on few large features, never as speckle. Inner glow follows clause 8 and, INFERRED, should be the main carrier of form breaks at battle distance, placed along real structural lines so the glow traces the ship's actual shape instead of decorating it.

## 5. How shipped space games keep hulls readable at strategy distance

Sourcing note for the chair: two claims below are sourced in the pack itself. The rest are from my own knowledge and are marked UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE, chair to screen or cut, because this leg works from the pack only and cannot browse.

Sourced in the pack (settled paint stack section): Homeworld 3 ships carry packed texture sets with a mask channel (dust and dirt, engine, panel, team colour) plus a dynamic damage splat. EVE Online's V5++ system uses packed PBR maps, a material map and a dirt map per ship. The shared lesson the pack draws: shared maps, masks, trim and decals, not unique projected paint. Readability-relevant point: in both systems the large scale colour layout comes from a small set of masked zones (team colour, material, dirt), and fine detail lives in separate channels that do not fight the zone layout. That is clauses 1, 2 and 6 in production form.

UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE: Homeworld and Homeworld 2 built ship identity on large flat team-colour panels plus a stripe, with mostly bare hull elsewhere, so faction and facing read at full zoom-out. This is the classic argument for clause 7's rarity rule: the coloured area is a minority of the hull and always in the same places.

UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE: Battlefleet Gothic: Armada leans on silhouette and faction-wide colour identity (Imperial green-bronze, Chaos black and brass, Eldar smooth pale hulls) with very large unbroken surfaces, so at fleet camera distance the shape and one colour carry recognition and detail is reserved for close inspection.

UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE: Sins of a Solar Empire uses strong per-faction colour coding and clean, low-noise hull surfaces with detail concentrated at edges and lights, plus heavy use of icons and overlays at far zoom, accepting that the hull itself becomes a simple readable shape at strategy distance. Voidsworn's owner rates Sins as the presentation benchmark, per standing context this leg was given outside the pack: chair to confirm that framing before use.

UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE: EVE Online at tactical zoom replaces hull readability with brackets and overview icons entirely; the hull paint only has to work at hangar and mid-range distances. The lesson for Voidsworn is a boundary one: EVE can let paint be rich because the game does not ask paint to carry recognition at maximum distance. Voidsworn's battle camera at r=170 does ask that, so EVE's dirt and material richness is the part to copy (masked, zoned), not its freedom at range.

UNSOURCED KNOWLEDGE: across real-time strategy games generally, the repeated pattern is detail budgets split by distance band, with texture resolution and shader detail fading as the camera pulls out. That is the industry form of clause 6's two band rule: richness is a close-range property, structure is the long-range property, and shipping both in one texture at one strength is what produces the round 5 complaint.

## 6. Owner questions (five, with picks)

1. Does 1080p bind the standard, or 4K? Pick: 1080p binds. The test captures are 1920 by 1080 and most players will not be at 4K; a standard that only passes at 4K fails in the owner's own review sheets.
2. Should the blurred greyscale test gate every hull's paint sign-off, or only new factions and flagships? Pick: every hull. It is a per-hull unattended run once scripted, and round 5 proved colour bins passing is not approval, so the gate has to sit where the failure actually showed up.
3. Should the 3 tone cap include rust on Hullbreakers hulls? Pick: yes, rust as a plate counts as a tone; rust as break-up inside a plate is wear under the 15 percent cap. Otherwise the faction that caused the problem gets a loophole exactly where it failed.
4. Should a hull that fails construction (the clause 10 gate) be painted at all before rebuild? Pick: no, base tone only until construction passes. Paint bakes on melted hulls were thrown away in rounds 4 and 5, and the masks cannot be trusted on those surfaces.
5. Does the close band get its own richness target (minimum grain and wear strength in close crops), or is close range left to the builder's eye? Pick: set a simple target later, after two hulls pass the battle test. Round 4's "too subdued" complaint is real, but fixing both bands at once is how round 5 got one recipe serving neither; sequence it.
