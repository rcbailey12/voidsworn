## What 2B already gets right (blind view)

Mockup 2B (Action Column, bold) reads the decision in order down one column: Nemesis, the ship that is up, move, fire with per-weapon odds and expected damage, orders, the target with an aggregate outcome line, then the end box with what happens next and End ship last in the mouse corner. The roster carries state words (Ready, Done, LOW, Braced) and segmented bars; the top bar carries the round's acted pips; the log is a four-line ticker with a full-log key. Against the pattern list below, 2B already implements: odds at the point of decision, expected damage next to odds, decision-order layout, consequence preview before ending, roster state at a glance. The gaps I see blind are: enemy intent is prose, not geometry; there is no turn order beyond the next ship; keyword help is absent; end-of-round safety is implicit; and the threat picture (who can reach whom) lives in one sentence in the end box.

## The pattern list (blind ranking)

1. **Telegraph enemy intent on the board, not in text.** Into the Breach shows every enemy's target and damage before you act, which turns tactics into a solvable read. ASSUMED as the single highest-value pattern for a Nemesis-driven game: Voidsworn already computes who can reach the up ship (the end box says "3 can reach Long Watch" in prose). The board version is threatened-hex shading or intent lines for the coming enemy phase. This is the pattern 2B most lacks.
2. **Show the outcome, not just the odds.** XCOM 2 shows hit percentage at the moment of the shot; its known failure is that a percentage alone invites grievance on a miss. Battletech lists per-weapon hit chances and lets the player feel the salvo as a distribution. 2B's "64%, about 22 damage" pairing is the right synthesis; the extension is the aggregate salvo line on the target (2B has it: "about 31 damage") and, blind candidate, a hull-after-salvo marker on the target's bar showing where the bar lands if the expected case hits.
3. **Decision-order layout beats spatial layout.** FTL and Slay the Spire both put the next decision's controls in one fixed place and never move them. 2B is this pattern already; the research should confirm no top-tier game scatters the active unit's controls around the board (Layout 3's following card is the counter-design, and it costs the most to build).
4. **A visible turn order, not just a next-up line.** Battletech's initiative phases and many roguelikes (Monster Train's wave preview) let the player plan two or three activations ahead. 2B names only the next friendly ship, in the end box. A compact order strip (Layout 3 has one) or an order readout in the top bar is the borrowable piece; blind view: a short "next three" line under the acted pips, not the full strip.
5. **End-of-turn safety that scales with what is unspent.** The genre standard is a warning when ending with actions unspent (XCOM 2's confirm on unacted soldiers is the famous friction point; FTL's jump warning is the quiet version). Voidsworn's unit of ending is the ship, not the round, so the safety belongs at the round boundary: when the last Ready ship ends, the end box already previews the enemy turn; the open question is whether ending a ship with unspent move or fire needs the same treatment. Blind pick: no per-ship nag, one round-level line ("4 of yours have not fired") in the end box, which 2B nearly has.
6. **Keyword help on hover, one layer deep.** Slay the Spire and Cobalt Core define their terms inline (hover a keyword, get the definition; hover a status icon, get the number and the rule). Voidsworn's vocabulary is growing (Braced, LOW, Heat, adaptation terms, facing names). 2B shows the words with no visible help layer; the spec's locked rules do not forbid tooltips, and the mock round notes tooltips as not yet shown. Blind: this is a build requirement, not a style option.
7. **The outliner pattern for roster at scale.** Stellaris's outliner and Sins 2's empire tree are the strategy genre's answer to many units: a persistent, sortable, alert-carrying list that never scrolls at expected scale. 2B's roster at 20 ships (one line plus bars, captain line only on the up ship) is the tactics-scale version of the same idea and the stress image shows it fits. The borrowable detail: alert badges on the roster (a ship in danger carries a marker), which ties back to pattern 1.
8. **A resource gauge the player watches like a health bar.** Battletech heat and Starsector flux are the models: one number that gates aggression, always visible, with the cost of the next action shown before committing. Voidsworn has Heat 3 in the top bar as a bare number. Blind candidate: show the heat cost of the selected weapon inline (the fire rows are the place) and the heat state as segments, not digits, matching the bar language everywhere else.
9. **Facing and arcs drawn, not described.** Battlefleet Gothic and Rogue Trader's voidship battles live on arcs; Nebulous draws range and bearing constantly. 2B's fire rows say "Starboard, 5 x 7, range 5" in text and the field shows an arc for the up ship. Blind: the pattern to verify is whether the best fleet games ever let a firing decision happen without the arc visible on the board; if not, the rule for the build is that selecting a weapon raises its arc, which the mock's field already hints at.
10. **The log as a combat record, not a chat feed.** FTL's event log and Deadlock's post-battle readouts treat the log as the story of the fight. 2B's ticker plus full log on L is the right shape; the borrowable detail is damage floaters or hit markers on the field tying log lines to places, which neither the mock nor the spec settles. Blind: rank this last; it is polish, not structure.

## First-pass changes to 2B (blind, to be revised by the chair after the legs)

Keep: the column order; per-weapon odds plus expected damage; the target panel's aggregate line; the roster state words; the ticker log; End ship's corner placement.
Change: Heat from a top-bar number to a segmented gauge with per-weapon heat cost in the fire rows (pattern 8); the end box's threat sentence to a counted line ("enemy turn: 10 ships, 3 can reach Long Watch") plus board shading (pattern 1).
Add: threatened-hex or intent display for the coming enemy phase (pattern 1); a next-three order line (pattern 4); hover keyword definitions (pattern 6); roster danger badges (pattern 7); a hull-after marker on the target bar (pattern 2).
Cut candidates: none in 2B blind; the following-card layout (3) is the cut at the layout level, on build cost and eye travel, unless the search legs surface evidence that board-anchored cards outperform fixed columns.

## Campaign and end-of-run implications (blind)

The same patterns point the campaign map toward the outliner discipline (pattern 7: fleets and threats as a persistent list with alert badges, the Nemesis clock as a gauge, not a number) and the end card toward the war-diary read (the #270 synthesis already borrowed this): a formal, ordered record of the run with the decisive lines first. Slay the Spire's run-history screen and Deadlock's campaign readouts are the references the search legs should check.
