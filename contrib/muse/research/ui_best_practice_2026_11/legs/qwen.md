2. Rulings on the Splits

S1, Determinism of preview. Rule: adopt (c) hull-after-expected marker and (d) per-facing shield depletion forecast. Reject (b) per-weapon best/worst range. Reading reasons: the Fire step is the densest block (five weapons, six-plus data points each at 12 px). Adding ten range numbers (five low, five high) increases text volume by roughly 30 % in that block and forces the eye to parse three numbers per weapon instead of two. The hull-after marker is a single visual tick on the Target panel, zero added text, one glance, answers the kill question. The shield-break clause is one short sentence, well within budget. Expected value stays as the primary number; the marker and clause are secondary channels. This respects the one-scan rule and the 12 px floor.

S2, Undo and soft commit. Rule: recommend a post-confirm, pre-fire move undo as a UI control, but flag that the game-rules permission is NOT IN PACK and must be decided by the owner/designer. Reading reasons: the control is one button (~20 px) in the Move step. It does not add text to any other block. It supports the flagship-at-31 %-hull scenario directly. From the reading axis, the cost is negligible; the benefit is a safety valve that prevents the "bad move, no recovery" spiral. However, this study cannot confirm whether the current build's rules allow it. The pack says the move step has a confirmation gate; whether undo after confirmation exists is unstated. Defer the rules question; recommend the UI slot.

S3, Weapon arc/range text. Rule: CHANGE, not CUT. Keep a compact text token (e.g. "P 1-2" for prow, range 1-2) but do not add a separate icon; instead, free horizontal space by moving the heat cost (P5) to a fixed-width right-aligned tag. Reading reasons: cutting the text entirely (Flash) removes the player's ability to compare weapons across targets without re-selecting, which matters in a five-weapon moment. Adding an icon per Grok adds a visual element that must be designed, tested, and kept consistent across all factions (locked rule: one consistent UI). A compact text token at 12 px is cheaper and already legible. The real problem is total row width; the fix is to make heat cost a right-aligned fixed-width column so the eye scans name, hit %, damage, heat in a consistent left-to-right order, with arc/range as a secondary token between name and hit %. Scan order is preserved.

S4, Turn order display. Rule: reject (b) and (c). Keep (a), the status quo End-box line. Reading reasons: the enemy phase is a block, not an interleaved sequence, and the player chooses friendly activation order freely. A "next three" display implies a fixed sequence that does not exist, creating a false mental model. The End box's existing sentence ("next friendly ship, then enemy turn with 3 ships reaching the flagship") conveys the same information honestly. Adding an order strip or next-three line costs 20-40 px of top-bar or column space and introduces a misleading glance. The acted-ship pips in the top bar already show how many ships have gone. That is sufficient. [CHAIR: see header note on the set variant.]

S5, End-of-round safety. Rule: adopt the counted line, conditional on non-zero. Reading reasons: in this moment, "2 ships still Ready, unfired" is the single most important fleet-level nudge. It prevents the player from ending the flagship's turn and forgetting two ships exist. At ~20 px, it is cheap. Making it conditional (visible only when ships remain unacted or unfired) means it is absent in the common case, preserving the End box's baseline height and training the player to notice it when it appears. A per-ship nag (listing each unacted ship by name) would be too much text; a single counted line is the right granularity.

S6, Column length budget. Rule: the net add from P5 (+~60 px Fire step), P2 marker (+0 px), P2 shield clause (+~18 px Target), S5 counted line (+~20 px End, conditional) totals roughly 80-100 px. This fits within 1080 px if: (i) the up-ship panel's CREW and EVA stats move to tooltips (freeing ~15-20 px), (ii) the Move step and Orders blocks remain at their current compact size, and (iii) no new block is inserted. The one-scan rule holds. Grok's warning is respected: nothing pushes the column past one vertical scan. If a future addition threatens the budget, the next candidates for tooltip demotion are TURNS and ARM, in that order, because they change less often than MOVE or hull/shield values. C3. P1 board shading x P6 arc overlay. Both draw on the board during the Move step. P1 shows threat zones (enemy reach); P6 shows weapon arcs from the hovered hex. C6. P7 tooltips x P3 one-scan column. Tooltips are overlays, not column content. They do not add to the column's resting height.
