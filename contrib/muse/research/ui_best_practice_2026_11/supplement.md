# #292 chair supplement: Stellaris and Sins of a Solar Empire 2

Written by the chair (Rey) because neither search leg gave these two Ryan-named games a full entry: Flash omitted both, Grok mentioned Sins 2 in one unexpanded sentence and Stellaris not at all. Sources are the search results cited inline; claims drawn from community threads are marked as player-reported.

## Stellaris: the Outliner

What it is: a persistent list pinned to the right edge of the screen listing the empire's planets, fleets, and starbases with status at a glance; clicking an entry jumps the camera to it. It is the game's answer to roster at scale in a real-time strategy game with far more than 20 managed objects.

What it does best for the next action: it makes the whole empire monitorable without leaving the map, and every entry is also a navigation control (select in the list, arrive at the thing). The fleet operations players actually perform (merge, split, reinforce) are run from the Outliner.

What it gets wrong, and why it matters here: Paradox's 2023 Outliner rework split the single list into category tabs, and the player backlash is the instructive part: players reported constant back-and-forth between tabs, argued they would rather scroll one combined list than click between split ones, and asked for user-configurable tabs instead. Separately, density pressure is real: a popular "tiny outliner" mod exists purely to shrink row size so more entries fit without scrolling. Paradox's own UX team publicly works this territory (a developer UX walkthrough of new interface work, including fitting new screens into the existing UI's limits).

Screenshot reference: the game's Steam store page media shows the Outliner in play; store page linked from the search results at https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/281990/view/687521707909447820 (news post; the app page is app 281990).

Pattern for Voidsworn: a roster list earns its screen edge only while it stays ONE list. The moment scale pressure splits it (tabs, pages, modes), monitoring breaks. At 20 ships Voidsworn is far below Stellaris scale, so the rule is cheap to keep: never split the roster; shrink rows first (2B already does: one line plus bars at 20 ships). And every roster row should be a jump control, not just a display.

## Sins of a Solar Empire 2: the Empire Tree, the Bookmark Bar, and the Selection Window

What it is: the Empire Tree is a persistent hierarchical list (planets, their structures, and fleets) with auto-pin settings controlling what appears; entries can be pinned and unpinned manually, and clicking navigates. Sins II's developers describe two additions made because the tree alone was not enough: a customizable Bookmark Bar (blocks sized by entity type, hover shows force composition, click cycles through what each block represents) and a redesigned Selection Window for tactical-level selection, described by the developers as faster and more stable to read than the old Empire Tree in a battle; detailed statistics sit behind holding Alt. A selection-based empire window mode also exists, showing only the selected entities and their locations, for players who find the full tree cluttered.

What it does best for the next action: threat reading. Players report that in multiplayer it is easier to judge what kind of threat you are facing in the tree than by looking at the battle itself, and that the tree is the fastest way to react when a planet comes under attack, because clicking goes straight there.

What it gets wrong: the full tree clutters and jumps around as the empire grows; players split between the full tree, the selection-based cut-down mode, and turning it off, and the developers shipped multiple overlapping systems (tree, bookmarks, selection window) rather than one settled answer.

Screenshot reference: developer video blog, "Evolution of Sins II Pt. 2: Game-Changing RTS Features", https://forums.sorcererking.com/528312/video-blog-evolution-of-sins-ii-pt-2-game-changing-rts-features

Pattern for Voidsworn: two, and they point in opposite directions, which is why this pair belongs in the study. (1) A persistent list is where experienced players read threat, which supports putting danger state INTO the roster (badges on threatened ships), not only on the field. (2) A list that tries to show everything about everything collapses into clutter and spawns escape hatches; 2B's discipline (bars plus one state word, detail in the column and in tooltips) is the right side of that line, and the Alt-for-detail habit matches the keyword layer (pool P7): detail on demand, never permanent.

## What the supplement changes in the pool

- P4 (roster triage) gains its strongest strategy-genre evidence: Stellaris and Sins 2 both prove the persistent list at scales far beyond 20 ships, and both prove its failure modes (splitting, clutter). Add to P4: roster rows are jump controls; danger badges on rows are supported by the Sins threat-reading reports.
- No new pattern is added. The supplement's main effect is on the S-splits: it argues against any change that splits, tabs, or pages the roster (relevant to S6's column and roster budget), and it supports P7's detail-on-demand.
