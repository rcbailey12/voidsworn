1 SCOPE AND PURPOSE

1.1 The measurement

This standard is a specification for a measurement, not a specification of how to generate the images that are being measured. It specifies a measurement of a feature-free ship or station image. It may be applied to a wider class of ships or stations and/or other classes that present identical feature-free images.

This standard applies to a large corpus of available pertinent canonical surface measurements. In Phase I, you will use a single image. Through Phase II, you will use the complete corpus, which is an average of 50 images per target class. The work is nevertheless a complete phased task: the measurement is a measurement, not a model, and you will use the completed Phase II analysis as a "synthetic data" task at the rate at which a proponent of the concept produces a real measurement, provided the proponent is "good," to use that data to create an artificial adversary that is statistically signed as a movie's story arc of the image's actions, and out of the black hole of mathematical equivalence classes.

You measure a CAVE OF NO MEANING. It is a measurement of an image, a measurement of a surface detail structure that will act as a metric, a CAVE that is a dimensionless number (see 1.2).

What you measure is not the meaning of the ship or station; it is the structure of the ship or station. The purpose of this standard is to establish a with-noise hull and zero colour range in ONE image of a constituent image of a ship or station. The combined measurement uses a single image per ship or station, but this is cheap compared to the CAVE OF NO MEANING for each ship or station. That value is the CAVE of the hull. This standard therefore specifies the CAVE of the hull.

The hull is the surface of a ship or station. You measure its detailed properties: the distance-based 3D locations and shape, and the distribution of the ship or station's components along the exterior texture of the ship or station. You measure its detailed properties: the structural detail of the panel lines, the weapon emplacements, the thruster placements, and the annotations of the ship or station.

The image you measure must have the properties you wish to have measured, that is, a CAVE that is a dimensionless number. The value of the CAVE is the density of the surface texture. The CAVE gives you a measure of the density of the surface texture -- that is, the density of panel lines, weapon mounts, thrusters, and so on -- of the hull. The value of the CAVE is the parameter of the ship or station as the relation between a target image and the number of items it contains.

The CAVE is a graph-like structure. It represents the distribution of the ship or station under measurement. A CAVE distribution is not a product of the human movement, but the ship or station's "ideal" distribution, which is the exact distribution of the ship or station under measurement.
