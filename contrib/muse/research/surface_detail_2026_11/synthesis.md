# Close-range surface detail standard: chair synthesis (board issue 200)

Chair synthesis of four legs (blind Rey leg, DeepSeek, Qwen, Argon review), grounded in the issue 200 evidence pack: FINDINGS R96, R98, R99, R100, R104, R105, R108 and R109, the readable-paint standard (issue 188 synthesis, ruled R105), the station detail brief (issue 172 synthesis, ruled R100) and the ship surface methods research (issue 112 synthesis, ruled). FINDINGS rulings override every other doc. Numbers are labelled as in the pack: derived (computed from the camera anchors, arithmetic shown), quoted (from a ruling or filed synthesis, source named), or INFERRED (a chosen threshold, marked). No human-hour estimates anywhere (R99); effort is stated in agent terms only.

## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS. The standard in section 2 is ready to bind the station detail pass (issue 199) and all later hull work, subject to the five owner picks in section 5. The one-line lesson: the paint standard (R105) governs colour and value, but a surface can pass it and still read as plain shapes (R109 on stations) or as a long box (R108 on the round 6 V3). What was missing is a geometry floor: how much detail, of what size, how densely packed, and what it may cost. That floor is this standard.

## 1. The two failures this standard answers

R109 (stations): the station repaint followed the paint standard and still "looks super plain and just like regular shapes." The station geometry baseline explains it: a 46-unit spine whose top faces all sit on one plane, with detail kinds the station brief itself confines to a narrow band. Paint cannot manufacture form that is not there.

R108 (ships): the hand-built round 6 V3 passed the construction bar (7 of 9 clean cells) with the standard paint and still read as a long box, because what it lacked was silhouette-internal mounted detail, not surface finish. Round 7 (issue 198) answers by adding the reference's outline features back (dishes, scoops, gun barrels, folded claws, chains, rocket clusters). This standard generalises that answer into clauses every hull is checked against, so the fix does not depend on one owner's screenshot per ship.

The interlock problem, from the pack: geometry detail creates edges, and edges are where the paint masks (curvature, AO, edge wear) fire. Too little geometry and the paint has nothing to hold onto; too much small geometry and every mask fires everywhere, which is the round 5 clutter failure (issue 188, mechanism 4). The standard therefore regulates detail as clusters with floors and ceilings, never as scatter.

## 2. The standard

Scales (derived from the pack's camera anchors): battle band judged on the binding crop at r = 170 units, 1:1 pixels, 1920x1080, about 7.5 px per unit. Close band judged at 46 units (about 27.7 px per unit: 7.5 x 170/46) and 30 units (about 42.5 px per unit: 7.5 x 170/30) for stations, and on the nine-cell studio renders for ships. A 2-unit feature is 15 px at the battle camera; a 0.5-unit feature is about 14 px at the 46-unit zoom and about 21 px at the 30-unit zoom.

A face, for measurement: a contiguous surface region bounded by construction edges, authored break lines, or major form breaks (definition INFERRED, from the blind leg; it is what makes the area clauses measurable).

### Part A: detail kinds and minimum sizes

D1 (bands judged separately). A feature is specified, built and judged per band. Passing the battle band does not excuse failing the close band, and the reverse. This is the geometry side of paint clause S11.

D2 (battle band shape floor, 2 units). At the battle band a mesh feature counts as shape only at 2 units or larger, measured by its largest dimension across the visible surface; station additions count by proud height instead (at least 2 units proud of their surface, per the issue 172 additions doctrine). Shape kinds: equipment pods, sensor housings, antenna array bases and grouped antenna arrays, dishes and dome tops at 2 units and up, engine bells and nozzles, armour plate stacks, bridge tiers, grouped masts, tanks and drums, docking hatch assemblies, heat radiator panels, gun mounts and barrels, and the round 7 outline catalogue (dishes, scoops, gun barrels, folded claws, chains, rocket clusters). The CLAUDE.md blob sweep rebuild catalogue is the standing list of forms that always carry hand detail: guns, bridge tiers, masts, antennas, tanks and drums, bow, engine nozzles, armour plate stacks, sponsons, thin spiky sticks rebuilt as grouped forms.

D3 (secondary band, 1 to 2 units). Features of 1 to 2 units are secondary read at the battle band. Kinds: clamp housings, hatch rings, dish and dome tops under 2 units, dock lip frames, sensor heads, coupler assemblies. A secondary feature may support a shape feature or a cluster; on its own it does not break a plain region at the battle band (INFERRED, blind leg: a 7.5 to 15 px element does not change how a large face reads at r = 170).

D4 (close band mesh). At the close band, mesh detail is legal from 0.5 units up (about 14 px at the 46-unit zoom, about 21 px at the 30-unit zoom). For ships this 0.5 to 1 unit band (bolt rings, coupler collars, raised guide strips, recessed light housings, vent recesses, grouped conduit runs) is an INFERRED extension proposed by the blind leg and adopted by the chair: the pack derives that 0.5-unit geometry is fully legible at the judged close zooms, and the read rules only forbid it as wasted geometry below 0.5 units. For stations the issue 172 language binds unchanged: close-zoom mesh detail lives in the 1 to 2 unit band, and below 1 unit is trim sheet and normal content. Below 0.5 units anywhere, and below 1 unit at the battle band, content is trim sheet, decal or normal work, never mesh.

D5 (never model). No mesh below 0.5 units anywhere on a hull or a station (pack read rule: wasted geometry). No feature between 0.5 and 1 unit may be presented or counted as battle-band detail. Subdivision of a displaced or wavy generated surface is not detail (issue 112 mechanism: adding triangles subdivides the wrong surface and does not flatten it). Detail comes from part splitting, mounted parts, trim sheets, mesh decals and floaters, and baked maps (the issue 112 method set).

D6 (mounting and facing). Every mounted detail part sits on the real surface on a visible mount and carries the surface detail level of its host (CLAUDE.md). Guns face forward or to the side, never backward (CLAUDE.md). Soft or blobby generated areas near the bridge and superstructure are rebuilt by hand before detail is judged there (CLAUDE.md blob sweep).

### Part B: plain area, density and spacing

D7 (plain area, battle band; thresholds INFERRED, DeepSeek leg, adopted over the blind leg's looser 10 units and 100 sq units). On any face, no unbroken flat region may exceed 6 units in any direction or 36 sq units in area, measured on the battle crop (a 6-unit run is 45 px at the battle camera). A region is broken by any of: a shape feature per D2; an outline feature per D2; a construction edge carrying a major value break (at least 25 luma, paint clause S3); a recess meeting S9 (at least 12 luma below the surrounding plate). Trim seams, grain and decals do not break a region at the battle band; they are sub-floor content under S2 and S7. Paint-only value changes inside one plate do not break a region.

D8 (plain area, close band; thresholds INFERRED, DeepSeek leg, adopted over the blind leg's 4 units and 16 sq units). On any primary face (a face that fills a full cell of the nine-cell sweep or a full close capture), no unbroken flat region may exceed 3 units in any direction or 9 sq units in area, measured on the close captures or the nine-cell renders (a 3-unit run is about 83 px at the 46-unit zoom). A region is broken by any D2 or D3 feature, a composed fine cluster per D10, or a recess meeting S9 whose relief is at least 0.5 units proud or deep (relief floor INFERRED, blind leg). Trim seams alone do not break a close-band region: seams at about 1.5-unit spacing are trim content (issue 172), and a face broken only by trim is exactly the surface that reads plain in a close capture.

D9 (spacing, the noise gap, and the density ceiling). At the battle band, elements closer than 4 units edge to edge read as noise (pack read rule; 4 units = 30 px). Elements inside 4 units of each other are legal only as one composed cluster: sharing a mount, sharing a base plate, or aligned on one construction edge, so the group reads as a single form at least 2 units across (composition test INFERRED, blind leg). Derived density ceiling: minimum pitch is 2 + 4 = 6 units, so at most one shape feature or cluster per 36 sq units of face at the battle band (arithmetic from the pack's 2-unit floor and 4-unit gap).

D10 (density floor, DeepSeek leg, thresholds INFERRED; cluster discipline, blind leg). Battle band: at least one detail cluster per 100 sq units of face, where a cluster is 3 to 8 elements each at least 2 units across. Close band: at least one detail element of 0.5 units or larger per 25 sq units of face. Fine detail (0.5 to 1 unit) must sit in clusters anchored on a feature of at least 1 unit or on a construction edge; even scatter of fine detail across a face is banned. Inside a composed cluster, minimum edge-to-edge spacing is 0.5 units (INFERRED: about 21 px at the 30-unit zoom, so neighbouring elements stay individually legible instead of merging into grain). Uncomposed elements keep at least 2 units spacing at the close band. Fine clusters cover at most 30 percent of any single face (INFERRED), leaving clean plate for S2 zones and S3 value steps to work on. Read together, D9 and D10 make density a band with a floor and a ceiling, not a minimum to exceed: detail denser than the D9 ceiling is the round 5 clutter failure wearing geometry's clothes.

D11 (outline requirement, from R108; blind leg). Surface detail alone cannot pass a hull. In each cell of the top row and each cell of the side row of the nine-cell sweep, at least one mounted or outline feature must punctuate the silhouette or the major outline of that cell's segment. Station outlines are governed by the additions doctrine (R100, issue 172) and are not re-judged by this clause; the plain-area rules D7 and D8 do apply to stations. (DeepSeek's alternative formulation, at least 30 percent of battle-band clusters being outline features, was considered and not adopted: two metrics for one failure invites double bookkeeping, and the per-cell count is the check the owner's sheets already support.)

### Part C: interlock with the paint standard (S1 to S12)

D12 (tone budget, with S1 and S2). A detail element that carries its own tone counts against S1 (at most three base tones plus one recess tone). Default: detail takes the host plate's tone. An element under 2 units across may not carry its own tone at the battle band; it takes the largest neighbour's tone or becomes trim under S8. Trim carried on detail elements counts toward the S8 cap (at most 2 percent of the battle crop) exactly like trim anywhere else (DeepSeek leg, adopted).

D13 (mask discipline, with S5 and S6). Geometry detail creates edges, and edges are where the paint masks fire. Every detail edge is a potential wear edge under S5, so the density rules D9 and D10 are also paint clauses: they are what stops wear and AO from firing everywhere, the round 5 clutter mechanism. Detail edges are bevelled by default (bevels plus weighted normals, the issue 112 method set) so wear reads as controlled lines. Detail must not fragment a rust plate into patches below the S2 floor (S6: patches at or above the floor; Hullbreaker rust 5 to 15 percent of the battle crop).

D14 (dead band, with S7 and S11). Geometry between 0.5 and 1 unit, spread outside clusters, produces visible but non-structural grain at the battle band. S7 bans exactly that band in paint; D10's cluster rule is the geometry side of the same ban: inside a cluster of at least 2 units across, the group reads as structure at r = 170 while its internal grain stays in the close band, and its paint treatment follows the S11 fade (full strength close, faded at battle distance).

D15 (lights, with S8). Recessed lights are geometry housings; the glow inside them obeys S8: glow only from real lamps, engine throats and faction glow features, and a glow element smaller than the S2 floor at 1080p is removed or merged into a strip. Light housings count as detail elements for D9 spacing and D10 clustering (INFERRED application, blind leg).

D16 (value mass guard, with S9 and S10). Detail adds AO and wear edges, and both darken. After any detail pass, the battle crop median luma is re-measured against S10; a detail pass that pushes the median outside the faction band fails, however good the detail reads up close. This guard is written from the R108 outcome: both round 6 hulls came out nearly all black. Recesses created by detail must meet S9 on the sheet (at least 12 luma below the surrounding plate), verified by that measurement, not assumed from recess depth. (DeepSeek's proposed build rule, recesses at least 0.5 units deep, is recorded as build guidance, INFERRED, not as a clause: S9 is a measured-value clause and the measurement governs.)

Sequencing note (from R105). R105 defers close-range PAINT clauses until two hulls pass the battle-distance test. Nothing in part C writes those clauses early. Part C constrains geometry so the existing battle-band paint clauses still hold, and states how close-band geometry behaves under the already-ruled S7 and S11 fade. The close-band paint clauses, when they come, sit on top of D4 and D10 unchanged.

Not adopted: DeepSeek's LOD-set requirement (three shipped LODs per class, battle LOD dropping sub-1-unit features). Nothing in the pack or the measured pipeline establishes that the game ships per-class LOD sets, and the R96 ceiling numbers were measured on single meshes in the test scene. A detail standard judges the shipped mesh on the sheets; if frame costs later force an LOD architecture, that is an Opus and Codex engine call, and this standard's per-band clauses then apply per LOD. The requirement is recorded here so the idea is not lost, but it is not a clause.

### Part D: budgets

D17 (all-in budgets). Each class budget below includes every triangle in the hull asset: shell, surface detail, and the R99 break kit (rim bands at break lines and openings, the decimated dark inner liner, break-piece interiors). Nothing rides on top of the cap. This is the reading that enforces R99's own words (the intact ship stays inside the R96 ceiling) as a render-scene number, since the ceiling itself was measured on ships in a scene.

D18 (detail share; shares INFERRED, blind leg). Of a hull's triangle budget, close-band-only detail (features under 2 units) may take at most 25 percent on fleet-default hulls (variant 2, clean shell plus filtered bake) and at most 40 percent on hero hulls (variant 1, A+ rich surface). The variant split itself is the ruled production default from issue 112. Battle-band shape detail is not share-capped; D9 spacing caps it. The share cap exists because close-band detail is invisible as shape at r = 170: uncapped, it eats the D17 break-kit room and the fleet reserve while buying nothing at the battle camera.

D19 (fleet budget table; per-class figures INFERRED, blind leg). The R96 ceiling is about 306,000 triangles and 3.9 ms at 20 ships in the test scene (quoted, R96). Assumed fleet mix (INFERRED, stated so the sum is checkable): 8 small craft, 6 frigates, 4 cruisers, 2 capitals or flagships; 8 + 6 + 4 + 2 = 20 ships.

Triangles:
- Small craft: 1,500 each x 8 = 12,000
- Frigate: 10,000 each x 6 = 60,000
- Cruiser: 20,000 each x 4 = 80,000
- Capital / flagship: 60,000 each x 2 = 120,000
- Fleet total: 272,000. R96 ceiling: about 306,000. Reserve: 34,000 (about 11 percent), held for break-state pieces in play and hero-hull escalation inside D18.

GPU (ms in the R96 test scene): the ceiling implies an average rate of 3.9 / 306 = about 0.0127 ms per 1,000 triangles; at the budgeted 272,000 triangles that rate predicts about 3.47 ms. Per-class caps (INFERRED) sum slightly above that prediction, to absorb per-object overhead on the small classes, and stay under the ceiling:
- Small craft: 0.025 ms each x 8 = 0.20 ms
- Frigate: 0.14 ms each x 6 = 0.84 ms
- Cruiser: 0.27 ms each x 4 = 1.08 ms
- Capital / flagship: 0.75 ms each x 2 = 1.50 ms
- Fleet total: 3.62 ms. R96 ceiling: 3.9 ms. Reserve: 0.28 ms.

Cross-check: DeepSeek's independent derivation, a linear rate from its own mix, lands at 0.0122 ms per 1,000 triangles against the blind leg's 0.0127 implied by the ceiling; two routes to the same rate support the caps' order of magnitude. DeepSeek's per-class figures themselves are the raw Sins of a Solar Empire II anchors, which the pack states are explicitly not a Voidsworn budget, and its table counts two stations inside the ship ceiling; that framing is rejected, because the issue 172 synthesis (ruled under R100) already corrected it: a station budget is additive headroom to be justified, not a carve-out of the 20-ship ceiling.

D20 (stations; quoted, R100). Design target about 20,000 triangles and 0.35 ms per station; hard ceiling 40,000 to 45,000 triangles and 1.0 ms. Detail additions spend only the gap between the counted baseline and the design target; if the counted baseline already exceeds the target, additions are capped at the hard ceiling minus the baseline (INFERRED rule, blind leg; no measured triangle count for the current stations exists yet, and the first Codex detail job counts first). Station detail follows the additions doctrine: locked primitives do not move, resize in plan, or get deleted. Station budgets sit outside the 20-ship fleet sum in D19; whether a combined scene needs its own ceiling is owner question Q3.

### Part E: break-line fit (R98, R99)

D21 (no crossings). No detail element may cross an authored break line. Break lines per R98: two or three authored lines per hull, with matching interior faces and damage textures. Per R99, capital hulls break on two transverse lines plus one longitudinal line. Per the issue 172 brief, stations carry two authored break lines per station kind at structural junctions. Every detail element sits wholly inside one break segment.

D22 (clearance and rim bands; distance INFERRED, blind leg). Mounted detail keeps at least 1 unit clear of a break line, on both sides. Rim bands at break lines and at openings (R99) carry no mounted detail, no trim and no decals: they are reserved for the hot-edge read. One exception, quoted from R100: station truss and gantry members get authored damage decals at break lines instead of interior faces. DeepSeek's wider 2-unit clearance is owner question Q5; the chair pick is 1 unit, on the blind leg's arithmetic (section 5).

D23 (liner and interiors are exempt, by design). The decimated dark inner liner (R99) and all interior faces take no surface detail, and D7, D8 and D10 do not apply to them; plain there is correct, because the liner's job is to never read as empty (R98 game side: every cut and broken piece gets a solid inside). Full interiors exist only in the break pieces (R99): their decks and bulkheads are structure, not surface detail, they are not judged by the density clauses, and their triangles count inside D17.

D24 (damage room). Damage masks live inside the R96 paint graph at the break lines (R99). The D22 clearance is what leaves them room to read. In the intact state, detail outside the clearance may carry S5 wear; it may not carry damage state. A detail element that would be cut in half by a break is a D21 failure at build time, not a damage effect.

D25 (Chorus). Chorus interiors are flesh and bone (R98), and broken Chorus pieces show flesh and bone insides with hot edges that cool, as at every other faction's break lines. For Chorus hulls the close-band catalogue in D4 is read organically: bone ribs, flesh folds and growth rings count as breaking features under D8, and panel-seam language does not apply to Chorus surfaces (INFERRED application of R98, blind leg). The plain-area maxima and the cluster rule still apply; only the vocabulary of what counts as a feature changes.

## 3. The pass/fail check

The check runs on the sheets that already exist: the ship nine-cell sweep (bow, midship, stern, each from top, side and below, studio renders, each cell judged clean or rebuild by hand), the paint standard's binding battle crop (r = 170, 1080p), and the station judgement set (4K plan view, battle angle at about 170 units, close zoom at 46 and 30 units, with a docked battleship and frigate for scale). The issue 194 proposals (a screen-space feature filter in the kit assembler; automating the measurable half of the paint gate) are design input for automating the sheet measurements below; they are proposals, not rulings, and the check does not depend on them.

Sheet-carried measurements (Codex measures on the sheet; a pass is a number inside the clause limit):
- D2, D3, D4 feature sizes: measure feature widths on the battle crop at 7.5 px per unit and on close captures at about 28 and about 42 px per unit. A tone-carrying element under 15 px wide on the battle crop fails D12.
- D7 plain area, battle band: largest unbroken run and area on the battle crop, px converted at 7.5 px per unit (limits: 45 px run, 36 sq units = 2,025 sq px area).
- D8 plain area, close band: largest unbroken region on each nine-cell render (ships) and each close capture (stations), converted at the capture's px per unit (3-unit limit: about 83 px at the 46-unit zoom, about 128 px at the 30-unit zoom).
- D9 spacing: edge-to-edge gaps between uncomposed elements on the battle crop; any gap under 30 px between elements that do not form one cluster fails.
- D10 clustering: judged on close captures and nine-cell renders; scatter outside clusters fails against the clause. Cluster coverage per face is estimated from the same captures for sign-off; the exact coverage figure is a mesh statistic (see below).
- D11 outline: count per cell on the top and side rows of the nine-cell sweep; a cell with no outline or mounted feature fails that cell.
- D12, D15, D16 paint interlock: the existing paint test machinery, re-run after the detail pass (tone count, trim share, glow audit, median luma against S10, recess drops against S9). Detail is judged only on sheets whose paint test still passes.

Clauses that honestly need a mesh statistic, not a sheet:
- D17, D18, D19, D20 budgets: triangle counts per hull, per class and per part group (shell vs detail vs break kit), plus test-scene timing for the ms caps. No sheet shows a triangle count. Cost of the check: a one-time script build, then a per-hull unattended run.
- D7 and D8 true area: a sheet shows projected area, and a face seen at an angle understates its true plain area. The sheet check above is the judgement of record (the owner judges by sheets), but the true largest unbroken region per face is a mesh face analysis (same one-time script build). If the two disagree, the mesh figure governs the clause and the sheet figure governs the sign-off conversation.
- D8 relief floor (0.5 units): relief reads on a sheet only at grazing angles. Verify by mesh measurement.
- D21 and D22 break-line fit: crossings and the 1-unit clearance need the mesh with break lines marked, or one added render per hull, a break-line overlay sheet (one-time script build, per-hull unattended run). Not judgeable from the standard sheets, which do not draw the break lines.
- D5 sub-floor audit: the smallest modelled feature on a hull is a mesh statistic (a per-hull feature-size report from the same script build).

Verdict rule. A hull passes the detail standard when all three hold: (i) the battle crop checks pass (D2, D7, D9, and the D12 to D16 paint re-run); (ii) at least 7 of 9 cells of the nine-cell sweep are clean with the detail clauses applied per cell (the 7 of 9 bar is R105 and S12 for paint; applying the same bar to detail sign-off is INFERRED); (iii) the mesh statistics sit inside D17 to D19. A station passes when no capture in its judgement set fails its band's clauses (INFERRED threshold; the station set has no cell grid to take a fraction of) and its mesh statistics sit inside D20. Detail sign-off is separate from paint sign-off under S12, and a hull needs both: R108 is the proof that one without the other still ships a plain box.

## 5. Owner questions (five, chair picks first)

Q1. Does the fleet budget bind to a fixed test mix (8 small craft, 6 frigates, 4 cruisers, 2 capitals, D19), or do per-class caps stand alone? Chair pick: adopt the fixed mix. The R96 ceiling is defined at 20 ships, and a stated mix is what makes the fleet sum a single checkable number (272,000 tris, 3.62 ms). Cost of the other option: per-class caps alone cannot fail a battle that stacks capitals; every new composition needs its own sum, a one-time checker script build plus an unattended run per composition, and the standard loses its single pass figure.

Q2. Are the plain-area maxima right as adopted (battle band 6 units and 36 sq units; close band 3 units and 9 sq units, D7 and D8, INFERRED, the tighter of the two leg proposals)? Chair pick: adopt as adopted and recalibrate after the first two hulls pass, mirroring R105's sequencing of close-range paint clauses after two hulls pass the battle test. Cost of loosening now: the R109 plain read survives on exactly the large faces the rule exists to fix. Cost of tightening further: more detail per hull, more unattended build iterations before a hull reaches its sheet, and more owner review minutes per sheet round.

Q3. Does a scene that fields a station together with 20 ships need a combined ceiling (fleet 3.9 ms plus station up to 1.0 ms at the R100 hard ceiling), or is the station judged alone under R100? Chair pick: judge the station alone for now; write the combined ceiling only when a level actually fields both at once. Cost of the other option: one combined test scene (one-time build) plus a timing run per station kind, spent before any such scene exists.

Q4. Do the close-band detail share caps stand (25 percent fleet default, 40 percent hero, D18, INFERRED)? Chair pick: adopt. Cost of the other option: nothing to build, but every later hull carries the round 5 clutter risk uncapped and the break kit competes for the same triangles with no rule to settle the argument.

Q5. Is the break-line clearance 1 unit (D22, INFERRED, chair pick) or 2 units (DeepSeek leg)? Chair pick: 1 unit. Arithmetic on the 41.6-unit Pyre-Barge with two transverse break lines (R99): clearance sterilizes 2 lines x 2 sides x 1 unit = 4 units of hull length, 4 / 41.6 = about 10 percent; at 2 units it sterilizes 8 units, about 19 percent, before the longitudinal line's band is counted. Cost of the other option: fewer legal mounting positions near the middle of the hull and thinner detail on the midship cells, which are the cells that already read plainest.
