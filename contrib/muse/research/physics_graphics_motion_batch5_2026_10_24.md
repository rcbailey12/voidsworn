# Research batch 5: ship physics, graphics, and motion for Voidsworn

Read-only synthesis. No posts, votes, messages, or repo writes. Sources are ranked by usefulness to Voidsworn, not by popularity. Sourced claims are tied to a listed source; anything else is labeled [Inference]. Summaries are in my own words; no code and no long passages are pasted. Note on scope: the feel target (BattleTech weight plus Sins of a Solar Empire 2 presentation plus Rogue Trader small-fleet turn structure) is already decided, so this report covers how to achieve it, not whether to pursue it.

## Priority 1: Ship motion in turn-based space tactics

**1. Battlestar Galactica: Deadlock (WEGO turns, hologram path previews, 3D climb and dive)**
https://primagames.com/featured/battlestar-galactica-deadlock-review
https://wolfsgamingblog.com/2017/09/05/battlestar-galactica-deadlock-review-time-to-frack-some-toasters/
Substance: Deadlock is the closest shipped analogue to Voidsworn's structure: a turn-based WEGO system where both sides input orders, then the turn plays out in real time over about 15 seconds. Movement is plotted with glowing green holograms showing where ships will move, and the player adjusts heading, yaw, and height (climb or dive) from that preview. Space is 3D with a Z axis, so vertical separation avoids collisions and gives a vertical advantage. Ships have directional armor (top, bottom, sides, rear), so players rotate ships to take damage on undamaged faces, and turret firing arcs are directional, so positioning to bring guns to bear is the core skill. A ship posture mechanic lets the player bias each vessel toward attack or defense for that turn. Fighters can be set to defense around important ships and can shoot down approaching missiles. Battles can be replayed with an auto-camera that follows the action.
Relevance: ADOPT the preview pattern and the playback shape. One line why: hologram path previews plus a short fixed playback window are exactly how a turn-based space game shows movement before commitment and then resolves it cinematically without letting animation time balloon.

**2. Vector movement: drift markers and the half-thrust rule**
http://theminiaturespage.com/boards/msg.mv?id=201632
https://blog.firedrake.org/archive/2014/04/Painfully_Realistic_Vector_Movement.html
Substance: Tabletop vector-movement games split movement into two phases: a drift phase where the ship moves according to a drift marker placed last turn, then a thrust phase where the player moves or turns the ship (or moves the drift marker), with the thrust effects felt on the next turn. The classic example: drift three spaces, thrust two more, and next turn you drift five. A separate analysis points out the common simplification error: most games apply the full speed change on the turn of thrust, but acceleration applied gradually over a turn means only half the speed change should land on the thrust turn, with the rest arriving on subsequent turns, and it gives a two-marker procedure for tracking this exactly.
Relevance: ADAPT the drift-marker idea, not the full bookkeeping. One line why: Voidsworn already has delta-v offsets from a reference plane, and a visible drift indicator (where this ship will carry to if it thrusts nothing) gives movement the same readability that Deadlock's hologram paths give to plotted moves, while the half-thrust rule is a warning against instant speed changes that feel weightless.

**3. Nebulous: Fleet Command (angle-tanking, component damage by facing, heading versus travel)**
https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/nebulous-fleet-command-is-the-hardcore-version-of-homeworld-i-didnt-know-i-wanted
Substance: Every ship is a 3D arrangement of components and armor plating, and the components on the side facing the enemy get hit first, so charging straight at an armor-piercing enemy fills your forward components (including bow thrusters) with holes and leaves you unable to reverse out. The counter-skill is angle-tanking: give the ship a heading that forces it to fly at an angle to its direction of travel, accepting inefficient thruster use so incoming shells strike armor at an angle and deflect some damage, and keep rolling the ship to spread punishment across faces rather than letting one face absorb everything. Damage control teams roam the ship schematic repairing systems under fire, and a breached reactor is itself a weapon. Missile launches can be "cold" (thrusters ease the missile around the launching ship before the main engine engages) to guarantee a clean line of flight.
Relevance: ADAPT the facing lesson, SKIP the full simulation. One line why: per-location damage already exists in Voidsworn's feel target, and Nebulous shows why facing and roll matter tactically (which face takes the hit is a decision), while its full component simulation is far heavier than a 20-ship turn-based game needs.

**4. Homeworld formations and stances (formation costs maneuverability, stance routes power)**
https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/mac/178957-homeworld-remastered-collection/faqs/26234
https://www.gameskinny.com/?p=191263
Substance: Homeworld's formations are a trade-off, not a free bonus: ships in formation must move as one group, so a formed group of capital ships performs time-wasting maneuvers to turn, while an unformed group simply rotates and moves, roughly ten times faster to turn and attack. Formations still earn their place for strike craft (Delta and X for strafing runs, Claw to concentrate fire on one target, Sphere to guard a ship from nearly any angle), and stances route power: Aggressive sends power to weapons and slows the ship, Evasive sends power to engines for speed and maneuverability at the cost of firepower and faster fuel burn, Neutral splits power evenly. Homeworld 3 guides repeat the same shape: keep groups small, because faster ships wait for slower ones and large groups lose maneuverability.
Relevance: ADAPT the trade-off rule. One line why: any formation or stance in Voidsworn should cost something visible (turn rate, speed, or firepower), and capital ships should never be forced into formation choreography that delays bringing guns to bear.

## Priority 2: Physics presentation

**5. The game-feel canon: juice ingredients and starting values**
https://www.manuelsanchezdev.com/blog/game-feel-principles-animated-godot
https://www.slashskill.com/game-feel-and-juice-the-complete-guide-to-making-your-game-satisfying/
Substance: The canon comes from Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho's 2012 talk "Juice It or Lose It," Jan Willem Nijman's "The Art of Screenshake," Squirrel Eiserloh's camera work, and Steve Swink's Game Feel. The ingredients that recur across summaries: hitstop (freeze 3 to 6 frames, about 0.05 to 0.1 seconds, on a heavy impact), camera shake (0.2 to 0.5 seconds, decaying, proportional to the event, with directional bias away from the impact), material flash on the hit target (about 0.1 seconds, never more than 3 flashes per second), knockback and recoil scaled to damage, anticipation (a 0.3 to 0.6 second wind-up before a strike), trails and motion blur for fast movement, and audio carrying half the weight (pitch varied a few percent per hit so repeated impacts do not sound identical). Two rules govern all of it: contrast (quiet moments make loud moments louder, so do not juice everything equally) and easing (nothing in nature moves at a constant rate, so linear motion reads as mechanical and dead).
Relevance: ADOPT as the tuning table for every weapon and impact in the game. One line why: these are the cheapest weight in games (a few lines plus an evening of value tweaking), and they are exactly what turns a damage number into a felt hit without touching the simulation.

**6. Star Citizen damage pipeline (squibs, holes, burn, location-tied particles)**
https://starcitizen.tools/Comm-Link:The_New_Damage_System
Substance: When a ship part detaches, squib and particle effects detonate along the separation edge: squibs cause holes and burn damage to appear on the skin, revealing the ship's interior so the piece looks torn away, while particles throw off smoke, explosions, and sparks. The damage visuals progress through stages (no damage, burn color, dents, bumps from burnt paint and metal, exposed underlying metal, holes cut through the hull, dents at hole borders for depth), and the effects are placed per location based on the ship's systems there, so damage reads as tied to what was actually hit. GPU particles are called out as 10 to 100 times cheaper than CPU particles for the same count, which is what lets the system tie effects precisely to damage state, and the pipeline is explicitly engineered to still run fast with twenty fighters attacking a capital ship.
Relevance: ADAPT the staged-damage language, SKIP the full pipeline. One line why: Voidsworn's damage states should be a short visible ladder (scorch, dent, exposed structure, hole, fire) tied to the location hit, with GPU particles carrying the volume, rather than a single health bar plus a generic explosion.

**7. Outer Wilds component damage (damage that changes handling, and the over-punishment lesson)**
https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/ship-damage-and-more
Substance: Outer Wilds rebuilt its ship as physically modeled systems (landing camera, headlights, autopilot, cockpit window, fuel tank) so damage is specific: land too hard and the landing camera breaks, crash the cockpit and the headlights break, run into an electric field and lights and computers go haywire. The diversity and specificity of the damage make the ship feel more real and more fragile. But the first attempt at this idea failed: deactivating thrusters when they took damage made the ship harder to pilot and forced repairs, which proved far too challenging, and players abandoned ships or lost control and died.
Relevance: ADAPT the specificity, heed the failure. One line why: Voidsworn's soft crits (engine slows, shields off, weapon off, fires ticking) are the right shape because they change behavior without removing control, and the Outer Wilds failure is the warning against any damage state that strands the player.

**8. Missile and torpedo flight time: EVE math plus the lead-error problem**
http://wiki.eveuniversity.org/index.php?title=Missile_mechanics&diff=216833&oldid=85232
https://en.topwar.ru/291858-cham-20-pochemu-vysokoskorostnoj-torpede-trudno-popast-v-cel.html
Substance: EVE's missile model is explicit: a missile accelerates from the launcher, then proceeds at best speed until its fuel runs out, so maximum distance is roughly speed multiplied by fuel seconds (a light missile at 5 seconds and 3750 m/s travels about 18,750 m; a torpedo at 6 seconds and 1500 m/s travels about 9,000 m). Because EVE runs on 1-second ticks, fractional flight times round probabilistically. Critically, range is not a fixed number against a moving target: a target moving away takes longer to reach and may outrun the fuel, while a target moving toward the missile is in range from farther away. The Shkval analysis makes the complementary point: a high-speed torpedo on a pre-calculated trajectory cannot correct after launch, so any target course change after firing creates a growing error between the predicted and actual rendezvous point, and the longer the flight time, the larger the error a modest maneuver can create.
Relevance: ADOPT as the torpedo decision framework. One line why: flight time is not just a delay, it is a dodge window whose size scales with distance, so the torpedo question for Voidsworn is really two questions (does flight time exist, and does the target get to react during it), and both need visible telegraphing or the miss will feel random rather than earned.

## Priority 3: Graphics pipeline for hulls

**9. PBR beat-up metal: get the metal right clean, then add gunk**
https://polycount.com/discussion/135152/pbr-beat-up-metal
https://www.playtex.ai/downloads/the-game-developers-pbr-texture-handbook.pdf
Substance: The core rule from working artists: get the metal correct clean before adding any dirt, because if the metal does not look right clean it will never look right dirty. Edge wear should be slightly smoother than the base metal so it catches better highlights (the modern equivalent of painting scratches white). Scratches behave differently at different scales: individually visible scratches reduce roughness, but scratches as micro detail increase it. Grime belongs as a low-opacity layer in the roughness map (around 5 percent opacity of a contrasty grime texture still reads). Rust and paint are dielectrics, so they belong at metallic 0, while clean exposed metal sits near metallic 1 with roughness spanning roughly 0.15 to 0.75 by polish and oxidation. The typical failure is gray metallic everywhere, which produces a muddy hybrid response.
Relevance: ADOPT as the material audit checklist. One line why: this is directly the language of the kit's paint and material unification work (one metal authority, wear layered on top, rust and paint never metallic), and it gives the audit pass/fail criteria in one paragraph.

**10. Hard-surface modeling: bake the detail, keep the weight low**
https://pinpdf.com/6-techniques-that-define-high-quality-3d-hard-surface-modeli-f2975e3f1330bc88350abde1946ea372.html
Substance: Combined with normal map baking, artists retain the appearance of high-end models at much lower weight and cost, which is one of the main benefits of organized hard-surface modeling. The texturing principles are: each surface should reflect its intended history, wear and tear should be used rationally (placed where handling, heat, and impact would actually put it), the PBR workflow must be adhered to, and realism must be confirmed through lighting tests, because even a plain object can look complicated and operational with proper material mapping.
Relevance: ADOPT as the kit baking rationale. One line why: bake high-poly detail into normal maps for the in-game hulls, and place wear where the fiction says it belongs (mounts, seams, bolt lines, engine grime), which is the same wear-placement rule the barge upgrade draft already states.

**11. Three-point lighting, including the space cheat**
http://ucbugg.github.io/learn.ucbugg/lighting-a-scene/
https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/basic-illumination-for-games-part-1-applying-art-concepts-on-interactive-media
https://flylib.com/books/en/3.57.1.39/1/
Substance: The standard rig is key (strongest, above and to the side, usually the only shadow caster), fill (opposite side, lower, roughly half the key's intensity or less, softening shadows), and rim or back light (behind the subject, drawing a bright edge that separates it from the background). Games can assign lights to depth layers: a key in the midground, a fill in the foreground, a rim stretching from background to foreground, so only the midground gets all three at once and the rest of the scene stays readable. The space-specific note matters: in deep space there is nothing to reflect light back into shadows, yet audiences are so used to reflected light that it is represented even in space scenes, so a fill light in a space scene is a deliberate cheat, not a mistake, provided it stays dim enough not to destroy the illusion of volume.
Relevance: ADOPT as the battle lighting rig. One line why: Voidsworn's two-light rig plus bloom threshold already follows this shape, and the fill-as-cheat note justifies a dim fill for hull readability while the rim does the separation work against dark backdrops.

**12. Babylon.js performance: draw calls, materials, and texture memory**
https://dev.to/paulcrinigan/making-a-babylonjs-game-run-well-in-a-mobile-browser-4j5
https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/best-practices-for-optimizing-babylon-js-scenes-not-just-on-lower-end-devices/58688
Substance: A desktop browser handles roughly 500 to 1000 draw calls at 60fps (mobile browsers top out around 100 to 300). The standard cuts are: merge static meshes that share a material into one draw call, use instances for repeated objects sharing geometry and material, and thin instances for thousands of identical objects at almost no CPU cost. Merging has a catch: a merged mesh with more than one material still costs mesh times material count in draw calls, so reducing material count means baking materials into one texture per channel. Texture memory is the other hard limit: one uncompressed 4096x4096 texture uses 64 MB, and KTX2 with Basis Universal compression cuts texture memory by about 75 percent versus PNG by transcoding at runtime to whatever the GPU supports (BC7 on desktop). GPU particles are called out as fast, and freezing world matrices on non-moving objects removes CPU cost.
Relevance: ADOPT as the measurement frame for the engine question. One line why: at 20 ships, the budget question is material count times ship count plus effect bursts, and these numbers are what the Babylon measurement pass should be priced against (frame time, draw calls, material count, CPU versus GPU split).

## Priority 4: Camera and motion pacing in turn-based games

**13. Two cameras, two jobs: planning view versus drama view**
https://dev.to/retrorom/shining-force-ii-the-16-bit-tactical-rpg-that-made-turn-based-combat-cinematic-4jpp
https://www.shacknews.com/article/150724/fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-review-score
Substance: Shining Force II's signature trick still works: the tactics phase plays on a top-down grid (moving units, checking ranges, planning ahead), and when the player commits to an attack, the camera swings down behind the attacking unit so the attack plays out as a small animated duel, with the enemy looming in the background. The design point is explicit: the grid shows everything needed to plan, the battle view shows everything needed to feel, two cameras with two jobs and zero clutter. A modern tactics game repeats the pattern (a hybrid version keeps the battlefield visible while the camera zooms in for attacks in real time, and follows a projectile like an arrow to its target), and Fire Emblem's current entry zooms into a cinematic view on attack, with critical hits triggering a unique attack animation.
Relevance: ADOPT as the camera doctrine. One line why: Voidsworn's battle camera should stay wide and readable for planning, then cut or glide in for resolution moments (a crit, a kill, a flagship duel), and never let the drama camera hide the board state the player still needs.

**14. Pacing controls: watch settings, simultaneous resolution, and the unskippable warning**
https://web.phenixxgaming.com/2020/03/11/how-not-to-make-a-turn-based-tactics-game/
https://tildes.net/~games/192w/i_desperately_want_to_enjoy_turn_based_games_but_can_never_get_into_them_what_can_i_do_to_change
https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/scrappymechanic/turnbasedstrategy
Substance: Wargroove's answer to animation pacing is a watch setting: watch your turn never, always, or just commanders, so the player decides what is worth animating, on the grounds that if health and damage information is conveyed, there is no need to play every bow pullback or sword swish. A structural alternative is simultaneous moves: both sides schedule their next turn and everything happens at once, which removes the dead air of watching each unit act in sequence. The pacing complaint that recurs in player discussion is that tactics games show only one action at a time even when many things are happening, so encounter pace is limited by animation speed that often cannot be adjusted. The cautionary example is Super Robot Wars Shin, whose reputation suffered specifically from slow load times and absurdly long, unskippable animations, including cut-ins and close-up portraits on attacks.
Relevance: ADOPT the watch-setting pattern as the fix shape for the 95 to 120 second late-fight waits. One line why: the waits are a presentation problem with three known fixes (watch settings, simultaneous animation of independent actions, and a global accelerate control), and the unskippable-animation reputation damage is the cost of shipping without any of them.

**15. Sins presentation: ponderous menace and light playing over hulls**
https://www.pcgamer.com/sins-of-a-solar-empire-rebellion-preview/
https://steamcommunity.com/app/1575940/discussions/1/578298707369444055
Substance: The Sins presentation that Voidsworn takes as its benchmark is described in two consistent images: capital ships that move with ponderous menace, slowly rotating to bring massive slab-like sides to bear for a crushing broadside, with the lights of incoming weapons playing over mottled sheet-metal hulls, and battle visuals that are astonishing when the player zooms out and watches the chaos unfold, with explosions rippling across hulls. The same discussion notes the failure mode on the other side of the presentation: a UI so plain and disconnected from the fiction that it constantly pulls the player out of the experience, which is a warning that presentation includes the interface, not only the battle.
Relevance: ADOPT as the motion timing reference. One line why: capital ships should rotate and accelerate slowly enough to read as massive, with weapon light and explosions doing the spectacle work, while the UI warning is already answered in Voidsworn by the faction-trimmed, lore-consistent interface work.

## Priority 5: Anti-patterns and warnings

**16. Floaty, arcade, or unskippable: the three feel failures**
http://forums.atomictorch.com/index.php?PHPSESSID=1qktaihevduum5a6s2ga2t6i96&topic=137.0
https://www.slashskill.com/game-feel-and-juice-the-complete-guide-to-making-your-game-satisfying/
Substance: A VoidExpanse player complaint captures the floaty failure exactly: acceleration and reverse feel far too fast and arcade-like for a big ship in space, and an instant-stop cruise control that halts the ship immediately breaks the fiction (the player notes it should have crashed into the station), with the developer answer being that heavier ships do feel the full inertia. The juice literature names the other two failures: too much of everything (constant shake and flashes make it hard to read what is happening), and hiding latency with animation (long wind-ups feel cinematic in a trailer and sluggish in the player's hands). Deadlock's own reviews add a fourth: a UI large enough to obscure parts of cluttered battlefields, with turret range overlays off by default and never explained, so players miss the readability tool that exists.
Relevance: ADOPT all four as acceptance checks. One line why: every motion and effect choice in Voidsworn should pass four gates (does a big ship feel big, can the player still read the board through the effects, does any animation hide waiting rather than dramatize action, is the readability overlay on and discoverable).

Cross-cutting takeaway [Inference, built on sources 1-16]: the cheapest weight in the game is not simulation depth but presentation discipline. A turn-based space game earns BattleTech weight from three things: slow, eased capital-ship motion with visible acceleration and banking; a short, fixed playback window with hologram-style previews so the player always knows what will happen before it happens; and a juice table (hitstop, decaying shake, material flash, recoil, anticipation) applied with contrast, so big events feel big and small events stay quiet. Torpedo flight time belongs in the same frame: it is a dodge window, not a delay, and it only works if the incoming torpedo, its path, and its arrival time are all visible. The 95 to 120 second wait problem is solved by presentation controls (watch settings, simultaneous animation, accelerate), not by cutting the spectacle.
