Headline
The three-sector campaign map should adopt the Sins of a Solar Empire 2 trick of making the star system itself the interface, so the Hollow Sun, its accretion disc, and the orbiting stops do the storytelling that flat panels currently attempt, and the single most valuable technique to steal from the research is Sins 2's continuous zoom that lets one 3D scene serve both the cinematic wide shot and the tactical close-up without any mode switch. Every panel, label, and threat diamond then layers onto that scene as holographic overlays that appear on demand, matching the R72 register of cinematic first with information on request. The map reuses the F4 battle world sky bakes, side-lit planets, five rock kinds, and two-part accretion disc wholesale, so the sector map and its battles share one visual identity from the first jump to the last boss.
Research notes per game
Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (Ironclad Games, 2024)
Camera: the camera orbits freely around a star system and zooms continuously from a ship-tracking close view to a full-system strategic pull-back. At the far end the whole system, star, planets, and jump network fit on screen; at the near end you see individual hulls. There is no mode switch; the same scene serves both. Source: the Steam store page and Ironclad developer updates at https://store.steampowered.com/app/1895040/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II/ and https://www.ironcladgames.com/.
Scale tricks: star systems are compressed so planets sit close to their star and orbits are visible in seconds. The jump network is drawn as glowing lines linking systems, making the whole map feel like one connected constellation rather than isolated pockets. This is my impression from gameplay footage and the launch trailer on the Steam page.
Icons over 3D: at strategic zoom, fleets collapse into faction-coloured icons with a ship-count badge. Planets remain 3D spheres at all zoom levels but gain ring overlays and label text only when zoomed to a readable distance. Hovering a fleet or planet pops a contextual panel. Source: Ironclad dev blog posts at https://www.ironcladgames.com/.
Route lines: jump lanes are persistent glowing lines. Ownership colours the line (your colour, enemy colour, neutral grey). A fleet in transit animates along the line. Danger is not drawn on the lane itself but shown by the garrison icon at the destination system. Source: Steam page screenshots and the official manual wiki at https://sinsofasolarempire.fandom.com/wiki/Sins_of_a_Solar_Empire_II_Wiki.
Fog and information hiding: unexplored systems are hidden entirely. Once a scout reaches a system it reveals the star, planets, and lane connections but not garrison strength until you move closer. Source: the wiki above.
Danger and reward: system tooltip on hover shows resource output, garrison fleet size, and any anomalies. There is no pre-commit "threat diamond" system; you read the garrison number and decide. Source: the wiki page on system management.
Stellaris (Paradox Development Studio, 2016)
Camera: a top-down galaxy map at the widest zoom, rotating into a 3D star render as you zoom into a single system. The galaxy view is the strategic layer; the system view is a small 3D diorama. Source: the official Stellaris wiki at https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/wiki/Galaxy_map and the Steam page https://store.steampowered.com/app/281990/Stellaris/.
Scale tricks: the galaxy is a node graph. Systems are dots on spiral arms, and hyperlanes connect them. Inside a system, planets orbit the star at compressed distances. The sense of scale comes from the number of nodes (hundreds) rather than physical distance. Source: the wiki galaxy map page.
Icons over 3D: fleets show as small ship silhouettes with a numeric badge. Starbases get a distinct icon. At the galaxy zoom level everything flattens to coloured dots and lane lines. Planet icons appear only at system zoom. Source: the wiki pages on fleets and systems.
Route lines: hyperlanes are thin lines, coloured by the owning empire. A selected fleet draws a bright path preview along its intended route. Trade routes overlay as a second coloured line on the same lane. Source: https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/wiki/Hyperlane.
Fog and information hiding: unexplored systems are black. A science ship must survey each system to reveal its contents. Surveyed but out-of-sensor-range systems show last-known data with a staleness marker. Source: the wiki exploration page.
Danger and reward: system tooltips list resource deposits, anomalies, and a numeric fleet-power comparison (your fleet versus theirs) shown as a green, yellow, or red indicator. Source: the wiki fleet power page.
Homeworld Remastered Collection (Relic Entertainment / Gearbox, 2015, remaster of 1999 original)
Camera: fully free 3D. The player orbits, pans, and zooms through open space with no fixed plane or top-down constraint. A sensor manager inset shows the whole map as a simplified radar. Source: the Steam page https://store.steampowered.com/app/244160/Homeworld_Remastered_Collection/ and the original game manual, archived at https://www.moddb.com/games/homeworld.
Scale tricks: space is large but navigable because the game uses nav points, fleet formations, and the sensor manager to anchor the player. Distances matter for travel time but the visual density of ships and debris makes space feel populated. This is my impression from the remastered campaign.
Icons over 3D: ships carry faction-colour selection brackets and health bars that appear only when damaged or selected. There are no persistent map icons; everything is a 3D object you fly to. The sensor manager draws simplified diamond and triangle glyphs for fleet composition. Source: the manual and the Steam community guide at https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=222836701.
Route lines: movement orders draw a temporary line from the ship to the destination point. There are no persistent lane structures because movement is free-form. Source: the manual.
Fog and information hiding: ships outside sensor range are invisible. Probes and sensor platforms extend range. The sensor manager shows contacts as last-known positions that fade. Source: the manual section on sensors.
Danger and reward: there is no pre-commit danger readout. You scout with probes and read the tactical overlay. This is my impression. The reward is implicit: capturing a resource asteroid or a wreck is visible in 3D before you commit.
Endless Space 2 (Amplitude Studios, 2016)
Camera: the galaxy map is a node graph viewed from above. Zooming into a system transitions to a 3D orbital view with planets circling the star. The transition is seamless. Source: the Steam page https://store.steampowered.com/app/392110/Endless_Space_2/ and the Amplitude community forum at https://community.amplitude-studios.com/.
Scale tricks: the galaxy feels vast through the number of systems and the curvature of the lanes. Inside a system, planets orbit at readable distances and each planet is a distinct 3D model with biome-appropriate texturing. Source: Steam screenshots and the official game guide PDF available through the Steam community hub.
Icons over 3D: fleet icons are faction-shaped glyphs with a ship count. At galaxy zoom, systems show a small status icon (colonised, contested, anomaly). At system zoom, planet tiles and population icons appear. Source: the Amplitude forum guide posts and the Steam page.
Route lines: fleet movement draws a curved arc between systems, coloured by faction. Trade routes are a second overlay. Ownership of a system colours the node and its immediate lanes. Source: the Steam community guide.
Fog and information hiding: unexplored systems are dark and show only their lane connections. Exploring reveals the star type and planet count but not detailed resources until you survey. Source: the wiki at https://endless-space.fandom.com/wiki/Exploration_(Endless_Space_2).
Danger and reward: system tooltips show a military strength bar comparing your fleet to the defender. Resource icons float over planet tiles. Quest markers and anomaly icons advertise points of interest. Source: the fandom wiki above.
Steal and avoid
Worth stealing:
The Sins 2 continuous zoom. This game has one fleet, forward-only lanes, and three sectors. A single 3D scene per sector that zooms from a cinematic wide shot (Hollow Sun dominant, all stops visible) to a close-up on the next stop removes any need for a separate "strategic map" screen. It matches R72 cinematic-first.
The Endless Space 2 seamless galaxy-to-system transition. Sector transitions can borrow this: the camera pulls back from sector 1's system, the Hollow Sun grows as you cross the boundary, and sector 2's system resolves in. No loading screen, no mode switch.
The Stellaris fleet-power comparison colour (green, yellow, red) on hover. This maps directly to the threat diamonds already in the map. Keep the diamonds but add a one-word strength readout on hover.
The Homeworld sensor-manager inset as a reference for the Nemesis clock and core positions: a small persistent glyph cluster, not a panel.
Worth avoiding:
Stellaris's top-down galaxy map. This game has three sectors, not hundreds of systems. A flat node graph would flatten the Hollow Sun and lose the cinematic register the owner wants.
Homeworld's free-form movement. This game is lane-based and forward-only. Free movement would contradict the design.
Endless Space 2's per-planet tile UI. Too much information for a roguelite run where the player needs at-a-glance route choice, not colony management.
Sins 2's garrison-number readout as the only danger signal. This game already has threat diamonds and danger bands, which read faster. Keep those.
The design
System view. The Hollow Sun stays centred. Replace the separately built map black hole with the F4 battle world's two-part accretion disc (dense inner ring, see-through outer gas) and the sky-lensing shader from world.gd. The disc's Doppler beaming (approaching side brighter) carries over unchanged. The photon ring and lensed arcs from the current map build remain. The difference is the disc now matches the battle version exactly, so the player recognises the same object in combat. Reuse world_bake.gd to bake the sector sky once and share it between the map and all battles in that sector, per the F4 README.
Lanes. Jump lanes become soft glowing arcs rather than straight lines, following the orbital curvature of the stops. Ownership is not relevant here (no territory capture), so colour the lanes by danger band: safe lanes in a cool tone, dangerous lanes in the threat colour. The safest-and-richest route lighting on hover stays but now uses the same arc geometry. The fleet token's jump animation follows the arc. Reuse the lane glow shader from the F4 world's gas-wisp material at middle distance for a consistent look.
Stops. Each stop keeps its current 3D object (orbital station, shipyard, drydock, field tender, planet-edge, asteroid belt, graveyard, nebula pocket, unknown signal, flagship). Update the planet-edge stop to use the F4 side-lit planet shader from world_planet.gd so it has a lit face, soft edge, and dark side, matching the battle planets. Update the asteroid belt stop to use the five rock kinds from world_rocks.gd, picked by sector region, with shader craters and tumbling. The shipyard and drydock keep their procedural geometry but gain the F4 dust-speck and gas-wisp depth layers from world_living.gd so they sit in a believable space.
Panels and text. Replace all permanent sidebar panels with holographic overlays that appear on hover or selection, per R72. The hover card for a stop shows: stop kind icon, faction crest, threat diamonds, reward label, and a one-word strength readout. The card fades after two seconds of no input. The Nemesis clock moves from a panel to a thin arc around the Hollow Sun's shadow, filling clockwise. Core-in-range names float as small holographic tags near their system positions. The fleet page, shipyard page, and battle page remain full overlays over the dimmed star system, as they are now, but their headers adopt the F4 painted-nebula backdrop so they feel part of the world.
Sector transitions. When the player defeats the sector flagship, the camera pulls back from the sector system. The Hollow Sun grows in frame as the camera crosses the gap between sectors. The next sector's sky bake fades in (different colour palette per faction mix). The new system's stops resolve from silhouette to full detail. This mirrors the Endless Space 2 galaxy-to-system zoom. The Hollow Sun is the constant visual anchor across all three transitions.
Information layer. Threat diamonds stay in the danger band colour. On hover, a single-line text under the diamonds reads the threat in words (light, moderate, heavy, extreme) rather than a number, keeping the cinematic register. Reward labels show only on hover and use icon plus short word (salvage, blueprint, crew, fuel). Visited stops dim and their ring collapses to a thin outline. Burned lanes behind the fleet fade to a dark remnant. The Nemesis clock arc pulses when the clock is near full, and the Nemesis fleet's arrival is foreshadowed by a red glow at the system edge.
Mock layout per sector
Sector 1: Hullbreakers and Hegemony space. The Hollow Sun sits at the centre of the view, slightly below frame centre so its accretion disc arcs frame the upper stops. The sky bake is warm amber and deep rust, matching Hegemony industrial palette. Rock kinds are stone and iron, per the F4 region table. Stops orbit in two loose rings: the inner ring has the orbital station, a shipyard, and a drydock; the outer ring has two asteroid belt stops, a field tender, a planet-edge stop with a barren world and one moon, and an unknown signal. Lanes connect them in a forward web with two branch choices. The flagship sits at the far edge, past the last ring, lit by a harsh Hegemony floodlight colour. On arrival the player sees the whole system with the Hollow Sun dominant and roughly ten stops visible. Mid-sector the camera has moved inward and three or four stops are dimmed behind. At the boss, the flagship fills the right third of the frame against the Hollow Sun's glow.
Sector 2: adds Chorus and Glasswrights. The sky bake shifts to cold teal and pale violet, the Chorus and Glasswrights palette. Rock kinds add carbon and ice. The stop layout is denser: three rings of stops, with a nebula pocket and a ship graveyard added. The Hollow Sun is the same object but the camera starts slightly closer, so the disc fills more of the background, signalling that the player is deeper in. Faction crests colour the stop rings: Chorus stops have a teal ring, Glasswrights a silver-violet ring, Hegemony holdovers keep amber. On arrival the player sees the increased density and the new faction colours. Mid-sector the two branch paths diverge clearly around a central nebula pocket. At the boss, the flagship sits against the Hollow Sun's photon ring, backlit.
Sector 3: adds Eclipse. The sky bake goes to deep indigo, black, and a single hot white band, the Eclipse palette. Rock kinds include crystal alongside carbon and ice. The stop count is similar to sector 2 but the lanes are longer and more punishing, with fewer rest stops. The Hollow Sun is closest here: the camera default zoom puts the disc's outer gas at the frame edges, so the player feels inside the system. A ship graveyard and two unknown signals add tension. On arrival the dark palette and tight framing communicate finality. Mid-sector the Nemesis clock arc is likely pulsing. At the boss, the Eclipse flagship is silhouetted against the Hollow Sun's shadow, the brightest object in an otherwise dark frame.
