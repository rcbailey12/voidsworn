# Campaign map synthesis: the next step is a presentation pass, not a rebuild

Chair synthesis of four legs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Argon, Rey) for Job 6 of the Nov 1, 2026 four-job commission. The starting fact every leg had to respect: the G3 stream already built the thing R51 asked for in outline (a real 3D star system per sector, the Hollow Sun as a black hole always visible, one clear 3D object per stop kind, jump lanes with safest and richest lighting on hover, camera zoom, threat diamonds, a Low quality setting). What reads cheap now, from the owner's own R51 list, is the layer on top: panels and text are the least rebuilt part, and the F4 battle world has moved past the map in sky, planet, and rock fidelity. Owner rulings R51 (Sins 2 star-system look, show the big story pieces) and R72 (cinematic register) govern throughout.

## Verdict

GO WITH CONDITIONS. All four legs converge on the same shape: unify the map with the battle world's assets, tier the information by zoom and by a held scan view, and rebuild panels and text in the battle register. The design below is that convergence, with the disagreements settled and flagged. Nothing here adds a mechanic, moves a lane, or hides a stop; it is presentation only, which is what keeps it honest against the run's core decision (route choice under scarcity, with danger and reward advertised).

## 1. Research takeaways that survived screening

Sins of a Solar Empire 2: continuous zoom from system to hull, lanes as the geography, danger advertised as change and forecast rather than only colour. Its orbiting planets and re-linking lanes are rejected for this game (section 3), but its lesson stands: the map is one view, and information scales with zoom instead of switching screens. Sources: Stardock's official wiki and feature articles (DeepSeek and Rey both cite them; Argon's Sins 2 Steam link is the correct app id, 1575940).
Stellaris: the one hard, citable mechanism in the whole research set. Its galaxy view defines exact zoom thresholds at which detail appears and disappears (Paradox wiki defines, via DeepSeek). Adopt the pattern, not the scale: thresholds written down in one place, so far and near views are designed, not accidental. Its intel tiers inspire the presentation-only version in section 2: exact numbers near, bands and kinds far.
Homeworld: NLIPS (ships scaled up with distance so they stay selectable) and the Sensors Manager (a held abstract view over the live 3D scene). The technique to steal is the hold-to-reveal overlay, not a separate mode (DeepSeek, Rey, Argon all land here). Qwen researched Homeworld Remastered rather than Homeworld 3 and its Sins 2 link is wrong; its Homeworld-derived claims are used only where another leg establishes the same point.
Endless Space 2: three-state shading (unexplored, known, watched) shown by overlay and brightness alone, and directional emphasis on the next hop. The presentation-only steals are burned-stop dimming and the forecast of the next sector; its fog and probes are mechanics and stay out.

## 2. The design

Sky and sun (unanimous). One world_bake.gd bake per sector, shared by the map and that sector's battles, so a fight looks like the place the map promised. The map's Hollow Sun is rebuilt with the F4 battle dead star treatment: two-part disc, sky lensing, bent far side over the shadow, keeping G3's photon ring and Doppler beaming. The map stops being a cousin of the battle world and becomes the same world.
Stops (unanimous). Planet-edge stops use world_planet.gd (side lighting, atmosphere glow, clouds, night lights, rings, moons). Belt stops use world_rocks.gd with the region's rock kind, shader craters, true dark sides, slow tumble. Graveyard stops mix the G3 death pieces with tumbling battle-scale rocks. Stations, shipyard, drydock, and tender keep their G3 silhouettes and take battle lighting. Every stop is lit from the Hollow Sun first: bright face to the centre, terminator, true dark side out.
Lanes. Curved arcs following the orbital flow (Qwen, Argon), in four presentation states: a dim base line for every lane ahead; brighter directional flow on lanes out of the current stop; burned lanes behind cooled to dark ash with no flow (no going back, said without text); bypassed branches faded to ghost once a diverging choice makes them unreachable (Argon). Hovering a stop lights its whole chain, extending G3's safest and richest lighting two lanes out (Rey), with the safest chain and richest chain in distinct rails (Argon).
Information tiers. Far out, a stop shows only kind, danger band (threat diamonds, count visible at all zooms), and its single best reward tag. Near in or on hover, it shows name, holder, and full reward lines. Thresholds are defined once, Stellaris-style, so the two views cannot drift. One word of threat readout on hover (Qwen) keeps the far view honest without numbers.
Scan view. A held key dims the 3D system and flattens it to symbols: threat diamonds, lane colours, reward tags, core points (DeepSeek's hold-to-reveal; Rey's scan toggle; Argon's Scan View). Release returns to cinematic. This is the answer to panel crowding at busy sectors, and it doubles as the Low-friendly view. It is an overlay on the live scene, never a separate screen and never where play actually happens (the Homeworld avoid in every leg).
Panels and text (the main spend). One panel family in the battle register: same dim level as the battle pages that already sit over the system, same headers, one type scale, short labels everywhere. Stop labels become compact pills on short leader lines (Argon), showing icon, holder colour, and threat diamonds at rest, expanding on hover. Run state (admiral, fleet, credits, clock state in words) moves to a slim bottom ribbon (DeepSeek) instead of sidebars. All text uses current lore names; the G3 label priority rules stay.
Cores and the Nemesis clock, in the world. Cores in range become faint coloured points on the disc rim toward where they can appear, name on hover, with the ribbon keeping the text list (Rey). The Nemesis clock becomes a ring segment around the Hollow Sun's shadow, filling in Nemesis colour as it advances (Rey, Qwen), keeping G3's full-state change to every fight. No new HUD dial is needed; the sun is already the centre of every frame. Argon's top telemetry bar with core sockets is the alternative and is not adopted; it reintroduces the permanent furniture R72 argues against. Flagged as an owner call.
Sectors and transitions. The three sectors ring the same Hollow Sun, and the presentation should say so: each sector sits closer in, the sun reads larger, the light gets harder (all four legs' mock layouts agree on this arc). Beating the boss triggers a jump flight toward the disc, light fills the frame, and the next sector resolves with its own sky bake (Rey; Qwen's pull-back with sky crossfade is the cheaper variant and the Low fallback). Before the boss, the next sector shows only as a forecast arc beyond the boss stop: its sky tint and faction colours, no stops (Rey). Argon's labelled distant beacons for sectors 2 and 3 say more than the forecast should; the arc keeps it a promise, not a preview.

## 3. Avoid list (agreed)

Moving orbits and re-linking lanes (Sins 2): they would make safest and richest lighting dishonest and break the guaranteed reachable elite route. A second galaxy-level map (Stellaris): three sectors, one fleet, one screen each; the Hollow Sun must never leave the frame. Fog of war and scouting (Endless Space 2, Stellaris): hiding stop kinds or rewards fights route choice under scarcity, which needs advertised trade-offs; only the light veiling of the boss and burned-stop dimming survive. NLIPS enlargement of stop objects (Homeworld): G3 already fixed small-object readability with rim light and framing, and enlarging stops breaks shared scale with battle planets. Empire ribbons, outliners, and permanent multi-panel HUD (all four games): one fleet owns no territory, and R72 forbids the spreadsheet look.

## 4. Mock layouts

Sector 1 (Hullbreakers, Hegemony). Sun centre frame, slightly high, disc running left to right, warm light, stone and iron rocks, steel blue and scrap orange holder rings: the calmest sky. Stops fan forward from lower left (arrival) to upper right (flagship beyond a belt stop). On arrival: four to five bright stops ahead, boss as a large flagship silhouette, the sector 2 forecast arc as a faint tint beyond it. Mid sector: two chains compete, one past a side-lit planet stop, one through the belt; safest and richest lighting point at different chains. At the boss the view tightens, the flagship fills the right third, threat diamonds stack in the highest band.
Sector 2 (adds Chorus, Glasswrights). Same sun, larger. Stops in two arcs: an inner arc near the disc with event signals pulsing in nebula pockets, an outer arc with shipyard and drydock. Chorus stops carry wet organic light; Glasswright stops glint with the battle crystal rock material at small scale. Mid sector: a rich inner line past a volcanic planet stop against a safer outer line past repair stops. At the boss the flagship waits across the disc shadow, framed by the lensed arcs, two core points on the rim.
Sector 3 (adds Eclipse). The sun reads largest; stops sit closer to the disc in a tighter spiral ending at a flagship almost touching the photon ring. Eclipse stops are dark violet-black with trapped-light points. On arrival: fewer, larger stops, longer lanes across open dark, the Nemesis ring at its fullest. Mid sector: burned stops leave a dark trail, every remaining stop shows a high danger band, so reward tags carry the choice. At the boss, disc light is the main light, an ice world at the edge, ice rocks tumbling, the flagship lit from the disc alone. Coldest sky, strongest contrast: the sun feels like the destination, not the backdrop.

## 5. Build order

Each step names what the owner sees, since the owner judges by screenshots, and its Low fallback.

1. Shared sky bake. One world_bake.gd bake per sector feeds map and battles. Owner sees map shots with battle skies behind the stops. Low: lower bake resolution, fewer stars (the existing Low rules).
2. Hollow Sun unification. Map sun rebuilt on the F4 two-part disc with lensing. Owner sees the map sun and a battle dead star side by side, indistinguishable in treatment. Low: static pre-bent disc texture, lensing off.
3. Stops to battle assets. Planet stops to world_planet.gd, belt stops to world_rocks.gd, G3 structures relit from the sun. Owner sees before and after close-ups beside matching battle shots. Low: fewer rocks, no cloud motion, tumble frozen off-hover.
4. Lane pass. Curved arcs, four states, whole-chain hover lighting. Owner sees one wide and one hover shot per sector with safest and richest chains lit. Low: single unlit scrolling strip instead of layered glow.
5. Panels and text. One panel family, leader-line pills, bottom run ribbon, sidebar removal. Owner sees a hover card, the fleet page, and the shipyard page over the dimmed system, beside the old set. Low: solid dark fill instead of backdrop blur.
6. Information tiers and scan view. Zoom thresholds defined once; held-key scan overlay. Owner sees one scan shot per sector beside its cinematic twin. Low: the scan view is itself the fallback.
7. Cores and Nemesis ring. Disc-rim core points, Nemesis ring segment around the shadow, disc pulse as the clock fills (DeepSeek). Owner sees full map shots at three clock states: empty, half, full. Low: flat sprites for points and ring.
8. Sector framing and transitions. Per-sector camera framing on the inward spiral, boss jump flight through disc light, forecast arc. Owner sees the sector 2 arrival shot and a short transition capture. Low: crossfade through disc light instead of the flight.
9. Performance confirmation at the F4 bar. The map must stay lighter than a battle scene on the owner machine (heaviest battle setting holds 60 fps, about 200 average uncapped, 1 percent low 45 on the RTX 5080, per the pack). Owner sees the heaviest sector (sector 3) running at full stop count with the frame readout. Low: steps 1 to 8 fallbacks engaged as a set.

## 6. Screening notes

DeepSeek: the best-sourced research leg (Paradox defines, Stardock wiki and video blog, the Amplitude design document), and the origin of the zoom-tier and hold-to-reveal synthesis; its bottom ribbon and disc pulse are adopted. Qwen: the continuous-zoom framing and the Hollow Sun swap are adopted, but its Homeworld research covers the Remastered original rather than Homeworld 3 and its Sins 2 Steam link names the wrong app; those sections were used only where corroborated. Argon: the most detailed design leg (lane states, leader-line pills, per-sector palettes and camera angles) and mostly adopted, with two cautions: it states a list of six F4 planet classes the pack never enumerates, and it assigns specific colours to the threat diamond bands that the pack describes only as danger-band colours; both are treated as proposals, not facts. Rey: the forecast arc, the in-world Nemesis ring, and the mock layouts form the spine of sections 2 and 4; its Stellaris camera notes are marked impressions in its own text and are not relied on.

## 7. Owner calls

1. Nemesis clock as an in-world ring around the sun (chair, Rey, Qwen) versus a HUD dial in a top bar (Argon). The ring is in the build order.
2. Bypassed branches shown as ghosts (Argon) versus simply left dim. Ghosts advertise the cost of a choice already made; some owners would rather the map forget. Ghosts are in the build order and easy to strike.
3. The scan view's key and behaviour (hold versus toggle) is a feel call best made with hands on the build; the design assumes hold, matching the existing full-grid key's pattern from R72.

## Not checked

No leg saw the G3 map running; every layout is argued from the G3 hand-over notes and the F4 world notes, not from the live scene. Exact stop counts per sector are assumed (roughly ten to fourteen) and the pill and leader-line layout has not been tested at the true maximum. Whether the campaign scene can call the world builders without a new pipeline is assumed, not verified. The transition flight's cost on the owner machine is unmeasured until build step 8. Primary sources were reachable for Sins 2, Stellaris defines, the Endless Space 2 manual, and the Homeworld 2 manual; Homeworld 3 specifics rest on developer updates and marked impressions.
