## 1. What the rounds already proved (the ground this stands on)

Round 1 (#75): remesh plus PBR melts detail and rounds armour (owner: looks bad, R83); the authored rebuild is controlled but sparse, blocky, and changed proportions. Round 2 (#111): A+ rich surface is the pick but still more regular than today's ship; copied-detail baking removes broad waves but ghosts medium machinery; all three variants cost roughly twice today's triangles and more frame time than today at 20 ships. Round 3 (#129, repair in place): most of today's apparent detail was paint (strip the projected albedo and the AI normal map and the colour and detail go with them); flattening geometry alone moves panel deviation about 6 percent, because the waves live in the maps (R86); crease separation improves plate edges but cannot unfuse parts; hand-built engine, guns and bridge beat AI parts generated one at a time (17.5 to 25.1 million native triangles each, three documented misreads); stage 5 (separation plus hand parts plus weighted normals) is the pick at 305,857 tris. The owner then retired the old concept (R92): the refreshed Hullbreaker look is the target, and stage 5 against that reference is untested, because round 3's "rebuild by hand" sweep was scored against the retired concept and is void.

## 2. Question 1: painting and materials (settled)

Why the old projection gridded and double-lit (both writer legs, mechanism INFERRED in part): a painted concept is already lit, with painted highlights, shadow and occlusion; projecting it into albedo and lighting the ship again in the engine adds real light on top of painted light. The grid is the projection and UV structure showing through: oblique surfaces stretch texels, UV island borders and creases break the mapping, and any lattice in the source image is painted on as well. The AI normal map carries the same low-frequency shading, which is why geometry flattening alone changed almost nothing (R86, round 3 stage 2).

The settled stack, in order:
1. Blender procedural smart materials driven by masks baked from the repaired geometry itself: curvature (Geometry Pointiness / Cycles pointiness), ambient occlusion, edge wear, driving soot, rust break-up and bare-metal exposure. Because the masks come from the ship's own geometry, wear lands on real panel edges and nothing pre-lit or view-dependent enters the albedo. Organised once per faction, on top of the faction trim sheet.
2. Trim sheets and decal atlases as the detail layer: panel lines, markings, hazard stripes, and the things masks cannot invent, above all trophy plates in other factions' faded colours (flagship only, per the refreshed look pack). Decals stay "where they earn it" (R82 Q2).
3. Open-source painters (Material Maker for procedural texture sets; ArmorPaint or Blender texture paint for hand work) for hero touch-up on flagships and problem panels only. Per-hull hand painting does not amortise across 36 hulls; round 1 priced per-hull hand finishing at 12 to 20 hours on the Pyre-Barge.
4. Local AI texturing (StableProjectorz, Hunyuan3D-Paint and similar multi-view projection) ranked last. It is still projection of generated imagery onto the mesh, the family of the step R82 Q2 killed. It earns a place only if a tool demonstrably outputs unlit PBR channels (flat albedo, separate roughness and metalness) and passes the grazing-angle crop test on one calibration hull first. DeepSeek adds the working conditions if it is ever tried: a deliberate delighting step and strict albedo value checks.

Shipped-game practice (DeepSeek, sourced in its leg): Homeworld 3 ships carry three UV channels and a packed texture set (base colour, emissive, normal, ARM, a MASK channel carrying dust/dirt, engine, panel and team colour) plus a dynamic splat damage texture; EVE Online's V5++ rework is packed PBR maps, a material map and a dirt map per ship. The pattern is shared maps, masks, trim and decals, not unique projected paint. That matches our rounds: the outlier was us.

Costs, stated as the legs gave them, not averaged: the per-hull materials slice inside round 3's measured rollout estimate is 1 to 2 focused hours per mid-size hull once the faction setup exists. The one-time faction setup (material graphs plus trim plus decal atlas) is unmeasured: DeepSeek estimates 60 to 100 human hours (INFERRED, treat as the conservative bound), the blind leg estimates hours rather than days by analogy to the round 2 trim sheet, and Qwen's 1 to 2 hours total is not credible for authoring a graph, a trim sheet and an atlas. Planning position: budget the per-hull slice from round 3's own numbers, expect the first faction setup to be a real project measured on the Pyre-Barge, and do not quote Qwen's figure.

## 3. Question 2: mixing AI mesh and hand-built (settled)

1. The round 3 split stays the production default: today's AI mesh repaired in place as the hull body, hand-built hero parts (engine, gun pair, bridge in the test) merged into the hull at export (R82 Q3, turrets separate). It is the only split with a won comparison behind it, and its cost is the measured one: 8 to 12 focused hours per mid-size mechanical hull once tools and a faction kit exist.
2. AI generation as a reviewed per-faction part library is the sourcing channel, not a rival split. DeepSeek ranked the library first; the chair keeps it second, as round 3's own closing recommendation framed it: AI proposes candidate parts, a human reviews once, and only a part that wins shape, mounting and frame checks enters the library for reuse. DeepSeek's economic point is adopted (review cost paid once, not per hull) and so is its framing that round 3 indicted unreviewed per-part generation, not AI parts in principle. Until a candidate passes the gate, this channel produces nothing, which is the correct price for the measured failure rate.
3. Hand-built masses with AI detail baked on: third, effectively superseded. The mask bakes survive inside section 2; visible-detail transfer ghosts and softens (round 2 variant 2) and R86 moved the programme to repair in place.
4. Part-aware generation: research watch item only. The one part behaviour round 3 observed was a generated midships section that would not join its neighbours. DeepSeek's survey agrees on the current state: Part3DGen runs a low-VRAM mode at about 15 GB with at most 10 parts, PartCrafter is research code at 8 to 16 GB with unstable part counts and untextured occluded parts. Proposal and decomposition use only, no production geometry.

What has worked for small teams (DeepSeek, consistent with our three rounds): AI generates candidates at the edges of the pipeline, humans own the hero shapes and the review gate. Every route in our own bake-offs that removed the human from a decision point failed a screenshot test.

FLAGGED LICENCE ITEM for Opus: DeepSeek reports Pixal3D, the generator in the current production pipeline (docs/ship_pipeline), as academic / non-commercial licensed, which would block its output in a shipped commercial game. The chair did not independently re-fetch the model card in this job, so this is a verify-first flag, not a ruling: check the Pixal3D licence terms before further production reliance on it. TRELLIS.2 (MIT) is the settled default for new generation either way, so round 4 route 2 does not depend on the answer.

Input preparation (DeepSeek, adopted): never feed a composite multi-view sheet to a generator; it is read as several fused objects. Feed separate single-view images: orthographic front, back, left, right and top, clean neutral grey background, consistent scale and flat even lighting, prompted as an orthographic product shot rather than concept art (painterly prompts bake lighting into albedo). Add separate detail crops of the hero parts (bow hunting gear, boarding claws, chains, trophy plates, engine bells, weapon clusters) so they are not hallucinated into neighbouring structures. Expect 1 to 25 million native triangles from a full-resolution generation; a triangle budget and a decimation step are set before generation, not after.

Qwen's model section is not used: its entries (Hunyuan3D-2.5, TRELLIS successors, Step1X-3D, Hi3DGen) are self-labelled INFERRED and unverified, and its conclusion that no AI mesh should appear in round 4 contradicts R92 route 2, which the owner explicitly endorsed trying.

## 5. Question 4: the reference (settled)

Build two artefacts from the refreshed Hullbreaker look, Pyre-Barge first:
1. The annotated master sheet for humans: orthographic front, back, left, right and top at consistent scale; one three-quarter view (the flagship prompt specifies three quarter from front below); a scale bar; the one-line silhouette rule (low, heavy, lopsided; bulk, asymmetry and red light at battle distance); material-zone callouts with VALUES, not just swatches (palette hexes soot rust #6a2c22, dried blood red #b8402a, hot red #e0402c, plus roughness and metalness targets per zone, a Qwen detail the chair adopts); detail callouts for the mandated features (listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops open at the bow, boarding claws folded along the forward hull, chains between sections, trophy plates flagship only and annotated as such, far too many rockets, no lances).
2. The generator set: the same views as separate single images (about 1024 square), grey background, no perspective, flat lighting, per section 4.

Scope order, unanimous across the usable legs: Pyre-Barge sheet first, and stage 5 re-judged against it before anything is generated (the round 3 sweep it replaces was scored against the retired concept). Calibration set second (Eclipse zenith, Ninefold Maw; the Maw is organic, so its sheet and methods differ, and no mechanical kit is applied blindly to Chorus or Glasswrights, per round 3). Fleet-wide concept regeneration last and only in build order, a few hulls ahead of production: concepts are reference, not paint (R82 Q2), and 36 up-front regenerations would spend the most effort on the least-tested artefact in the programme. DeepSeek's first-sheet cost estimate is 16 to 24 human hours (INFERRED), 4 to 6 per subsequent hull on the same template; the blind leg estimates a few hours per hero sheet from the existing look pack prompts. The truth will be measured on the Pyre-Barge sheet itself; either way it is one sheet, not 36.

## 6. Round 4 proposal for the Pyre-Barge (issue #146)

R92 fixes the three routes; this proposal sequences them and states what each must change versus rounds 1 to 3. The owner's words stand behind routes 1 and 2 ("i'm down to try 1 and 2 though"); route 3 is the control and fallback.

Step 0: build the Pyre-Barge reference sheet (section 5). No route is judged until it exists.

Route 1, run first: #129 stage 5 plus the full paint stack from section 2 (baked-mask procedural materials in the faction palette, shared Hullbreaker trim sheet, decal trophy plates and markings), judged against the NEW reference. Geometry unchanged: this route isolates the paint variable, and it is the addition the owner already reacted to ("actually looks okay-ish, especially if we add more texture and material, maybe paint to it"). Cost: the materials slice of the rollout estimate plus decal placement and captures, about 3 to 6 focused hours (blind leg estimate, INFERRED). Success: the nine-cell sweep re-run against the new reference no longer reads as a different ship at battle distance, and grazing crops show wear on edges instead of waves.

Route 2, generated in parallel: the whole ship regenerated at full resolution from the sheet's generator set, with TRELLIS.2 as the generator, three seeds, and a triangle budget set before generation. What changes versus rounds 1 to 3: the inputs (orthographic separate views from the refreshed look, not a single concept crop against the retired concept) and an honest cost statement: regeneration does not skip repair, a fresh AI mesh brings its own waviness (R86), so route 2's true cost is generation plus the stage 1 to 5 repair pass at the same 8 to 12 focused hours. It wins only if its broad masses land closer to the refreshed reference than stage 5's retained masses, the one thing route 1 cannot fix. Stop after one cleaned candidate if waves or fused parts persist at round 3 levels.

Route 3, built only if routes 1 and 2 leave the owner undecided: hand-modelled against the sheet's orthographic views with kit parts at today's detail density, meaning the stage 4 hand-part standard (bevels, seams, fasteners, cooling fins, recessed bores) extended across the hull, not round 1 route A's sparser kit (which came out sparse, blocky, boat-like and changed proportions). Budget at round 1's anchor, 12 to 20 hours. Its role is tiebreaker and fallback; if route 1 passes the owner's screenshot judgement, round 4 has its fleet answer for machine hulls (repair in place plus this paint stack) and routes 2 and 3 become per-hull escalations, the posture R82 Q1 already approved in principle.

Judgement set for every route, identical conditions: battle-distance screenshot at in-game on-screen size (silhouette read: bulk, asymmetry, red light, bow hunting gear); grazing crop on a large panel and on the bow (flatness, wave residue, seams); head-on material crop (soot plate, rust patch, trophy plate, engine bell; albedo cleanliness, material separation, palette read); and a stated line of triangle count, 20-ship GPU time and human hours spent. A route that wins on beauty but doubles draw cost or cleanup hours is not a fleet route.
