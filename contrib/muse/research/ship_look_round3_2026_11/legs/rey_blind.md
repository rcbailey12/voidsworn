## Question 1: painting and materials

### Why the old projection produced the grid and the double lighting (INFERRED mechanism; the pack records the symptoms, not the mechanism)

The old chain painted a 2D concept, then projected that painting back onto the mesh as albedo. Two failure modes follow mechanically. First, the grid: a generated concept carries the image model's own regular artefacts, and a projection bakes whatever is in the source image into the texture at the projection's sampling pattern; where the surface turns away from the projection direction the texture stretches, and where multiple views meet there are seams. Round 3 stage 1 confirms the size of the problem from the other side: stripping the projected albedo and the AI normal map removed most of the apparent detail, meaning most of what read as hull detail was the painting, artefacts included. Second, double lighting: a concept painting is already lit. It has painted highlights, painted shadow and painted ambient occlusion. Projecting it into albedo and then lighting the ship again in the engine adds real light on top of painted light, so forms read lit from two directions at once and the painted shading fights the real shading whenever the ship turns. The R86 note completes the picture: the waviness lives in the AI normal map and the projected paint as much as in the geometry, which is why flattening geometry alone (tried Oct 22, and again as round 3 stage 2, mean panel deviation down only about 6 percent) changed almost nothing on screen.

The fix is therefore not a better projection. It is paint that is generated FROM the repaired geometry (masks baked off the actual mesh) plus authored surface material, so nothing view-dependent and nothing pre-lit ever enters the albedo.

### Ranked recommendation

1. Blender procedural smart materials driven by baked masks (curvature / Cycles pointiness, ambient occlusion, edge wear), organised as one shared material setup per faction on top of the faction trim sheet. This is the only route on the list whose inputs are the ship's own repaired geometry, so panel wear lands on real panel edges by construction and there is no projection step to grid or double-light. Round 2 already proved the baking half of this works in our pipeline: variant 2 baked normals, AO and Cycles pointiness from a source mesh, and its failure was ghosted machinery detail in the colour/normal transfer, not the mask bakes themselves. Cost per hull: inside the round 3 rollout estimate, materials and seams are 1 to 2 focused hours per mid-size mechanical hull once the faction material setup exists; building that setup is a one-time faction cost (INFERRED: comparable to the shared trim sheet effort in round 2, hours not days). What it fixes in our sheets: the "paint was the detail" problem from stage 1 (real geometry gets real wear), the flat look that made route A read blocky (wear and tonal variation break up large clean faces), and the owner's round 3 note that stage 5 "actually looks okay-ish, especially if we add more texture and material, maybe paint to it" (R92) - this route is exactly that addition.
2. Trim sheets and decal atlases, as the detail layer on top of route 1. Already the fleet's working pattern: round 2 ran all three variants on one shared Hullbreaker trim sheet, and R82 Q2 keeps decals "where they earn it". Decals are the right home for the things masks cannot invent: trophy plates in other factions' faded colours (flagship only, per the refreshed look), markings, hazard stripes, patched scrap plate with a different tone. Cost per hull: low once the atlas exists (INFERRED: under an hour of placement per hull, inside the materials hours above). What it fixes: identity at battle distance, which the refreshed look defines by bulk, asymmetry and red light rather than surface noise; decals carry faction identity without touching the base material.
3. Open-source painters for hero touch-up only: Material Maker for generating procedural texture sets, ArmorPaint or Blender texture paint for hand work on the flagship and on specific problem panels. Ranked third because hand painting is per-hull human time that does not amortise across 36 hulls, and round 1 already showed what per-hull hand finishing costs (route A Pyre-Barge estimated 12 to 20 hours to finish). Use it where the camera lives: the Pyre-Barge and other flagships, and to author the decal atlas in route 2. Cost per hull: flagship-scale hours (INFERRED: 3 to 6 hours for a hero pass), not a fleet route.
4. Local AI texturing (multi-view diffusion projection with depth/normal control, the paint stages of Hunyuan3D or TRELLIS, StableProjectorz-style tools). Ranked last, and it should stay last until proven otherwise: it is still projection of generated imagery onto the mesh, which is the family of the step R82 Q2 killed permanently. It could only earn a place if a tool demonstrably outputs true unlit PBR channels (albedo with no baked shading, plus roughness and metalness) and passes the same grazing-angle crop test that caught the waves, on one calibration hull first. Whether any current local tool meets that bar is a factual claim this leg cannot source from the pack; it is assigned to the writer legs for sourced verification. Cost per hull if it worked: mostly machine time (INFERRED), which is precisely why it is tempting, and precisely why the burden of proof sits on it.

### What shipped space games use at RTS distance

The pack sources no shipped-game material practice, so this leg makes no sourced claim here. The unsourced general pattern (INFERRED, to be checked by the writer legs) is tiling materials plus trim sheets plus decals, with per-hull unique painting reserved for hero ships, because at RTS distance the read is silhouette, tonal blocking and light, not unique surface detail. Our own refreshed look pack points the same way: the Hullbreaker family read is "bulk, asymmetry, and red light", with a three-colour palette (soot rust #6a2c22, dried blood red #b8402a, hot red #e0402c). Routes 1 and 2 above are the version of that pattern our three rounds have already validated locally.

## Question 2: mixing AI mesh and hand-built

### Ranked recommendation

1. The round 3 split, kept as the default: today's AI mesh repaired in place as the hull body, hand-built parts for the hero parts (engine, gun pair, bridge in the test), merged into the hull at export per R82 Q3, rotating turrets separate. This is the only split with a won comparison behind it. Stage 4 is explicit: the hand parts, with bevels, seams, fasteners, cooling fins and recessed bores, beat the AI parts on shape, mounting and read; stage 5 (stage 3 separation plus hand parts plus weighted normals) is the round 3 pick and the R92 round 4 route 1 baseline. Cost per hull: the round 3 rollout estimate, 8 to 12 focused hours per mid-size mechanical hull to nine clean cells once tools and a faction kit exist (about 2 concept and cut map, 3 to 5 replacement and fitting, 1 to 2 materials and seams, 2 to 3 captures and corrections). What it fixes: unfused, correctly shaped hero parts (stage 3 separation "cannot unfuse a gun or tank into a correctly shaped part"), while keeping the donor hull's irregular masses that every clean-rebuild route lost.
2. AI generation as a reviewed per-faction part library proposer only. This is round 3's own closing recommendation and I adopt it as written: AI proposes candidate parts, a human reviews once, and only a part that wins shape, mounting and frame checks enters the library for reuse. Ranked second because round 3 measured the current failure rate directly: Pixal3D parts from isolated concept crops arrived at 17.5 to 25.1 million native triangles each, needed cleanup, and still read wrong (the engine crop expanded into a stern assembly with neighbouring structures, the bridge retry invented a tall stepped tower instead of the low wide bridge, the gun read as disconnected masses). A library gate converts that failure mode from per-hull rework into a one-time rejection cost. Cost: review time per candidate (INFERRED: minutes to an hour per part including cleanup assessment), paid once per part, not once per hull. Until a candidate passes, this route produces nothing, which is the correct price for the evidence we have.
3. Hand-built primary masses with AI detail baked on (the round 2 variant 2 pattern). Ranked third and effectively superseded: variant 2 did remove broad waves (low-frequency vector RMS 0.569 to 0.053 after high-pass filtering the normal transfer), but it ghosted medium-scale donor machinery onto panels and softened fine detail, and R86 then moved the whole programme to repair in place. The bake half survives inside question 1 route 1 (masks and AO from geometry are good); the visible-detail transfer does not. Cost per hull from round 2: 8 to 14 hours to finish the Pyre-Barge by this route, for a worse result than route 1 above. Not recommended for machine hulls.
4. Part-aware generation that splits a ship into parts automatically. Ranked last on our evidence: the one part-aware behaviour round 3 actually observed was negative, a generated midships section whose independent end contours would not join its neighbours, excluded from the build. A generator that emits parts still has to solve joins, mounts and shared proportions, and nothing in the pack shows one doing so. Whether newer part-aware models change this is a currency claim for the writer legs to source; on pack evidence alone it is a research watch item, not a production route.

### What has worked for small teams

The pack contains no sourced external case studies, so the only production evidence available to this leg is our own, and it is unusually direct: a solo-scale pipeline ran three bake-offs on the same hulls, and the pattern that survived all three is human judgement at the decision points (which parts are hero parts, which generated candidates pass) with machines doing volume work (the donor mesh's masses, baked masks, trim). Every route that removed the human from a decision point failed a screenshot test: route B's automatic remesh "looks bad" (R83), route A's fully authored rebuild went boat-like and changed proportions, AI part generation misread all three test parts. Labelled INFERRED as a general claim; as a description of our three rounds it is sourced from the pack.

## Question 3: AI mesh models

What the pack establishes, and only this: the current production generator is Pixal3D / TRELLIS.2 (ComfyUI multi-views in, mesh out), and only one hull (C19_51) has completed the full chain. Round 3 gives the hardest pack-sourced data point on part-level generation: single parts from isolated concept crops came back at 17.5 to 25.1 million native triangles, needed cleanup, and misread their subjects in three distinct ways (context bleed into neighbouring structures, invention of a different part than the crop showed, disconnected masses). The pack names Hunyuan3D 2.x, TRELLIS and successors, Step1X-3D, Hi3DGen and part-aware models as the survey field, but gives no versions, licences, VRAM figures, resolutions or hard-surface benchmarks for any of them. This leg therefore asserts no currency claims; the following is an evidence-ranked position plus the test any challenger must pass.

### Ranked recommendation

1. Keep Pixal3D / TRELLIS.2 for whole-hull mass generation, where it is already integrated and where its output is the thing rounds 1 to 3 learned to repair; stop using single-crop part generation as a production step (round 3 evidence above) and route part needs through question 2 routes 1 and 2. The known weakness to manage is resolution control: a generator whose parts arrive at tens of millions of triangles has no usable native budget, so any generation step needs a stated triangle target and a decimation step before it enters the pipeline (INFERRED as process advice from the round 3 numbers).
2. Challenger models (Hunyuan3D line, TRELLIS successors, Step1X-3D, Hi3DGen, part-aware models) earn adoption only through the calibration set, not through demos: run each candidate on the three R82 Q4 calibration hulls' hardest pack-documented tests - the engine, gun pair and bridge as separate parts, and one whole-hull generation from the refreshed reference views - and judge on the round 3 criteria (shape fidelity to the reference, separate parts where the reference has separate parts, mounting faces that join, triangle count before cleanup, cleanup hours). Which of these models is currently best at hard-surface mechanical shapes, what each needs in VRAM on a 16 GB card, its licence, and its input resolution requirements are exactly the claims the writer legs must source; this leg ranks none of them above another.
3. Input preparation, whatever the model: full-ship reference views over isolated crops. All three round 3 part failures are consistent with context loss - a crop of an engine does not tell the generator where the engine ends, so it invents the stern around it (INFERRED as the mechanism; the failures themselves are pack-sourced). Prepare orthographic views (front, side, top) plus the three-quarter hero view, on clean flat backgrounds, at the generator's full input resolution, from the question 4 reference sheet, so every candidate sees the same controlled inputs and results stay comparable across models and rounds.

## Question 4: the reference sheet

### Ranked recommendation

1. Build the Pyre-Barge reference sheet first, from the refreshed look pack, and test what we already have against it before generating anything new. R92's sharpest consequence is easy to miss: round 3's final blob sweep marked all nine cells "rebuild by hand" against the OLD concept, and that verdict is now void, because the target changed. Stage 5 against the new reference is untested. The sheet should carry, on one document per ship: orthographic front, side and top views; the three-quarter hero view (the flagship prompt already specifies three quarter from front below); the palette as swatches with hex values (#6a2c22, #b8402a, #e0402c for Hullbreakers); and callouts naming the signature features the refreshed look mandates (listening dishes and wake sniffer scoops open at the bow, boarding claws folded along the forward hull, chains between sections, trophy plates flagship only, far too many rockets, no lances). One sheet, two readers: the clean ortho views are the generator input (question 3 route 3), and the annotated version with callouts is the hand-modelling brief. Cost: INFERRED at a few hours per faction hero sheet using the existing FLUX / ComfyUI concept stage, since the look pack prompts are already written; the flagship prompt in the pack is detailed enough to generate from directly.
2. Extend the sheet format to the calibration set next (Eclipse zenith cruiser, Ninefold Maw), one sheet per faction hero, once the Pyre-Barge sheet has survived a round 4 judgement. The calibration set is the fleet's measurement instrument (R82 Q4); reference sheets for its other two hulls make every future model or method test comparable. Note the round 3 caution: do not apply a mechanical kit blindly to Chorus or Glasswrights, and Ninefold Maw is organic (round 1 priced its route B as an 8 to 16 hour organic sculpt and route A as unsuitable), so its sheet and its methods will differ from the machine hulls'.
3. Regenerate concepts for the whole fleet LAST, and only in the sheet format that round 4 validates. Ranked third because the fleet-wide pass is the expensive version of an unproven format: 36 hulls, of which exactly one has ever completed the full existing chain. Regenerating everything first would spend the effort before knowing whether the sheet drives generators and modellers to the same ship, and R82 Q2 already settled that concepts are reference, not paint, so a concept only earns its cost when a hull is actually entering the build queue. Fleet regeneration should follow build order, a few hulls ahead of production, not precede it.

## Round 4 proposal for the Pyre-Barge (issue #146, per R92)

R92 fixes the three routes. What follows says what each route must change versus rounds 1 to 3, in the order I recommend running them, and how the owner should judge. All three are judged on the same evidence: before/after crops head-on and grazing (the issue #112 acceptance standard), in-game screenshots at battle distance and close zoom against the new reference sheet, and a stated triangle count and 20-ship GPU time, because every round so far has blown the performance budget at least once (round 2 variants 438,732 to 613,864 tris and 4.23 to 5.69 ms at 20 ships, against today's 256,468 tris and about 3 ms; stage 5 sits at 305,857 tris and 3.92 ms).

Route 1 (run first): #129 stage 5 plus a full texture, material and paint pass. What changes versus rounds 1 to 3: the target. Stage 5 was judged "rebuild by hand" against a concept the owner has since retired; route 1 re-judges the identical geometry against the refreshed reference, and spends its effort on the one thing stage 5 lacks and the owner already reacted to ("actually looks okay-ish, especially if we add more texture and material, maybe paint to it"). The paint pass is question 1 routes 1 and 2: baked-mask procedural materials in the faction palette, the shared Hullbreaker trim sheet, decals for trophy plates and markings. Nothing about the geometry changes, which is the point: this route isolates the paint variable. Cost: the materials slice of the rollout estimate, 1 to 2 hours, plus decal placement and captures (INFERRED total 3 to 6 focused hours). Success looks like: the nine-cell sweep against the NEW reference no longer reads as a different ship at battle distance, and the grazing crops show wear on edges instead of waves.

Route 2 (run second, machine time in parallel with route 1 if convenient): the whole ship regenerated at full resolution from new reference views. What changes versus rounds 1 to 3: the inputs and the target. Rounds 1 to 3 generated and repaired against the outdated concept; route 2 generates from the question 4 sheet (orthographic views, clean backgrounds, refreshed look, palette-locked prompt). Two honest warnings from pack evidence. First, regeneration does not skip repair: a fresh AI mesh brings its own normal map and waviness (R86), so route 2's true cost is generation plus the stage 1 to 5 repair pass, INFERRED at the same 8 to 12 focused hours per hull after generation, and it should be proposed to the owner in that full form, not as a shortcut. Second, resolution must be budgeted before generation, not after: round 3's part outputs at 17.5 to 25.1 million triangles show what unbudgeted generation returns. Success looks like: broad masses closer to the refreshed reference than stage 5's retained masses (the one thing route 1 cannot fix), at a triangle count that survives decimation without going blocky.

Route 3 (the comparison and the fallback): hand-modelled against the new reference with kit parts at today's detail density. What changes versus round 1 route A, which is this route's ancestor and its warning: route A came out sparse, blocky, boat-like, and changed the donor proportions. Round 4 route 3 must therefore model against the reference sheet's orthographic views (proportion control route A lacked), and "today's detail density" means the stage 4 hand-part standard - bevels, seams, fasteners, cooling fins, recessed bores - extended across the hull, with kit parts merged at export per R82 Q3, not round 1's sparser kit. Cost anchor from round 1: route A Pyre-Barge finishing was estimated at 12 to 20 hours after shared tools exist; the shared tools and faction kit are further along now (INFERRED), so I would budget the same range and treat beating it as a bonus. The owner's R92 quote endorses trying routes 1 and 2; route 3 stands in the comparison as written, and its role is to be the control that tells us whether either cheaper route actually closed the gap, and the fallback if both disappoint. Success looks like: the only route with zero AI-mesh artefacts, judged on whether its extra hours buy a visibly better ship at battle distance, not just at close zoom.

Sequencing pick: route 1 first and judged, route 2 generated while route 1 is painted, route 3 built only if the route 1 and 2 sheets leave the owner undecided, because route 3 is the expensive one and its information value is highest as a tiebreaker. If route 1 passes the owner's screenshot judgement, round 4 has its fleet answer for machine hulls (repair in place plus this paint stack) and routes 2 and 3 become per-hull escalations, which is the posture R82 Q1 already approved in principle.
