# How shipped games make a glass ship read as glass

Research for CD5 (issue 1058) and the Glasswright fleet, from issue 1141. Council: Rey blind leg first, then DeepSeek and the Gemini seat (ran on Hydrogen; the picker offers no Flash label), Rey chairs. Qwen seat blocked by its daily limit (the brief is parked in its chat for a retry); Grok seat blocked by the grok.com bot wall on the cloud browser (retry path is the Pixel in-app browser). Both blocks are recorded in the chair note at the end.

## The short answer

Glass in a dark sky does not read from transparency. It reads from three things the ship carries with it: something bright inside it, something bright for it to reflect that is not the sky, and hard bright edges. Every shipped example below that survives a dark scene and a small on-screen size cheats at least two of those three. Every CD5 failure so far is one of the three going missing.

## Why the four CD5 runs failed, in those terms

- Run 2, "smooth playdough with colors swirled": smooth normals and soft shading, no hard facet breaks and no bright edges. Soft gradients are the clay signal; glass needs sharp value steps.
- Run 3, smoked glass closest but "random streaks": the colour and dispersion were spread across whole panels, and screen-space refraction bends whatever sits behind the ship (stars, trails, other ships), which at battle size is noise. Dispersion and refraction artefacts read as glass only in small doses, at glints and grazing rims.
- Run 4, "dark, nearly black glass": the glass stayed, the interior went to one charcoal, and the read collapsed. The run 3 and run 4 sheets together are the control experiment: same smoked shell, bright mixed interior reads as glass, dark unified interior reads as a black hull. Real glass reflects only a few percent of light head-on and transmits the rest; against a dead-star sky there is almost nothing to reflect or transmit, so an honest material goes black.
- The lighting test (issue 1137) agrees: five lighting variants barely move the read, because the problem is what the material has to work with, not how much light the scene has.

## Shipped examples

1. Endless Space 2, Umbral Choir ships (Penumbra). A dark translucent outer shell over a bright emissive inner skeleton, with a strong Fresnel rim on the silhouette. The read is self-contained: the ship carries its own light source inside the glass, so black space cannot starve it. Survives dark sky yes, small size yes. Pictures: the fleet's 3D lead, Stevans Robert, walks through building these ships at https://www.blendernation.com/2019/05/10/behind-the-scenes-endless-space-2/ and his production renders are on his ArtStation (search "Stevans Robert Umbral Choir ArtStation").
2. Endless Space 2, Vodyani arks and warships. The opposite split: dark architectural ribs frame large emissive "stained glass" panels. The dark frame guarantees the silhouette, the glowing panels guarantee the glass read, and the high-contrast segmentation still reads at a few dozen pixels. Survives dark sky yes, small size yes. Pictures: https://www.gamepressure.com/endless-space-2/the-vodyani/z69cd6 (guide with in-game shots) and The Art of Endless Space 2, Vodyani chapter, https://www.scribd.com/document/705090086/The-Art-of-Endless-Space-2
3. Stellaris, Crystalline Entities. Faceted, flat-shaded crystal shards with hard specular steps as they turn, around a bright emissive core. At strategic zoom the facets flashing bright or dark in steps, plus the core glow, are the whole read; there is no reliance on seeing through anything. Survives dark sky yes, small size yes. Pictures and in-game description: https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Spaceborne_aliens
4. Subnautica, Ghost Leviathan. A transparent outer membrane over a smaller, opaque, bioluminescent inner body, in near-total darkness. The wiki description is the recipe in one line: a soft transparent outer membrane covering a muscular inner body with pulsating bioluminescent stripes. Survives dark yes; at tactics zoom the membrane alone would vanish, so the inner body has to be bright and simple. Pictures: https://subnautica.fandom.com/wiki/Ghost_Leviathan
5. Lightmatter, glass spheres and conduits. A puzzle game about light, and its glass is not refraction: it is a curated cubemap reflection, deliberately exaggerated and decoupled from the actual room, plus a crisp rim. The lesson is the decoupling: the reflection content is authored for the material, not captured from the scene. Technical breakdown: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/programming-visual-effects-for-lightmatter
6. StarCraft II, Protoss Void Ray and the SC2 crystal family. An emissive faceted crystal core inside an opaque structural cage. The cage gives silhouette and structure; the glowing core gives the crystal read at RTS distance. Survives dark sky yes, small size yes. Pictures: search "StarCraft 2 Void Ray prismatic core in game".

What does not survive: true transparency and screen-space refraction as the main mechanism (Overwatch's Mei ice is the clearest real-glass shader among the legs' examples, and it needs a bright environment and a large on-screen size; in a dark sky its centre goes dark and only the rim and internal cracks survive). Subtle tint differences do not survive either; run 4 is the proof.

## Ranked methods for CD5, for Codex

1. Opaque deep-core glass for the fleet (examples: Stellaris, Lightmatter). Keep the hull opaque. Give it hard faceted normals, a bevel or curvature edge mask that adds emission along chamfers and silhouette edges just above the bloom threshold, and a private high-contrast reflection cubemap (sparse stars plus two or three bright pale strips) sampled by the glass material only, Fresnel-gated so centres stay smoked and rims light up. Fake the interior with a one-layer parallax core texture at close camera; on the lower tier, swap the parallax for a static emissive core texture. What it costs: one opaque draw per ship, one cubemap sample plus one texture fetch, no backbuffer copy, no transparent sorting, no overdraw; the legs estimate this as a small fraction of the 12.5 ms budget for dozens of ships (estimate, not measured; the scene 207 gate decides). This is the council pick for the fleet because it answers all three failures at once and has no weak tier.
2. Two-layer hybrid for the flagship CD5 only (examples: Umbral Choir, Ghost Leviathan, and CD5 run 3 B itself). A real smoked transparent shell, but only over a rebuilt interior: a few big, bright shapes in one ice-blue and white family with two or three saturated accent cores (run 3's purple core already works), plus the same private cubemap and rim treatment as method 1. What it costs: one extra draw and one layer of transparent overdraw per flagship, plus sorting care; that is why it is flagship-only. This is the pick for CD5 itself, because the close camera at radius 26 is where a real interior with true parallax earns its cost, and run 3 B proves the read is already there when the interior is bright.
3. Dark frame plus stained-glass panels (examples: Vodyani, Void Ray). Thirty to forty percent of the silhouette in dark opaque ribs, the rest emissive faceted glass panels with the method 1 cubemap. What it costs: a single opaque material with mask channels, under method 1's cost. The safest silhouette read at radius 170, and a good variation for other Glasswright classes so the fleet is not one recipe repeated; it reads as glass panels rather than one glass hull, which is the trade.
4. Facet contrast bake as the lower-tier fallback (examples: FTL's Crystal Cruiser, Stellaris). Paint the facet value steps into the hull textures so the crystal structure reads with no transparency and no cubemap at all, plus the rim. What it costs: texture work only. The run 3 sheet's tinted fallback panels are already halfway to this and read decently at radius 170.
5. Sparse emissive cores as garnish (example: Stellaris). A few small emissive nodes inside the hull (drive core, weapon hearts) for distance identity. What it costs: emissive texture areas and bloom care; keep them small and few so they stay inside the flash and brightness discipline the game already uses.

Avoid: screen-space refraction as a primary mechanism (it is the run 3 streak machine and forces a backbuffer copy), full-surface dispersion (small doses at rims and glints only), and two-pass back-face/front-face alpha shells for the fleet (the most fragile option with many overlapping ships; hero shots only).

## Chair note

The legs disagreed on one point: DeepSeek and the Gemini seat both put an opaque shell first; the blind Rey leg put a real transparent shell over a bright interior first, on the run 3 B evidence. The ranked list splits it by role rather than picking a winner: opaque for the fleet (cost and sorting), the real shell for the one flagship that lives at the close camera. All three legs independently rejected refraction as the mechanism, and all three independently arrived at the same three ingredients (bright interior or core, authored reflections, hard bright edges), which is why the chair is confident in the short answer even with two seats blocked. Legs' mechanism claims for proprietary games are inferred from shipped visuals and production art, not source code; the frame-time figures are technique-level estimates, not measurements.
