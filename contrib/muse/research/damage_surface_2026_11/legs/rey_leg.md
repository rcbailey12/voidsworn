## 1. Per-game breakdown

Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 1 and 2 (Tindalos Interactive). Light hit: shield flare or a brief impact flash, then almost nothing persistent on the hull [IMPRESSION]. Heavy hit: a critical result disables a named subsystem such as engines, weapons or generator, and fires can start and burn over time. Sources: Gamepressure, Battlefleet Gothic: Armada HUD guide, lists Emergency Repairs as regenerating hull and extinguishing all fires, and boarding as causing critical damage and fire; Magic Game World, Armada II Advanced Tips, states critical damage cripples ships by disabling parts, critical damage may trigger fires, and every 100 hull points lost disables a defence turret. Crippled section: the subsystem itself reads dead or burning rather than the hull opening up; the visual weight is smoke, flame and a darkened, sparking area around that system [IMPRESSION]. Mix: mostly particles (fire, smoke, sparks) and state changes on subsystem meshes, very little true hull cutting; surface scorch is minor compared with the fire read. Distance versus close-up: at tactical distance the read is fire plumes, smoke, listing or drifting motion and the UI critical icons; scorch detail only pays off on zoom [IMPRESSION]. Budget: fleets are small, usually well under 20 capital hulls on screen, so per-ship fire emitters are affordable; no developer source for a hard emitter cap was found [UNVERIFIED].

Homeworld 2 and 3 (Relic, then Blackbird Interactive). Light hit, Homeworld 2: small debris chips off, a brief crater or burn mark, per MobyGames Homeworld 2 reviews page, which describes chunks flaking off leaving a crater, ion cannons leaving burn marks, and plasma and smoke venting from damaged sections. Light hit, Homeworld 3: a weapon-specific impact decal at first. Heavy hit: Homeworld 3 replaces the sticker with Splat Damage. Source: Blackbird Interactive developer blog by Senior FX Artist Tim O'Connell, reported in Bleeding Cool, Homeworld 3 Releases New Blog Featuring Battle Details, and GamingTrend, Homeworld 3 devs release new blog post: four damage types (Kinetic, High Explosive, Energy, Ion) each with its own treatment; decals used in earlier Homeworld games give the mere impression of damage, while Splat Damage creates dynamic impact points that directly alter surface geometry and properties at the exact hit point. Crippled section: larger ships use a series of damage sockets placed around the hull where FX spawn as damage grows (same O'Connell blog, via GamingTrend summary: larger ships use damage sockets, fighters use a distinct camera-facing smoke sprite). Mix: Homeworld 2 is particles plus small mesh debris; Homeworld 3 is surface treatment first (decal and splat shading, with a heat edge ring added for Energy and Missile impacts, per Homeworld 3 Patch 1.3 notes on homeworlduniverse.com), particles from sockets second, and true geometry break-up reserved for death via its procedural Houdini Ship Breaking tool (O'Connell blog). Distance versus close-up: the socket smoke and fire carries at range; splat craters and heat rings are a close-up reward. Budget: socket counts are authored per hull, not per hit, which caps emitters by design; exact socket numbers per class are [UNVERIFIED].

Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (Ironclad Games). Light hit: shield shimmer and impact effects, hull untouched while shields, then armour, absorb damage. Source: gameplay.tips, Sins of a Solar Empire II Guide to Health and Protection Mechanics, confirms the three layers are shields, then armour, then hull, all regenerating after combat. Heavy hit: once in hull, ships trail fire, smoke and sparks and begin to look battered [IMPRESSION]; capital ships at zero hull enter a crippled state with a second hull bar before destruction, per TV Tropes, Sins of a Solar Empire II page. Crippled section: there are no named hull sections; crippling is whole-ship, shown by persistent fire and debris and loss of function rather than a local hole [IMPRESSION]. Mix: heavily particles and lighting, minimal mesh change; damaged ships do not lose chunks in normal play [IMPRESSION]. Distance versus close-up: this is the clearest distance-first design in the set: burning silhouettes and smoke columns read across a gravity well with hundreds of ships, while hull texture detail is for zoom only. Budget: fleets of hundreds are routine (Gaming on PC review notes sprawling engagements), so damage must be cheap sprites and shared materials, with no per-hit mesh work; a sourced developer cap for damage emitters was not found [UNVERIFIED]. Performance staying above 70 fps in large battles was reported by the Arcader review, which supports, but does not prove, that cheap particle damage is the enabler [IMPRESSION].

Star Wars: Empire at War (Petroglyph). Light hit: shield ripple, then scorch and small fires at the struck hardpoint. Heavy hit: the hardpoint itself (turbolaser battery, shield generator, engine, hangar) smokes, flames and then explodes and is gone. Source: GameSpot, Star Wars: Empire at War Walkthrough, confirms capital ships have targetable individual systems including engines and shield generators, destroying the shield generator drops shields and stops recharge; Total War Center review confirms ship health is a composite of hardpoint health and each hardpoint can be destroyed individually with different effects. Crippled section: a destroyed hardpoint stays as a burnt, missing or wrecked fixture on the hull, so the ship tells its own damage history by what is absent. Mix: mesh change dominates at the hardpoint (the part is removed or wrecked), particles support it, surface decals are minor. Distance versus close-up: at range you read which turrets or engines are missing and which are burning; fine scorch is zoom-only [IMPRESSION]. Budget: space battles field dozens of ships but only capital ships and stations carry full hardpoint sets, so expensive per-part damage is limited to a few hulls [IMPRESSION].

Nebulous: Fleet Command (Eridanus Industries). Light hit: armour is dented or stripped locally, a component loses HP, occasional atmosphere leak. Heavy hit: fires start inside hull components, and can spread. Source: NEBULOUS: Fleet Command Official Wiki, Damage Control page: fires deal 5 percent of a component max HP every 30 seconds and have a 25 percent chance every 45 seconds to spread to a component within 12.5 m; most components have a Functioning Threshold below which they cease to function, and repairs only restore up to a Max Repair cap above the lowest condition reached. Crippled section: the component stops working (turret, drive, reactor) and shows fire, leak venting or overload effects; reactor and magazine casualties can cascade. Mix: simulation first, visuals second: external read is modest scorch, venting gas and fire glow at the damaged area, with the detailed state carried by the damage control UI [IMPRESSION]. Distance versus close-up: fleets are small and the camera is close, so it can afford subtle reads; at Voidsworn distance most of its surface detail would vanish [IMPRESSION]. Budget: battles are typically under 20 ships total, and component counts per ship are high but visual emitters per casualty are few; no published frame budget was found [UNVERIFIED].

EVE Online (CCP), as the extra entry, building on Job 1. Source basis in the pack: fires trail once a ship is in hull damage, fire count scales by class (one for a frigate, two to three for cruisers and battleships, ten for structures), and fires are off at low settings. Light hit: shield or armour effects only, no hull fire. Heavy hit, in hull: persistent fire trails and smoke from the hull, plus repair effects visible only on damaged sections of the model (EVE University Wiki, Visual effects page, for armour and hull repairer visibility). Crippled: the ship stays whole; there is no local hole, the fire itself is the state. Mix: almost entirely particles and shader glow, no mesh cutting. Distance: fire trails are sized to read at fleet distance, which is the whole point in fights with far more than 20 ships. Budget: class-scaled fire caps and a low-settings off switch are the sourced lesson, already adopted in Job 1 step 7.

Star Citizen (Cloud Imperium), second extra, as a warning. Its stated design uses discrete damage states per part, each state with multiple break-off pieces, plus projectile hit decals with height maps for laser marks and bullet hits, and projectiles traced inside the hull to internal components (developer statement quoted on HardwareZone forums, Official Star Citizen thread). That fidelity is built for one ship at close range in first person. At a 20-ship tactical camera it buys nothing a player can see, and its cost model does not transfer [IMPRESSION].

Transferable lessons:
- Homeworld 3: author damage sockets per section and spawn stage FX there; never let hit count decide emitter count.
- EVE Online: cap fires by ship class and give low settings a simplified form; the fire trail, not the hole, is the distance read.
- Empire at War: damage that removes or darkens a recognisable part reads at range; anonymous holes do not.
- Battlefleet Gothic: Armada: fire plus a disabled function is what players remember; reserve mesh change for that moment.
- Sins of a Solar Empire 2: if it does not read as smoke, flame or silhouette change at full zoom-out, it is decoration, not damage information.

## 2. Staged damage spec

Built on the shipped ladder in godot/fx/fx_ship.gd sync() (about line 748): stage 0 at f above 0.8, stage 1 at f 0.8 to 0.6, stage 2 at f 0.6 to 0.4, stage 3 at f 0.4 to 0.2, stage 4 at f 0.2 or below or broken. Three sections per ship (eng, mid, prow, godot/fx/fx_hull.gd:12-15). Sizes below give feature diameter in world units, then pixels at 1080p at 7.7 px per unit and at 4K at 15.4 px per unit. Anything under about 10 px at 1080p does not exist at the battle camera.

Stage 0 (f above 0.8). Nothing persistent. Weapon marks from individual hits may show as fresh, small surface marks only (see marks rule below). No scorch overlay, no emitters.

Stage 1 (f 0.8 to 0.6). Surface only. Spreading scorch overlay as shipped in _plan() (line 839), radius L x 0.064, plus 1 smaller burn. Diameter: Frigate (L 10.4) 1.33 units, 10 px at 1080p, 20 px at 4K; Battleship (L 33.6) 4.30 units, 33 px, 66 px. Scorch heat 0.15 as shipped, no glow rim yet, no particles except a brief hit spark from the existing pooled hit FX. This is borderline on a Frigate and clear on a Battleship.

Stage 2 (f 0.6 to 0.4). Surface plus shallow mesh. Scorch grows to radius L x 0.098 (Frigate diameter 2.04 units, 16 px, 31 px; Battleship 6.59 units, 51 px, 101 px), 2 smaller burns, and the shipped dent at radius L x 0.05, depth 22 percent of radius (Frigate diameter 1.04 units, 8 px, 16 px; Battleship 3.36 units, 26 px, 52 px). Add the new persistent layer the pack says is missing: one small fire or smoke emitter at the section anchor for weight class 2 and up, smoke only for weight 1, none for weight 0. Add a glowing hot rim on the dent edge using the faction damage_glow colour, fading over a few seconds after the stage change, then settling to a dark rim. Stage 2 is the first stage a player can reliably read from the full-board view on every class from Frigate up, because scorch diameter passes 15 px and the smoke column adds height above the hull.

Stage 3 (f 0.4 to 0.2). Heavy surface, still no open hole. This is the main change against shipped code, which cuts a hole here. Scorch radius L x 0.132 (Frigate diameter 2.75 units, 21 px, 42 px; Battleship 8.87 units, 68 px, 137 px), scorch heat 0.3 as shipped for stage 3, 3 smaller burns, the dent deepens (reuse dent kind with depth 35 percent of radius, radius L x 0.06) and gains a crater kind at the anchor for explosive marks. Persistent fire at the anchor plus smoke trail from the section, occasional venting gas puffs and spark showers on a slow timer, all pooled. Engine section at stage 3 keeps the shipped engine sputter. One panel or fitting at the anchor may be shown displaced or missing as a shallow recess, using the dent and crater path, never a through-cut. At zoom this stage should look torn and burning; at distance it reads as a large dark patch with fire and smoke.

Stage 4 (f 0.2 or below, or broken). The only open break. One break per section, at the anchor, radius L x 0.08 proposed, larger than the shipped stage 4 hole at L x 0.046. Diameter: Frigate 1.66 units, 13 px at 1080p, 26 px at 4K; Battleship 5.38 units, 41 px, 83 px. Weight 2 and up may add the shipped second break at L x 0.032 only if the first is built. Streaming debris, spark showers and internal pops stay as shipped in _build_breach().

The cardboard problem, answered directly. Shipped stage 3 and 4 holes fail because the cavity behind a clean torn outline shows flat interior faces and straight lines on a detailed hull. The stage 4 break in this spec differs in four ways. One, it sits inside the stage 3 crater and dent field, so the rim is already buckled and scorched before it opens; the eye reads a wound in damaged metal, not a cut-out in clean metal. Two, the opening is smaller relative to its damaged surround (break diameter about 60 percent of the stage 3 scorch diameter on a Battleship) instead of a hole punched in an intact surface. Three, the interior is filled, not empty: Job 1 interior darkness rule (back faces near-black with ember noise) plus a recessed dark throat, layered fire inside using damage_glow, drifting ember particles and smoke issuing from the break, so no flat lit plane is ever the brightest thing in the hole. Four, peeled plates are irregular and overlap the rim asymmetrically, with at least two plates bent inward, breaking the flat outline. If a capture still shows a readable straight interior edge, the break fails the test in section 4.

Per-weapon marks. Caps stay: MARK_CAP 12 per ship, SEC_MARK_CAP 4 per section (godot/fx/fx_breach.gd about line 63), repeats grow to GROW_MAX 1.6. Kinds that stay surface at every stage: dent (0), crater (3), cluster (4, pits only, the few torn-through pits are removed), surface (5, ion). Kinds that change: hole (1) is no longer placed by weapon hits at all; it is created only by the stage 4 ladder. Slot (2, lance and laser burn-through) stays a surface slot with molten lips at stages 0 to 3; at stage 4, a slot that lands inside an already broken section may join the existing break edge, but never cuts a new opening. Hatch and ram styles stay surface crumple and scorch. In short, weapons write the surface record; only the section hull fraction opens the hull.

Counts and frame budget at 20 ships. Marks: at most 12 per ship, 240 total, all in one interior mesh and one overlay mesh per ship as today, rebuilt on the worker thread only when a mark is added or a stage changes. Emitters: at most 1 fire plus 1 smoke per damaged section, and only from stage 2 up, capped by class as in EVE (weight 0 and 1: smoke only, 1 per ship; weight 2: up to 2 sections live; weight 3 and 4: up to 3 sections live). Worst case, 20 capital ships, 60 fire and 60 smoke emitters; typical mixed fleet, about 25 to 35 live emitters. Particles come from the existing pools in godot/fx/fx_pool.gd with a proposed global cap of about 4,000 live damage particles across all ships, allocated to the most damaged sections first. The 3 counted flashes per second cap in godot/fx/fx.gd:22-27 is untouched: stage changes use the existing uncounted spark burst (fx_ship.gd about line 1140), internal pops at stage 4 must not register as counted flashes, and under reduce-flashing the fire glow is steady, not flickering. Safe mode (play_godot_safe.bat, R49): stages show as scorch overlay plus a dark crater disc with a plain glow dot, no hole mesh, no venting; every new element above needs that simplified form. Frame check is a build-order task, not a promise here.

Repair. Repair already drops stages via _apply_stages() in fx_ship.gd. Spec: when a section stage falls, reverse in order. Stage 4 break seals first into the stage 3 crater form (a worker-thread rebuild, plates shown as patched, dark scar plate with weld-bright edge for metal factions), fires go out at stage 2 to 1, scorch shrinks one step per stage but a faint permanent scar overlay at stage 1 size remains until the section returns to full, so a repaired ship keeps a history. Weapon marks do not erase on repair; they fade to dull scorch at half contrast.

Squadrons. Keep stage 0 always, as shipped. Reason: a squadron hull is L 2.6, so even a stage 3 scorch would be about 5 px at 1080p, below the existence threshold, and per-squadron mesh rebuilds are the worst cost-to-signal in the system. Squadron damage stays on the existing burning flag and smoke sprite, the Homeworld fighter solution.

## 3. Faction variants

Chorus (organic). Build on _build_organic_section (godot/fx/fx_ship.gd:1322) and wound_kind 1 (godot/fx/fx_breach.gd:1641, :1653), with the softened bend, ring width and buckle already in _plan() about line 870. Stage 1: bruising, a darkened, swollen patch in place of scorch, colour shifted toward the yellow-green damage_glow [0.66, 0.92, 0.16], surface gloss rises so it looks wet. Stage 2: the bruise splits into shallow fissures, dent becomes a pressed-in hollow with raised lips, spore puffs replace smoke (trail style 2 and engine_extra spores already in faction_looks.json), no fire yet; instead a slow seep glow in the fissures. Stage 3, the heavy stage: an open-looking wound that is still surface, torn flesh flaps (the crater path with soft buckle), exposed brighter tissue in the recess, spore venting and drifting motes, occasional twitch pulse of the rim [IMPRESSION as a proposal, not a sourced fact]. Stage 4, the break: a real tear, edges curled outward, inside it layered muscle-dark interior under the Job 1 darkness rule tinted green, glowing ichor embers, and flesh strands bridging part of the opening so it never reads as a clean hole. Regrowth reverses visibly and differently from metal repair: at each stage drop, wound edges grow toward each other, stage 4 tear knits to a raised scar ridge at stage 3 size, fissures close at stage 2, and a pale new-skin patch, smoother and lighter than the hull finish (Chorus finish is 0.05 metal, 0.38 roughness), remains at stage 1 until full. Regrowing sections should shed the spore emitter first, so a healing Chorus ship stops venting before it looks whole.

Glasswrights (crystal, id ithar, wound_kind 2). Scorch does not belong on clear crystal and silver, so scorch is replaced by frosting: a milky, opaque crazed patch in the overlay, same radii and stage growth as the standard scorch, reading white against the pale crystal at distance. Stage 1: a frost patch plus a single bright crack line from the anchor, which catches light and flashes as the ship moves, using the prismatic shield_style 3 language already in the faction table. Stage 2: crack network spreads inside the frost patch (surface kind with a crack pattern in place of ion pattern), dent becomes a chipped cone crater with sharp facets, no glow rim; the damage_glow pale blue-white [0.85, 0.92, 1.0] appears only as light leaking from the deepest cracks. Stage 3: heavy crazing, part of the patch goes fully opaque and silvered, a few crystal splinters shed as debris instead of sparks, soundless venting shown as glittering dust, no fire and no smoke at any stage, which makes Glasswright damage instantly distinct on a mixed board. Stage 4, the break: a shattered opening with jagged crystal teeth, interior shown as dark, light-swallowing depth with slow blue-white glints deep inside, never a flat disc; edges refract the backdrop so the hole distorts what is behind it at the rim. Repair: cracks heal from the edge inward and the frost clears last, leaving a faint seam line at full repair.

Eclipse (machine, id deathless). There is no wound_kind for deathless today; add wound_kind 3. It is worth one variant because Eclipse is a named done-when faction and its damage verbs are different, not just its colour. Within the same stages: stage 1 scorch becomes a darkened, dead panel patch, panel lines go black and any lit conduits in the section cut out in a small area, so damage reads as power loss on a dark, high-metal hull (finish 0.55 metal). Stage 2: dent plus arcing, short violet arc sparks (damage_glow [0.72, 0.5, 1.0]) snapping between the dent rim and the nearest fitting on a slow timer, and a thin coolant vent, pale vapour, instead of smoke. Stage 3: panels hang displaced, severed conduit ends glow violet and leak embers (engine_extra embers already in the table), fires are rare and small; the main read is a dark section with intermittent arcs and one guttering light (trail style 3). Stage 4, the break: a torn machine opening with bent frame members, cable bundles hanging into the opening, interior darkness with violet ember noise, and arcs still crossing the gap while the section is broken. Repair: panels reseat in visible steps and conduit lights come back in sequence, so a repaired Eclipse section visibly reboots.

Sunward Crusade (id voidsworn). Black iron and ember orange [1.0, 0.5, 0.14]. Standard staged shape, with the heaviest scorch contrast in the game: near-black scorch on dark iron reads by its orange hot rim and by fire, so Crusade ships get the strongest stage 2 and 3 rim glow and the most persistent open fires. Dents show bright torn edges. A Crusade ship at stage 3 should be the easiest ship on the board to read as hurt.

Hegemony. Pale grey hulls, steel blue accents, damage_glow warm [1.0, 0.72, 0.38], shield users. On pale plate, scorch is high contrast and needs no help: keep scorch smaller in feel by using the standard radii but lower overlay darkness, and let neat, panel-aligned damage be the character, dents follow panel boundaries, fires are tidy and short, smoke thin. Hegemony damage should look controlled and contained until stage 4, where the break is the same size as everyone else's, which lands harder for the contrast.

Ashfall Hullbreakers (id corsair). Rust red, deep orange glow [1.0, 0.3, 0.07], engine_extra soot. Their hulls start dirty, so scorch alone will not read: damage adds fresh bright metal in dents and craters, heavy soot halos wider than the standard scorch, and greasy black smoke from stage 2 instead of grey. Stage 3 adds leaking, burning fuel trails. The character is a ship that was already patched and scarred, taking new damage that looks fresh against old repairs.

## 5. Owner questions

1. Where should the first open break appear? A: Stage 4 only, section at 20 percent or broken (chair pick). B: Late stage 3, below 30 percent. C: Keep stage 3 as today. Stakes: A makes breaks rare and meaningful and kills the cardboard read in normal combat; B and C keep more holes on screen and keep the complaint alive.

2. How much fire and smoke should a hurt ship carry at battle distance? A: Class-capped, one fire and one smoke per damaged section from stage 2 (chair pick). B: More, every damaged section burns hard from stage 1. C: Less, smoke only at stage 3 and up. Stakes: A reads clearly at 20 ships and protects the frame budget; B is the most cinematic and the most likely to hurt fps and readability; C is safest and blandest.

3. Should repaired ships keep visible scars? A: Yes, a faint permanent scar per section until full repair, Chorus knits, metal patches (chair pick). B: No, repair returns a clean hull. Stakes: A gives veteran ships character and a readable history across a run; B is cleaner to build and reads simpler.

4. Glasswright damage: no fire at all? A: Yes, frost, cracks and leaking light only, never fire or smoke (chair pick). B: Give them pale blue fire at stage 3 and 4 like everyone else. Stakes: A makes one faction instantly recognisable by how it hurts; B keeps one shared damage language and is easier to learn.

5. How far should Eclipse machine damage go? A: Full wound_kind 3, arcing, coolant, dead panels, rebooting repair (chair pick). B: Colour only, violet glow in the standard metal stages. Stakes: A costs one more variant build in Task 6; B saves that work but leaves a named faction feeling like a recolour.
