Headline
The battle soundscape has solid weapon and UI coverage but fails at the moments that should feel most cinematic: staged capital-ship death fires all its audio at once with no progression, small-ship death leaves the run and the lingering chunk silent, and the hulk outcome has no sound at all. The single most important gap is the three confirmed missing action cues for boarding, brace, and Nemesis adaptation, because they represent player and enemy decisions that happen in total silence and break the feedback loop the rest of the system works to maintain. This report defines every missing and adjusted cue, names checkable sources, and gives the mixing and build guidance to land them without shouting over a 20-ship fight.
Cue List
Hand-over (player). Cue id: ui_your. Exists. Fires once at the first hand-over of each round per the F1 flow. Does not fire on subsequent hand-overs in the same round. No faction difference. No change needed.
Hand-over (enemy). Cue id: ui_enemy. Exists. Fires once per round with the violet strip. Does not repeat for each enemy ship. No change needed.
Plan confirm. Cue id: ui_confirm. Exists. Fires on click-confirm or Enter for a planned move. ui_select fires on target pick, ui_cancel on Esc. Whether planning needs a softer variant is a design call; I recommend keeping ui_confirm as-is and lowering its gain by 3 dB in the planning context via a gain override in cues.json rather than adding a second cue. No new cue.
Fire. Existing gun, torp, and lance families. No new cue. The defer defect is addressed in the mixing note.
Hit by weight. Existing hit, hit_heavy, patter_hull, patter_shield, shield, shield_break. No new cue. Far-camera legibility is a design question outside this scope.
Staged capital death. Five new cues replace the single simultaneous boom_l plus bass_drop plus wreck firing.
cap_powerdown: a low descending hum, about 0.4 seconds, pitch dropping from a mid drone to near silence, like a large transformer losing charge. Fires at the moment running lights and engine glow die. Does not fire for small ships.
cap_vent: a short pressurised hiss with a faint metallic ring, about 0.3 seconds, like a steam valve releasing in a large pipe. Fires at the venting-along-the-cut moment, roughly 0.4 seconds after powerdown. One instance only.
cap_crack: a sharp, dry, high-mid snap with a brief sizzle tail, about 0.25 seconds, like thick glass or ceramic fracturing under heat. Fires at the thermal crack line. One instance.
cap_split: a deep structural groan into a muffled impact, about 0.6 seconds, pitch low and heavy, like a ship hull tearing. Fires at the split into two halves. One instance. This replaces the current boom_l trigger point.
cap_coreflash: a contained, bright, short bloom, about 0.5 seconds, a rising whoosh into a soft thump, like a distant furnace igniting once. Fires at the counted core flash inside one half. One instance. Follows the slow-motion beat (see mixing note).
Small-ship death run. New cue: die_run. A brief sputtering crackle with a descending pitch wobble, about 0.5 seconds, like a small engine seizing and sparking. Fires at the start of the death run for small ships only. Does not fire for capitals. One instance per death.
Small-ship burst. Existing boom_s. No change to the cue itself. It fires at the counted flash.
Small-ship lingering chunk. New cue: die_ember. A very quiet, slow cooling tick with a faint metallic ring fading out, about 1.2 seconds, like a hot piece of metal cooling in vacuum. Fires 0.3 seconds after boom_s. Gain low, sits under other action. One instance. Does not fire under reduce-flashing (see mixing note).
Hulk outcome. New cue: hulk_drift. A single soft gas release, almost silent, about 0.8 seconds, like a sigh of air from a sealed room, with a faint creak. No explosion layer. Fires only when the destruction system resolves a hulk. One instance. Replaces any boom call for that outcome.
Reinforcement arrival. New cue: arrive_ship. A short, bright, two-note chime with a slight upward interval, about 0.4 seconds, like a sonar ping resolving into a tone. Distinct from ui_alert (which is a warning) and from launch (which is wings departing). Fires once per arriving ship with the banner. Does not stack if multiple ships arrive the same tick; stagger gap handles it. No faction difference.
Enemy turn start. Cue id: ui_enemy. Exists. No change.
Boarding. New cue: board_cue. A short, aggressive, mid-range thud with a metallic scrape tail, about 0.35 seconds, like a breaching charge hitting a hull plate. Fires when the board branch executes (battle.gd:1308). Fires for both player and enemy boarding. No faction-specific variant needed at this stage. Once per board action; no per-round cap because boards are rare.
Brace. New cue: brace_cue. A low, firm, resonant lock sound, about 0.3 seconds, like a heavy bolt sliding home into a socket. Pitch sits below the UI range. Fires when the brace order is issued (battle.gd:1291). Fires for both sides. Once per brace action.
Nemesis adaptation. New cue: nemesis_adapt. A slow, unsettling two-tone swell, about 0.7 seconds, rising a semitone and settling, like a distant alarm changing key. Fires once per battle at the adaptation event (rules.gd:1252). Does not repeat. No side difference; it is a systemic event.

Hullbreaker Track
What the faction track must do: it must feel like ambush made audible. The Hullbreakers are silent, dark, pack hunters who strike with violence and take trophies. The track should sit low and rhythmic, driven by percussion and sub-bass rather than melody, with long quiet stretches broken by sudden loud stabs. It should not sound heroic or orchestral in a sweeping sense. It should feel like being hunted. Tempo around 100 to 120 BPM. No choir, no strings carrying the theme. Brass stabs, deep drums, metallic hits, silence between.
Candidate tracks to audition against the incumbent (which nobody in this process has heard, as the Ogg is absent from the snapshot):
OpenGameArt, "Tribal Battle Drums" by yd, CC0 1.0, https://opengameart.org/content/tribal-battle-drums (unverified exact page; search for percussion-only CC0 battle tracks).

Freesound user "zagi", "War Drums Loop", CC0 1.0, https://freesound.org/people/zagi/ (unverified).
These are candidates to audition, not verdicts. The incumbent fac_corsair is credited to Davieplier, CC0 1.0, and may be perfectly fit. The question is fit, not legality.
Mixing Note
Bus and gain: all new battle cues route to the existing category bus for combat SFX. cap_powerdown through cap_coreflash sit at gain 0.9 relative to bus, matching boom_l. die_run at 0.7, die_ember at 0.4 (it is background texture). hulk_drift at 0.35, deliberately quiet. arrive_ship at 0.75. board_cue at 0.85. brace_cue at 0.7. nemesis_adapt at 0.65 (it is informational, not explosive). Voice cap for the five capital death cues: max 1 each, stagger 0.15, defer 0.5. For die_run and die_ember: max 4 (four small ships can die near-simultaneously), stagger 0.1, defer 0.3.
Salvo defer fix: the current defect is that gun_sq (gap 0.05, defer 0.08) and gun_shell (gap 0.045, defer 0.14) drop starts when stagger exceeds defer in dense volleys. Fix: set defer to at least 2.5 times the stagger gap in every cue. gun_sq defer becomes 0.15. gun_shell defer becomes 0.12. Apply the rule globally in cues.json validation.
Volume-zero floor: sfx.gd:97 floors at about minus 98 dB. This is not true mute. For the reduce-flashing setting and any user who expects silence at zero, patch the gain function to return a linear 0.0 when the input is 0.0 before the dB conversion. Music already mutes its bus properly; match that behaviour.
Slow-motion beat (R73, big ships and flagships only): during the slow-motion beat, audio does not slow or pitch-shift. Instead, the bus gain dips 6 dB over 0.2 seconds, holds for the beat duration (roughly 0.8 seconds of real time), then restores. cap_coreflash fires at the end of the dip, so it lands as the speed returns. This keeps the audio intelligible and avoids the artefact of time-stretched samples.
Reduce-flashing audio analogue: yes. When reduce-flashing is on, die_ember is suppressed entirely (it accompanies a visual glow). cap_coreflash gain drops by 4 dB. boom_s and boom_l lose their transient spike (apply a 2 dB soft-knee limiter in the pipeline) so the counted flash is not paired with a loud crack. No cue is removed; loudness is restrained.
Voice caps for 20-ship battles: total combat bus voice cap stays at 16. Gun cues max 6. Hit cues max 5. Death cues (boom family plus the new cap and die cues) max 4. UI cues max 3. If the cap is hit, the engine drops the lowest-priority queued voice, not the currently playing one. The salvo group mechanism already handles weapon grouping; extend it to death cues so two capital deaths in the same 0.3-second window stagger rather than stack.
