# Leg: Rey (blind), issue 2060

Written before any external leg ran, from the brief pack alone (tables.json COMMANDS, CMD_POW, CMD_RIDERS, BOSS_CMD, ADMIRALS, FAC_RULES; PR 2038 balance table; issue 2033 called shots brief).

## My frame before the detail

Three facts drive everything below.

1. The charge mechanic is the real command system. A command held to round 3 is worth far more than one fired on round 1, and losing a ship charges it. Any faction set that changes WHEN a command wants to be fired changes the faction's whole battle rhythm. That is the cheapest distinctiveness available: same power budget, different clock.
2. The power budget is already balanced. PR 2038 landed every faction within about 3 points of the mean with the shared templates. So the sets below are built only from effect primitives the engine already has (free volley, damage percent for a round, shield refill, hull repair percent, section patch, extra wing, movement and turn gains, accuracy down on the enemy, aim and crit up, armor strip, point-defense ignore, rear-arc bonuses). Where a set wants something outside that list, I flag it as a new rule and keep it small.
3. Two factions have no shields and one cannot Brace. A faction set must never hand an admiral a command their own hulls cannot use. Today Eclipse's Khepra holds iron (shield refill) on shieldless hulls; that is the clearest proof the templates were assigned without the faction rules open.

Structure I propose: one signature command per faction, held by all four of its admirals, with the existing per-admiral riders doing the within-faction variation they already do. The signature replaces the template; the rider stays. This is the smallest change that makes a faction feel like itself (the command button is the thing a player learns per faction), it keeps the boss renames working (BOSS_CMD already renames per faction), and it keeps the balance surface small: six effects to tune instead of twenty-four. The four-admiral variety Ryan sees today is mostly rider variety already, and riders survive untouched.

## The six signature commands

### Sunward Crusade: "Burn and broadside" (from barrage)
Every ship that has moved at least 2 hexes this round fires a free volley with its flank (side) weapons at the best target in reach; ships that have not moved do not fire. Tier shape: tier 1 flank volleys only, tier 2 flank volleys at +10 percent damage, tier 3 flank volleys and lance weapons join at normal damage.
Why: Line of battle already pays side guns for moving 2 or more hexes. The shared barrage pays every ship including the ones sitting still, which teaches the opposite of the faction rule. This version makes the command and the rule the same lesson: keep the line moving and the command is a second broadside; park and it is nothing. Reuses: barrage's free volley, Vex's existing rider (+10 percent flank damage in the volley) becomes the tier 2 baseline and Vex's rider can step up to lances joining at tier 2 for him alone. New rule: none; the volley filter (moved 2+, flank weapons) is a targeting condition on an existing effect. Balance: strictly narrower than barrage (fewer ships fire), paid back by the damage tier; if anything it starts slightly weak, which is the safe direction for the faction that was lowest on Veteran before the balance pass (31.7 percent pre-pass, now 43.8).

### Meridian Directorate: "Designate and execute" (from barrage and hunt)
The player marks one enemy ship. Every synced ship (within 3 hexes of the command ship) fires a free volley at the marked ship if it is in reach; synced ships that cannot reach it gain +30 percent damage against it for the rest of the round instead. Tier shape: tier 1 volleys only, tier 2 volleys plus the damage window, tier 3 the marked ship also loses 10 aim for 2 rounds (Sekhmar's rider effect, borrowed across).
Why: Synchronized fire is already a focus-fire rule; the command should be the moment the whole net fires on one name. It also makes the command ship's position matter twice, which sharpens Meridian's one real vulnerability (lose the command ship, lose sync) into a decision the enemy can play against. Reuses: barrage volley, hunt's +30 percent window, an existing aim-down rider effect. New rule: the mark itself is a UI state, not a rules change; flagging it anyway because the target bar needs a "designated" state the enemy AI must also respect on Veteran. Balance: a focused volley is worth more than a spread one against a single target and less against a fleet; roughly neutral, and Meridian sat mid-table on both difficulties in PR 2038.

### Ashfall Hullbreakers: "Board and burn" (from surge and hunt)
Every ship gains +2 movement and +1 turn (the surge base). This round, boarding strength is doubled, and a ship that ends its move adjacent to an enemy may make one free rocket salvo at it (point defense thins it as normal). Tier shape: tier 1 movement only plus boarding strength, tier 2 adds the free salvo, tier 3 salvos ignore half of point defense (flag: partial PD ignore is a new tuning knob; Malek's rider already has full PD ignore for torpedoes this round, so the primitive exists for torpedoes and would extend to rockets).
Why: Ashfall's rule is Close in: boarding and rockets, no lances. Their admirals today hold surge, hunt, scramble and restore, none of which mentions boarding, their signature act. This command is the charge turn: burn in, board, and let the rockets speak on arrival. Reuses: surge numbers whole, hunt's reload logic is not needed, boarding strength is an existing stat with a multiplier. Balance: surge is already one of the stronger templates on a fast faction; the salvo is gated on ending adjacent, which is where Ashfall ships are most exposed, so the power is paid for in risk.

### The Chorus: "Feed the brood" (from restore)
Every living ship regrows hull now equal to two rounds of its normal regrowth, and for the rest of the round, kills feed double: the killer and nearby allies regrow twice the usual amount. Tier shape: tiers scale the instant regrowth (one round of regrowth at tier 1, two at tier 2, three at tier 3) and the feed window opens at tier 2.
Why: Chorus has no shields, so half the template list was never theirs; their economy is hull and feeding. Restore as written (repair 25 percent and patch sections) is a workshop repair; the Chorus version should be a meal. Note what this deliberately does NOT do: patch destroyed sections. A Chorus ship that lost a section regrows it only through the normal rules, which keeps restore-with-patch as the Meridian and Sunward style of repair and gives Chorus a different weakness profile. Reuses: the Feed regrowth numbers, restore's instant-repair slot in the UI and charge tiers. New rule: none. Balance: Chorus was the strongest faction on Veteran pre-pass (45.4) and sits at 45.0 now; an instant heal tied to their existing regrowth rate scales with what they already have instead of handing them a flat 25 percent, which on big bio hulls is worth more than on anyone else's. That scaling is the balance argument: percent-of-max repair is hull-size-biased, regrowth-rate repair is faction-fair.

### The Glasswrights: "Prism overcharge" (from iron)
All shields refill (the iron base). For the rest of the round, a ship whose shield is full deals +15 percent beam damage, and ships may still slip away after firing as their rule allows. Tier shape: tier 1 refill only, tier 2 refill plus the full-shield beam bonus, tier 3 refill plus bonus plus shields recharge at double rate next round even if hit (their prism charge rule already has the double rate; this extends its window).
Why: Glasswrights are the shield faction: strong shields, fragile hulls, no Brace. Iron's hull-damage reduction half is nearly wasted on them because a Glasswright whose shield is down is already dying; what they want from a defensive command is the shield back and a reason to keep it up. The full-shield damage clause turns the command from a panic button into a tempo play: fire it early, fight behind full prisms. Reuses: iron's refill, Vex-style damage percent, the prism charge double-rate. New rule: none. Balance: they cannot Brace, so a shield command is their only defensive lever; the damage clause is capped by the full-shield condition, which enemy fire removes one hit at a time.

### Eclipse: "Recall the archive" (from restore, and the faction rule itself)
The Archive restore fires early: one eligible fallen ship reassembles NOW at 35 percent hull instead of next round (flagship excluded, as the rule already says), and every ship gains fold-step: this round, movement ignores blocking by ships, rocks and wrecks entirely (their drives already do this at no extra cost; the command extends it to a free reposition: each ship may also make one free 2-hex fold move before firing). Tier shape: tier 1 the early restore only, tier 2 adds the free fold move, tier 3 a second eligible ship returns at 20 percent hull.
Why: Eclipse's admirals today hold restore, barrage, hunt and iron, and iron on shieldless hulls is the template system's worst misfit. Eclipse's identity is that death is a filing error. Their command should be the archive opening on the admiral's word instead of on the round boundary. This is the one set piece that touches a faction rule's timing, so flag it plainly: NEW RULE (timing): archive restore normally resolves at the next round's start; the command resolves one restore immediately. Everything else reuses existing numbers (35 percent hull, flagship exclusion, eligibility). Balance: Eclipse was the weakest faction on Veteran pre-pass (29.2 percent) and the strongest on Standard post-pass (74.6), so their command must be strong in shape and modest in size: one ship, at the hull the rule already grants, one round early. The tier 3 second ship is the part I am least sure of; see below.

## Deployment step

Options scored:
- A, none: player value zero agency at the one moment ranges, sync radius and terrain are all visible at once. AI handling trivial. Teaching load zero, but it also teaches nothing, and the game's tactics are positional (sync within 3, flank after moving 2, prism ships wanting distance, Hullbreakers wanting to start close). Build cost zero.
- B, place-in-zone: player places each ship in a zone on their side before round 1. Player value high: starting range bands decide who fires first, whether Meridian starts synced, whether Chorus starts in feed range of terrain. AI handling: the enemy fleet needs placement too; FACTION_AI already carries a per-faction range preference (Meridian +3, Glasswrights +2, Eclipse -3, Ashfall -2, Chorus -1), so enemy placement can be a template keyed to that preference plus jitter, and Veteran can mirror the player's spread. Teaching load moderate: one new pre-battle screen; an Auto-place button with a sane default keeps it optional, and the first battle can pre-place and simply let the player drag. Build cost: one placement phase, zone validation, enemy templates, and saved-battle fixtures need a placement record (the checks suite replays saved battles, so placement must serialize; that is the real cost, call it one build run plus fixture work).
- C, formation pick: pick one of three or four named formations, ships auto-arrange. Player value real but coarse; the pick is over in five seconds and the formations become a solved menu (one best per faction) within a few runs. AI handling easiest of the two active options. Teaching load lowest. Build cost lowest: formations are data, no free placement UI, fixtures record one formation id.

Pick: B, place-in-zone, with Auto-place as the default button and the player's last layout remembered per admiral. The reason is the same as the command argument: this game's rules are positional, and deployment is the only decision that touches all of them before any dice. Formation pick (C) is the fallback if the fixture cost grows: it captures maybe half the value at a third of the cost, and B can grow out of C's data later, while C cannot grow into B.

Strongest argument against my pick: deployment lengthens every battle start in a roguelite built on quick runs, and a bad deployment the player does not understand yet will feel worse than a bad default, because it was their own choice. The mitigations (auto-place default, remembered layout, zone kept shallow) are doing real work in this proposal; without all three I would pick C.

## Called shots interaction (issue 2033)

Three rulings the command sets need from the called-shot build:
1. Free volleys granted by commands (Sunward, Meridian) honor the firing ship's current called-shot priority if one is set, at no extra cost. A command is the fleet's big moment; making it ignore the player's section plan would set the two systems against each other. The called-shot trade-off (aim penalty or cooldown, per 2033's pick) is paid when the shot is set, not again per volley.
2. Meridian's Designate and execute sets a fleet-called section for the marked ship for that round: synced ships without their own called shot use the designated section; a ship with its own called shot keeps its own. One override, stated once, no stacking of the +15 crit (the crit bonus applies once per shot however many systems point at the section).
3. Chorus acid already strips armor on hull hits; a called shot on a section whose armor is stripped should be the intended combo, so no command of theirs needs a called-shot clause, and none should get one: their command power is in regrowth, not targeting. Ashfall's free rocket salvos do NOT take called shots (rockets are area weapons thinned by point defense; routing them to a section would need a new rule and would blur the line between rockets and torpedoes, which do take them via Malek's rider today).

## Least-sure decision

Eclipse tier 3 (a second ship returns at 20 percent). It is the only place I let a command exceed the current templates' value in kind rather than in tuning, and Eclipse's Standard win rate (74.6 percent, top of the table) says their margin for gifts is thin. If the chair needs a cut, cut there first; tiers 1 and 2 carry the identity on their own.

## Biggest risk

Signature-per-faction collapses variety if riders are too weak to matter: today a player choosing between two admirals of one faction is choosing a template AND a rider; under my structure they choose only a rider and a doctrine. If riders read as fine print, admiral choice within a faction gets thinner even as faction choice gets richer. The mitigation is that several riders are already command-sized (battery shots cannot miss; torpedoes ignore point defense), but the chair should say plainly that this trade is the price of the structure, and the alternative (four distinct commands per faction, 24 effects to balance) is the price of avoiding it.
