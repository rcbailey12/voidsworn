Audio Seat Report: Voidsworn Red Team Audit
Seat: Music and SFX surface
Date: October 31, 2026
Evidence base: sfx.js, sfx_data.js, godot/music/ listing, godot/audio/ listing, shared core pack. No prior audio audit exists in the filed evidence. This seat builds its findings from code and asset inventory alone.

Findings (ranked by severity)

1. The shipped build has no music. None.
OBSERVED. The browser build, described in the pack as "the playable truth today," contains sfx.js (364 lines) and sfx_data.js. sfx.js implements a synthesis engine, a bus graph, voice management, and two stings: "sting(win){ this.duck(.8,3); return this.play(win?'sting_win':'sting_loss');}". No music playback system, no track sequencer, no adaptive layer, no loop manager, and no reference to any music file appears anywhere in the browser code inventory. The 27 recorded clip keys in sfx_data.js include "victory" and "ambient" but no music track. Meanwhile the Godot tree holds 27 OGG music tracks totalling over 30 MB. The player of the shipped game experiences a fleet-tactics roguelite with generational progression, Nemesis encounters, and six faction identities in silence broken only by SFX and two short stings. The cost is the entire emotional register of the game: tension during the Nemesis hunt, relief at regroup, faction identity, the weight of a capital-ship kill. A roguelite with an 80 percent loss rate needs music to make losing feel tragic rather than merely repetitive.

2. The music credits question is open and unauditable from this pack.
OBSERVED. The core pack names one open item for this seat: "the music credits question." The file godot/music/CREDITS.md exists at 18,739 bytes, and sfx_data.js opens with "Kenney (CC0) and Sonniss GDC 2026 bundle (royalty free, no attribution required)." However, the contents of CREDITS.md are not reproduced in this pack. The 27 music tracks in godot/music/tracks/ have no visible attribution in any file shown. Whether those tracks are original, licensed, or sourced from a bundle requiring credit cannot be determined from the evidence provided. If the Godot build ships with uncredited or improperly credited music, the exposure is legal and reputational. This is the single named open item for the seat and it remains unresolved.

3. SFX repetition will fatigue across long sessions and multi-battle runs.
INFERRED. The round-robin system is implemented: "_pick(arr,key){ if(arr.length<2) return arr[0]; let ix=Math.floor(Math.random()*arr.length)". The Godot clip listing shows the variant counts: hullHit has 6 takes, laserLarge has 5, boomSmall has 4, boomBig has 3. The browser build's sfx_data.js holds 27 clip keys total. A typical 20-ship battle fires weapons every activation phase. The gun_shell cue alone is the "house sound" per its comment. Across a campaign run of maybe 8 to 12 battles plus events and shops, the player hears the same small pool hundreds of times. The pitch jitter ("const u=Math.random()*2-1" applied as rate variance via "L.pj") helps but does not mask identical timbral content. The cost is perceptual: the sound design stops registering as feedback and becomes wallpaper, undermining the tactical clarity that distinct weapon and impact sounds should provide.

4. Voice budget saturates in large fleet engagements.
OBSERVED. The code states: "maxVoices(){ return G.mobile?20:34; }". The standing ruling says "20 ships is a typical-battle target, not a cap." In a 20-ship battle with phased activation, multiple weapon types fire per ship, impacts land, shields break, and UI cues play. The stealing logic exists: "this._steal(this.active[vi],now)" with a fade. But stealing means a player loses audio feedback for an event they may need to track tactically. The salvo system tries to consolidate: "gain*=Math.max(.42,1/Math.sqrt(1+.3*(s.n-1)))" and limits to "max:6" per cue. Even so, 34 voices across weapons, impacts, explosions, UI, and ambient buses is tight when a broadside from four ships overlaps with incoming fire and a shield break. The cost is dropped or faded cues at exactly the moments the player needs audio information most.

5. No adaptive or reactive music system exists in either build's playable state.
INFERRED. The Godot tree contains music_player.gd (11,550 bytes) and music.json (19,339 bytes), suggesting a designed system. But the Godot migration is described as underway, with the sequence "rules parity checks, one playable battle, then the full run, effects pass, cutover." The browser build has no music system at all. The track names suggest intent: "hot_heat," "hot_pursuit," "hot_qazi," "hot_theme_a" imply escalation layers; "calm_darktimes" and "calm_tactical" imply state-based selection. None of this is playable today. The Nemesis system, described as a hunter that "adapts between meetings," has no musical counterpart in any shipped or playable form. The cost is that the game's most distinctive structural feature, the generational campaign with a persistent antagonist, has no musical narrative arc to support it.

6. Spatial audio is limited to stereo pan and distance attenuation.
OBSERVED. The play function resolves position via "const w=this._where(o.pos)" and applies "if(w) gain*=w.att" for attenuation and "p.pan.value=panv" for stereo placement. No HRTF, no elevation, no occlusion. For a hex-grid top-down tactics game this is defensible, but the pack describes a "3D fleet-tactics" game rendered in Babylon.js with a 3D scene (scene3d.js, 686 lines). Ships exist in 3D space visually while their sounds are placed on a 2D stereo field. The cost is a mismatch between what the player sees and what they hear, reducing the sense of spatial awareness that a fleet battle should provide.

7. The ambient bus is thin and its implementation is not shown.
OBSERVED. The bus definition sets ambient at level .5 with zero reverb send: "['ambient',.5,0]". The init function calls "this.ambient()" but the ambient method body is not included in the pack excerpt. The duck function manipulates the ambient bus with a fixed base: "const t=Math.max(c.currentTime,at||0), base=.5". The sfx_data.js clip list includes "ambient" as a single key. One ambient clip at half gain with no reverb, ducked during stings, is the entire atmospheric bed for a game set in a sector campaign with distinct factions and environments. The cost is a flat, unchanging sonic backdrop that fails to differentiate the calm of a map screen from the dread of a Nemesis approach.

8. The browser build loads all recorded audio as base64 in a single JS file.
OBSERVED. sfx_data.js is described as containing "base64-encoded Ogg Opus clip data (482,216 bytes total)." The file comment states: "recorded clips are base64 in sfx_data.js." This means roughly 482 KB of encoded audio is parsed as JavaScript text before decoding. On the target platform (browser, frozen to break fixes), this adds to initial load time and memory. The Godot build uses discrete OGG files in godot/audio/clips/, which is the correct approach. The browser build's method is a legacy constraint of the "one self-contained file" design noted in sfx.js: "The page stays one self-contained file." The cost is load performance and the inability to stream or lazy-load audio, though at 482 KB this is a minor concern compared to the missing music.

9. Faction identity has no sonic signature in the shipped build.
INFERRED. The game has six factions: Sunward Crusade, Hegemony Navy, Ashfall Hullbreakers, the Chorus, Glasswrights, Eclipse. The Godot music directory contains faction tracks: fac_chorus, fac_corsair, fac_deathless, fac_hegemony, fac_ithar, fac_voidsworn. The browser SFX code has minor faction branches in legacy wrappers: "lance(ion,fac,pos){ return this.play(fac==='deathless'?'lance_dark':fac==='ithar'?'lance_choir':ion?'lance_ion':'lance',{pos}); }" and a torpedo variant per faction. These are three-cue variations within one sound palette. No faction has a distinct weapon timbre set, impact character, or UI voice in the shipped build. The cost is that the six factions, which the design contract says should feel distinct across "stats, price, and CP," sound identical in play. Music, which could carry faction identity, is absent.

10. The Godot audio build pipeline is present but unverified against gameplay.
OBSERVED. The godot/audio/ directory contains build scripts: "render_ambient_bed.py" and "render_synth.js," plus a clips directory and index.json. The godot/music/ directory contains a test: "test/music_check.gd" (4,223 bytes). These indicate a pipeline and a test harness. However, the Godot migration status is "rules parity checks, one playable battle, then the full run." No evidence in the pack confirms that the music player, the clip index, or the ambient bed have been exercised in a playable Godot session. The cost is that the entire audio future of the game rests on an untested integration. If music_player.gd and the SFX clip system conflict on bus routing, voice limits, or timing, the problem will surface late in the migration.

Verdict
The shipped game is sonically incomplete: it has a competent, well-engineered SFX system and no music at all, which is like fielding a fleet with engines but no crew. The single most important attack is Finding 1. A 3D fleet-tactics roguelite with a Nemesis, six factions, and an 80 percent loss rate ships to players today with zero musical content. Every emotional beat the design depends on, the grief of a lost generation, the dread of the Nemesis clock, the relief of a regroup, plays out in silence. The Godot tree holds the answer in 27 tracks and a player script, but that answer is not playable, and the credits question attached to it remains open.
