## 1. Risks of a 3 to 4 times bigger enemy fleet

Numbers do not scale linearly, they scale worse. In ranged combat, Lanchester's square law says a force 3 times bigger is roughly 9 times stronger if it can bring all guns to bear. Ernest Adams explains this well in "The Designer's Notebook: Kicking Butt by the Numbers" (Game Developer, 2004): https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-designer-s-notebook-kicking-butt-by-the-numbers-lanchester-s-laws The same math is discussed for StarCraft in "Lanchester's Law: why small advantages swell in StarCraft" (Win-Vector, John Mount, 2010): https://win-vector.com/2010/09/17/lanchesters-law-why-small-advantages-swell-in-starcraft/ Your enemy fleet must not be able to bring everything to bear, or the player fleet gets deleted. This is the single biggest risk in your plan.

Action economy and tempo. If each ship acts once per round, the enemy gets 3 to 4 actions for every 1 of yours. "Out With the Action Economy" (Colin Spiridonov, Drolleries) puts it bluntly: if you are outnumbered in an action economy system you are at a severe disadvantage. https://drolleries.substack.com/p/out-with-the-action-economy The player spends the whole battle reacting, and each player mistake is punished by a full enemy volley.

Alpha strike and focus fire. With 20 enemy ships, whoever acts first can often kill a player ship before it moves. BattleTech culture treats focus fire as core doctrine, and guides are built around taking one target off the field fast. See "Use heavy mechs effectively in Alpha Strike" (mechstrategen.de, 2025): https://mechstrategen.de/en/use-heavy-mechs-effectively-in-alpha-strike/ Losing 1 of 6 player ships is a 17 percent power drop in one event. Losing 1 of 20 enemy ships is noise. The attrition curve is tilted against the player by design, so you need counterweights.

Turn length. This is a known failure point at fleet scale. Battlefleet Gothic: Armada players reported that big fleets become "untenable" to manage even with slow motion (Steam discussion, "Why NOT Turn-Based", 2017): https://steamcommunity.com/app/363680/discussions/0/1318836262645894268/ Rock Paper Shotgun's review of Battlefleet Gothic: Armada (2016) called translating the tabletop to digital "a Herculean feat": https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/battlefleet-gothic-armada-review The tabletop BattleTech community had the same problem and solved it with a whole second game. Alpha Strike was made so you can play bigger battles faster, by giving each unit one action and collapsing many weapons into a single damage number. See the Alpha Strike Quick-Start Rulebook (Catalyst Game Labs): https://battletech.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/01/Alpha-Strike-Box-Set-Quick-Start-Rulebook-3rd-Print-12-29a.pdf and the interview "Talking Alpha Strike with Joshua Franklin" (Sarna.net): https://www.sarna.net/news/alpha-strike-interview-associate-developer-joshua-franklin/ Lesson: when unit count grows, cut the per unit workload. Batch the filler ships, or the enemy phase will eat your game.

Readability. 20 enemies means 20 threat cones, health bars, and intentions on a 3D board. Into the Breach (Subset Games, 2018) is the gold standard fix. It shows every enemy plan one turn ahead and gives perfect information, so being outnumbered reads as a puzzle, not chaos. See "Into The Breach And Dynamic Puzzles" (blogofarcanesecrets, 2018), quoting co-creator Matthew Davis calling it "a puzzle game wrapped up in a strategy game": https://blogofarcanesecrets.wordpress.com/2018/03/09/into-the-breach-and-dynamic-puzzles/ and "Into the Breach's UX makes you feel smart" (prototypr.io, 2018): https://blog.prototypr.io/into-the-breachs-ux-makes-you-feel-smart-a9cb03210757 It also lets the outnumbered player win by pushing enemies into each other, so enemy numbers become the player's weapon. Fire Emblem does something similar with visible "aggro lines" that show each enemy's intended target: https://fireemblem.fandom.com/wiki/Aggro_Line

What comparables do about being outnumbered, in short:
- XCOM: Enemy Unknown and XCOM 2 never activate the whole map. Enemies sit in pods that wake up only when approached, so the real fight is always small. See "Alien Pod Mechanics" (UFOpaedia / Long War): https://www.ufopaedia.org/Alien_Pod_Mechanics_(LWR) and "Overwatching: An Analysis of X-Com Enemy Unknown" (Game Developer): https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/overwatching-an-analysis-of-x-com-enemy-unknown
- Fire Emblem community design notes: player phase focused games use fewer, tougher enemies, while enemy phase focused games use lots of fragile cannon fodder that dies fast. See "The Juggernaut Index" (FEUniverse forum, 2021): https://feuniverse.us/t/the-juggernaut-index-a-brand-new-astounding-metric/14402 For your 20 ship battles, most of the 20 should be fragile filler that the player can sweep, or turns drag and deaths compound.
- Battlefleet Gothic games organize ships into squadrons rather than solo units: https://rotorob.com/2016/05/06/video-game-review-battlefleet-gothic-armada/ Squadron level activation is your best tool for 14 to 20 enemy ships.
- Hardspace: Shipbreaker is not a tactics game. It is a real time salvage sim. What you can borrow is the feel of stripping a big hull into clean, sorted, valuable parts, and the debt tab that keeps the economy from becoming trivial. See the Gaming Nexus review (2022): https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/7679/Hardspace-Shipbreaker/ and the Barrel Drill review (2022): https://www.barreldrill.com/hardspace-shipbreaker-review/ It is a reference for your "stripped for parts" option, not for battle tempo.

Bottom line for Q1: the 3 to 4x gap is fine only if the enemy cannot concentrate fire freely, filler ships are cheap to run and fast to kill, activation is batched, and threats are telegraphed. Your old 8 ship cap was probably protecting turn time and readability. At 20 ships you must buy those protections back with design, not just accept them.

## 2. How the ramp should scale

Scale both, but with different jobs. Count creates the "big fleet" spectacle and board pressure. Quality creates the difficulty spikes. Neither alone works. Pure count makes long turns and trivial filler. Pure quality makes HP sponges and slow attrition duels.

A sane shape for your 10, 14, 20 guide:
- Hold the number of "real threats" low. At 20 enemy ships, maybe 3 to 5 are elites plus the flagship. The rest are fragile escorts that die in one or two volleys. This matches the cannon fodder pattern in enemy phase Fire Emblem games noted above (FEUniverse, "The Juggernaut Index").
- Keep total enemy actions per round roughly bounded as count grows. Batch escorts into squadron activations that act once, like Alpha Strike's one action per unit (Catalyst Game Labs, Alpha Strike Quick-Start Rulebook).
- Spend the ramp on quality of the few, not the many. Elite ships get new abilities and readable silhouettes, not just more HP.
- Stagger activation so the enemy does not act as one 20 ship bloc. XCOM's pod model shows why: the player should fight 4 to 8 active enemies at a time even when 20 exist (UFOpaedia, "Alien Pod Mechanics").

Difficulty settings. Prefer rule changes over stat inflation. Hades (Supergiant Games, 2020) does this with the Pact of Punishment: players pick named conditions that add Heat, and rewards scale with Heat, instead of a flat enemy HP slider. See the Hades Wiki Pact of Punishment page: https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment and the RPGSite guide: https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/10287-hades-pact-of-punishment-heat-modifiers-and-how-to-maximize-your-rewards For your game, consider separate levers: enemy count (intensity), elite share and accuracy (lethality), and prize or credit values (economy). A hard mode could swap escorts for elites, give flagships extra abilities, or thin the capture economy, rather than adding five more filler hulls. Starsector also shows it is fine to let players cap battle size in settings directly (Starsector Wiki, "Battle types"): https://starsector.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_types

## 3. Capture economy pitfalls and guardrails

Snowballing. Wins grant ships, ships win more wins. "The Snowball Effect (And How to Avoid It) in Game Design" (Tuts+ Game Development, 2014) covers this positive feedback loop: https://code.tutsplus.com/the-snowball-effect-and-how-to-avoid-it-in-game-design--cms-21892a Your 4 to 7 fleet cap is already your main guardrail, and auto scrap when full is good. Add cost pressure so a full elite fleet is not free: Starsector (Fractal Softworks) makes big fleets pay in supplies and lose combat readiness, so fleet size has a real upkeep cost. See the Starsector Wiki pages on Supplies and Economy: https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Supplies and https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Economy Sins of a Solar Empire uses a fleet supply cap for the same job: https://wiki.sinsofasolarempire.com/index.php/Fleet_Supply Even a soft version, like repairs costing more for bigger ships, keeps a full fleet from being pure upside.

Credit floods. Ransom is a faucet with no obvious drain. Starsector's own forums logged an "Infinite Money Exploit" thread (2019): https://fractalsoftworks.com/forum/index.php?topic=15843.0 In Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag, plundering ships was simply the fastest way to make money (Steam guide, "Easy Money Making", 2013): https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=196648773 Guardrails that work: strong sinks (repairs, refits, module installs, crew), ransom prices that fall when you flood one sector market, rising shop prices per sector, and one time offers that cannot be farmed. Also price the three options so stripping competes with ransoming for the same hull, which makes each choice real.

One option always wins. In Master of Orion 2, capturing enemy ships and scrapping them for tech was a known dominant strategy for certain races (Steam discussion, "Most dominant setups"): https://steamcommunity.com/app/410980/discussions/0/3129415856248694370/ If add, strip, or ransom is best 80 percent of the time, players will notice and the choice becomes busywork. Keep the three values close and situational: strip value high when you need a module now, ransom high when cash is short, add high when the hull beats your weakest ship.

Decision fatigue after every battle. You fight more often as the run goes on, and each prize asks a three way question. Fixes are UI level: one batch screen for all prizes, side by side values, per class default policies ("destroyers auto strip"), remember last choice, and a single undo. Auto scrap when full is fine, but show what was lost and why, so it reads as the fleet cap biting, not the game eating rewards. Note the flagship rule is a good one: it protects set piece battles and blocks late run power spikes.

Where captured ships can go instead. Black Flag's Kenway's Fleet sends captured ships to passive income and trade missions rather than into battle (Ubisoft News, "Deep dive into the naval gameplay"): https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/2ld4JIABg5aOvoiUlJlUWF/assassins-creed-black-flag-resynced-deep-dive-into-the-naval-gameplay If your ransoms feel weak, a "send prize home" option that pays out later is a fourth lane that also acts as a credit sink valve.

## 4. Five things to measure in the simulation

1. Rounds to first player ship lost, per sector. If player ships routinely die in round 1 or 2 against 14 or 20 enemies, the enemy concentrates fire too hard or acts too early. Pair this with a first mover win rate: what percent of battles are won by whoever acts first. A high number flags an alpha strike problem.
2. Effective action ratio. Count enemy ship actions versus player ship actions per round, after batching and activation rules. The nominal ratio is 3 to 4x. Your mechanics should pull the effective ratio down toward 2x or less. Also log how many enemy ships actually get in range and fire each round. If all 20 always fire, Lanchester math will crush the player (Ernest Adams, "Kicking Butt by the Numbers").
3. Battle length and enemy phase workload. Log rounds to battle end and simulated steps per enemy phase at 10, 14, and 20 ships. Rounds and steps should not blow up at sector 3. This is your proxy for real turn time, the thing Battlefleet Gothic: Armada players complained about (Steam discussion, 2017) and the thing Alpha Strike was built to fix (Sarna.net interview).
4. Wealth curves across a run. Log credits earned, credits spent, total prize value, and player fleet value after each battle. Snowballing shows up as fleet value growing faster than enemy value each sector. Credit floods show up as cash exceeding everything the shops sell by sector 2. The Starsector exploit threads are the cautionary tale (Fractal Softworks forum, 2019).
5. Prize choice distribution and auto scrap rate. Log percent of prizes added versus stripped versus ransomed, and how often auto scrap fires. If one option beats 80 percent, reprice the others. If auto scrap fires on most prizes, either prizes arrive too fast or the 4 to 7 cap is too tight for late sectors. This mirrors the Master of Orion 2 lesson about dominant capture strategies (Steam discussion, 2021).

One last note. Your ramp replaces an 8 ship cap, which means the cap was doing hidden work on tempo and clarity. Keep measuring item 3 the whole time, because "10, 14, 20" is a feel target before it is a balance target.
