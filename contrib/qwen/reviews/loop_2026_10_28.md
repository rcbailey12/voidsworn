OVERALL VERDICT
THE LOOP LEAKS. The loop is strongest at run 1 (novelty, discovery, first permanent-loss shock) and weakest at run 4, where the meta has not yet delivered a meaningfully different puzzle and the 83 %-loss grind has begun to feel like a tax rather than a teacher.

FINDINGS (ranked most severe first)

1. BLOCKER - The unlock ladder is bypassed at boot, gutting the cross-run progression spine.
OBSERVED. The brief states: "at boot, the game currently grants all LEGACY items and all admirals unless a saved option (unlockAll) is explicitly false." The entire renown to LEGACY to new-hull pipeline that should make run 5 feel richer than run 1 is handed to the player on first launch. Renown still accumulates, but its primary sink (12 LEGACY items, admiral unlocks at 0/30/45/80) is pre-spent. The loop as designed has a progression arc; the loop as shipped does not. This review judges the shipped state here and the designed state where noted.

2. BLOCKER - No pre-win difficulty dial; the 17 % win rate is a wall, not a ramp.
OBSERVED. "There is no difficulty selection before the first win." Heat, the only explicit difficulty lever, is gated behind a win. A player on run 3 who has lost twice at a sector boss faces the same veteran AI with no way to trade challenge for survivability. FTL's sector routing and Slay the Spire's elite-or-skip choice give the player a difficulty dial inside the run; Voidsworn offers none until after the first clear. INFERRED: for a player who likes the battles but loses three in a row, the absence of any adjustable pressure is the single most likely quit trigger before run 5.

3. BLOCKER - Failure pays too thinly to sustain an 83 %-loss game.
OBSERVED. A lost run yields "renown (more with Heat), admiral mastery XP for the admiral who led, core sync progress, and stats." With LEGACY and admirals already granted (Finding 1), renown has no immediate sink. Mastery XP accrues toward five levels for one admiral; core sync accrues toward four tiers for one core of six. There is no narrative beat, no story unlock, no "you discovered X" moment on death. Compared to Hades, where "failure is the progression engine, not a tax on it," Voidsworn's failure payout is a slow drip into buckets the player cannot yet see filling. INFERRED: the emotional read after a sector-2 fleet wipe is "I lost four captains and got 40 renown," not "I unlocked something that changes next time."

4. FIX - Run 5 has no distinct new question for the player.
INFERRED. By run 5, assuming the player has not yet won (statistically likely at 17 %), they have: seen most node archetypes, encountered the Nemesis, tried 2-3 admirals, and accumulated modest mastery/sync progress. The brief lists no run-5-specific unlock, event, or modifier. Heat is still locked. The 33-event pool is finite and likely half-seen. The game is asking the same question it asked on run 1 ("can you win?") without a new verb. Slay the Spire's Ascension and FTL's ship quests give run 5+ a different question; Voidsworn does not.

5. FIX - The Nemesis is a strong retention hook but is invisible between encounters.
OBSERVED. The Nemesis "adapts to your build, gathers strength on a clock advanced by loud choices and wins, and can be permanently killed only in sector 3." This is the game's best "one more run" thread: a rival that remembers you. However, the brief does not describe any between-battle Nemesis feedback - a taunt, a visible power meter, a scouting report. INFERRED: if the player only meets the Nemesis in combat and loses the run before sector 3, the Nemesis feels like a harder boss rather than a persistent antagonist. The hook is load-bearing in design but under-surfaced in presentation.

6. FIX - Core sync and protocol drafting are the deepest strategic layer but are gated by run repetition.
OBSERVED. "Four of the six cores are in range each run; routing toward a core's signals builds that core's protocols into the fleet." Core sync has four tiers per core and "eventually grants a starting protocol." With six cores and four tiers, full sync requires substantial run volume. INFERRED: this is the correct long-tail system, but at 17 % win rate and ~45-minute runs (assumption - brief does not state run length), reaching tier 4 on all six cores is a 25+ hour investment before the system fully reveals itself. The pacing may outrun patience.

7. FIX - Post-win content is Heat only; no second campaign, no challenge modes, no daily seeds.
OBSERVED. "There are no daily seeds, no challenge modes, and no post-win content beyond Heat and completion across factions and admirals." Heat is a self-imposed modifier worth +10 % renown each. This is the Slay the Spire Ascension model but without Ascension's 20 named steps or its mechanical variety. INFERRED: after the first win, the game's answer to "what now?" is "do it harder with the same tools." For a subset of players this is enough; for the majority it is not a second campaign.

8. NOTE - Permanent captain death is the game's emotional signature but risks feeling punitive without a memorial payoff.
OBSERVED. "Every hull you lose stays lost, and so does its captain." The Hall of the Fallen "remembers fallen captains across runs." This is BattleTech's pilot-loss sting. INFERRED: the Hall is a memorial, not a reward. If a beloved captain dies in sector 2 on run 7, the player gets a name on a list. BattleTech at least returns salvage from the wreck; here the loss is pure subtraction. The emotional weight is real but the design does not convert grief into gameplay.

9. NOTE - The 3-take-1 protocol choice with reroll/decline is well-designed micro-tension.
OBSERVED. "A protocol offered as 3-take-1 with a paid reroll or a decline-for-credits option." This mirrors Slay the Spire's card reward structure and is the correct shape for build-shaping decisions. The reroll cost and decline-for-credits create a real economy of choice. This works.

10. NOTE - The aspect system (3-of-N pick, transfers on flagship death) is elegant but its depth is unclear from the brief.
OBSERVED. "Pick one of 3 (sometimes 4) aspects for the flagship; the aspect shapes the flagship's fighting for the whole run and transfers to the next-largest ship if the flagship falls." The transfer rule is a good safety valve. INFERRED: with mastery unlocking a fourth aspect offer, the system deepens, but the brief does not state how many aspects exist per faction or whether they interact with protocols. The strategic ceiling is not assessable from this pack.

EXPLICIT CHECKS

a. THE HOOK
The "one more run" hook as built is the Nemesis plus the protocol/build puzzle: a persistent rival that adapts to your choices, combined with a routing and drafting problem (which cores to chase, which protocols to stack, which captures to keep) that reshuffles every run. This hook is load-bearing but currently fractured. The Nemesis gives a face to the next run; the protocol draft gives a mechanical question. However, the hook depends on the player wanting to draft a different build, and with all admirals and LEGACY hulls unlocked at boot (Finding 1), the "new toy" incentive that normally fuels roguelite replays is spent before run 1 begins. The battles themselves are described as "the product" and are presumably excellent, but battle quality alone is a session hook, not a retention hook. The Nemesis and protocol drafting carry the retention load; if a player does not engage with routing (goes autopilot through nodes), the hook collapses to "try the same fight again." Verdict: load-bearing in design, partially decorative as shipped due to the unlock bypass.

b. POWER SCALING
Inside a run: scaling is legible and plan-able. Command points grow by sector, credits buy hulls and refits, protocols stack rule-changing bonuses, captured prize ships can be added or stripped, and captains gain ranks and perks. A player can look at the node map, see reward labels and core signals in advance, and route deliberately. The CP cap creates a hard ceiling that makes every hull choice a trade-off. This is comparable to FTL's scrap economy: visible, finite, forcing decisions. This layer works.
Across runs: scaling is designed but largely neutralised as shipped. Renown is meant to buy LEGACY upgrades (12 items: hull classes, war chests, CP, price cuts) and unlock admirals on a 0/30/45/80 cost ladder. With the boot-state granting all LEGACY and all admirals, renown accumulates without a sink. Admiral mastery (5 levels) unlocks an alternate fleet, a fourth aspect, a second Command choice, and a veteran flag captain - these are genuine power unlocks but they require repeated runs with the same admiral, which conflicts with the variety-seeking behaviour the game otherwise encourages. Core sync (4 tiers per core) improves protocol offers and grants a starting protocol - this is the best cross-run scaling lever because it changes the draft rather than the stats. Heat adds +10 % renown per rule but only after a win. Compared to Hades' Mirror (every run buys something tangible) or Slay the Spire's Ascension (every clear raises the stakes), Voidsworn's cross-run scaling is thinner in felt impact, especially in the shipped state. A player can plan around mastery and sync, but the plan is slow and the rewards are incremental rather than transformative.

c. RUN 1 / RUN 5 / RUN 10
Run 1. The player picks a faction, an admiral, and an aspect, and encounters the hex-battle core for the first time. The game asks: "Can you survive sector 1 and understand the node map?" The player earns their first experience of permanent captain death, meets the Nemesis, and sees a protocol draft. On loss (83 % likely), they receive renown, mastery XP, core sync progress, and a Hall of the Fallen entry. The newness carries this run. The milestone lands because everything is first contact.
Run 5. Statistically the player has lost 4 of 5 runs. Mastery on one admiral may have reached level 1 or 2 (brief does not specify XP rates - NOT COVERED). Core sync may have ticked up on one or two cores. If the player has not won, Heat is still locked. The 33-event pool is partially explored. The game is asking the same question as run 1: "Can you win?" No new mechanic, no new faction pressure, no modifier has entered. This milestone fails to land. There is no "run 5" moment analogous to FTL's second ship unlock or Hades' first story beat after a few deaths. The player who liked the battles is now grinding toward a mastery unlock that will change one admiral's starting kit. INFERRED: this is the leak point.
Run 10. If the player has persisted, they have likely won once (cumulative probability at 17 % per run is ~84 % by run 10). Heat is now available, offering self-imposed difficulty for +10 % renown. Mastery may be level 3-4 on a favoured admiral, granting the alternate fleet or fourth aspect. Core sync may be tier 2 on a couple of cores. The game's question shifts to "Can you win under constraints?" and "Can you complete across factions?" This is a real shift, but it arrives late and is gated behind a win the player may not have achieved. The milestone lands only for the player who survived runs 5-9. For the player who won on run 6 or 7, run 10 is the first Heat run, which is genuinely new. For the player still losing, run 10 is run 1 with more stats in a menu.

d. THE LEAK
Run 3 or 4, mid-sector 2, at the second or third consecutive fleet-wipe. The player liked the battles (they made it past run 1's novelty). They have lost twice. The meta payout from those losses - renown with no sink (LEGACY pre-unlocked), a few mastery XP points, a core sync tick - does not feel like progress. They start run 3 or 4 with essentially the same tools. In sector 2, the node modifiers and archetype mix are familiar. They lose a captain they have promoted. The Nemesis shows up and punishes the build they are still learning. The fleet is ground to two ships. They lose. The Hall of the Fallen gets another name. The game offers no new question, no new tool, no story beat. The player closes the application. INFERRED: the specific moment is the post-loss screen after a sector-2 boss or transition fight, where the reward summary (renown, XP, sync) is visible and visibly insufficient. The absence of any difficulty adjustment (Finding 2) means the player cannot even choose an easier path; they can only retry the same wall.

e. FAILURE'S PAYOFF
A failed Voidsworn run pays: renown (modest, with no immediate sink as shipped), admiral mastery XP (slow, single-admiral), core sync progress (slow, single-core), and a stat line (runs, deepest sector, wins). There is no narrative advancement, no story beat, no "you discovered a new enemy type" log, no currency that buys a tangible next-run advantage in the short term. The Hall of the Fallen memorialises dead captains but does not convert their loss into gameplay.
Compared to the reference loops: Hades gives Mirror currency, story, and relationship progression on every death - failure is literally the engine. Slay the Spire gives nothing on loss but makes runs short and Ascension the long-tail goal. FTL gives nothing on loss but the run structure and ship quests provide variety. BattleTech converts mech losses into salvage and pilot injuries into roster management.
Voidsworn's failure payoff is not enough to make losing feel like progress in the shipped state. It would be closer to sufficient if the LEGACY/admiral unlock ladder were intact (renown would buy something visible), and if mastery/sync progress were surfaced more prominently ("2 more runs to unlock the alternate fleet"). As built, failure pays in slow-drip currencies with no visible bucket. The emotional contract is "your captains died and here is a number." That is a tax, not a payoff.

THE ONE CHANGE
Reinstate the unlock ladder as the default boot state and make renown the primary failure currency that buys visible, run-altering unlocks.
Specifically: remove the blanket LEGACY/admiral grant at boot. Let the first three admirals be free; gate the remaining 21 and all 12 LEGACY items behind the renown cost ladder as designed. Add a post-loss summary line that shows exactly what the player can now afford ("30 renown to unlock Admiral Voss - 12 more needed"). This single change restores the cross-run progression spine, converts failure into tangible progress, and gives runs 2-5 a new question ("what am I saving for?"). It also makes Heat's +10 % renown bonus meaningful because renown now has a sink.
Cost: moderate. Requires gating logic (the unlockAll flag already exists, so the infrastructure is present), rebalancing renown drop rates to pace the 0/30/45/80 ladder across roughly runs 1-12, and UI work on the post-run summary to surface purchasable unlocks. No new systems needed. The risk is that players who have already played with everything unlocked will perceive a downgrade; this is mitigated if the change ships before or alongside a public release.

PROTECT (do not change under retention pressure)
1. Permanent death of hulls and captains within a run. "Every hull you lose stays lost, and so does its captain." This is the game's identity and its emotional stakes. Softening it (respawns, captain insurance, hull rebuilds) would collapse the routing tension and make the node map decorative. The Hall of the Fallen is the correct memorial. Do not dilute this.
2. The 3-take-1 protocol draft with paid reroll and decline-for-credits. This is the core build-shaping decision and the correct micro-tension inside the loop. Adding a fourth option, making rerolls free, or auto-selecting would flatten the choice architecture that makes each node matter.
3. The Nemesis as a persistent, adapting antagonist killable only in sector 3. This is the game's strongest narrative-retention thread and the thing that makes the run feel like a story rather than a gauntlet. Do not make the Nemesis skippable, do not let it be killed before sector 3, and do not let it scale down to match a struggling player. The Nemesis should be feared.

QUESTIONS FOR THE DESIGNER
1. The unlock-all boot state: is this a debug convenience that will be removed before release, or an intentional design choice to let players self-select difficulty? If intentional, the entire renown to LEGACY progression spine needs to be redesigned around a different sink, because as shipped it is decorative. This is the single most consequential design decision in the current build.
2. What is the intended emotional arc of losing a long-promoted captain in sector 2 on run 7? The Hall of the Fallen memorialises them, but the gameplay consequence is pure subtraction. Should the death of a high-rank captain trigger any mechanical echo (a legacy perk, a revenge flag against the Nemesis, a one-time refit discount), or is the subtraction itself the intended design and the grief is the point?
3. Is the 17 % win rate a tuning target or an accident of the current AI and economy? If it is a target, the game needs a much richer failure-payoff layer to sustain it. If it is an accident, the pre-win difficulty dial (Finding 2) may be a tuning problem rather than a design gap. The answer changes whether the fix is systemic or numerical.
