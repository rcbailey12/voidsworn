## 2. FINDINGS (ranked most severe first)

### F-1 · [BLOCKER] Ram-enhancement triplet collapses into one build path

READ. Three boons stack the same job: r_prow gives "Rams deal +35% damage", halved recoil, and a hull-cap increase. d_jugg gives "Rams deal +50% damage and the rammer takes no recoil." L_rook gives "Rams deal 50% more, can take 70% of a ship's hull, and the rammer takes no recoil." d_jugg strictly dominates r_prow on both the damage number (+50 vs +35) and recoil (none vs halved). L_rook then strictly dominates d_jugg by adding the 70 % cap on top of identical +50 / no-recoil. A Rook player who draws all three is taking the same boon three times with increasing numbers. No decision changes between them; the only "choice" is magnitude. INFERRED: a player who holds r_prow and is offered d_jugg will always take d_jugg, making r_prow feel wasted. The duo slot and the legendary slot should each add a new ram mechanic (splash, chain-ram, momentum transfer) rather than re-rolling the same percentage.

### F-2 · [FIX] Kill-explosion duplicated between e_chain and L_ember

READ. e_chain: "Enemy ships you destroy explode, dealing 20 damage to every enemy within 2 hexes." L_ember: "Every enemy ship you destroy explodes for 40 damage to every enemy within 2 hexes." The mechanic is identical (on-kill, 2-hex radius); only the number changes (20 to 40). e_chain's epic tier adds "the blast sets them burning", which is the one differentiator, but at base magnitude the legendary is a straight upgrade of the normal boon. INFERRED: an Ember player who holds e_chain and then receives L_ember gains no new decision, only a bigger number. The legendary should add a qualitatively new effect (e.g., explosions chain recursively, or destroyed ships leave a fire field) rather than doubling the same radius damage.

### F-3 · [FIX] Three generic stat boons dilute patron identity

READ. s_ev: "+6 evasion across the fleet." a_aim: "+5 aim for every weapon." i_plate: "+8% armor across the fleet." None of these reference the patron's stated theme. Vyre's theme is "Shields, ion fire and lightning"; +6 evasion is none of those. Astra's theme is "Lances, precision and critical hits"; +5 aim is adjacent to precision but reads as a generic to-hit buff. Kharon's theme is "Broadsides, armor and holding the line"; +8 % armor technically fits but is the weakest expression of "holding the line" in the iron slice. INFERRED: these three boons are the most likely off-patron filler candidates because they are universal and weak, which feeds the Q7 defect: they become the off-patron picks a player skips, reinforcing the hidden-penalty feel the ruling warns against.

### F-4 · [FIX] s_ion is the weakest storm boon and rarely the correct pick

READ. s_ion: "Your weapons strip 22% more shield per hit." Compare within the storm slice: s_cap gives +20 % shields, s_arc gives a flat 9 damage on shield knockout, s_regen gives +18 % regen per turn, s_chain (rule-changing) spreads shield knockout to adjacent facings, and L_storm refills all shields every turn. s_ion is a flat percentage on a stat (shield strip) that is already served more dramatically by s_arc (damage on knockout) and d_ionlance ("Lances strip double shield per hit"). INFERRED: in any three-choice offer where s_ion appears alongside s_arc or s_cap, a competent player takes the other boon. s_ion is the storm boon most likely to sit unpicked.

### F-5 · [FIX] a_crit buries a rule-changing rider inside a number boon

READ. a_crit: "+6% critical chance, criticals deal x1.75, and every critical hit wrecks a ship system." The first two clauses are pure numbers. The third - "every critical hit wrecks a ship system" - is a decision-changing mechanic that alters target priority, system-stripping strategy, and the value of aim/crit stacking. It is presented as a rider on a stat boon rather than flagged as the rule-changing boon for Astra. Astra's actual rule-changing slot is a_mark. INFERRED: a player reading a_crit may undervalue the system-wreck clause, or may take a_crit over a_mark without realising the system-wreck is the more consequential effect. The system-wreck should either be its own boon or a_mark should be repositioned.

### F-6 · [NOTE] h_chitin is the weakest hunger boon

READ. h_chitin: "Squadrons have +30% hull." Compare: h_hatch adds a whole extra wing, h_wings gives +25 % squadron damage, h_feast gives sustain on kill ("its carrier gains a spare wing and repairs 5% hull"), h_board adds a sabotage rider, and h_brood (rule-changing) launches a free wing on capital-ship kill. h_chitin is a flat survivability number on units that are already fragile and numerous. INFERRED: a competent Hunger player takes h_hatch, h_wings, or h_feast before h_chitin in virtually every offer configuration.

### F-7 · [NOTE] e_kindle is passive and does not change a decision

READ. e_kindle: "Hull hits have a 12% chance to start a fire." The player does nothing differently; fires proc on existing hits. Compare e_chain (changes kill priority to cluster targets), e_hunt (changes target priority to wounded ships), e_reload (changes torpedo timing), and e_spread (changes aiming calculus). e_kindle is the only ember boon that asks nothing of the player. INFERRED: at 12 % per hit the expected fire count is low enough that the boon rarely swings a battle, making it a weak pick in any three-choice offer.

### F-8 · [NOTE] BOON_NEED gating is invisible to the player

READ. The code defines BOON_NEED with 20 entries (e.g., h_brood:'carrier', a_mark:'lance', e_spread:'torp'). The comment states "offers skip them when it doesn't" apply. The commissioner's question notes that "reward labels are hidden on event nodes only; elsewhere the player sees full information." However, the pack does not show whether the player is told why a boon is absent from an offer. INFERRED: if the player never sees h_brood because they lack a carrier, they may not realise the boon exists or what fleet composition would unlock it. This is a discoverability gap, not a balance defect, but it weakens the "builds" side of the commissioner's question.

### SPILLOVER VALUE

The strongest case for an off-patron boon being the right pick:

A Kharon (iron) player running a slow, broadside-heavy fleet is offered:
- Slot 1: i_plate (+8 % armor) - patron
- Slot 2: r_burn (+1 speed, +6 evasion at m >= 1.5) - off-patron (Rook)
- Slot 3: h_board (+25 % boarding) - off-patron (Hunger)

The fleet's problem is getting into broadside range. r_burn's +1 speed directly solves this. i_plate's +8 % armor does not. INFERRED: r_burn is the correct pick here because speed is a universal stat that changes positioning decisions, while +8 % armor is a marginal number. This is a real, if narrow, case where the off-patron boon wins.

A second case: an Astra (star) lance build is offered:
- Slot 1: a_aim (+5 aim) - patron
- Slot 2: s_ion (+22 % shield strip) - off-patron (Storm)
- Slot 3: e_kindle (12 % fire) - off-patron (Ember)

s_ion helps lances punch through shields faster, which is directly useful for a lance build. a_aim's +5 is a smaller contribution to hit probability than s_ion's +22 % is to shield removal. INFERRED: s_ion is the correct pick if the player's bottleneck is shield penetration rather than accuracy.

Can the case always be made? No. INFERRED structural reason: the patron's own slice always contains the high-impact options (rule-changing boons, legendaries, duo synergies) that off-patron slots cannot match. The off-patron fillers that leak through are the universal-stat boons (s_ev, a_aim, i_plate) which are the weakest boons in any patron's slice. So the spillover value is real but capped: an off-patron pick can beat a weak patron option (i_plate, a_aim) but can never beat a strong one (r_slip, i_wall, a_mark). The Q7 ruling's defect - hidden rarity penalty on off-patron fillers - would further compress this already-narrow window. The current numbers preserve spillover value in the sense the ruling demands, but the margin is thin.

### NUMBERS VERSUS DECISIONS

Method: A boon "changes a decision" if it alters what the player does in fleet composition, map navigation, or battle tactics (movement, targeting, activation order, formation, brace timing, torpedo aiming, kill priority). A boon "adds a percentage" if it makes an existing action numerically better without changing which action the player chooses.

Pure percentage / stat boons (14):
i_broad, i_plate, s_cap, s_regen, s_ev, s_ion, h_wings, h_chitin, a_lance, a_aim, a_crit (the +6 %/x1.75 core; the system-wreck rider is noted in F-5), e_war, e_kindle, r_shock.

Decision-changing boons (34):
i_still, i_brace, i_line, i_wall, s_arc, s_chain, h_hatch, h_board, h_feast, h_brood, a_long, a_rear, a_mark, r_burn, r_mom, r_jink, r_prow, r_slip, e_reload, e_chain, e_hunt, e_spread, L_iron, L_storm, L_hunger, L_star, L_rook, L_ember, d_jugg, d_bastion, d_ionlance, d_burnswarm, d_hunter, d_inferno.

Ratio: 14 numbers (29 %) vs 34 decisions (71 %).

INFERRED judgement on whether the ratio is right: For a between-battles layer in a roguelite, 71 % decision-changing is healthy. The player should feel that each boon pick alters their approach, not just their spreadsheet. However, the 29 % that are pure numbers are concentrated in the normal (non-rule-changing, non-legendary) tier. This means early-run offers - before the player has two boons from a patron and legendaries start appearing - are more likely to be pure percentages. INFERRED: the early-game offer experience feels more like "buff numbers" than the late-game experience, which could make the first few boon picks feel flat. The rule-changing boons (one per patron) and the BOON_NEED gating help, but the normal-tier pool leans numeric.

A boon's value may shift dramatically if a specific module doubles the relevant stat. My dead-pick and strict-take claims assume no module/relic synergy.

## 5. QUESTIONS FOR THE DESIGNER

1. The ram trio (r_prow to d_jugg to L_rook) is a single stat line, not three boons. The stakes: if Rook's identity is "rams, speed and momentum," the ram path should branch (splash, chain-ram, momentum transfer) rather than stack the same +%/recoil number three times; otherwise Rook players experience one build with three checkpoints instead of a build with choices.
2. Should a_crit's system-wreck clause be split into its own boon or moved to a_mark's slot? The stakes: a_crit currently bundles a rule-changing effect (system destruction on crit) with a number boost (+6 %/x1.75), which muddies the one-rule-changing-per-patron structure and makes a_mark feel redundant for players who already took a_crit.
3. What is the intended offer frequency for the three generic-stat boons (s_ev, a_aim, i_plate) in off-patron slots? The stakes: these are the boons most likely to appear as off-patron fillers, and they are the weakest boons in their own patron's slice; if they are the primary spillover offering, the Q7 ruling's requirement that "off-patron picks must retain real value" is met only at the lowest bar, and the player will learn to skip off-patron slots entirely.
