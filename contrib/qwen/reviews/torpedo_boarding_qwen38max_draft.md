# VOIDSWORN TORPEDO AND BOARDING RULES PROPOSAL

Model: Qwen3.8-Max, Thinking ON, Web search OFF. Filed verbatim from the Qwen chat report; em dashes normalized to hyphens, wording otherwise unchanged.

## PART ONE: TORPEDOES ON A HEX GRID

### 1.1 Launch: Action Cost, Firing Arc, and Range Limits

Action cost. Launching a torpedo volley is a ship's full weapon activation for that turn. A ship that fires torpedoes cannot fire its guns that same activation. INFERRED: this preserves per-activation decision weight by making the torpedo launch a genuine trade-off against a broadside or lance strike, rather than a free bonus layered on top of normal fire. The brief's feel target is "per-activation decision weight" (OBSERVED: "per-activation decision weight as the feel target"), and the Rogue Trader benchmark supplies "one to three player capital ships per battle, so every activation carries weight" (OBSERVED: "one to three player capital ships per battle, so every activation carries weight"). A torpedo launch must therefore be a choice that costs something visible.

Firing arc. Torpedoes may only be launched from the ship's forward arc. On the hex grid this means the three hexes directly ahead of the ship's facing (the hex the bow points into, and the two hexes flanking it at +/-60 degrees). INFERRED: this is drawn from the Rogue Trader benchmark's "facing and firing arc discipline" (OBSERVED: "facing and firing arc discipline") and from the standing art rule that "guns never face backward" (OBSERVED: "guns never face backward"). A torpedo is a committed, forward-projected weapon. Firing one locks the ship into a facing decision for at least one turn, because turning to re-acquire a launch solution on a different bearing costs the next activation.

Range limit (launch range). Torpedoes may be launched at any target hex within a maximum range of 10 hexes from the launching ship. INFERRED: at the stated battle scale of "around 20 ships" (OBSERVED: "Target battle size stays near the current scale (around 20 ships)"), a 10-hex launch envelope is large enough to threaten the enemy formation from outside immediate gun range but small enough that the torpedo's multi-turn flight remains tactically legible on the board. Beyond 10 hexes the launch is illegal; the firing solution cannot be computed. This is not a range penalty or a to-hit modifier. It is a hard gate: you either have a solution or you do not.

Ammunition. Each ship carries a finite torpedo magazine. INFERRED: a magazine of 2 to 4 volleys per ship, depending on hull class, keeps torpedoes as a scarce commitment rather than a spammable ability. When the magazine is empty, the launch action is gone for the rest of the battle. No resupply during combat. This scarcity reinforces the decision weight of each launch.

### 1.2 Travel: Speed and Path on the Hex Grid

Travel speed. Torpedoes move 3 hexes per turn along their plotted course. INFERRED: at a launch range of up to 10 hexes, a 3-hex-per-turn speed means a torpedo fired at maximum range arrives in roughly 3 to 4 turns. This is slow enough that the target has multiple activations to react, reposition, or screen, which is the entire point of the herding mechanic. It is fast enough that the torpedo remains a credible threat and does not become irrelevant background noise. At shorter ranges (4-6 hexes), arrival in 1-2 turns creates a sharp, immediate pressure that forces the target to act now.

Course plotting. At launch, the player selects a target hex (not a target ship). The torpedo plots a straight-line course to that hex. On a hex grid, this is a line of adjacent hexes from the launch point to the target hex, resolved by shortest-path with the player choosing among equal-length routes when the geometry allows it. INFERRED: locking the course to a hex rather than a ship at launch time is what makes the arrival-turn target selection meaningful (see 1.4). The torpedo is a dumb projectile in flight. It goes where it was aimed. It does not track.

Course is immutable after launch. Once a torpedo is in flight, its heading cannot be changed. INFERRED: this is critical for the herding mechanic. The enemy can see the torpedo's projected path and must plan around it. If the torpedo could be redirected every turn, it would become a slow homing missile and the herding tension would collapse into simple kiting. The fixed path is what makes the torpedo a spatial hazard, a line on the board that constrains movement.

What the player sees at battle zoom during travel. INFERRED: the torpedo is rendered as a small, bright engine-flare icon moving hex by hex along a visible dotted or dashed course line. The projected arrival hex is highlighted with a pulsing warning reticle. The course line and arrival hex are visible to both the firing player and the opponent (no fog-of-war concealment on torpedoes in flight). The icon is small but high-contrast against the board, readable at the fleet-scale zoom where all 20 ships are visible. No particle trail at this zoom; the flare and the line are sufficient. At closer camera zoom the flare gains a short exhaust streak, but the information content does not change.

### 1.3 Interception: Can Torpedoes Be Shot Down En Route?

Yes, but only through screening, not point-defence fire. INFERRED: giving every ship a passive point-defence roll against every torpedo that passes within range would add a bookkeeping check per hex of flight per torpedo, which violates the constraint that "per-activation decision weight must survive" and that the system must not "turn a turn into bookkeeping." Instead, interception is a positional, deliberate act.

Screening rule. A friendly ship may attempt to intercept an enemy torpedo if the torpedo's course passes through a hex adjacent to the screening ship (within 1 hex of the ship's hull). The screening player must declare the intercept during their own activation, spending that ship's weapon activation for the turn (the same cost as firing guns or launching torpedoes). INFERRED: this makes interception a real decision. You spend your activation to swat a torpedo, which means you are not shooting at something else. The cost is symmetrical with the launch cost.

Intercept resolution. The intercept is a flat, deterministic check modified by the screening ship's size class. INFERRED: a larger ship (cruiser, battleship) has more point-defence coverage and intercepts on a lower threshold; a smaller ship (escort, frigate) intercepts on a higher threshold. The check is resolved immediately when declared. If successful, the torpedo is destroyed; its icon is removed from the board with a brief detonation flash. If failed, the torpedo continues on its course. There is no partial damage, no degraded torpedo. It is destroyed or it is not.

No stacking. Only one intercept attempt may be made against a given torpedo per turn, regardless of how many friendly ships are adjacent to its path. INFERRED: this prevents the torpedo from being smothered by a screen of escorts, which would negate the weapon entirely and remove the herding pressure. The torpedo is a commitment; the defence against it is also a commitment. One ship, one activation, one attempt.

What the player sees at battle zoom during interception. INFERRED: when an intercept is declared, the camera does not zoom or lock. The screening ship's icon pulses once, a short tracer line is drawn from the ship to the torpedo's current hex, and the torpedo icon either flashes and vanishes (hit) or continues moving (miss). A small floating text result ("INTERCEPTED" or "MISSED") appears at the torpedo's hex for two seconds. This is readable at fleet zoom without requiring a close-up.

### 1.4 Arrival Turn: Target Selection, Evasion, Damage, and Area Effect

This is the core Rogue Trader verb. The brief states the reference: "torpedoes that travel across turns with the target chosen on the arrival turn" (OBSERVED: "torpedoes that travel across turns with the target chosen on the arrival turn").

Target selection on arrival. When the torpedo reaches its plotted arrival hex, the firing player selects the target at that moment. The target must be an enemy ship currently occupying the arrival hex or any hex adjacent to it (the 6 surrounding hexes). If no enemy ship is in the arrival hex or its ring of 6 neighbours, the torpedo detonates harmlessly in empty space. INFERRED: the 1-hex adjacency window gives the torpedo a small but meaningful kill zone, rewarding the firing player for predicting where the enemy will be rather than where the enemy was. It also means the enemy cannot trivially dodge by moving exactly 1 hex away; they must move 2 or more hexes from the arrival point, which is a larger commitment on the grid.

The herding mechanism, stated plainly. Because the torpedo's course and arrival hex are visible from the turn of launch, the defending player knows 2-4 turns in advance where a detonation will occur. They must move ships out of the arrival hex and its 6 neighbours, or accept being targeted. INFERRED: this is the spatial pressure that "herd[s] enemy movement" (OBSERVED: "torpedoes with travel time that herd movement"). The torpedo does not chase. It denies space. The enemy formation bends around the projected arrival point, and that bending disrupts firing arcs, screening positions, and formation cohesion. Multiple torpedoes in flight from different bearings create corridors the enemy cannot safely occupy, channeling their movement into kill zones the firing player has prepared with gunnery. This is how torpedoes shape the battle without ever needing to hit.

Evasion. The targeted ship may attempt to evade. Evasion is a single, flat check modified by the ship's current speed (how many hexes it moved this turn) and its size class. INFERRED: a ship that moved 0 hexes this turn (held position) evades poorly; a ship that moved its full movement evades better. This rewards the defending player for keeping ships mobile and punishes stationary anchoring. The check is resolved once. There is no re-roll. If the ship evades, the torpedo detonates in the arrival hex with only area effect (see below). If the ship fails to evade, it takes a direct hit.

Damage on direct hit. INFERRED: torpedo damage is high, a single large-impact hit rather than a sustained damage-over-time effect. The damage value is fixed per torpedo type (e.g., a standard torpedo deals X hull damage; a heavier variant deals more but has a shorter magazine). The hit also triggers a critical hit check against a named ship system. The Rogue Trader benchmark includes "critical hits on named ship systems that change what a ship can do" (OBSERVED: "critical hits on named ship systems that change what a ship can do"). A torpedo crit should therefore target a specific system (engines, weapons, shields, crew quarters) and degrade or disable it. The crit result is drawn from a small table: minor degradation (system operates at reduced capacity this turn), major damage (system is offline for 1-2 turns), or catastrophic (system is destroyed for the rest of the battle). One roll, one result, applied immediately. No bookkeeping.

Area effect (near-miss detonation). If the target evades or if no ship is in the arrival hex, the torpedo still detonates. INFERRED: the detonation applies a reduced splash damage to any ship in the arrival hex itself (not the adjacent ring). This prevents the torpedo from being a pure all-or-nothing gamble and gives the firing player a small consolation for a well-placed shot even when the target slips away. The splash damage is roughly one-third of a direct hit. No critical hit check on a splash. This keeps the area effect simple and fast to resolve.

What the player sees at battle zoom on the arrival turn. INFERRED: the torpedo icon reaches the arrival hex. If a target is selected, a targeting bracket snaps onto the target ship for half a second. The torpedo detonates: a bright, short-lived flash at the hex, a shockwave ring expanding one hex outward (purely visual), and damage numbers floating up from the target ship. If the torpedo crits a system, the affected system icon on the target's status bar flashes red. If the torpedo misses or splashes, the flash occurs but the shockwave ring is smaller and no targeting bracket appears. All of this is readable at fleet zoom. No camera lock, no slow motion. The activation continues.

### 1.5 Multi-Turn Torpedoes and Hex-Tactic Integrity

The concern. Multi-turn projectiles can break hex tactics if they create hidden information, if they force the defending player into pure reactive movement, or if they require per-turn bookkeeping that slows the game.

The solution, as designed above. Torpedoes are fully visible from launch to arrival. Their course, speed, and arrival hex are known to both players. The defending player's response is movement, not bookkeeping. They look at the board, see the line, and decide whether to move a ship out of the arrival zone or to screen it with an intercept. INFERRED: this keeps the decision weight on the defending player's activation (move or screen?) and on the firing player's activation (where to aim? which arc? do I sacrifice my gun activation for this launch?). No hidden state. No per-turn upkeep. No dice to track across turns. The torpedo is a line on the board. It moves 3 hexes. It arrives. Someone gets hit or doesn't. The turn moves on.

Interaction with 20-ship battles. INFERRED: at the stated scale of "around 20 ships" (OBSERVED: "Target battle size stays near the current scale (around 20 ships)"), the board is large enough that 2-3 torpedoes in flight simultaneously create meaningful spatial pressure without cluttering the grid. More than 4-5 simultaneous torpedoes would risk turning the board into a minefield and shifting the game from fleet tactics to torpedo avoidance. The magazine limit (1.1) naturally caps this.

No homing, no course correction, no splitting. INFERRED: these are explicitly excluded to preserve the herding mechanic and to keep the torpedo's behaviour legible at a glance. A torpedo is a straight line, a fixed speed, and a detonation. Anything more complex would require the player to track state changes across turns, which is the bookkeeping the brief forbids.

## PART TWO: BOARDING

### 2.1 Initiation: Range, Delivery Method, and Action Cost

Range. Boarding may be initiated only when the boarding ship is in a hex adjacent to the target ship (1 hex apart, sharing a hex edge). INFERRED: this is the tightest possible range on the hex grid short of occupying the same hex, and it reflects the physical reality of sending crew across the gap between two hulls in vacuum. It also means the boarding ship must survive at least one turn of enemy fire to close the distance, making the decision to board a genuine commitment. The Rogue Trader benchmark treats "boarding as a real verb" (OBSERVED: "boarding as a real verb"), and a real verb requires a real cost to initiate.

Delivery method. Boarding parties are delivered by shuttle or boarding torpedo. INFERRED: the visual language should show a small craft or projectile crossing the 1-hex gap between the two ships. This is not a teleport. The delivery takes a portion of the activation and can, in principle, be intercepted (see Counterplay, 2.4). The delivery method is chosen at the time of initiation and does not change the resolution mechanics; it is a visual and thematic choice that may interact with specific ship upgrades or faction abilities in the future.

Action cost. Boarding is a full activation. The boarding ship spends its entire turn action to launch a boarding party. It cannot fire guns, launch torpedoes, or perform any other offensive action that turn. INFERRED: this mirrors the torpedo launch cost (1.1) and preserves the activation economy. At the stated battle scale, each activation matters. Spending one to board is a significant commitment, especially because the boarding ship must be adjacent and therefore exposed.

Crew commitment. The boarding player selects how many crew sections to commit to the boarding action: one section (a raiding party, low risk, low reward) or two sections (an assault party, high risk, high reward). INFERRED: this gives the boarding player a meaningful decision within the activation without adding bookkeeping. The choice is made once, at initiation, and affects the resolution check (2.2). It is not a per-turn resource drain; the crew sections are committed and resolved in the same activation.

What the player sees at battle zoom during initiation. INFERRED: the boarding player selects the adjacent enemy ship and chooses the board action from the activation menu. A short animation plays at fleet zoom: a small shuttle icon or torpedo-shaped projectile detaches from the boarding ship's hull and crosses the 1-hex gap to the target. A "BOARDING ACTION" label appears briefly above the target ship. No camera lock. The action is readable as a small, bright object crossing a short distance between two ship icons.

### 2.2 Resolution: Opposed Check

Resolution is a single opposed check. INFERRED: the boarding player's crew sections are pitted against the defending ship's crew complement in one roll. This is not a multi-turn sub-game. It is not a mini-battle played out on a separate board. It is one check, resolved immediately, with a clear outcome. This is essential to the constraint that the system must not "turn a turn into bookkeeping" (OBSERVED: "no system that turns a turn into bookkeeping").

The check. The boarding player's value is based on the number of crew sections committed (1 or 2) plus any boarding-specific modifiers (faction traits, ship upgrades such as breaching charges or combat stimulants). The defending player's value is based on the ship's crew complement, modified by the ship's internal defence rating (a stat representing security bulkheads, automated defences, and crew training). INFERRED: both values are visible to the respective players before the check is resolved, so the boarding player can assess the odds before committing. The check is resolved by comparing the two values with a single random modifier (a d6 or equivalent). If the boarding player's modified value meets or exceeds the defender's, the boarding action succeeds. If not, it fails.

No officer posts. The brief states: "Voidsworn has no officer posts; do not import them" (OBSERVED: "Voidsworn has no officer posts; do not import them"). INFERRED: the opposed check therefore does not reference any officer, commander, or specialist character. The crew sections are abstract. The ship's defence rating is a hull stat, not a character stat. The verb is borrowed; the post structure is not.

Degrees of success. INFERRED: the check produces one of three outcomes, not a binary pass/fail.

Failure (attacker's value below defender's): the boarding party is repelled. The crew sections committed are lost (returned to the ship's crew pool as wounded, unavailable for the rest of the battle). No damage to the defender. The boarding ship's activation is spent.

Success (attacker's value meets or exceeds defender's): the boarding party breaches the hull and achieves a boarding objective (see 2.3). The crew sections committed are returned to the boarding ship at the end of the turn (they fight, accomplish the objective, and extract). The defender suffers consequences.

Critical success (attacker's value exceeds defender's by a margin of 3 or more): the boarding party achieves a boarding objective and also inflicts a secondary effect (see 2.3). This is rare and should feel earned.

### 2.3 Consequences: Fires, Engine Crippling, System Damage, Crew Loss, and Capture

The brief specifies that boarding should carry "consequences such as fires and crippled engines" (OBSERVED: "consequences such as fires and crippled engines").

On success, the boarding player selects one consequence from the following list. INFERRED: allowing the player to choose the consequence keeps the decision weight on the boarding player and avoids a random table that might produce an irrelevant result. The choice is made immediately after the check resolves.

Fire. The target ship suffers a fire. The fire persists for 2 turns. At the start of each of those turns, the ship takes a small amount of hull damage (roughly one-quarter of a standard gun hit) and one random ship system is degraded (operates at reduced capacity) until the fire is extinguished. INFERRED: fire is a slow, grinding consequence that punishes the defender over the next two activations without requiring them to track multiple statuses. One fire token on the ship's status bar, resolved at the top of the turn.

Engine cripple. The target ship's movement is reduced by half for 2 turns. INFERRED: this is the most tactically significant consequence on the hex grid. A crippled ship cannot reposition effectively, cannot flee, and cannot close to its own firing arcs. It is a spatial consequence, not just a damage number. The engine system icon on the ship's status bar flashes red and shows the reduced movement value.

System sabotage. One named ship system chosen by the boarding player (weapons, shields, sensors, or a special system) is knocked offline for 2 turns. INFERRED: this mirrors the Rogue Trader benchmark's "critical hits on named ship systems that change what a ship can do" (OBSERVED: "critical hits on named ship systems that change what a ship can do"). The system is not destroyed; it is disabled temporarily. The defender can bring it back online after 2 turns. This is less severe than a torpedo crit that destroys a system permanently, reflecting the fact that boarding is a precision tool, not a brute-force weapon.

Crew damage. The target ship loses one crew section permanently (for the rest of the battle). INFERRED: this reduces the defender's ability to resist future boarding actions and may degrade their gunnery or system operation if crew sections are tied to those functions. It is a long-term weakening rather than an immediate tactical disruption.

On critical success, the boarding player selects one consequence from the list above AND inflicts a second consequence from the list. INFERRED: the critical success is powerful but not a one-turn kill. Two consequences applied simultaneously (e.g., fire plus engine cripple) put the target ship in serious trouble but do not remove it from the battle. This preserves the "fewer, heavier activations" feel (OBSERVED: "fewer, heavier activations beat many light ones") without making boarding an instant-win button.

Capture is not a standard outcome. INFERRED: capturing an enemy ship outright (removing it from the board and adding it to the player's fleet) is too swingy for a 20-ship battle and would break the activation economy. However, capture may be offered as a future, high-cost variant: a boarding action that commits three crew sections and succeeds with a critical margin could force a surrender check on a ship already below a hull threshold. This is flagged as an open question (4.1) and is not part of the base proposal.

What the player sees at battle zoom on resolution. INFERRED: the boarding shuttle icon reaches the target ship. A brief flash at the target's hull (a breach spark, not a large explosion). The result text appears: "BOARDED - FIRE" or "BOARDED - ENGINES CRIPPLED" or "REPELLED." The relevant system icon on the target's status bar changes colour (red for fire, amber for engine cripple, grey for offline system). The shuttle icon returns to the boarding ship. All of this takes roughly two seconds of animation at fleet zoom. No camera lock, no boarding cutscene, no interior view. The information is conveyed through the status bar and the floating text.

### 2.4 Counterplay for the Defender

Pre-emptive counterplay: do not be adjacent. INFERRED: the strongest counterplay against boarding is positional. The defending player can avoid adjacency by maintaining range, using the hex grid to keep ships at 2+ hexes from enemy boarders. This is the same spatial logic that applies to torpedo evasion (1.4). The boarding threat is a movement-shaping tool even before it is executed.

Active counterplay: repel boarders. If the defending player has a ship adjacent to the boarding ship (not the target, but a third ship in the vicinity), that ship may spend its activation to provide a defensive bonus to the target ship's opposed check. INFERRED: this represents the adjacent ship firing on the boarding shuttle in transit, disrupting the assault. The bonus is a flat +1 to the defender's check value. Only one adjacent ship may provide this bonus per boarding action. This gives the defending player a way to use their formation to protect against boarding, but it costs an activation, preserving the decision-weight trade.

Active counterplay: intercept the delivery. INFERRED: if the boarding party is delivered by shuttle (as opposed to boarding torpedo), a defending ship with an unused intercept action (1.3) may attempt to shoot down the shuttle in transit. This uses the same intercept mechanics as torpedo interception: one attempt, one activation, flat check. If the shuttle is destroyed, the boarding action fails and the committed crew sections are lost. This counterplay is only available against shuttle delivery, not boarding torpedo delivery, giving the boarding player a reason to choose one method over the other.

Reactive counterplay: damage control. INFERRED: if the boarding action succeeds and inflicts a fire or system sabotage, the defending ship may spend its next activation on damage control instead of firing or moving. Damage control extinguishes a fire early (removing the remaining duration) or brings a sabotaged system back online one turn early. This is a recovery action, not a combat action. It costs the ship's turn. The defender must choose between fighting back and repairing.

No counterplay that requires officer posts. INFERRED: consistent with the brief's instruction, no counterplay mechanic references an officer, commander, or specialist. The repel bonus is a ship-to-ship fire action. Damage control is a ship-level action. The defence rating is a hull stat. The verb is borrowed; the post structure is not.

### 2.5 Turn Cost

Boarding resolves within a single activation. INFERRED: the boarding player spends their activation to initiate and resolve the boarding action. The opposed check is rolled, the consequence is applied, and the activation ends. There is no multi-turn boarding sub-phase. There is no "boarding continues next turn" state. The action is atomic: it happens or it doesn't, in one turn.

The consequences persist across subsequent turns. INFERRED: fire lasts 2 turns. Engine cripple lasts 2 turns. System sabotage lasts 2 turns. Crew damage is permanent for the battle. These are status effects on the target ship, resolved at the start of the affected ship's turn or at the end of the round. They do not require additional actions from either player to maintain. They tick down automatically. This is the only bookkeeping, and it is one token per consequence on the ship's status bar, visible at fleet zoom.

## PART THREE: OPEN QUESTIONS

These are questions I could not resolve from the brief and the granted context documents.

Capture as a boarding outcome. Should boarding ever allow the capture of an enemy ship (removing it from the board, adding it to the player's fleet mid-battle)? The brief does not address this. The Rogue Trader benchmark treats boarding as "a real verb" (OBSERVED: "boarding as a real verb") but the granted context does not specify whether that verb includes capture. I have excluded capture from the base proposal and flagged it as a possible high-cost variant. INFERRED: capture is too swingy for the base rules but could be a campaign-layer reward (the ship is captured after the battle, not during it).

Torpedo types and faction asymmetry. The brief does not specify whether different factions field different torpedo types (heavier warheads, faster engines, guided variants, cluster munitions). The five owner decisions on the asymmetry decision page are listed as undecided (OBSERVED: "The five owner decisions on the asymmetry decision page"). INFERRED: torpedo and boarding rules should be faction-neutral in the base proposal, with faction-specific modifiers added once the asymmetry decisions are made.

Interaction with shields. The brief does not specify how shields interact with torpedo hits or boarding. The colour law states "Cyan means shields" (OBSERVED: "Cyan means shields and nothing else is cyan"), confirming shields exist as a mechanic, but no rules for shield interaction with torpedoes or boarding are provided. INFERRED: torpedoes should be partially or fully blocked by active shields, giving shield-bearing ships a natural defence and making shield degradation a prerequisite for torpedo strikes. Boarding should bypass shields entirely (you cannot shield against a shuttle docking with your hull). Both are assumptions requiring confirmation.

Hex grid orientation and facing. The brief references a "3D hex-grid" (OBSERVED: "3D hex-grid fleet tactics roguelite in Babylon.js") but does not specify whether the hex grid is flat (2D plane) or volumetric (3D layers). INFERRED: the proposals above assume a flat hex grid with 6 neighbours per hex and 6 facing directions. If the grid is volumetric, firing arcs, movement, and adjacency would need to be redefined. This is a structural question that affects every rule above.

Crew section granularity. The boarding rules reference "crew sections" as the unit of commitment and loss. The brief does not define what a crew section is, how many a ship has, or whether they are differentiated. INFERRED: crew sections are abstract, undifferentiated units (like hit points for personnel). A ship has 2-4 crew sections depending on size class. They are not individual characters. This is an assumption requiring confirmation.

Boarding and capital ship scale. The Rogue Trader benchmark features "one to three player capital ships per battle" (OBSERVED: "one to three player capital ships per battle, so every activation carries weight"). INFERRED: if the player's fleet includes only 1-3 large ships and the rest are smaller escorts, boarding a capital ship should be harder (higher defence rating) than boarding an escort. The opposed check's defender value should scale with ship class. This is assumed in the proposal but the specific scaling is not defined here.

Torpedo flight and the animation time problem. The context notes "the animation time problem the sims keep finding in Voidsworn's late fights" (OBSERVED: "the animation time problem the sims keep finding in Voidsworn's late fights"). INFERRED: torpedo flight animations must be fast and skippable. A 3-hex-per-turn torpedo should animate its movement in under one second at fleet zoom. If the player is watching 5 torpedoes move 3 hexes each, that is 15 hex-steps of animation per turn. At one second per hex-step, that is 15 seconds of watching dots move. The animation must be compressible to a fraction of that, or the torpedo movement must be shown as a jump rather than a per-hex crawl.

## PART FOUR: WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD CHANGE THIS PROPOSAL

1. If the hex grid is volumetric (3D layers), not flat. The firing arcs, adjacency, and movement rules would need to be redesigned for 3D hexes. The torpedo course would need to account for vertical movement. Boarding adjacency would need to include vertical neighbours. This would be a structural change to both proposals. Evidence: a design document or code reference specifying the hex grid as multi-layered.

2. If the battle size grows significantly beyond 20 ships. The brief states the target is "around 20 ships" (OBSERVED: "Target battle size stays near the current scale (around 20 ships)"), but also notes "any growth is modest and secondary." If the battle size grew to 40+, the torpedo herding mechanic would need to be re-tuned (more torpedoes in flight, larger board, longer flight times), and boarding would need to be restricted to prevent it from becoming the dominant strategy in a large melee. Evidence: a revised battle-size target from Ryan.

3. If shields are removed or fundamentally changed. The torpedo and boarding rules assume shields exist as a damage-absorption layer. If shields are removed, torpedoes would need a different defence mechanism (armour, point defence, or evasion only), and the interaction between torpedo hits and hull damage would change. Evidence: a design document or ruling that removes or redefines shields.

4. If the feel benchmark shifts away from Rogue Trader. The entire proposal is built on the Rogue Trader verbs: multi-turn torpedo travel with arrival-turn targeting, and boarding as a deliberate, consequential action. If Ryan's stated mix (OBSERVED: "BattleTech plus Sins of a Solar Empire 2 plus Rogue Trader space battles") shifts weight toward BattleTech's per-location damage model or Sins 2's real-time spectacle, the turn structure and resolution mechanics would need to be re-examined. Evidence: a new ruling from Ryan reweighting the three benchmarks.

5. If officer posts or specialist characters are added to Voidsworn. The brief explicitly forbids importing officer posts (OBSERVED: "Voidsworn has no officer posts; do not import them"). If this changes, the boarding opposed check and the torpedo intercept mechanics could be enriched with character-driven modifiers. Evidence: a design document or ruling introducing officer or specialist mechanics.

6. If playtesting reveals that the opposed boarding check is too swingy or too deterministic. The current proposal uses a single check with a small random modifier. If playtesting shows that boarding either always succeeds (making it a guaranteed damage action) or always fails (making it a wasted activation), the resolution mechanic would need to be adjusted, possibly by adding a small dice pool or by introducing a deterministic threshold with modifiers. Evidence: playtest data showing a win rate for boarding actions outside the 40-60% range.

7. If the torpedo travel speed of 3 hexes per turn produces the wrong tempo. The 3-hex speed is INFERRED from the 10-hex launch range and the desired 2-4 turn flight window. If playtesting shows that 3 hexes is too fast (torpedoes arrive before the defender can react) or too slow (torpedoes are irrelevant by the time they arrive), the speed should be adjusted. Evidence: playtest data showing that torpedoes hit more than 70% of the time (too fast, not enough evasion window) or less than 20% of the time (too slow, defender has too much time to reposition).

End of proposals. Both deliverables are presented in full. No content has been summarized or shortened.
