MEASUREMENTS HOLD
"does not widen in sector 3 (25 pp, inside this sample's noise)" | At n = 20 one battle equals 5 pp; the 95 % binomial interval on a 25 pp delta (5 battles) spans roughly 3-47 pp, and on the sector-2 35 pp delta (7 battles) roughly 12-58 pp. The two intervals overlap almost entirely, so "does not widen" is an absence-of-evidence statement recast as evidence-of-absence. The verdict leans on a non-significant sector 2 to 3 comparison. | Re-run the sector-2 and sector-3 nemNoWeak cells at n of at least 100 (or 200) each; a 10 pp true widening would then be detectable at 95 % power.
"weakness value +10 pp" (sector 3) | Ten percentage points at n = 20 is exactly two battles. The pack labels sector 1's -5 pp as noise but reports sector 3's +10 pp in the same table without an equivalent flag, letting it contribute to the "intel loop pays out on schedule" narrative. Two battles cannot distinguish a real effect from a coin-flip. | Pool sector-3 nem vs nemNoWeak across repeated seeds (e.g., 100 battles) or report a binomial exact CI; if the CI includes zero, reclassify as noise.
"KEEPING PACE, after one hard step. Not snowballing." stated as a general verdict | The sim applies the standard adaptation cadence (one adaptation per sector advance) to every faction. The Chorus Nemesis adapts after every meeting with a cap that grows with beaten + scars, and its fed bonus stacks +4 % hull per player ship lost, cap 8 stacks. The pack acknowledges the understatement in Limitations but the verdict line does not qualify itself to standard-cadence factions. A Chorus Nemesis at 2 scars, 3 adaptations, and 5 fed stacks by sector 3 is a materially different opponent than the one measured. | Run the same 3-sector nemMatrix arm with the Chorus-specific cadence and fed stacking enabled; compare growth-cost trajectories.
"the base fleet is indexed to the player's power, so there is no channel through which he can run away" | The indexing argument traces genEnemyFleet to fleetPower at generation time. The pack's own Limitations note that at the ruled 80 % loss rate players often arrive battered, and fleetPower's hull term means a battered arrival faces a relatively stronger Nemesis. The sim seats the player at full strength in every cell, so the measured win rates are conditional on an arrival state that the ruled loss rate says is atypical. The "no channel" claim is true for the generation formula but incomplete for the encounter as experienced. | Re-run sector-2 and sector-3 nem cells with the player fleet at 60 % and 40 % hull integrity (matching expected post-loss states) and report the shifted win rates.
"favour set uniformly to 0, 4 or 25" | Favour is tested only at tier boundaries. The tax's shape between tiers (favour 1-3, 5-24) is unmeasured. Because rollRarity's favour boost is continuous in the code, the tax presumably ramps smoothly, but the pack's "17 % less expected effect" headline is anchored to the extreme (tier 3) and could mislead about the typical mid-run experience where favour sits between 5 and 20. | Sample favour at 0, 2, 4, 8, 15, 25 (six levels across the existing grid) and plot mean-m gap versus favour; report the tax at median expected favour for a three-sector run.
"Adjacent observation, same shape: duo boons enter offers at a hardcoded rarity of Common" | The framing as "adjacent" and "same shape" understates a distinct defect. The off-patron tax is a missing-argument bug (no patron passed to rollRarity), fixable by threading favour through. The duo Common is a design decision to skip rolling entirely (r:0, never calls rollRarity). Different root cause, different fix, different player-facing promise broken (duo boons are marketed as special rewards, yet always arrive at the lowest tier regardless of favour or sector). Lumping them as "same shape" risks the Q7 fix patching the rollRarity path and leaving duo untouched. | File as a separate defect with its own fix spec; verify post-fix that duo picks roll rarity with the offering patron's favour applied, then re-measure the 4,936-pick sample.
"the premium slightly narrows as the player grows" (base CP / player CP: 1.36 to 1.34 to 1.29) | Three sector means from 60 generated fleets each are presented as a trajectory. The spread across those 60 fleets is not reported; the 0.07 drop from sector 1 to 3 could be within the per-sector variance of the elite budget roll. Calling it a "narrowing" implies a directional trend the sample size may not resolve. | Report the standard deviation or interquartile range of the CP ratio per sector; if the sector-1 and sector-3 intervals overlap, restate as "flat within noise."
"the bot still wins 60, 65 and 45 percent of meetings" used to frame the encounter as fair | The pilot is the game's own auto-battle bot, which does not exploit the Nemesis's two live weaknesses the way a human player with the intel screen can, nor does it make the strategic fleet adjustments a player would after a sector-1 loss. The 80 %-loss-rate context means most players arrive at the Nemesis already degraded. Presenting bot win rates as the fairness benchmark without noting that a skilled human would outperform the bot, and a battered human would underperform it, leaves the "fair" reading ambiguous. | Run at least one arm with a scripted "informed player" policy (focus-fire on weaknesses, retreat-and-repair before the Nemesis node) and one arm at 70 % fleet hull, to bracket the human experience.
Not checked
- Actual RNG stream independence across the 184,800 boon offers and the 180 sim battles; a seeded or correlated generator would shrink the effective sample below the stated n.
- The Godot port's parity with the browser build at commit 264f441d; the pack explicitly scopes to browser and states the port should reproduce these numbers, but no Godot-side execution is verified here.
- The genEnemyFleet budget formula beyond the three sector spot-checks (e.g., behaviour at extreme fleetPower values, or when the player fields unusual hull compositions that could break the linear scaling assumption).
- Whether nemLearn / nemBeatTeach actually select the adaptation most counter to the player's realised strategy in a real run, or whether the "counter to how the player has been fighting" claim degrades when the player switches tactics mid-run.
- The interaction between the legendary-eligibility masking (minR 1, held at least 2) and the backfill cliff in live play frequency: the pack notes it at 100 % in eligible configs but does not weight how often elite/boss reward nodes appear in a typical three-sector run relative to standard nodes.
- Battle-round-length differences (7.2-9.3 vs 6.4-7.8 rounds) and whether longer fights shift resource attrition (repair, ammo, cooldowns) in ways the win-rate headline does not capture.
- Any player-facing UI text or tooltip that promises rarity behaviour the measured distributions contradict (the tier-3 "never Common" promise is cited but the exact wording and its legal/design scope are not audited here).
