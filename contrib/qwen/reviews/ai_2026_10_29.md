Overall verdict: COMPETENT BUT HOLLOW. The AI makes real tactical decisions-pathfinding, threat weighting, ability timing, torpedo-lane avoidance, escort spacing-but it has no retreat, no visible wing-stance logic, and its targeting is an implicit offence score rather than a readable priority, so it fights like a competent automaton rather than an opponent with intent you can read and outthink.

FINDINGS
1. [BLOCKER] - No retreat or disengage path exists for enemy ships.
READ. aiUnit's only defensive options are brace (a stationary damage-reduction stance) and blink (a short escape jump for one specific ability). There is no code path that removes a ship from the fight or moves it toward an exit. Every engagement is to-the-death or a stationary turtle. The commissioner's question asks whether the AI is "an opponent or a target range"; an opponent that cannot choose to leave is closer to a target range with hit points. The hysteresis caution curve (aiHurtW) modulates aggression but never presence.
2. [BLOCKER] - Targeting is an implicit scalar, not a rule the player can read or bait.
READ. Movement scoring is score = off - threat*tw where off = evalOffense(v, ready, foes) (or the vet variant with focus). The evalOffense and evalThreat functions are not in the pack, but the calling pattern shows the AI picks whichever hex maximises a blended damage number. There is no visible priority (e.g. "focus the lowest-shield facing," "finish the cripple," "ignore the tank"). INFERRED: this makes the AI's fire allocation feel uniform and un-baitable; a player cannot misdirect it the way one misdirects a human opponent.
3. [FIX] - The thinning factor (RAMP.thin) is declared and described but never applied in the visible genEnemyFleet code.
READ. The comment states "per-ship toughness eases a little as numbers rise (thin)" and RAMP defines thin:[0,.73,.675,.62], yet the genEnemyFleet function never references RAMP.thin[s]. INFERRED: either the factor is applied at ship-instantiation time in a code slice not included in this pack, or it was removed and the comment is stale. If the latter, sector-3 ships are at full toughness and full numbers, making the difficulty spike harsher than the tuning notes claim.
4. [FIX] - The power-feedback exponent (pw = 0.6) compresses the clamp range so heavily that the 0.75-1.3 bounds are almost never approached in normal play.
READ. The formula is Math.pow(fleetPower()/refP, 0.6). INFERRED: to hit the 1.3 cap the player must be at roughly (1.3)^(1/0.6) ≈ 1.6× the reference fleet power. To hit 0.75 the player must be at (0.75)^(1/0.6) ≈ 0.62× reference. In a roguelite where the player is often at 50-80 % strength mid-run, most engagements cluster near 0.9-1.05 of the budget. The clamp looks generous but is functionally narrow.
5. [FIX] - Wing stance control is absent from the AI code.
READ. Squadrons are handled by sqAct(u) with the comment "squadrons follow the swarm rules," but sqAct is not in the pack and no stance-setting call (aggressive/defensive/escort) appears anywhere in aiTurn or aiUnit. INFERRED: wings operate on a fixed behaviour; the player's stance system is effectively player-only.
6. [FIX] - The escort field in RAMP (0.78) is declared but never referenced in the visible genEnemyFleet code.
READ. escort:.78 sits in the RAMP object. The boss branch applies RAMP.boss[s] to the remaining budget, but no line multiplies by RAMP.escort. INFERRED: either escort sizing is handled in a code slice not provided, or the field is vestigial. The CHAIR CENSUS lists "escort ratios" as part of the system, so this is at minimum a pack-completeness gap.
7. [NOTE] - The heavy-archetype filter has a subtle exclusion clause that may produce empty rosters.
READ. roster.filter(x=>x.cp>=2&&(!x.car||!roster.some(y=>y.cp>=3&&!y.car))). INFERRED: for a faction whose only ≥2 cp hulls are carriers, the !x.car clause fails and the !roster.some(y=>y.cp>=3&&!y.car) rescue only helps if no non-carrier big hull exists. Edge-case, but the fallback is if(big.length&&…) so an empty big silently drops the archetype to the generic fill loop.
8. [NOTE] - The death-cliff penalty (-25) is a flat number, not scaled to ship value.
READ. if(vet&&!u.isSq&&threat>=u.hp) score-=25;. INFERRED: a 1-cp scout and a 4-cp cruiser both avoid the same hex with the same -25 penalty. In large fleets this makes cheap ships disproportionately cautious relative to their replaceability, flattening the swarm archetype's intended expendability.
9. [NOTE] - Mid-fire during movement is disabled when the nova ability is off cooldown.
READ. const midFire=!u.isSq&&!runner&&!(u.ab==='nova'&&canAbility(u))…. INFERRED: nova ships never fire mid-move, even if their nova is being saved for a later hex. This is a small but exploitable tell: a player can identify nova ships by their movement-then-stop-then-fire cadence.
10. [NOTE] - The gravity well is scored (aiWellScore) but the function is not in the pack.
READ. score+=aiWellScore(u,v); is called in the movement loop. INFERRED: without the function body I cannot judge whether the AI uses the well offensively (pulling enemies in) or merely avoids it. Listed here for completeness.

EXPLICIT CHECKS
COMPOSITION
genEnemyFleet builds a single scalar budget from sector base, column modifier, and RAMP.grow, then lets archetypes reshape the spending without adding to it. Swarm filters the roster to the cheapest hulls; heavy picks the single most expensive affordable hull first, then fills around it; carrier picks the biggest carrier hull and then fills with non-carriers. This gives each archetype a legible silhouette: swarm = many 1-cp pips, heavy = one anchor plus screen, carrier = one launch hull plus escort. The intent is readable.
The thinning factor is described as reducing per-ship toughness so that "total threat follows the old difficulty curve" while ship counts rise. INFERRED: if it works as described, sector 3 fields ~20 ships at 62 % toughness each-long fights, many targets, low per-hit pressure. That matches the commissioner's "bosses measure long rather than lethal." However, as noted in Finding 3, the factor is not visibly applied in this code slice, so I cannot confirm it is live.
Is thinning the right lever? INFERRED: partially. It keeps total DPS roughly constant while inflating target count, which pads fight duration. But it does nothing for composition pressure-it cannot make a swarm feel different from a heavy line in terms of what the player must prioritise. The lever controls duration, not texture.

POWER FEEDBACK
The formula: budget *= clamp((fleetPower / refP)^0.6, 0.75, 1.3).
Who it helps: A player at low fleet power (just lost ships) gets a budget discount down to 75 %. This softens the death spiral.
Who it punishes: A player who has built a strong fleet pays up to 30 % more budget. In a game that wants 80 % of runs lost, this means the reward for good play is a harder next fight.
Is the clamp honest? The 0.75-1.3 range reads generous, but the 0.6 exponent compresses it. INFERRED: a player at half reference power gets (0.5)^0.6 ≈ 0.66, clamped to 0.75-only a 25 % discount. A player at double reference gets (2)^0.6 ≈ 1.52, clamped to 1.3-a 30 % surcharge. The asymmetry is slight but real: the surcharge is easier to reach than the discount because fleet power is unbounded upward but bounded below by "you still have a ship."
Against the 80 %-loss ruling: the system is compatible with that ruling but does not serve it. The feedback is a rubber band, not a difficulty dial. It keeps fights "fair-ish" rather than ensuring the player loses. INFERRED: the actual loss rate is driven by RAMP.grow, thin, and boss ratios, not by the feedback clamp. The clamp prevents blowouts in either direction but does not create the 80 % loss.

BATTLE DECISIONS
Targeting rule (one line): The AI picks the hex that maximises evalOffense(v, readyWeapons, foes) minus evalThreat(v, foes) * threatWeight, where threatWeight scales with hull-fraction-based caution; there is no explicit target-priority list.
Ability rule (one line): Abilities fire in a fixed phase order-pre-move (surge, evasive, phase, regrow, reclaim, ionpulse, overburn, launch, blink, reload), post-move/aim (focus, frenzy, reload for torps), post-fire/escape (blink)-gated by canAbility cooldowns and simple threshold checks.
Retreat rule (one line): There is no retreat; the closest analogue is brace (stationary damage reduction) when thr > hp*0.45 && myOff < thr*0.35 && hp < maxHp*0.7, plus blink as a one-shot escape jump for that specific ability.
Most exploitable habit: The fixed speed-order activation in Standard mode (const order=alive('enemy').sort(…b.speed-a.speed)). READ. A player who knows the activation order can predict exactly which ship moves next and pre-position. In Classic mode aiPickActivation picks by opportunity, but Standard is deterministic. INFERRED: this is the single largest informational asymmetry the player has.
Smartest behaviour: The death-cliff check combined with the hysteresis caution curve. READ. if(vet&&!u.isSq&&threat>=u.hp) score-=25 prevents the AI from walking a ship into a hex where expected fire kills it, and aiHurtW with its 30 %/45 % hysteresis band prevents a ship from oscillating between charging and fleeing every turn. Lines: the aiHurtW function and the death-cliff if in the scoring loop. INFERRED: this makes the AI feel cautious in a consistent way rather than randomly bold or timid, which is the closest thing to "personality" in the code.

WINGS AND SPECIALS
Launch: Used. if(pre&&u.ab==='launch'&&canAbility(u)) picks the hex nearest to foes and calls useAbility. The AI launches squadrons toward the player.
Wing stances: Not visible. Squadrons are routed to sqAct(u) ("squadrons follow the swarm rules") but sqAct is not in the pack. No stance-switching call appears in aiTurn or aiUnit. INFERRED: wing stances are effectively player-only.
Torpedoes: Partially used. The reload ability checks w.k==='torp' and reloads spent tubes when a target is in reach. torpThreat(u,v) is subtracted from movement scores, so the AI avoids torpedo lanes. But there is no visible logic for choosing when to fire torpedoes versus guns; that is presumably inside aiVolley, which is not in the pack. INFERRED: torpedo defence is AI-controlled; torpedo offence timing is unclear.
Gravity well: Scored via aiWellScore(u,v) in the movement loop. The function body is absent. INFERRED: cannot judge whether the AI uses the well offensively (luring the player in) or merely pathfinds around it.
Systems effectively player-only: Wing stances (confirmed absent), and possibly gravity-well offensive use (unverifiable). Retreat/disengage is also player-only in practice, since the AI has no flee path.

DIFFICULTY TEXTURE
Sector 3 RAMP numbers: grow 1.26, light 5, thin .62, refP 20, ceil 20, boss .53.
Base budget for sector 3 is 14 + col*1.1, multiplied by 1.26. The ceiling is 20 ships on board (minus the player's living ship count, min 4). The light weight of 5 heavily biases the fill loop toward 1-2 cp hulls. The boss ratio of 0.53 means the boss itself takes 53 % of the budget, leaving 47 % for escorts.
INFERRED: Sector 3 difficulty comes overwhelmingly from volume-many light ships filling the 20-ship ceiling-rather than from per-ship lethality (thin at .62 reduces individual toughness) or from AI behavioural sophistication (the AI code is the same in every sector). The boss fight is long because there are ~10 escort ships to clear before the boss's HP bar matters, and the boss itself is a large HP pool. This matches "long rather than lethal": the player is not burst down; they are worn down by action economy across many targets.
The source of difficulty (volume + low per-ship threat) is consistent with the commissioner's description. INFERRED: the risk is that it becomes tedious rather than tense, because the AI's behaviour doesn't escalate-only its numbers do.

QUESTIONS FOR THE DESIGNER
Is the absence of enemy retreat intentional, or is it a gap you have not yet addressed? The stakes: if the AI cannot disengage, every fight is an attrition check and the player never faces the decision of whether to pursue or let go, which removes a layer of strategic choice from the roguelite loop.
Where is RAMP.thin actually applied, and is the 0.62 sector-3 value tuned against the current ship-stat baseline or an older one? The stakes: if thin is stale or applied to the wrong stat, sector-3 difficulty is either silently harsher or softer than the sim results suggest, and the Oct 27 tuning pass that "brought wins from 13.5 % back to about 20 %" may be built on a phantom number.
Should wing stances be AI-controlled, or is their player-only status a deliberate asymmetry? The stakes: if the AI never switches wing stances, the player's stance system is a free power lever with no counterplay, and carrier fleets (the archetype most dependent on wing management) are strictly weaker on the enemy side than on the player side.
