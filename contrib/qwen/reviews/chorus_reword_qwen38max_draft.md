# CHORUS ADMIRAL REWORD DRAFTS - VOIDSWORN DESIGN (PC-ONLY, BABYLON)

Model: Qwen3.8-Max, Thinking ON, Search OFF. Filed verbatim from the Qwen chat report (chat titled "Chorus Reword Brief"); em dashes normalized to hyphens if any, wording otherwise unchanged. The granted brief file was attached to the chat and the model was instructed to follow it exactly.

STATUS: DRAFT OPTIONS FOR RYAN. NOT A RULING. Ryan has not yet decided between reword, regenerate, or waive for any of the three admirals below. These rewords are one option among those alternatives and are presented for comparison only.

OBSERVED STANDING RULES (Chorus)
All Chorus forms are grown flesh. They are gross and disgusting.
No familiar Earth-animal anatomy is permitted, including sea creatures.
No constructed things, buildings, or instruments.
A Chorus admiral's name describes what it does or the formation it holds.
Razormaw is EXCLUDED from this exercise. Its blade-formation description is permitted by the ruling's formation carve-out. It is not reworded here.

## 1. MATRIARCH OF NINE THROATS
Original description: "a faceless column of membrane organ pipes"
Identified problem (OBSERVED): "organ pipes" reads as a constructed musical instrument, violating the no-constructed-things rule.
Visual to preserve: A tall, faceless column made of tubes. The tubes produce sound or resonance.

PROPOSED Reword A
"a faceless column of wet membrane tubes, each one a raw throat that pulses and weeps mucus as it drones"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. The visual (tall column of tubes) is unchanged. Only the language shifts from "organ pipes" to "membrane tubes" and "raw throat," removing the instrument connotation.
What is lost (if anything): The word "organ" carried a faint musical grandeur; that register is gone, replaced by wetness and mucus. The structure is the same.

PROPOSED Reword B
"a tall, faceless pillar of nine grown hollow stalks of glistening membrane, each stalk a living throat that hums and drips"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. The column-of-tubes visual is intact. "Grown hollow stalks" replaces "organ pipes" to emphasize organic growth rather than construction.
What is lost (if anything): "Organ pipes" implied a uniform, orderly array. "Stalks" leans slightly vegetal in connotation, though the membrane and throat language keeps it fleshy.

PROPOSED Reword C
"a faceless tower of nine fused membrane columns, each column a raw, open throat lined with ridged tissue that vibrates and spits"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. Still a tall column of tube-like forms. "Fused membrane columns" and "ridged tissue" describe grown anatomy without referencing any instrument.
What is lost (if anything): The word "pipes" is gone entirely, which removes the clean geometric shorthand. The description is longer and more viscerally detailed as a result.

## 2. VOICE OF THE DEEP
Original description: "an abyssal comb-jelly bell"
Identified problem (OBSERVED): "comb-jelly" names a real sea creature (ctenophore), which is the clearest violation of the no-familiar-Earth-animal-anatomy rule, sea creatures included.
Visual to preserve: A bell shape, deep/abyssal setting.

PROPOSED Reword A
"a deep-grown bell of translucent, veined membrane, trailing wet fronds of raw tissue that pulse with bioluminescent rot"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. The bell silhouette is preserved. "Comb-jelly" is removed entirely. The bell is now described as a shape grown from membrane, not as an animal.
What is lost (if anything): "Comb-jelly" gave an instant visual reference of iridescent, gelatinous transparency. The reword keeps translucency but loses the specific iridescent-comb-plate imagery, replacing it with veined, rotting tissue.

PROPOSED Reword B
"a hollow bell of abyssal membrane, its walls slick and faintly luminous, its rim a ragged lip of regrowing flesh that sheds strips into the dark water"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. Bell shape retained. No Earth-creature reference. The description emphasizes grown, regrowing flesh and grossness.
What is lost (if anything): The compactness of the original (five words) is gone. The reword is more verbose. The elegant, alien simplicity of "comb-jelly bell" is replaced by something more viscerally described.

PROPOSED Reword C
"a sunken bell of layered, weeping membrane grown around a core of calcified cartilage, its interior a wet echoing chamber"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. Bell shape intact. "Layered, weeping membrane" and "calcified cartilage" are grown tissues, not animal references.
What is lost (if anything): The sense of delicate, drifting transparency associated with a jellyfish is fully gone. This version reads heavier, denser, more structural. Whether that is a loss depends on whether the design intended fragility.

## 3. MENDING CHOIR
Original description: "a mass of pulsing throat sacs and membrane chambers stitching a broken hull with fleshy sutures"
Identified problem (OBSERVED): "stitching" and "sutures" read as sewing, a constructed action, violating the no-constructed-things rule.
Visual to preserve: A mass of throat sacs and membrane chambers. It repairs broken hulls using its own flesh.

PROPOSED Reword A
"a mass of pulsing throat sacs and membrane chambers that presses its own raw flesh into the cracks of a broken hull, the tissue growing and fusing over the wound in wet, glistening layers"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. The visual (mass of sacs and chambers repairing a hull with flesh) is unchanged. "Stitching" and "sutures" are replaced with "presses," "growing," and "fusing," which are organic actions.
What is lost (if anything): "Sutures" gave a vivid, specific image of thread-like strands crossing a wound. That image is replaced by a broader "fusing" that is less visually precise but more biologically honest.

PROPOSED Reword B
"a pulsing mass of throat sacs and swollen membrane chambers that vomits hot, wet tissue into the gaps of a shattered hull, the secreted flesh hardening and gripping as it regrows"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. Same visual. Repair is described through secretion and regrowth rather than sewing. "Vomits" and "secreted" keep the grossness high.
What is lost (if anything): The deliberate, almost surgical care implied by "stitching" is gone. This version reads more chaotic, more like uncontrolled biological overgrowth. The "choir" coordination implied by the name may feel slightly less present.

PROPOSED Reword C
"a heaving mass of pulsing throat sacs and membrane chambers that flows into the split hull and grows new flesh across the breach, the tissue rooting and knotting into the wound as it feeds on the damaged plating"
Fix type: TEXT-ONLY. Visual preserved. "Flows," "grows," "rooting," and "knotting" are organic processes, not constructed actions. "Feeds on the damaged plating" adds the Chorus-eats-and-regrows tone.
What is lost (if anything): "Knotting" skirts close to a constructed metaphor (knots are things one ties), though here it describes tissue tangling into itself, which is a grown process. The line between is thin and worth flagging for Ryan.

## CLOSING ASSESSMENT: WHICH IS HARDEST TO SAVE BY REWORDING ALONE?
PROPOSED observation, not a ruling:
The Voice of the Deep is the hardest of the three to save by rewording alone.
Argument: The Matriarch's problem is a single compound noun ("organ pipes") that can be swapped for "membrane tubes" or "grown stalks" without touching the visual at all. The Mending Choir's problem is two verbs/nouns ("stitching," "sutures") that can be replaced with organic growth language while the visual stays identical. Both are clean text-only fixes.
The Voice of the Deep is different. "Comb-jelly" is not just a word choice; it is the entire visual concept. The design brief says the bell shape must be preserved, but the comb-jelly reference is doing heavy lifting in the original description. It tells the viewer: transparent, gelatinous, iridescent, trailing cilia, drifting. Remove "comb-jelly" and you must rebuild that visual impression from scratch using only grown-flesh language. The rewords above attempt this, but each one drifts toward a different texture (veined and rotting; layered and weeping; calcified and dense) because there is no single organic-flesh equivalent of "comb-jelly" that carries the same instant read. The rewords work, but they change the feel more than the other two rewords do.
Whether regenerate would serve the Voice better is Ryan's call. A regenerate could produce a new visual concept that is natively grown-flesh and bell-shaped, avoiding the translation problem entirely. A reword keeps the existing art (if any) intact but asks the language to do more compensatory work. If the existing Voice art leans heavily on comb-jelly translucency and cilia rows, rewording the text while keeping that art may still feel like it references the animal regardless of the words. In that case, regenerate is the cleaner path. If the art is abstract enough that it reads as "bell of wet membrane" without the viewer thinking "jellyfish," then reword holds and saves the regeneration cost.
This is argument, not decision. Ryan decides.
