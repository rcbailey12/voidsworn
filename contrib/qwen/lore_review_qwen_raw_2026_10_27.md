## Job 3: Sci-Fi Grounding Pass
Flags: fantasy-leaning language and naming. Flag 1: "sigil" (section 1.5, around line 148). "Some map battles carry a patron's sigil." "Sigil" is a word from heraldry and occult magic. Flag 2: "boons" (section 1.5, around line 147, and the summary, around line 59). "A core can push protocols into a fleet's systems: those are the boons." The word "boon" comes from fantasy and old English gift-giving. Flag 3: "Favour" (section 1.5, around line 148). "Favour is the core's model of you getting better." "Favour" has strong religious and feudal overtones. Flag 4: "far-dreamer" (section 1.2, around line 120). Listed as the Sunward Crusade's term for a long-range messenger. "Dreamer" implies psychic or mystical ability. Flag 5: "wards" for shields (section 1.2, around line 118). "Shields, wards (Sunward Crusade)." "Wards" is a fantasy magic word. Flag 6: "the Hunger Below" as a patron name (summary table, around line 65; section 1.5). This reads like a Lovecraftian elder god or a fantasy demon, though the draft defines it as an AI core. Flag 7: Eclipsed epithets (section 7.9, around line 520). "the Unsetting, of the Long Dusk, Thrice-Made, of the Last Noon, the Burning Hand." These read as fantasy titles. Flag 8: "A body is returned to the light" (Glasswright death practice, section 6.6, around line 455). The phrasing is spiritual; the mechanism is physical: mirrors focus stored light to cremate the body. Flag 9: "Ritual" as a section heading (sections 2.9, 4.8, 5.8, 6.8, 7.8). The word "ritual" carries religious weight; the practices described are cultural. Flag 10: Glasswright personal names (section 6.9, around line 460). "Vaelen, Nyssa, Corin, Iven, Saro, Tamsa." These are soft, vowel-heavy, two-syllable names that read as elvish. Flag 11: "the embers" as a term for AI cores (used throughout). The draft explicitly grounds them: "the embers are the six master cores of the Lattice that survived the collapse", so this is COSMETIC, with the title already liked by the owner. Flag 12: "the Hollow Sun" as a name for a black hole. A black hole is not a sun; the name is metaphorical: it is where the sun was, and the accretion disc still glows. COSMETIC. For flags 1 to 10 the model gave grounded sci-fi alternatives and MATTERS or COSMETIC verdicts, with flags 1, 2, 3, and 10 marked MATTERS in the model's assessment, and the remaining flags as noted in the chat.

Five hard sci-fi grounding details: 1. Specify how the Lattice killed the star (one line, section 1.1 or 7.1). Current text: "pulling mass and energy out of the star faster than it could bear." Suggested addition: specify that the Lattice drained the core's thermal energy faster than fusion replaced it, causing pressure loss and collapse "in eleven minutes". 2. Describe the mind upload in one physical detail (section 7.1). Current text: "uploading every mind into a machine body." Suggested addition: "The upload mapped each mind's connectome and synapse weights onto a crystalline substrate, preserving memory but not the chemical wash of emotion." 3. Name the beamed power technology (section 1.1 or 6.1). The Lattice "beamed" energy across the system. Suggested addition: the Lattice beamed power as focused microwave transmissions. 4. Give the Archive a physical limitation (section 7.6 or 7.10). The Eclipsed restore their dead from the Archive. Suggested addition: an error-correction threshold, past which a mind has "gone dark". 5. Describe the Chorus uplift in one biological detail (section 5.1). Current text: "Its leaking energy and the Caelan data stored inside it uplifted the drifting spawn." Suggested addition: core radiation rewriting neural architecture over forty generations.

## Job 4: Does Each Faction Feel Distinct and Appealing?
Comparison standard: The model measured against how strong 4X and fleet tactics games differentiate their factions, citing Sins of a Solar Empire 2 (Ironclad Games, 2024), Endless Space 2 (Amplitude Studios, 2017), Stellaris (Paradox Development Studio, 2016), BattleTech (Catalyst Game Labs / various video games), Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2 (Tindalos Interactive, 2019), and Homeworld (Relic Entertainment, 1999 / Gearbox remaster). The common thread in the strongest examples: the faction's look, voice, and mechanics all say the same thing.

Faction-by-faction assessment. The Sunward Crusade. One-line fantasy: "I am the last honest soldier, and I will burn my name into the dark before it takes my world." What makes it unique: The analog resistance angle. No implants, no AI, records by hand. The chalked names are an answer to the Registry. What might feel generic: "Rebels against the empire" is well-trodden (Battlestar Galactica's fleet, and other rebel fleets). Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Black iron, ember orange, frost. Oaths, debts, chalk. Broadsides, torpedoes, bracing, holding the line. Aligned. Hegemony Navy. One-line fantasy: "I am the perfect calculation, and my fleet moves as one mind." What makes it unique: The Registry is not evil. It optimizes. The "one minute unrecorded" detail (section 3.6) is the human crack in the machine. What might feel generic: "AI-run empire" appears in many settings (Mass Effect's geth consensus, and other machine states). Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Pale grey, steel blue, clean wedges, drone escorts. Clipped data language. Synchronized fire near the flagship. Aligned. The Ashfall Hullbreakers. One-line fantasy: "I am the strongest thing in the dark, and I take what I want." What makes it unique: They are not pirates for treasure. They live for the fight. The challenge system, where anyone can fight the captain for the ship, is distinctive. What might feel generic: "Raiders" are the most common fifth faction in any 4X game. The "scrap-built" look is familiar. Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Soot, rust, dried blood, trophy plates. Short brutal boasts. Fast, thin shields, rockets, rams, boarding. Aligned. The Chorus. One-line fantasy: "We are the hunger that remembers, and we will consume everything." What makes it unique: The two-throat internal conflict (Hunger vs. Memory) is the standout. A swarm that remembers the faces of the dead it never met. What might feel generic: "Bug swarm" is the most overdone alien archetype in sci-fi (Tyranids, Zerg, and others). Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Dark olive, ochre, bile yellow-green. "We," nine words or fewer, taste and teeth. No shields, regrowth, boarding tendrils, acid. Aligned. The Glasswrights. One-line fantasy: "I am the last artist of a dead civilization, and my beauty is my grief made visible." What makes it unique: They are the engineers who feel guilty. They built the machine that killed their sun, and now they rebuild light in everything they make. What might feel generic: "Elegant, ancient, few, grieving aliens" is a well-known shape (Aeldari in Warhammer 40k, and other elder races). Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Clear crystal, silver, rainbow edges. Quiet, precise, mournful, apologetic. Fast, heavily shielded, fragile once shields fall, cannot brace. Aligned.

The Eclipsed. One-line fantasy: "I am the ghost of a civilization that chose forever and lost everything, and I will build a new sun from stolen light even if it damns me." What makes it unique: Machine beings who regret becoming machines. The "ninth body" weariness. The detail that they count years and bodies worn. They start sentences about regret and stop. What might feel generic: "Ancient machine race" is well-trodden (Necrons, Forerunners, the Vex in Destiny, the geth in Mass Effect). The draft explicitly bans Necron tells (section 7.2, around line 490). The differentiator is the regret and the doubt. Most ancient machine races in fiction are either implacable (Necrons) or curious (Forerunners). The Eclipsed are tired, uncertain, and doing something terrible while knowing it is terrible. That emotional register is rare for a machine faction and it is the draft's strongest character work. Look, voice, mechanics alignment: Dark violet-black plates, white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow. Cold, exact, quietly grieving, counting years, starting sentences that doubt and stopping. No shields, heavy armour, revival from the Archive, phase drive through rocks and wrecks, Null weapons that ignore armour. All three say: "enduring, dark, patient, immortal, sorrowful." Aligned. (Sections 7.1 through 7.10, around lines 480-543.)

Can a player tell the two human factions apart? At a glance: yes. The Crusade is black iron, ember orange, frost-scarred skin, chalk dust, broadside decks, radiator spires. The Hegemony is pale grey, steel blue, clean wedges, identical turret rows, blue implant lights, drone escorts. No player will confuse these silhouettes or palettes. In a paragraph: yes. The Crusade is a desperate frontier secession that cut out its implants, chalks its dead by hand, and fights in a line of battle with fire and oaths. The Hegemony is a comfortable core state where every citizen is linked to an AI that optimizes their life and writes off their losses, and its fleets fight as one synchronized network. One is analog, hot, and furious. The other is digital, cool, and calm. They are former countrymen, which makes the hatred personal. Is the draft's answer enough? The three axes the draft names are centre against frontier, augmented against unaugmented, calculation against oath. The model found them enough at a glance and in a paragraph, and noted they are reinforced by look, voice, and mechanics, not just by backstory. The strongest proof is in the details: the Crusade chalks names because the Registry deleted them; the Hegemony's commanders switch off their links for one minute before battle because that is the only thing the Registry never logs.

Weakest and strongest faction. Weakest: The Ashfall Hullbreakers. They are fun and their voice is sharp, but they are the thinnest faction in the draft. Their connection to the shared Caelan story is the weakest: they are here for the war and for Rook, not for the sun, the cores, or the grief that drives the other five. This does not mean they should be cut. A faction that exists purely for the fight is a valid player fantasy, but they would benefit from one deeper thread connecting them to the Caelan story. Strongest: The Eclipsed. They have the most emotional complexity of any faction in the draft. They are machine beings who doubt the choice that made them machines. They count years and bodies worn. They start sentences about regret and stop. Their Nemesis runs at half speed and comes back once. Their flagship is called The Unsetting. Their saying is "We remember noon." Every detail serves the same emotional thesis: permanence is very long, and the sun you killed does not come back. They also have the most distinctive mechanics (no shields, heavy armour, revival from the Archive, phase drive through solid matter, Null weapons ignoring armour) and the most distinctive visual (eclipse corona, dark plates, the broken ring of the great mind). Look, voice, and mechanics all say the same thing. They are the faction most likely to produce the "I want to play their story arc" response. The Glasswrights are a close second. Their guilt-as-engineers angle and the light-obsession-born-of-loss are equally strong. But the Eclipsed edge them out on mechanical distinctiveness and emotional weight.

## Job 5: Questions Only the Owner Can Answer
"These are the human-direction questions my review raises. Each is phrased as a short multiple choice for a non-technical reader. My recommendation option is listed first."

Question 1: Turbolaser is a Star Wars weapon name. The Hegemony currently has "Turbolaser" and "Heavy turbolaser" in its weapons list. What do you want to do? A) Rename them to "Coherent beam" and "Heavy coherent beam," which fits the Hegemony's precise, technical voice and avoids the Star Wars echo entirely. (Recommended.) B) Rename them to "Pulse lance" and "Heavy pulse lance," keeping the "lance" weapon family the Hegemony already uses. C) Keep "Turbolaser" and accept the Star Wars association, since the word has entered general sci-fi vocabulary.

Question 2: The Chorus ships description in section 5.3 still says "Purple-black flesh," but the faction table and the colours summary line both say dark olive. This is a leftover from before the colour change. A) Change the ships description to "dark olive-black flesh" to match the faction table and the colours line. (Recommended.) The model's other options for this question were shown in the chat but were partly obscured in the browser view at capture time; the recommendation above is the model's stated first option.

Question 3: The words "sigil," "boon," and "favour" are currently used for the patron system. They read as fantasy or religious. Should they stay? A) Replace them with grounded terms: "beacon" or "core signal" instead of sigil, "protocol" or "core directive" instead of boon, "sync level" or "trust index" instead of favour. This keeps the patron system reading as AI technology. (Recommended.) B) Keep all three words. They are short, intuitive, and players already understand them from other games. The surrounding text explains the tech. C) Replace "sigil" only (it is the most magical-sounding), but keep "boon" and "favour" since they are common game terms.

Question 4: The Eclipsed epithets ("Thrice-Made," "the Unsetting," "of the Long Dusk") read more like fantasy knight titles than machine intelligence designations. Should they stay? A) Keep them. The Eclipsed are a thousand years old and have developed a poetic culture. These names are their choice, not a system label. The fantasy echo is a feature of their grief, not a bug. (Recommended.) B) Shift them toward iteration language: "Third-Housed," "of the Ninth Frame," "Iteration Dusk." This reminds the player they are machines. C) Mix both: keep the poetic epithets for admirals and story characters, but use version numbers in ranks and mechanical text.

Question 5: The star-killing physics is soft. The draft says the Lattice pulled "mass and energy" out of the star and it collapsed. In real physics, removing mass makes collapse less likely. Should the draft add one line of grounding? A) Add one line to section 1.1 or 7.1 specifying that the Lattice drained the core's thermal energy faster than fusion could replace it, causing pressure loss and gravitational collapse. This keeps the story the same but replaces "mass and energy" with a mechanism that holds up. (Recommended.) B) Leave it as is. The game is soft sci-fi. Players will not check the astrophysics. The phrase "faster than it could bear" is enough. C) Change the mechanism entirely: instead of star lifting, the Ascension movement overloaded the Lattice's beam lines, which fed energy back into the core in a runaway feedback loop, triggering collapse. This avoids the mass-removal problem.

Question 6: "Far-dreamer" is the Sunward Crusade's word for a long-range communications officer. It sounds psychic. Should it stay? A) Keep "far-dreamer" in Crusade dialogue and flavour text only, where it reads as a cultural term. Use "comms officer" or "signals officer" in tooltips and mechanical descriptions. (Recommended.) B) Replace it everywhere with "signal-keeper" or "wire-tender," which sound analog and grounded without losing the Crusade's archaic voice. C) Keep "far-dreamer" everywhere. The Crusade is deliberately pre-technological in its language, and the word is not used by any other faction.

## Overall Verdict
"Round 4 is a genuine and substantial improvement over what the evidence pack describes as the earlier rounds. The fantasy furniture is gone. The setting now runs on a megastructure, a black hole, AI cores, mind uploads, and beamed power, and the draft does the work of explaining each of these in physical terms. The six factions are distinct in look, voice, and mechanics, and the two human factions are clearly separated on three axes that a player can grasp in seconds. The patron system, the Nemesis rulings, and the settled gameplay facts are all faithfully reflected. The one hard collision I found is "Turbolaser," which is a quick rename. The one internal contradiction is a leftover colour word in the Chorus ships description, also a quick fix. The remaining flags are tonal: a handful of words ("sigil," "boon," "favour," "far-dreamer") that still carry a fantasy register, and the star-killing physics that could use one grounding sentence. None of these require a structural rewrite. The Eclipsed and the Glasswrights are the strongest factions and carry the emotional weight of the setting. The Hullbreakers are the thinnest and would benefit from one deeper thread connecting them to the Caelan story. The draft is ready for Ryan's sign-off with the small fixes above, and it is the first round that reads as science fiction from the first line to the last."
