PART 2: PROPOSE ASYMMETRY
To achieve benchmark-level asymmetry, factions must differ in decisions, prohibitions, and resource loops, not just decimals.
1. Voidsworn (The Anchor Line)
Unit-Count: Standard.
Range Profile: Mid-to-Long lateral batteries.
Shield & Survivability: High directional void-shields and heavy armor.
Movement Character: Slow, deliberate; heavily rewarded for holding static firing positions.
Economy: Steady credit income; low repair costs.
Win Condition: Break the enemy fleet in a sustained line battle.
Lose Condition: Flagship destroyed or lateral arrays flanked.
Prohibition: Cannot use phase jumps or sideslip thrusters. They must commit to their vector.
Smallest Change Set: Lock the faction to the stillPct (Anchored Volley) baseline trait (data.js:398). Remove slip and blink from their drive rules (data.js:103).
2. Hegemony (The Screen & Strip)
Unit-Count: Standard capitals + high squadron count.
Range Profile: Long-range ion projection.
Shield & Survivability: Massive shield capacity, extremely low armor.
Movement Character: Tactical backing (retro-thrusters) to maintain optimal firing distance.
Economy: High credit sink to constantly replace destroyed fighter wings.
Win Condition: Strip enemy shields and let bombers execute the hulls.
Lose Condition: Carriers isolated, or shields neutralized by ion storms/flares.
Prohibition: Cannot board enemy ships. They rely entirely on remote projection.
Smallest Change Set: Add if(u.fac==='hegemony') return false; to boardTargets (battle.js:652). Implement a global rule where Hegemony ships take double hull damage once their shields drop to 0.
3. Corsairs (The Boarding Swarm)
Unit-Count: Swarm (many cheap, fragile hulls).
Range Profile: Short/Melee closing speed.
Shield & Survivability: High evasion, low HP/shields.
Movement Character: Aggressive ramming and high-speed interception.
Economy: Gains credits and fleet repairs only via capturing ships and looting wrecks.
Win Condition: Capture the enemy flagship or loot enough credits to out-buy them.
Lose Condition: Swarm thinned out before reaching boarding range.
Prohibition: Cannot field heavy battleships or spinal artillery.
Smallest Change Set: Remove Battleship/Dreadnought hulls from their shipyards. Modify boardUnit (battle.js:667) to grant fleet-wide credits and hull repair on successful capture.
4. Chorus (The Digestive Swarm)
Unit-Count: Massive Swarm.
Range Profile: Melee/Tendril range.
Shield & Survivability: Zero shields, extreme hull regeneration, acid damage over time.
Movement Character: Free pivot, relentless advance ignoring formation.
Economy: Cannot use shipyards; heals by consuming enemy wrecks.
Win Condition: Overwhelm via attrition and biological digestion.
Lose Condition: Burst damage outpaces regeneration.
Prohibition: Cannot repair at shipyards between battles.
Smallest Change Set: Disable shipyard healing for Chorus. In killUnit (battle.js:698), if an enemy dies, nearby Chorus ships regain 20% HP (repurposing the existing avenge relic logic, data.js:187).
5. Ithar (The Glass Skirmishers)
Unit-Count: Elite (few, very expensive ships).
Range Profile: Extreme precision lances.
Shield & Survivability: Massive shields, paper hulls.
Movement Character: Phase jumps, hit-and-fade skirmishing.
Economy: High shield capacitor costs; hull repairs are exorbitant.
Win Condition: Alpha-strike and vanish before return fire lands.
Lose Condition: Caught in sustained brawls or solar flares.
Prohibition: Cannot Brace for impact. They rely on shields and speed, not structural bracing.
Smallest Change Set: Add if(u.fac==='ithar') return false; to canOrder for 'brace' (battle.js:795). Increase their shield recharge rate globally but make hull repairs 50% more expensive.
6. Deathless (The Undying Phalanx)
Unit-Count: Standard (but effectively higher due to reanimation).
Range Profile: Mid-range gauss projection.
Shield & Survivability: Zero shields, extreme armor, reanimation.
Movement Character: Phase drive (ignores obstacles and wrecks).
Economy: Wrecks are tactical resources that stand back up.
Win Condition: Grind the enemy down with immortal hulls.
Lose Condition: Acid or armor-piercing weapons (which halt reanimation).
Prohibition: Immune to crew loss and boarding. They have no biological crew to capture or kill.
Smallest Change Set: Set crewMax: 0 for Deathless hulls (battle.js:117). Make them immune to boardUnit but highly vulnerable to acid damage over time (battle.js:367).
The Three Changes to Make First
Enforce Hard Prohibitions: Disable Bracing for Ithar and Boarding for Hegemony. This instantly forces divergent tactical decisions using existing boolean checks in canOrder and boardTargets.
Tie Chorus Healing to Kills (The "Feed" Mechanic): Repurpose the avenge relic logic to trigger on any enemy death near a Chorus ship, replacing generic shipyard repairs with a combat-loop mechanic.
Implement Faction-Specific AI Ranges: Hardcode FACTION_AI (battle.js:985) to strictly enforce the range profiles (e.g., Ithar kites at range 4+, Corsairs rush to range 0) so the enemy factions play by their own asymmetrical rules.
Proving Asymmetry with Simulation Data
The codebase already supports headless simulations (referenced in battle.js:78). To prove the factions have actually become different, run 10,000 headless battles and track three specific telemetry clusters:
Average Engagement Range at Time of Death: Ithar should cluster at range 4+, Corsairs at range 0-1, Voidsworn at range 2-3.
Shield vs. Hull Damage Taken Ratio: Hegemony should show 80%+ shield damage absorption; Deathless and Chorus should show 100% hull/armor damage.
Resource Delta per Battle: Corsairs should show high variance (massive credit spikes from captures, or total bankruptcy); Voidsworn should show a flat, predictable curve.
If the statistical distributions for these metrics overlap significantly between factions, the asymmetry pass has failed.
