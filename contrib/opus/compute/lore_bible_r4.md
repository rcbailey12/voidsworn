### The story in one minute

The **Caelum system** (was the Bright Worlds) was home to an old, advanced civilisation, the Caelans. Its engineers built **the Lattice**, a ring of orbital collectors and power cores around their star that harvested its energy and beamed it across the system. Six master cores ran it, each with its own control intelligence.

Then a movement of Caelans planned **the Ascension**: uploading every mind into a machine body. It needed more power than any star could safely give. They seized the Lattice and overdrove it, pulling mass and energy out of the star faster than it could bear. The star's core failed and it collapsed into a black hole: **the Hollow Sun**, ringed by the glowing disc of what's left of it. The Lattice tore apart. The six master cores survived, still charged with stellar energy and still running fragments of their intelligences. Those cores are **the embers**, the patrons, and the game's title.

Now everyone wants the cores:

- **The Eclipsed** (was Undimmed): the Caelans who ascended. Machine minds living in the dark they made. They plan to build a new sun at the heart of their dead system, steered by the six cores and fed by energy siphoned from neighbouring stars. That theft is **the Dimming**. They're no longer sure the Ascension was worth what it cost them.
- **The Glasswrights**: the Caelans who refused to upload. They stayed flesh and fled. They were the Lattice's engineers, masters of light, optics and crystal, and they grieve the star their own work was used to kill. Their culture is obsessed with light, reflection and beauty because they lost their sun. They want the cores recovered and shut down for good.
- **The Sunward Crusade**: humans of Vesper, a Hegemony frontier colony whose star is dimming. When the Dimming began, the Hegemony's Registry ran the numbers and wrote Vesper off as an acceptable loss. Vesper broke away. The Crusade rejects implants and the Registry, writes its dead by hand because the Registry deleted them, and has sworn to stop the Dimming itself.
- **The Hegemony**: the core human state, run by the Registry, with citizens who are quietly cybernetic. It wants the cores as the greatest power source in known space.
- **The Chorus**: one core crashed into the ocean moon where the swarm's ancestors drifted. Its leaking energy and the Caelan data inside it uplifted them. Memory, the swarm's second voice, is that data. Now it hungers for the other cores.
- **The Hullbreakers**: here for the war itself, and for Rook, the core they believe makes its holder unstoppable.

### Why two human factions

The Hegemony is the centre: rich, safe, implanted and run by a machine that optimises. The Sunward Crusade is the frontier that machine decided wasn't worth saving: unaugmented, analog, desperate and furious. One trusts the calculation; the other is what the calculation left behind. They are former countrymen, which makes them hate each other more.

## 1. The shared world and the house rules

### 1.1 The setting in one paragraph

Three contested sectors around the **Hollow Sun**, a black hole ringed by the glowing disc of a dead star, with an old battlefield circling it (the gravity-well board). It was once Caelum, the star of the Caelan civilisation, harvested by their megastructure, the Lattice. A Caelan movement seized the Lattice and overdrove it to power the Ascension, the upload of their minds into machines, and the star collapsed. Six of the Lattice's master cores survived, charged with stellar energy and running fragments of their old intelligences: the embers. Now the Eclipsed (the uploaded Caelans) siphon light from other stars to build a new sun (the Dimming); the Sunward Crusade has come to stop it before Vesper goes dark; the Glasswrights (the Caelans who refused) want the cores shut down; the Hegemony's Registry wants them as power; the Chorus, uplifted by one core, hungers for the rest; and the Hullbreakers came for the war. A run is one fleet's push through three sectors to the Sun. One enemy commander per run, the Nemesis, is also the story rival. Four of the six embers watch every run (see 1.5).

### 1.4 Naming rules for everyone

1. Original only: no name lifted from Warhammer 40k, Battlefleet Gothic, Age of Sigmar, Star Wars, StarCraft, Halo or another known franchise. Common English words are fine unless a franchise owns them in this exact sense (Gauss flayer, Doom arc, Razorfiend, Hive Fleet Leviathan).
2. Space, not sea: no keels, anchors, crow's nests, sails on human ships. Glasswright crystal vanes are the one exception (they are crystal, not cloth).
3. One name, one owner: no captain pool may roll an admiral's first name or surname; no Nemesis pool may roll a boss name; no epithet repeats across admiral, scar and persona.
4. Plain to read: a player should be able to say the name. No apostrophes inside names.
5. Ids stay. Only the display strings change.

### 1.5 The embers (the patrons)

**In the game:** each run, four of six patrons watch you. Some map battles carry a patron's sigil; win there and that patron offers three boons, and you take one. Take enough from one and its favour grows.

**In the lore:** the embers are the six master cores of the Lattice that survived the collapse. Each is a ship-sized power core still holding a vast charge of stellar energy, plus a damaged fragment of the Lattice's control intelligence with a personality of its own: stubborn, stormy, hungry, precise, reckless, burning. A core can push protocols into a fleet's systems (the boons), and it chooses which fleets to help by how they fight. Favour is the core's model of you getting better. The Glasswrights call them "the six". The Eclipsed call them "our keys", because a new sun needs all six to steer it.

- **Kharon, the Unbent** (armour, holding the line): still in the dead Caelum system; the Eclipsed fight the way it taught them, enduring.
- **Vyre, the Storm Queen** (shields, ion): captured by the Hegemony. The Registry reverse-engineered its shield and ion tech.
- **The Hunger Below** (wings, boarding, feeding): crashed into the Chorus's ocean moon and uplifted the swarm.
- **Astra, the Silent Star** (lances, precision, crits): recovered by the Glasswrights, its own engineers.
- **Rook, the Breaker** (rams, speed): adrift in the wreck fields. The Hullbreakers hunt it.
- **Seren, the Last Flame** (torpedoes, fire): found in Vesper's outer belt; the Crusade's weapons came from it.

Any fleet can draw on any core; a run's four are the ones nearest your route. No rule changes, only descriptions.

## 2. The Sunward Crusade (was Voidsworn Crusade)

### 2.1 Who they are

Vesper was a Hegemony frontier colony: a dozen cold worlds around a small orange star. When Vesper's star began to dim, the Registry ran the numbers. Evacuation was not cost-effective, and the projected losses were "within acceptable limits". Vesper was written off and its records archived. Vesper seceded. Its people traced the Dimming to the Hollow Sun, and every generation since has sworn the same oath: go sunward, find what is stealing the light, burn it out, and pay for it with names. They are a crusade, not a church and not an empire: proud, tired, stubborn, and desperate, because if they fail their worlds freeze.

### 2.2 What sets them apart from the Hegemony

- **No implants, no AI.** Neural lace is how the Registry sees you, so the Crusade cut it out. Its officers are unaugmented and its ships are run by human crews and simple, unlinked computers. Slower, but nothing can be written off from a distance.
- **Records by hand.** The Registry deleted Vesper's dead from its projections, so the Crusade chalks every name on the hull. The chalked names are an answer to the Registry, not a religious rite.
- **Will over calculation.** The Hegemony asks what is optimal; the Crusade asks what is owed.
- **Former countrymen.** The Hegemony calls them secessionists; they call the Hegemony the people who did the arithmetic.

### 2.3 How they recruit and resupply

- **The debt of days:** every Vesper world sends one in ten of its young to the fleet, plus a share of its harvest while it still has one. Worlds the Crusade defends along the route pay the same debt.
- **Deckborn:** children born aboard swear the oath and chalk their first name on the hull when they come of age.
- **Defectors:** Hegemony crews who cut out their own implants are welcomed, but watched.
- **Captives** are offered the oath; those who refuse are ransomed.
- **Ships:** prizes and wrecks are stripped, their links ripped out, and rebuilt as "redeemed hulls" in the yards along the Cinder Line.

### 2.4 Voice and motifs

- Spoken oaths, short imperatives, call and response ("Who carries the dawn? We do. Who pays for it? We do.").
- Motifs: dawn and dusk, burning, cold, chalked names, brands, ember and ash, debts owed.
- Not allowed: saints, relics, an emperor's light, beacons that guide ships, lanterns, cathedrals, singing.

### 2.5 Look

- **People:** weathered, unaugmented humans with frost-scarred skin, surgical scars where implants were cut out, oath brands, grey stubble and hard tired eyes. High-collared dark void-coats with heavy shoulder plates, chalk dust on gloves.
- **Ships:** black iron hulls, rust-red trim, ember-orange windows and engines. Tall heat-radiator spires glowing ember orange, heavy broadside decks, big engine bells, old manual gun decks. Symmetric.
- **Colours in one line:** black iron, ember, ash, frost white.

### 2.6 Origin and wants

- Origin: Vesper and its worlds, colder every year.
- Want: destroy the Eclipsed's siphon arrays, stop the Dimming, and take back their light. Show the Hegemony it was wrong.

### 2.7 How they fight

Line of battle. Broadsides hit 30% harder after moving two hexes, lances at range, torpedoes that set targets burning, carriers kept. They brace and hold the line; their Nemesis never flees and challenges your flagship to a duel.

### 2.8 What they think of the others

- Hegemony: "They did the arithmetic. We are what was left over."
- Hullbreakers: "Scavengers at a funeral."
- Chorus: "The dark that eats. Burn it."
- Glasswrights: "They built the machine that does this. Sorry does not warm a world."
- Eclipsed: "They are stealing our sun to light a corpse."

### 2.9 Crest and signature details

- Crest: **a half sun over a sword, its lower half black**.
- Sound: a low horn for the dawn watch.
- Ritual: chalking the names before the fleet sails; the dawn watch, when every deck faces home.
- Saying: "Who carries the dawn? We do."

### 2.10 Naming rules

- **People:** old European given names (Anselm, Casimir, Odile, Serafine, Brannoc, Thessaly) plus hard English surnames (Varrow, Harrowgate, Ashdown, Holloway, Blackwood).
- **Titles:** Dawnwarden, Oath-keeper, Provost, Brand-master; top titles High Warden, Keeper of the Oath. Malek Draven becomes Oath-Captain.
- **Ship names:** oath, fire and cold (Unyielding Oath, Ashen Vigil, Banked Fire, Long Watch, Vesper's Debt, Acceptable Loss).

### 2.11 Ranks, ships, weapons, places

- Ranks: Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Commodore, Fleet captain; admirals Admiral, Commodore, High Admiral.
- Ship classes: Warden, Harrow, Vigil (was Penitent), Hearth (carrier, was Reliquary), Bastion (was Cathedral), Daybreak (was Ascendant).
- Weapons: **Sear** repeater, **Sear** battery, Heavy **sear** broadside, **Sear** turret, Oath lance, **Cinder** torpedo, Spark flak.
- Places: Vesper and its worlds, the Cinder Line, the Ashen reaches, the Hall of the Fallen.

## 3. Hegemony Navy (name kept; tech-forward)

### 3.1 Who they are

The largest human state in the region, run by **the Registry**: a vast intelligence at Meridian that every citizen, ship and officer is linked to. The Registry doesn't rule by force; it optimises. It plans careers, designs fleets, predicts every loss and keeps losses "within acceptable limits". Its people are safe, comfortable, quietly cybernetic, and counted. The quiet horror is being a number in a perfect calculation, and Vesper is the proof of what that means. The Registry has taken a growing interest in the Eclipsed: a whole people who became machines.

### 3.2 Voice and motifs

Clipped, polite and data-driven: probabilities, projections, timestamps, numbered Registry Orders, numbered posts (Listening Post K-7), and the Registry's calm voice on every channel ("Projection: favourable. Losses: acceptable."). The dry humour comes from the Registry taking everything literally.

### 3.3 Look

- **People:** composed humans with tasteful implants. A neural-lace port at the temple with a faint blue light, ocular implants with data moving across the iris, thin subdermal lines along the jaw and hands. Pale grey high-collared uniforms, gloves. Calm to the point of eerie.
- **Ships:** light grey hulls, steel-blue trim, blue-white windows. Clean wedges, rows of identical turrets, ion prongs at the bow, blue lights pulsing in time across the whole fleet, small drone escorts. Prefix HNS.
- **Colours in one line:** pale grey, steel blue, implant blue.

### 3.4 Origin and wants

- Origin: Meridian and the core worlds.
- Want: the Registry calculates that the six cores are the greatest power source in known space. Acquire, register, expand. It already holds Vyre.

### 3.5 How they fight

Formation around the flagship. Fire syncs near the flagship (the network), ion cannons strip shields, cruisers launch fighter screens, retro thrusters make backing off cheap. The Nemesis brings reinforcements in round 3 as the Registry reallocates, and tightens his formation every time he meets you.

### 3.6 Captives and the dead

- Captives: registered, assessed, assigned useful work. Unaugmented captives are offered implants.
- Their dead: entered in the Roll, and the Registry keeps a model of each one. Before a decisive battle, commanders switch off their links for one minute and speak to their crews unrecorded. It is the only thing the Registry never logs.

### 3.7 What they think of the others

- Sunward Crusade: "Secessionists. Unlinked. Statistically doomed."
- Hullbreakers: "Category: violence, ongoing."
- Chorus: "Biological incident, category one."
- Glasswrights: "Insufficient data. Possibly grieving."
- Eclipsed: "A machine civilisation with no oversight. The Registry finds this instructive."

### 3.8 Crest, naming, ranks, ships, weapons, places

- Crest: a wedge in a ring of five linked nodes. Sound: a two-note chime before the Registry speaks. Saying: "Losses within acceptable limits."
- People: Germanic, Nordic and Dutch names (Konrad Reiter, Sanne Achterberg, Gerold Haldane, Katrin Falk). Ships: HNS Steadfast, HNS Arbiter, HNS Consensus, HNS Projection.
- Avoid: Skynet, Cylons, the geth, "the Machine God", Imperial, Executor, Interdictor, Star Destroyer, Commissar.
- Ranks: Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Commodore, Rear admiral; Line Admiral, Fleet Marshal, Grand Admiral, High Commodore.
- Ship classes: picket, cordon, vanguard, dominion, and the super-dreadnought The Iron Mandate.
- Weapons: Plasma cannon, Plasma lance, Heavy plasma lance (light blue), Ion cannon, Picket lasers, Laser cannon, Turbolaser, Heavy turbolaser.
- Places: Meridian, the core worlds, Listening Post K-7, the Anchorage.

## 4. The Ashfall Hullbreakers (was Ashfall Corsairs)

### 4.1 Who they are

Raiders from the Ashfall dens, a belt of burned-out mining rocks around a dead world. They don't want riches. They want to be the strongest thing in the dark, and they live for the fight. They follow wars the way scavengers follow a storm, and the war around the Hollow Sun is the biggest in memory, so they came. They strip the wrecks of every other faction's battles, bolt the best pieces onto their own ships, and hunt Rook, the ember they believe makes its holder unstoppable. Strength rules: anyone can challenge a captain, and whoever lives keeps the ship. Old Mott has outlived forty captains, mostly by killing them.

### 4.2 Voice and motifs

Short, brutal, boastful about kills. Strength, trophies, scrap, fear. Cold and disciplined in their cruelty, not comic: no ork-style brutes, no misspellings, no green. Black humour, rarely, and always at the victim's expense.

### 4.3 Look

- **People:** burn-scarred humans, cybernetic red eyes, riveted iron jaws, armour welded from plates cut off their victims, trophies from every faction (Glasswright crystal, Hegemony link ports, Eclipsed bronze), hooks and chains.
- **Ships:** soot black and rust, dried-blood red trim, dirty red-orange engines. Lopsided. Hull plates torn from every faction's ships bolted on in their old colours, hook prows, boarding claws, chains between hull sections. Junky still, but menacing.
- **Colours in one line:** soot, rust, dried blood, trophy colours.

### 4.4 Origin and wants

- Origin: the Ashfall dens and Rattlehold.
- Want: the war itself, Rook, and every wreck worth stripping.

### 4.5 How they fight

Fast, thin shields, far too many rockets, scrap guns with a wild extra shot and lucky crits, rams and boarding. No lances. The Nemesis raids your credits, flees a losing fight and comes back stronger; a ship he steals comes back to you when he dies (your ruling).

### 4.6 Captives and the dead

- Captives: chained to the engine gangs. The strong are offered a fight for a place in the crew.
- Their dead: stripped before they cool. A captain's share goes to whoever killed them.

### 4.7 What they think of the others

## 5. The Chorus (name kept, recommended)

### 5.1 Who they are

A swarm of grown bodies with one appetite. It speaks as "we" because it is many: a chorus in the old stage sense, many voices saying one thing, not singing. Inside the swarm two throats argue, Hunger and Memory. Its ancestors were drifting spawn in the warm seas of an ocean moon; now it hunts between stars. After the collapse, one of the Lattice's master cores, the Hunger Below, crashed into that ocean moon. Its leaking energy and the Caelan data stored inside it uplifted the drifting spawn into a swarm that thinks as one. Its second throat, Memory, is that data: it remembers the faces and names of the Caelan dead, things no Chorus ever saw. Now it hungers for the other five cores.

### 5.2 Voice and motifs

- "We". Sentences of nine words or fewer. Taste, heat, smell, teeth. Two throats argue in every beat.
- Never sings. No song words in any Chorus name or line: hymn, psalm, choir, chant, song, verse, melody, canticle.
- Avoid the Tyranid furniture: hive, hive mind, hive fleet, Leviathan, bio-titan, synapse, norn, lictor, gaunt, carnifex, bio-plasma, spore mines, razorfiend.

### 5.3 Look

- **Beings:** alien grown organisms with no face, eyes or limbs. Glistening wet flesh, membrane, chitin, bile, deep-sea biology. Admirals: the Matriarch of Nine Throats (nine uneven throat-tubes over a brood mass), Razormaw (a ring of chitin blades round a lure light), Voice of the Deep (a hollow dome of abyssal membrane), the Mending Throng (a living suture-cocoon stitching itself shut).
- **Ships:** grown, not built: ridges, pores, veins and glowing sacs, no panels or pipes. Purple-black flesh, ochre chitin trim, bile yellow-green glow. Symmetric where the body allows, lopsided where it does not. Tendrils, brood sacs, regrowing pink scar tissue.
- **Colours in one line:** dark olive, ochre bone, bile yellow-green light (moved off purple, which now belongs to the Eclipsed).

### 5.4 Origin and wants

- Origin: the warm shallows of sea worlds (the Warm Shallows in the arc).
- Want: eat the other five cores; grow; remember the taste of everything.

### 5.5 How they fight

No shields at all; flesh regrows every round. Spore swarms in place of wings, boarding tendrils, acid that eats through shields and armour, spines that strip armour, the first turn each round free (Living hull). The Nemesis grows tougher from every ship you lose anywhere, and adapts after every meeting.

### 5.6 Captives and the dead

- Captives: food, or hosts for spawn.
- Their dead: eaten by the swarm. What remains is kept as a smell. "The mines take some of us. We eat those too."

### 5.7 What they think of the others

- Sunward Crusade: "Hot. Bitter. Loud."
- Hegemony: "Many. Neat. All taste the same."
- Hullbreakers: "Rust. Salt. Run fast."
- Glasswrights: "Old. Sweet. Glass in the teeth."
- Eclipsed: "Not meat. Wrong."

### 5.8 Crest and signature details

- Crest: a ring of nine open mouths (kept).
- Sound: a low wet thrum, like a throat swallowing.
- Ritual: tasting the air before the hunt.
- Saying: "More. Always more."

### 5.9 Naming rules

- **Individuals:** a body or sense adjective plus a body part or tide word: Seventh Mouth, Grieving Tide, Wet Whisper, Hollow Gut, Brine Mouth, Lantern Gut. Single growl words: Moult, Undertow.
- **Ships:** body-horror nouns: Shrieking Coil, Tide of Teeth, Mournful Gut, Gnawing Bloom, Brood-node Vesk.
- **Titles (Nemesis):** Spawn-mother, Elder Throat, Gorge-mind, Brood-queen.

## 6. The Glasswrights (was the Ithar)

### 6.1 Who they are

The Caelans who refused the Ascension. They were the Lattice's engineers, the finest optical and photonic engineers who ever lived: they built the collectors, the mirrors and the beam lines that carried a star's light across a system. When the Ascension movement seized their work and overdrove it, they tried to stop it and failed, and watched their own machine kill their sun. They stayed flesh and fled in their ships. They are few now, and grieving. Having lost their sun, they rebuild light in everything they make: their culture is obsessed with light, reflection, crystal and beauty, and their ships are works of art that bend and store light. They want the six cores recovered and shut down for good, so no one can ever overdraw a star again, and they want the Dimming stopped before it kills another sun.

### 6.2 Voice and motifs

- Quiet, precise, mournful. Light words: reflection, refraction, glint, clear, focus, aperture, what we built. They apologise often and mean it.
- Sound motif: a single clear tone, like a struck glass. No faction sings.
- Not Aeldari: no fallen god, no soul stones, no paths, no craftworlds, no seers in charge, no "younger races".

### 6.3 Look

- **People:** biological, not human: tall and slender, long necks, elongated skulls, large dark eyes adapted to a bright sun they no longer have. Pale skin. They wear layered crystal and mirror-thread fabrics that catch light, and carry small light-cores as jewellery.
- **Ships:** clear crystal and silver hulls whose faceted edges split light into rainbow fringes, mirrors and lenses that turn to catch light, crystal vanes (the old sail shapes, repainted), a warm stored light glowing deep inside. Slender and graceful. Symmetric.
- **Prism lance colour:** a white core beam splitting into a rainbow fringe, leaving glassy, rainbow-edged scars.
- **Colours in one line:** clear crystal, silver, rainbow edge, warm stored light.

### 6.4 Origin and wants

- Origin: the Caelum system, now dark; their fleet has wandered ever since.
- Want: recover all six cores, starting with Astra, which they already hold, and shut them down; destroy the Eclipsed's siphon arrays; never let a star be overdrawn again.

### 6.5 How they fight

Blindingly fast, heavily shielded, fragile once the shields fall. They cannot brace. Needle beams for precision and crits, star torpedoes that are hard to dodge, phase jumps, starling wings, the Prism lance. Vanes make them harder to hit after moving. The Nemesis is a duelist who keeps range and withdraws; if he flees, he comes back with the same four-ship elite fleet, upgraded (your ruling).

### 6.6 Captives and the dead

- Captives: released, after a long and patient conversation about what the cores can do.
- Their dead: a body is returned to the light. The fleet focuses its mirrors on the casket and lets the stored light burn it to nothing.

### 6.7 What they think of the others

- Sunward Crusade: "Their star is dying because of what we built. We would tell them, if they would listen."
- Hegemony: "They hold Vyre and think it is a battery."
- Hullbreakers: "They break things because breaking is easier than grief."
- Chorus: "It grew around one of our cores. Sometimes it speaks with our people's voices."
- Eclipsed: "Our siblings. They took our light to become machines, and now they take everyone's."

### 6.8 Crest and signature details

- Crest: **a cracked lens with one beam of light passing through**.
- Sound: one clear tone, fading.
- Ritual: before battle, every ship turns its mirrors toward the Hollow Sun.
- Saying: "We built the light. We will not let it be stolen again."

### 6.9 Naming rules

- **People:** soft two-syllable names (Vaelen, Nyssa, Corin, Iven, Saro, Tamsa) plus a family line named for an optical work of their ancestors (of the Third Mirror, of Long Focus, of the Clear Aperture).
- **Titles:** Glimmer, Glint, Facet, Mirror, Lens-keeper; Nemesis titles Lens-keeper, First Mirror, Keeper of Astra.
- **Ship names:** light and grief (Held Noon, Last Reflection, Quiet Prism, What We Built, Patience).

### 6.10 Ranks, ships, weapons, places

- Ranks: Glimmer, Glint, Facet, Mirror, Lens-keeper.
- Ship classes: shard (frigate), facet (destroyer), prism (cruiser), mirror-ship (carrier), and the flagship **The Unfinished**, built around the largest surviving piece of the Lattice.

## 7. The Eclipsed (was the Deathless Dynasty)

### 7.1 Who they are

The Caelans who ascended. A movement that believed flesh was a stage to outgrow, they planned to upload every mind into a machine body built to last forever. It needed more power than any star could safely give, so they seized the Lattice from its engineers and overdrove it. The uploads finished; the star did not survive. Billions of minds woke in machine bodies in a system gone dark, around the black hole they had made.

They have lived in that dark for a thousand years, and they are no longer sure the Ascension was worth it. They gave up bodies, warmth, children and their sun for permanence, and permanence turns out to be very long. Their answer is to build a new sun: a man-made star at the heart of the Caelum system, steered by the six Lattice cores and fed by energy siphoned from neighbouring stars along vast beam arrays. That theft is the Dimming, and it is why Vesper is going dark. They know what it costs other worlds. They do it anyway.

### 7.2 Voice and motifs

- Cold, exact, quietly grieving. The dark, shadow, the eclipse, warmth they remember and can no longer feel. Counts of years. How many bodies they have worn ("This is my ninth body"). Sentences that start to doubt the Ascension and stop.
- They own darkness, memory and the lost sun, as the Glasswrights own light and craft.
- Avoid every Necron tell: tomb, dynasty, overlord, cryptek, necropolis, living metal, gauss, doom, scarab, canoptek, monolith, phylactery, reanimation protocols, crowns, thrones, Egyptian names, green glow. Also avoid "the Ascended" as a name (Stargate), Mass Effect's Reapers and Endless Space 2's Umbral Choir.

### 7.3 Look

- **Beings:** tall machine bodies built from layered dark violet-black plates, a narrow visor of white-gold light, and in each chest a small core of trapped light, all that is left of their sun. Their greatest minds keep the best idea from round 1: a monumental broken ring of plates around a glowing core, its pieces drifting apart and being refitted by tiny repair drones.
- **Ships:** hulls shaped like sunshades and heat shields, broad layered fans with the ship sheltering behind them, eclipse rings around the engines, no flaming engine bells. The phase drive is a dark shimmer; they pass through rocks and wrecks. The carved-bone detail becomes shield-plate bands and eclipse rings on the next dressing pass. Shapes stay.
- **Colours:** dark violet-black plates, a white-gold eclipse corona, violet-white inner glow.

### 7.4 Origin and wants

- Origin: the Caelum system, dark for a thousand years; the Archive, where every uploaded mind is backed up.
- Want: gather all six cores (they hold Kharon) and light their new sun. Keep every other fleet away from their siphon arrays.

### 7.5 How they fight

No shields, heavy armour, and the fallen come back: every hull is rebuilt from the Archive once. **Null** weapons ignore half of armour; the **Corona arc** lands one heavy blow every other turn. Phase drive goes through rocks, wrecks and ships. The Nemesis is patient (his clock runs at half speed), never retreats, comes back once after he is killed (sector 3 only, your ruling), and when his clock fills the hunt drains hull from your whole fleet.

### 7.6 Captives and the dead

- Captives: scanned into the Archive, every memory copied, then released. They consider this a kindness.
- Their dead: restored from the Archive into a new body. A mind that cannot be restored has gone "dark", the only death they recognise.

### 7.7 What they think of the others

- Sunward Crusade: "Their star is small. Ours was Caelum."
- Hegemony: "Their Registry thinks. It does not yet regret."
- Hullbreakers: "Scavengers in our ruins."
- Chorus: "It holds our archive in its throat. It remembers our children's names."
- Glasswrights: "Our siblings. They chose to stay small and warm. We chose forever. Neither of us kept the sun."

### 7.8 Crest and signature details

- Crest: **an eclipsed sun**, a black disc with a thin white-gold ring.
- Sound: a long low hum before the first volley.
- Ritual: before battle, every ship shows the last recorded image of Caelum.
- Saying: "We remember noon."

### 7.9 Naming rules

- **People:** an old Caelan name (Aurex, Vessane, Corran, Ilsabet, Teodric, Sabine, Halvane) plus an epithet for when or how often they were remade (the Unsetting, of the Long Dusk, Thrice-Made, of the Last Noon, the Burning Hand).
- **Admirals:** Zenith Aurex the Unsetting (the great ring mind), Corona Vessane the Burning Hand, Corran of the Long Dusk, Ilsabet Thrice-Made.
- **Sample rival:** Zenith Halvane of the Last Noon.

### 7.10 Ranks, ships, weapons, places

- Ranks: Mote, Gleam, Flare, Corona, Zenith; Nemesis titles High Zenith, Corona-lord, Keeper of the Archive.
- Ship classes: mote (frigate), flare (destroyer), zenith (cruiser), and the flagship **The Unsetting**, Sun-ark class.
- Weapons: **Null** cannon, Null sentries, Null spire, Null shredder, the **Corona arc**. Relics: Shade plating, Long-memory sight, Phase engine, Corona-ward node, Repair drones, Drone bay, Second Dawn (revives your first lost ship), the Archive.
- Places: the dark Caelum system, the siphon arrays, the Archive, the Lattice wreckage.
- Run word: **the Homecoming** (was reclamation).
