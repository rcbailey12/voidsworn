# A2 engine test: the shared spec

Plan: `contrib/opus/go-forward-plan.md`, phase A2. One scene, built twice: once in Babylon.js 8.20 (today's engine), once in Godot 4.7.2 (installed portable on Ryan's PC, `Downloads\voidsworn_test\godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe`). Both builds follow this file exactly, so the comparison is fair. Neither build reuses the game's existing effects or shaders: Babylon gets no head start.

The barge's painted files come from Blender, whose glTF export already turns z-up into y-up, but the barge was built with its bow at -x. So rotate the barge 180 degrees about y, and its bow points +x like the others. Check by eye in the first screenshot: all six bows point the same way.

## Layout

- Scale every ship uniformly to a length of 24 units.
- Two rows of three, all bows toward +x.
  - Row 1 (z = +30): Voidsworn at x = -45, Hegemony at x = 0, Corsair at x = +45.
  - Row 2 (z = -30): Chorus at x = -45, Ithar at x = 0, Deathless at x = +45.
- Ship centres at y = 0. No board, grid or terrain.

## Background and light

- Background: `starfield_4k.png` in this folder (equirectangular, generated by `make_starfield.py`, same file in both engines), shown as the sky. It also gives a dim ambient term: ambient strength 0.25 of the key light, neutral colour.
- Key light: directional, from direction (x -0.45, y -0.70, z -0.55) toward the scene (so it comes from upper left, front), colour (1.0, 0.96, 0.90). Intensity: whatever makes the Hegemony hull (light grey) read near-white but not clipped in the close-up.
- Fill light: directional, opposite side (x 0.6, y -0.2, z 0.75), colour (0.55, 0.65, 0.85), 25% of the key.
- Tone mapping ACES in both. Exposure 1.0. Bloom: on, threshold about 0.8, moderate.
- Shadows: the key light casts shadows in both, one cascade set covering the formation, comparable resolution (2048 or 4096).

## Effects (cinematic bar, built fresh, same spec, equal time box in each engine)

Ryan, Oct 25, on the first barge effects: "The cracks in the ship don't make sense, it's just orange lines... It'd be like holes with streaming parts, fire, other damage. Not like someone just welded it. The weapon effects look pretty basic still, especially compared to our references of BattleTech, Sins of a Solar Empire and Rogue Trader. Same with engine trail. It should feel more realistic and cinematic." So the effect set in this test is built to that bar in both engines, and the result shows which engine gets closer. Grammar to follow (from contrib/muse/design/GRAPHICS_REFERENCES.md, sections on Sins of a Solar Empire II, BattleTech and MechWarrior, Rogue Trader and the Star Citizen damage notes):
- weapons read by rate, trail, count, smoke and timing, not only colour; volleys ripple (desynced), never fire as one blob;
- impact flash, debris and shake scale with weapon weight; the big guns stay special;
- damage is staged on the hull (scorch, then torn holes) with venting, sparks and fire at the breach, and parts that come off leave torn edges with squibs, never a clean swap;
- destroyed ships leave a drifting wreck with fires and debris, not a vanishing act;
- one key light, fixed exposure; effects add light (bloom, emissive, light flashes on the hull), they do not change exposure.

The ships drift forward slowly (along +x at 3 units per second, looping over 40 units with a 2-second fade at the loop point) so engine trails have something to trail behind.

Per ship, a 4-second loop staggered by 0.5 s in the layout order:

1. **Engines (always on).** A hot core at each nozzle (white-yellow centre, faction-tinted edge), a turbulent plume about 1.5 ship-widths long that flickers, and a long fading exhaust trail behind the ship (about 60 units, thinning and cooling in colour as it fades, slightly wavering). Corsair: sputters, coughs soot, uneven.
2. **Broadside macro-cannons (Rogue Trader style), at 0.0 s:** four guns along the port side ripple-fire 80 ms apart. Each: a big muzzle blast (flash, a flame tongue, a smoke ring that drifts and lingers about 2 s), a bright shell tracer travelling 160 units per second toward a point 70 units off the port side, and at impact a flash, sparks and a small debris spray. Faction tint on flash and tracer.
3. **Lance or beam (Sins style), at 1.5 s:** a thick beam from the bow to a point 80 units ahead, 0.7 s: white-hot core, faction-coloured glow, a charge-up flare at the emitter (0.25 s before), a heat shimmer or flicker along it, and at the far end a bright impact flare with a spark fountain.
4. **Shield or hull hit, at 2.4 s:** the ship is struck on the starboard side by an incoming shell (draw the shell arriving). Shielded factions (Voidsworn, Hegemony, Corsair, Ithar): a partial shield bubble ripples out from the hit point in the faction shield colour with a bright impact core, fading over 0.6 s; Corsair's shield flickers and is patchy. Chorus and Deathless: a hull hit, a flash, sparks and a burst of debris.
5. **Hull breach (persistent, from the start):** every ship carries one breach on its upper port flank, about 1/10 of the ship's length across: a dark scorched, torn hole (a decal plus a dark recess) with jagged glowing-hot edges (not lines across the hull), venting gas streaming out (a pale jet that wavers and puffs), small debris tumbling out of it, licking fire, intermittent spark showers, and a small secondary explosion every few seconds.
6. **Destruction event (once per 12 s, scene-wide, not per ship):** a seventh ship, a copy of the Hegemony hull at 0.6 scale placed at (0, 25, 0) above the formation, explodes: a blinding flash (lights the nearby hulls for a moment), an expanding shockwave ring, a rolling fireball with dark smoke, 3 to 5 large hull chunks (pieces of its own mesh, cut with simple planes, or prepared chunk meshes) tumbling apart with burning edges and squibs along the break, a spray of small debris and embers, and a burning wreck chunk that drifts on. It reappears whole 2 s before the next event (fade in).

Faction colours (linear RGB), from the game's ships3d.js FAC3D:

| Faction | Weapon (bolt, beam, flash tint) | Shield | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voidsworn | (1.0, 0.45, 0.15) | (1.0, 0.46, 0.16) | (1.0, 0.50, 0.16) |
| Hegemony | (0.55, 0.82, 1.0) | (0.38, 0.68, 1.0) | (0.45, 0.72, 1.0) |

Equal time box: each engine's build gets the same effort for effects. Stop polishing when the list above is covered at a good level; the comparison is the point, not perfection in one engine.

## Cameras (identical poses in both, so screenshots line up for the blind pick)

Vertical field of view 40 degrees in both.

- **Battle camera path** (benchmark): a 20-second orbit around the formation centre (0, 0, 0). Radius 150, height 70, starting at azimuth 200 degrees (measured from +x toward +z), turning 120 degrees over the 20 s at an even speed, always looking at (0, -5, 0).
- **Close-up** (stills only): camera at (+20, 14, +58) looking at the Corsair barge centre (+45, 0, +30).

Screenshots (fixed clock, all effects on, t = 0 when the scene starts): battle camera at t = 5.0 s and t = 12.0 s (the destruction event peaks at about 12.4 s, so t = 12.0 catches its flash starting); the barge close-up at t = 2.0 s (its broadside smoke and tracers, and its breach); a breach close-up of the Hegemony ship from (12, 10, 52) looking at (0, 2, 30) at t = 3.0 s; and the destruction event from the battle camera pose at t = 12.6 s. Use the same frame times in both engines, driven by a fixed clock (not wall time) for the stills.

## What each build must measure and write

A command-line run that needs no clicking. Each run writes one JSON file:

```
{"engine": "godot" | "babylon", "renderer": "forward_plus" | "gl_compatibility" | "webgl2" | "webgpu",
 "width": 3840, "height": 2160, "seconds": 20, "frames": N,
 "avg_ms": .., "p99_ms": .. (the worst 1% of frame times, averaged), "max_ms": .., "fps_avg": ..,
 "load_s": .. (process or page start to the first frame with all six ships and the sky shown),
 "gpu_mem_mb": .. (engine's own counter if it has one; the PC job also samples nvidia-smi), "notes": ".."}
```

Runs on the PC (the job loops these): 3840x2160 and 2560x1440, for Godot Forward+, Godot Compatibility, Babylon WebGL2. The benchmark uses the battle path with the effect loop running, after a 3-second warm-up that is not counted. VSync off where the engine allows (Godot `--disable-vsync`; Babylon in Chromium with `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit`), so frame time is real.

Babylon runs in Chromium through Playwright with the real GPU (the PC is an RTX 5080; launch args like fxshot.py's GPU mode, never the software `--use-angle=swiftshader` closeshot uses).

## Stress run (added Oct 25, Ryan: "makes me wonder if we have a ton more room to scale things")

The six-ship benchmark is a light scene, so each build also has a stress mode (`--stress`), measured the same way (3 s warm-up, 20 s battle orbit, JSON with `"mode": "stress"`):
- 24 ships: the six flagships stay exactly where the main layout puts them. Add 18 copies of the flagships scaled to 0.6 (cruiser size) in three extra rows at z = +60, 0 and -60, six per row at x = -60, -36, -12, +12, +36, +60, factions in the order Voidsworn, Hegemony, Corsair, Chorus, Ithar, Deathless along each row. Same orbit centre, radius 190, height 85.
- Every ship runs the full 4-second effect loop and its breach, staggered by 0.17 s in layout order so effects overlap all the time.
- The destruction event fires every 4 s instead of every 12.
- Report avg, p99 and max frame time; the worst 1% matters more than the average (it is the stutter you feel).
Runs on the PC at 2560x1440 and 3840x2160 for each renderer.

## Ceiling round (added Oct 25, Ryan: "should we do anything to scale up now, especially as we compare engines")

At 24 ships both engines have room to spare, so the differences show at the top end. Two extra modes, measured like the stress run (JSON `"mode"` as named):
- **Ship sweep (`--ships N`, modes `ships48`, `ships96`):** the stress layout extended with more rows of 0.6-scale copies (rows every 30 units in z, alternating sides of the formation, six per row, same x positions), all running the full loop and breach, destruction every 4 s. Orbit radius grows with the formation (radius = 150 + 40 per extra row pair, height = radius x 0.45). Report where each renderer stays under 16.7 ms for the worst 1%.
- **Rich effects (`--rich`, mode `rich`, on the 24-ship stress layout):** what extra headroom would buy:
  - real dynamic lights from every muzzle flash, impact, breach fire, beam and explosion, lighting nearby hulls (aim for 40+ moving lights at once; if the engine cannot, say how many it manages and how);
  - volumetric smoke or gas for breach venting, smoke rings and the destruction fireball (true volume, or the engine's closest built-in);
  - soft particles (effects fade where they meet hull geometry instead of hard-clipping);
  - breach holes with real depth (geometry or parallax, not only a decal).
  Stills in rich mode at the same five times and poses, written as `rich_<name>.png`, so Ryan gets a second "max quality" side-by-side.
Equal time box again. Note in the README what each feature needed (built-in, plugin, hand-written) and anything the engine could not do.

## Max-detail round (added Oct 25, Ryan: "scale up the ships' graphic detail and effects now in the comparison between engines. Not just amount of ships but level of detail in them." Picks: everything at maximum; today's faction looks, richer; one combined reveal)

**Shared assets (built once, the same files in both engines, so the comparison stays fair):** each of the six flagships gets a max-detail version in `enginetest\assets\hi\`:
- geometry raised well past today's (2 to 4 times the triangles): subdivision where it sharpens the shape, plus hand-style surface detail in the barge's manner (tools/blender/a1/detail_lib.py), following the CLAUDE.md ship rules (space not naval, matched pairs, snapped on, nothing floating; Chorus organic, not panels);
- 8K PBR textures: albedo (today's faction colours with panel variation, wear, grime, markings), normal, occlusion/roughness/metal, emissive (windows, running lights, engine glow);
- the barge keeps its signed-off look, re-baked at 8K with emissive running lights added.

**Max mode (`--max`, mode `max`):** the rich-effects mode on the hi assets, plus each engine's best detail features within the same time box: ambient occlusion (SSAO or better), reflections (SSR or probes), global illumination if the engine has it (Godot SDFGI or VoxelGI; Babylon image-based lighting from the sky), a tiling micro-detail normal layer for close cameras, contact shadows, temporal anti-aliasing. Note in the README what each needed and what the engine could not do.

**Hero stills (max mode only, fixed clock t = 3.0):** one close-up per ship, camera at (ship x - 8, 6, ship z + 20) for row 1 and (ship x - 8, 6, ship z - 20) for row 2, looking at the ship centre (with the drift offset at t = 3.0), vertical field of view 40. Files `hero_<code>.png`. Plus the five standard stills as `max_<name>.png`.

**Benchmark:** max mode on 24 ships at 1440p and 4K, and the ship sweep (48, 96) in max mode, same JSON. The blind side-by-side shows today's mode and max mode together.

## Push round: physics, presentation and the game slice (added Oct 25, Ryan: "truly push what's possible for each engine now given the intent of the game to compare. Level of detail, how it handles the effects, anything graphical, physics, presentation, etc")

The test is no longer a fair minimum: each build shows the best its engine can do for this game, inside an equal time box. Use the engine's strongest native tools (Godot: Jolt physics, GPU particles, FogVolume, SDFGI, Control UI, compositor effects; Babylon: Havok plugin, node materials, GPU particles, its post-process pipelines, GUI or HTML overlay). Note what was native, what was a plugin and what was hand-written.

**Physics (in max mode):**
- every debris piece, hull chunk and wreck from impacts, breaches and the destruction is a real rigid body that tumbles, collides with ship hulls (simplified or convex colliders) and with other debris, and keeps drifting;
- the destruction's chunks are pushed apart by the blast (an impulse from the centre), spin and strike nearby hulls;
- in the 96-ship sweep, aim for 1,000 or more live debris bodies; report the physics step time per frame alongside the frame time.

**Presentation (in max mode):**
- camera work: a "cinematic" path option (`--cine`) with smooth dolly moves, a focus pull with depth of field onto the barge, a short slow-motion kill-cam on the destruction (0.3 times speed for 1.5 s), screen shake on big hits scaled by weapon weight;
- lens and grade: motion blur if the engine has it, lens flare or lens dirt on the brightest flashes, colour grading (a LUT or curves), vignette, subtle film grain, light shafts from the destruction flash if possible;
- one key light and fixed exposure still apply (effects add light; they do not change exposure).

**Game slice (`--board`, the game's own view):** the battle seen the way Voidsworn plays: a top-down tactical camera (pitch about 55 degrees) over a hex board (flat-topped hexes, about 2.4 units across, 20 by 14) under the formation, with:
- crisp grid lines, a movement-range highlight on one ship, a firing arc wedge, and the enemy reach shading as soft fills;
- hover outline on the ship under the mouse and a selection ring on click (record the frames from click to visible ring);
- the engine's native UI: ship name labels, hull and shield bars, a small top bar and a tooltip, sharp at 4K and at 1440p;
- the ships, effects and physics running underneath. Stills `board_<name>.png` at t = 3 and 12.6.

**Shipping it:** export a standalone Windows build (Godot: an exported .exe; Babylon: an Electron package, or a browser page if Electron is out of the time box) and report its size on disk and its cold start time to the first frame.

All of this is measured and shown with the rest: one combined blind reveal (R18).
